Travelling with dogs and cats to Turkey: official requirements guide 2026

⚠ Standing rule · Turkey · 22/07/2026

Turkey requires rabies antibody titration with a 3-month wait — even though it is the only European country with dog-mediated rabies. The laboratory must be approved by the European Union, not by Turkey. Many commercial aggregators claim otherwise and mislead travellers. Read about the serology laboratories →

How long does it take to bring a dog or cat to Turkey?

From scratch, roughly four months. Turkey regulates entry as a third country with its own certificate: an ISO microchip before the vaccine, an inactivated rabies vaccine, a rabies antibody titre ≥ 0.5 IU/mL at a laboratory approved by the European Union with the sample taken at least 30 days after the vaccine, and —the point almost everyone misses— entry is only allowed 3 months after that blood sampling. A maximum of two animals per passenger.

The most common mistake: believing the commercial search engines that say Turkey "does not ask for a titre". The official document requires it, and gives its own example: vaccine on 01/04, earliest sample on 01/05, earliest entry on 01/08. It also bans the entry of six dog breeds. Requirements by country of origin — Quick guide

Unlike destinations such as Japan, Turkey does not publish a system of origin-country lists (high/low risk, designated/non-designated). The official document applies the same requirements —including titration— regardless of the country of departure. The exceptions do not depend on the country but on the animal's own history.

ONE CASE — Third countries, wherever you leave from
Full mandatory protocol: ISO microchip before the vaccine, an inactivated rabies vaccine, a rabies antibody titre with a 0.5 IU/mL threshold at a laboratory approved by the European Union, the sample taken at least 30 days after the vaccine, and a 3-month wait before entry, plus the official veterinary certificate of origin (Turkish EK-2 model). Where you leave from —Peru, Colombia, India, South Africa or anywhere else— does not change the file; it is endorsed by that country's veterinary authority (in Peru, SENASA).
EXCEPTION — Animal with a valid prior titre
If the animal's passport shows an acceptable rabies titre and vaccination kept up without lapses, the official document does not ask for the test again. And an animal of Turkish origin returning with a titre obtained before leaving is not subject to the 3-month wait.

Verify live before any operation: https://vskn.tarimorman.gov.tr/ (veterinary border control posts).

Global classification of the destination

Regulatory model

A third country with its own veterinary certificate. Turkey does not apply EU Regulation 576/2013 and does not issue the EU passport: it requires its own certificate of origin and health (EK-2 model), bilingual Turkish-English, issued and endorsed by the official veterinary authority of the country of departure. The EU passport works as the animal's identity document but does not replace the Turkish certificate. Legal basis: Regulation on non-commercial movements of pet animals, published in the Official Gazette on 05/12/2011 (No. 28133), under Law 5996.

Rabies status of the destination country

Not free of dog-mediated rabies. Turkey is the only European country where dog-transmitted rabies persists, according to the EU Reference Laboratory for Rabies at the WOAH GF-TADs regional conference (Belgrade, September 2025). This is the paradox of the destination: it requires titration of the animals entering while being itself a territory with dog-mediated rabies — which is why the European Union does not exempt Turkey from the test on the way back.

Origin-country classification system

No Turkish system of origin-country lists was found. Requirements apply uniformly regardless of departure. The only reference to origin is in the regime for unvaccinated puppies under 3 months, where authorisation is assessed case by case "taking into account the rabies situation of the country the animal is brought from". Any third country therefore follows the full protocol.

Requirements for dogs

Microchip

— Required standard: identification by a clearly legible tattoo or a microchip; if a microchip, a passive transponder compliant with ISO 11784 (HDX or FDX-B) and readable by an ISO 11785 reader. If the chip does not comply, the person responsible must provide the reader at inspection.
— Must it be implanted before the vaccine? Yes. The official document states that the rabies vaccination date "cannot be earlier than the microchip application date".
— If the chip was implanted later: the subsequent vaccination is the valid one; a vaccine given before the chip does not count.

Rabies vaccine

— Mandatory: Yes.
— Accepted type: an inactivated vaccine with at least one antigenic unit per dose, or a recombinant one expressing the rabies virus glycoprotein. Modified live-virus vaccines are not accepted. It must appear in the OIE/WOAH manual.
— Timing: the vaccination date cannot be earlier than the microchip date; the vaccination date counts as day 0.
— Revaccination: if the booster is given outside the previous vaccine's validity, it counts as a "first vaccination" (the clock restarts).
— Validity: that of the vaccination certificate, reflected in the EK-2 certificate.

Rabies antibody titration (FAVN/RNATT)

— Mandatory: Yes, as a general rule and not conditioned on a country list.
— Minimum threshold: 0.5 IU/mL of neutralising antibodies.
— Laboratory: approved by the European Union for rabies (not a Turkish list). Check: the list of EU-designated laboratories.
— Sampling: taken by an authorised vet at least 30 days after the last vaccination.
— Wait after sampling: 3 months. Entry may take place at the earliest 3 months after the sampling date. The official document's own example: vaccine 01/04 → earliest sample 01/05 → earliest entry 01/08.
— Document: the ORIGINAL titration report is presented at the border.

Scientific reference

The 0.5 IU/mL threshold alone does not guarantee a successful file; neutralising-antibody kinetics depend on the sampling window. And not every "reference" laboratory is on the list Turkey recognises: ask for an EU-approved lab. We document both points in our study on rabies serology and titre validation (RFFIT/FAVN) — see the FAVN technical resource.

Other vaccines required for entry

Not identified by name in the primary regulation consulted. The document generically requires the passport to show "the vaccinations and medicinal applications against other diseases specific to the animal's species", without listing them. The specific lists (distemper, hepatitis, leptospirosis, parvovirus) found in commercial sources should not be held as a legal requirement without confirmation.

That said, each origin-country health authority requires its own full vaccination scheme to certify export, regardless of what the destination asks. Without that scheme, the certificate of origin is not issued.

Antiparasitic treatments

The EK-2 certificate includes sections for treatment against ticks and against Echinococcus, both labelled "when required" (with product name, manufacturer, and date and time of application). The Turkish entry-conditions document does not list them as mandatory and does not publish the criterion that activates the "when required". Other countries' authorities differ (the US recommends them within the 30 days before travel; Canada states Turkey does not require them). Prudent conduct: apply and document them — over-documenting does not invalidate the certificate. Confirm with the Turkish authority for the specific case.

Health certificate

— Official model: certificate of origin and veterinary health (EK-2), bilingual Turkish-English.
— Issuer: the official veterinary authority of the country of origin; if signed by an authorised vet, the competent authority must endorse it.
— Validity: 4 months from signature/endorsement, or until the recorded vaccination expires, whichever comes first.
— One certificate per animal: multiple pets require individual certificates.
— In Peru, the endorsement is by SENASA; in each origin country, the equivalent national authority.

Official entry document

No prior import permit or advance notification requirement was found for non-commercial movement accompanied by a passenger. The three documents the official document requires at the border are: (1) the animal's passport or identity document; (2) the ORIGINAL rabies titration test; (3) the veterinary health certificate (EK-2 model). Each document is issued separately for each animal.

Mandatory digital forms

No mandatory digital pre-registration portal was found for pet entry. (The Ministry's PETVET system is for registering animals resident in Turkey and issuing the Turkish passport, and appears in exit —not entry— documentation.) Confirm with the authority.

Quarantine

As a general rule, there is no quarantine when the documentation is complete and compliant. It may be triggered in specific cases —for example, a missing valid titre, or puppies under 3 months— but the foreign official sources do not agree on the period (the US mentions 21 days; Canada, up to 6 months for puppies). The Turkish document found does not set quarantine periods. Confirm the exact scenario with the Turkish authority before travelling.

Breed restrictions

Yes. Turkey bans the ENTRY of six dog breeds determined as dangerous: American Pitbull Terrier, Dogo Argentino, Fila Brasileiro, Japanese Tosa, American Staffordshire Terrier and American Bully. Basis: Law 7332 (Official Gazette 14/07/2021, No. 31541), which amended article 14 of Law 5199 on Animal Protection, and the Ministry's circular of 07/12/2021. Any source listing only four breeds is out of date: American Staffordshire Terrier and American Bully were added by the December 2021 circular.

Minimum age for entry

There is no absolute minimum age. There is a 3-month threshold: unvaccinated puppies under 3 months may only enter if the conditions set by the Ministry are met, assessing the rabies situation of the country of origin (a discretionary decision, with no guaranteed outcome). In practice, for a puppy starting from scratch, the titration requirement with its 3-month wait after a vaccine that itself needs ~30 days beforehand means the realistic effective minimum age comfortably exceeds 5–6 months.

Mode of transport

A maximum of 2 animals per passenger (the official document gives the example: two cats, or one cat and one dog). The Turkish health regime does not regulate whether the animal travels in the cabin or the hold: that is up to the airline's policy and the IATA Live Animals Regulations (IATA LAR). A 2021 notice from a border post conditioned entry on the animal travelling on the same aircraft as the passenger, but justified it on the COVID-19 pandemic; it is probably obsolete, though no repeal was found. Confirm before planning a shipment as cargo.

Requirements for cats

The Turkish regime is the same for cats and dogs: the official document and the EK-2 certificate treat cats, dogs and ferrets together. The microchip before the vaccine, the inactivated vaccine, the titration (0.5 IU/mL + 30 days + 3-month wait), the EK-2 certificate, the three border documents and the maximum of 2 animals per passenger all apply identically.

Breed restrictions — cats

There is no Turkish list of cat breeds banned from entry: the Law 5199 ban was applied only to six dog breeds. Note the asymmetry: the Van cat and the Turkish Angora are regulated, but the other way around — a restriction on LEAVING, not entering (see "Did you know…?").

Other vaccines — cats

As with dogs: a generic requirement of species-appropriate vaccination without a list. The mention of FVRCP comes from secondary sources, not the primary regulation.

Variations by country of origin

The situation from Peru, Latin America and countries with dog-mediated rabies

Let's start with the case we are asked about most: Peru. And it serves as the pattern for everyone —it makes no difference whether you leave from Colombia, Mexico, Brazil, India, the Philippines or South Africa— because being Peru does not change the documentary file: with no Turkish country list, the requirement is the same as from the US, the EU or any country where rabies is still present. What changes is the execution: the certificate of origin is issued/endorsed by SENASA (it is worth confirming with SENASA that the EK-2 model is available before committing to dates), and the serum sample must be sent to an EU-approved laboratory, with a cold chain and traceability to the microchip. Critical path: microchip → vaccine (≥30 days) → sampling → +3 months → entry; realistic minimum ≈ 4 months from a virgin microchip.

The same protocol, wherever you leave from

Because Turkey does not publish an origin-country list, the full file —ISO microchip, rabies vaccine, a rabies antibody titre ≥ 0.5 IU/mL at an EU-approved laboratory and a 3-month wait— is identical for every third country. And it is precisely the file faced by the origins where dog-mediated rabies is still present. It applies the same whether you travel from any of these:

Latin America & the Caribbean: Peru, Colombia, Ecuador, Bolivia, Brazil, Mexico, Argentina, Chile, Paraguay, Uruguay, Venezuela, Guatemala, Honduras, El Salvador, Nicaragua, Costa Rica, Panama, Dominican Republic, Cuba, Haiti.

Asia: India, China, the Philippines, Indonesia, Vietnam, Thailand, Cambodia, Laos, Myanmar, Malaysia, Nepal, Sri Lanka, Bangladesh, Pakistan, Mongolia.

Africa: South Africa, Morocco, Algeria, Tunisia, Egypt, Nigeria, Ghana, Senegal, Ivory Coast, Kenya, Ethiopia, Tanzania, Uganda, Angola, Zimbabwe, Mozambique.

Middle East, Caucasus & Central Asia: Iran, Iraq, Jordan, Lebanon, Syria, Saudi Arabia, Georgia, Azerbaijan, Armenia, Kazakhstan, Uzbekistan, Afghanistan.

Europe outside the European Union: Russia, Ukraine, Serbia, Bosnia and Herzegovina, North Macedonia, Albania, Moldova.

In all of these origins, as third countries outside the EU —and mostly territories where dog-mediated rabies is still present—, the same full file applies for Turkey: microchip, vaccine, a rabies antibody titre ≥ 0.5 IU/mL at an EU-approved laboratory and the 3-month wait. Transit through the European Union

Decisive for flights that route through Europe: if the itinerary transits the EU, an EU transit health certificate is usually required in addition to the Turkish certificate. Plan the route with this in mind.

EU → Turkey and the return trip

The EU passport is not enough on its own: the EK-2 is required all the same. And titration remains mandatory, with its 3-month wait — which surprises Europeans used to moving within the EU without the test. Moreover, the return Turkey → EU is an entry into the Union from a third country, and since Turkey is not among the countries exempt from the test for EU purposes, the titre is also needed to come back.

Common mistakes

MISTAKE 1: Believing Turkey "does not ask for a titre"

What happens: search engines and commercial aggregators claim Turkey does not require titration from most countries.
Regulatory consequence: you arrive without the test and without having observed the 3-month wait; the animal does not meet the entry requirement.
How to prevent it: follow the official Turkish document, which requires the titre and the wait. This is the point where third-party information is most often wrong.

MISTAKE 2: Vaccinating before implanting the microchip

What happens: the certificate shows a vaccine dated before the chip.
Regulatory consequence: the earlier vaccination does not count; it restarts from the microchip date.
How to prevent it: physically check the chip with an ISO reader before any vaccination.

MISTAKE 3: Sending the sample to a lab not approved by the EU

What happens: an international reference laboratory not on the European Union list is used.
Regulatory consequence: the titre may not be recognised by Turkey.
How to prevent it: confirm the laboratory is on the EU list before taking the sample.

MISTAKE 4: Travelling with a banned breed

What happens: a ticket is bought for an American Staffordshire, an American Bully or another of the six banned breeds, trusting outdated lists of four.
Regulatory consequence: entry prohibited under Law 7332; there is no file that resolves it.
How to prevent it: check the breed against the current list of six before any step.

MISTAKE 5: Miscounting the 3 months

What happens: the wait is counted from the vaccine or the result, not from the sampling date.
Regulatory consequence: you arrive too early and do not meet the requirement.
How to prevent it: count the 3 months from the sampling day, and book the flight with margin.

The clinical file

A rejection at the border is rarely due to the animal's biology, but to a break in documentary traceability. We address the taxonomy of documentary error and how to build an unassailable file in the technical article on the export documentary chain.

Minimum calendar from scratch

Scenario — Animal with no prior history

— Day 0: ISO microchip implantation + first inactivated rabies vaccine.
— Day 30 (minimum): serum sample for the titre and dispatch to an EU-approved laboratory (the sample must be at least 30 days after the vaccine).
— Day 45 (approx.): result ≥ 0.5 IU/mL received.
— Day 120 (approx.): end of the 3-month wait from sampling; earliest date fit for entry.
— Minimum total time: roughly 4 months from the microchip. Add the time for the origin authority to issue and endorse the EK-2 certificate and the flight logistics.

Note: if the animal already has a microchip, current vaccination and a valid titre recorded in its passport without lapses, the exemption applies and the test is not repeated.

Turkey has a trap that almost no commercial guide documents well: they will tell you it "does not ask for a titre" because many neighbouring countries enter with less, yet its own official document requires titration and a three-month wait from the sampling date. The logic is not arbitrary: Turkey is the only European territory where dog-mediated rabies is still present, so it shields its border with the same test the European Union applies to third countries. The second surprise is the laboratory: not just any "reference lab" will do, it must be on the EU list. And the third, invisible to anyone who does not export: its native dogs and cats cannot leave without permission. Verify before you move a single piece.

🐾 Did you know…? Turkey, its dogs and its cats

  • Turkey protects its native breeds from leaving, not from entering. Its regulation on native animal genetic resources states, in article 4(2), that "without Ministry permission, they cannot be taken abroad for any reason". Registered animals may accompany their owner under conditions and a specific form. It is the mirror image of import restrictions.
  • The Kangal is the livestock guardian of central Anatolia (Sivas province). Press coverage of the Ministry's 2021 communication places among the breeds subject to an exit permit the Kangal, the Akbaş, the Kars shepherd, the Çatalburun —a split-nosed hunting dog— and also the Ankara and Van cats; the exact list is set by a National Committee.
  • 🐱 The Van cat swims. The Van kedisi, white and often with heterochromia (one blue eye and one amber), is conserved institutionally at the Van Cat Research Centre of Yüzüncü Yıl University. Adopters receive a record that notes, among other things, the colour of each eye and the animal's hearing status.
  • 🐱 The Turkish Angora is preserved in Ankara. The Ankara kedisi, long-haired, is bred at a municipal conservation centre in Ankara, with pure-bred specimens recognised by the international feline federation.
  • In 2021 Turkey stopped treating animals as "things". Law 7332 (Official Gazette 14/07/2021) moved offences against cats and dogs into the Penal Code and took owned animals out of the "damage to property" regime: protection now reaches the stray animal too, whether or not it has an owner.

Frequently asked questions about your case

Every pet journey is unique. These are the most common doubts — and why we always recommend checking your particular situation.

Are the health requirements the same for every destination country?

No. Each country sets its own demands: vaccines, laboratory tests, official certifications and waiting times that in many cases cannot be shortened under any circumstances. What is valid for one destination may be insufficient or even incompatible with another. Before starting any procedure, assess your case with us.

Do all airlines accept pets on international flights?

No. Policies vary by airline, route, aircraft type and season, and change frequently. Brachycephalic breeds face additional restrictions on most international carriers. Before buying your ticket, check the exact compatibility for your route and pet.

How far in advance should I start the international travel process with my pet?

It depends on the destination. Some protocols include laboratory tests with mandatory waiting periods that cannot be compressed. Starting late may invalidate documentation already obtained or force a full reschedule. Write to us and we'll work out your real timeline together.

What happens if my pet is refused at the destination's customs?

The animal may be held at airport facilities, sent to mandatory quarantine at the owner's expense, or repatriated. At destinations with strict biosecurity the consequences can be even more serious. Documentary verification before travel is the only way to avoid this scenario. Contact us before you travel.
