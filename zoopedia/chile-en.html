Chile Pet Import Requirements (Dogs & Cats) – Official 2026 Guide

How long does it take to bring a dog or cat to Chile?

Chile is one of the fastest destinations from Peru: the SAG resolution requires neither an antibody titer test (RNATT) nor quarantine. Since 27 July 2026 it requires permanent identification (ISO microchip or tattoo), a valid rabies vaccine, internal and external antiparasitic treatment between 5 and 30 days before the certificate is issued, and a SENASA-endorsed health certificate. Anyone entering with a pet must also declare it on the SAG sworn declaration (ingresoachile.cl).

What surprises most people: Chile applies independent zoo-sanitary regulations despite sharing a border with Peru. The SAG (Servicio Agrícola y Ganadero) sets its own requirements, and the resolution in force recalls that Chile declared itself free of canine rabies (variants V1 and V2) in 2010. In case of non-compliance, the SAG may apply sanitary measures at the point of entry, at the cost of the person responsible. Zoovet Travel manages the full process from Peru.

Requirements by country of origin — Quick guide

Chile applies the same requirements to all countries of origin. The only difference concerns the rabies vaccine: the resolution exempts animals that have lived for at least six months in a country free from dog-mediated rabies recognised by Chile. From Peru all requirements apply. Identify your case before continuing.

CASE 1 — From countries free from dog-mediated rabies recognised by Chile
The resolution exempts from the rabies vaccine animals that have stayed since birth, or for at least six continuous months, in one of those countries, as certified by its veterinary authority under the OIE/WOAH Terrestrial Code. We have not found a published list of the countries Chile recognises, so we cannot confirm whether Australia, New Zealand or the United Kingdom are included. The health certificate, the identification and the antiparasitic treatments are required all the same. Verify directly at: https://www.sag.gob.cl/ambitos-de-accion/ingreso-de-perros-gatos-y-hurones-mascotas-animales-de-compania-tradicionales
CASE 2 — From Peru and all other countries (most of Latin America)
Peru, Colombia, Ecuador, Brazil, Venezuela, Bolivia, Paraguay, Uruguay, Mexico and other countries. They require permanent identification (ISO microchip or tattoo), a valid rabies vaccine, internal and external antiparasitic treatment between 5 and 30 days before the certificate is issued, official health certificate with SENASA endorsement, and the SAG sworn declaration, completed at https://ingresoachile.cl up to one day before arrival. The resolution does not require RNATT.

Global classification of the destination

Regulatory model

Model C: no general quarantine, with measures at the border. Chile does not require routine quarantine. Pets enter directly after SAG verification at the entry point, provided they meet current health requirements. In case of non-compliance, the SAG may apply the sanitary measures established by law, at any authorised point of entry and at the cost of the person responsible (SAG Resolution N° 3.343/2026, amended by N° 5.444/2026).

Rabies status of the destination country

Free of canine rabies (variants V1 and V2) since 2010, as the SAG resolution itself states. On 14 September 2026 the WHO validated Chile for eliminating dog-transmitted rabies as a public health problem. The virus is still present in bats.
More information: WOAH — https://www.woah.org/en/disease/rabies/

Country-of-origin classification system

No. The resolution sets the same requirements for all countries of origin. The only exception is the rabies vaccine: animals that have lived since birth, or for at least six continuous months, in a country free from dog-mediated rabies recognised by Chile are exempt. We have not found a published list of those countries; as long as that recognition is not on record, all the requirements apply from Peru, Colombia, Ecuador, Brazil, Venezuela, Bolivia, Paraguay, Uruguay and Mexico (see SAG's specific pet direct-import standards).
Verification URL: https://www.sag.gob.cl/ambitos-de-accion/ingreso-de-perros-gatos-y-hurones-mascotas-animales-de-compania-tradicionales

Requirements for dogs

microchip

— Identification: mandatory since 27 July 2026, individual and permanent, by microchip (ISO 11784/11785) or tattoo (SAG Resolution N° 3.343/2026, point 1.2).
— The number must appear on the certificate and match the animal's.
— Before or after the vaccine? The resolution does not set that order. Recommendation: identify before vaccinating, so that each vaccine is linked to the number.

Rabies vaccine

— Mandatory: Yes (SAG Resolution N° 3.343/2026, point 1.3).
— Minimum age: the resolution sets none; it applies whatever the animal's age.
— SAG deadline: first-vaccinated animals must be vaccinated at least 21 days before entry. An animal counts as first-vaccinated if it receives its first dose or if its last dose was given after the previous one had expired.
— If not first-vaccinated: the certificate must show the last two doses; otherwise the animal is treated as first-vaccinated.
— Ministry of Health deadline: Decreto 1 of 2014 (art. 6) treats as current a vaccine given at least 30 days and at most 12 months before entry. Both rules are in force.
— At Zoovet Travel, pets travel with all their vaccines up to date and within the required deadlines.

Rabies antibody titer test (RNATT)

The SAG resolution does not require a rabies antibody titer test for the entry of pets (SAG Resolution N° 3.343/2026). Decreto 1 of 2014 of the Ministry of Health (art. 6) accepts, instead of the vaccination certificate, an antibody titration of at least 0.5 IU/ml carried out within the 12 months before entry.

✓ No mandatory RNATT. The SAG resolution does not require a rabies antibody titer test.

Other vaccines

The 2026 SAG resolution requires only the rabies vaccine for entry: it does not list other vaccines among its requirements. The multiple scheme (six-in-one or eight-in-one for dogs: Distemper, Parvovirus, Infectious canine hepatitis, Leptospirosis and Parainfluenza) belongs to export from the country of origin. At Zoovet Travel, pets travel with all their vaccines up to date.

SENASA — Peru's official veterinary authority — also requires this same complete and valid vaccination scheme to issue the International Export Health Certificate. Verify directly at https://www.gob.pe/senasa before starting the process from Peru.

Antiparasitic treatments

Mandatory, both internal and external (SAG Resolution N° 3.343/2026, point 1.4).

— Product: broad-spectrum antiparasitics, effective against internal and external parasites, approved by the competent authority of the country of origin and at the dose recommended by the manufacturer. The resolution does not name any particular parasite.

⚠ The window is counted up to the issue of the certificate, not up to entry: the treatment must be applied between 5 and 30 days before the certificate is issued.

— Mandatory time window: between 5 and 30 days before the international veterinary certificate is issued.
— What the certificate must show: date of treatment, trade name, active ingredient and dose of the internal and external antiparasitics.
— Who applies and documents it: licensed veterinarian in the country of origin.

Health certificate

— Official name: Official Export Health Certificate.
— Who can issue it: official health authority of the country of origin. In Peru: SENASA (https://www.gob.pe/senasa). In Colombia: ICA. In Ecuador: AGROCALIDAD.
— Validity time window: 10 days from official issue until entry to Chile.
— Does it require endorsement? Yes, by the national health authority of the country of origin. In Peru: SENASA (https://www.gob.pe/senasa).
— SAG reference URL: https://www.sag.gob.cl/ambitos-de-accion/ingreso-de-perros-gatos-y-hurones-mascotas-animales-de-compania-tradicionales

Official entry document

— Exact name: Official Health Certificate from the country of origin that meets SAG requirements.
— Who issues it: SENASA in Peru based on the requirements of SAG Resolution N° 3.343/2026.
— Time window: 10 days from issue.
— Reference URL: https://www.sag.gob.cl/ambitos-de-accion/ingreso-de-perros-gatos-y-hurones-mascotas-animales-de-compania-tradicionales

Mandatory digital forms

Yes. Every person over 18 entering Chile must complete the SAG sworn declaration, and anyone entering with a pet must declare it. The digital form can be completed up to one day before arrival; the SAG states that the declaration is made in digital or paper format.
Official URL: https://ingresoachile.cl

⚠ Declare your pet. The digital form can be completed up to one day before arrival and does not need to be printed.

Quarantine

— Mandatory: No. The resolution sets neither quarantine nor home confinement.
— In case of non-compliance (for example, antiparasitic treatment outside the window or an expired vaccine), the SAG may apply sanitary measures at the authorised point of entry, at the cost of the person responsible, without prejudice to any applicable penalties.

Breed restrictions

There are no breeds prohibited from entering Chile. Once in the country, Law N° 21.020 on Responsible Pet and Companion Animal Ownership applies, which establishes handling conditions for certain breeds considered potentially dangerous. Consult directly with Chile's Ministry of the Interior for post-entry conditions.

Minimum age for entry

SAG Resolution N° 3.343/2026 sets no minimum age for entry. The rabies vaccine is required whatever the animal's age, at least 21 days before entry for first-vaccinated animals; Decreto 1 of 2014 of the Ministry of Health states that the first vaccination is given once the animal is two months old.

Transport mode

Chile allows cabin baggage (PETC), hold (AVIH) and Manifest Cargo. The SAG resolution does not regulate the transport mode. Verify with the airline the conditions for the specific route from Peru.

Variations by country of origin

Requirements differentiated by origin? No, apart from the rabies vaccine exemption for countries free from dog-mediated rabies recognised by Chile.

Risk classification system

The resolution does not classify countries by risk. It provides a single exemption, from the rabies vaccine, for animals from countries free from dog-mediated rabies whose status is certified under the WOAH Terrestrial Code and recognised by Chile (SAG Resolution N° 3.343/2026, point 1.3.4).
Verification URL: https://www.sag.gob.cl/ambitos-de-accion/ingreso-de-perros-gatos-y-hurones-mascotas-animales-de-compania-tradicionales

Non-listed countries of Latin America

From Peru all the requirements of the resolution apply. Rabies vaccine is mandatory. No mandatory RNATT. Certification is the responsibility of SENASA (https://www.gob.pe/senasa). The same applies to: Colombia, Ecuador, Brazil, Venezuela, Bolivia, Paraguay, Uruguay and Mexico. Export certification in each case is the responsibility of ICA in Colombia, AGROCALIDAD in Ecuador, MAPA Brasil SDA in Brazil, SENASICA in Mexico, SENASAG in Bolivia, SENACSA in Paraguay, MGAP-DGSG in Uruguay and INSAI in Venezuela.

Situation of rabies-free countries

Australia, New Zealand and United Kingdom. Chile still requires an official health certificate and antiparasitic treatments even for these countries. The resolution exempts from the rabies vaccine animals from countries free from dog-mediated rabies recognised by Chile; we have not found the list of those countries. Verify directly with SAG before starting the process from any of these countries.

Transit through third countries

The SAG resolution does not regulate transit through third countries. If your route has stopovers, check with SAG before travelling.

What sets Chile apart from all other destinations in this collection

The SAG resolution does not require RNATT from any country of origin: no 90 days of waiting, no 180 days, no accredited laboratories to find. What it does require with precision are the deadlines: the rabies vaccine in time, the antiparasitic between 5 and 30 days before the certificate is issued, and a certificate valid for only 10 days.

Common mistakes

ERROR 1: Antiparasitic treatment outside the 5 to 30 day window

What happens: the owner applies the deworming more than 30 days before the certificate is issued, or less than 5 days before, thinking that any recent date is valid.
Regulatory consequence: the animal does not meet point 1.4 of the resolution. The SAG may apply sanitary measures at the point of entry, at the cost of the person responsible (SAG Resolution N° 3.343/2026).
How to prevent it: count the window from the date the certificate will be issued, not from the travel date.

ERROR 2: Poorly documented antiparasitic treatment

What happens: the certificate does not show the date of treatment, the trade name, the active ingredient or the dose of the internal and the external antiparasitic.
Regulatory consequence: the resolution requires those details to appear on the certificate; without them the requirement is not proven.
How to prevent it: use broad-spectrum antiparasitics approved in the country of origin and check that all four details are written on the certificate.

ERROR 3: Absence of microchip number on the certificate

What happens: the official certificate from SENASA or another Latin American authority does not include the 15-digit ISO microchip code, or the transcribed number has an incorrect digit.
Regulatory consequence: the certificate number must match the animal's; in case of non-compliance, SAG may apply sanitary measures at the point of entry, at the cost of the person responsible.
How to prevent it: verify that the official veterinarian transcribes the chip number exactly before SENASA endorses the certificate.

ERROR 4: Not declaring the pet on the SAG sworn declaration

What happens: the owner reaches the control without having completed the SAG sworn declaration, or completes it without declaring that they are entering with an animal.
Regulatory consequence: the SAG warns that failing to declare is associated with penalties and fines.
How to prevent it: complete the form at https://ingresoachile.cl up to one day before arrival and answer “yes” to entering with animals.

ERROR 5: Rabies vaccine applied less than 21 days ago

What happens: the owner vaccinates the animal days before the flight thinking it is already protected and can travel immediately.
Regulatory consequence: the animal does not meet point 1.3.2 of the resolution; the SAG may apply sanitary measures at the point of entry, at the cost of the person responsible (SAG Resolution N° 3.343/2026).
How to prevent it: vaccinate more than 30 days before entry, which meets both the SAG's 21 days and the 30 days of Decreto 1 of 2014 of the Ministry of Health.

ERROR 6: Presenting private health certificate without official authority endorsement

What happens: the document signed only by the private veterinarian without the official SENASA stamp is presented at SAG control.
Regulatory consequence: immediate rejection. Only the certificate issued or endorsed by the official health authority of the government of the country of origin is valid (SAG).
How to prevent it: obtain the official endorsement at SENASA (https://www.gob.pe/senasa) before travel. The private certificate without endorsement has no value before SAG.

Minimum calendar from scratch

Scenario A — Animal with identification and valid vaccines

— Day 0: application of broad-spectrum internal and external antiparasitic treatment by licensed veterinarian.
— From day 5: obtain clinical health certificate from veterinarian (the examination must take place within the 10 days before the official certificate is issued).
— From day 5: process Official Export Health Certificate at SENASA (https://www.gob.pe/senasa) with official endorsement. 10-day validity window.
— Up to one day before arrival: complete the SAG sworn declaration at https://ingresoachile.cl.
— Travel to Chile within the certificate's 10 days of validity.
— Minimum set by the Chilean rule: 5 days between the antiparasitic and the issue of the certificate, with vaccines already valid.

Scenario B — Animal with no prior history, from scratch

— Day 0: identification with ISO microchip + rabies vaccine, with the rest of the vaccines up to date.
— Day 21: the SAG minimum for first-vaccinated animals is met.
— Day 30: the 30-day minimum of Decreto 1 of 2014 of the Ministry of Health is met.
— Between 5 and 30 days before the certificate is issued: internal and external antiparasitic treatments.
— After day 30: clinical certificate and processing of the Official Health Certificate at SENASA (https://www.gob.pe/senasa).
— Up to one day before arrival: SAG sworn declaration at https://ingresoachile.cl.
— Minimum Scenario B: 30 days from the vaccine if both Chilean rules are to be met.

🐾 Did you know…? Chile, its dogs and its cats

  • The Chilean Terrier (or Chilean Fox Terrier) is Chile's first native dog breed, emerging in the late 19th century.
  • It came from crossing the Smooth Fox Terrier with local ratters, to hunt rats in cellars and stables.
  • It is the faithful "Washington," Condorito's dog in the classic Chilean comic.
  • 🐱 The güiña (or kodkod) is the smallest wild cat in the Americas (~2 kg), native to central and southern Chile; its name comes from Mapudungun.
  • 🐱 It lives in Chile's temperate forests and is threatened by habitat loss; conservationists are working to protect it.
  • 🐱 Chilean Nobel laureate Pablo Neruda dedicated his "Ode to the Cat" (1959) to it, describing the cat as the only "complete" animal, "born completely finished, it walks alone and knows what it wants."

Frequently asked questions about your case

Every pet journey is different. These are the most common questions — and why we always recommend consulting your specific situation.

Are health requirements the same for all destination countries?

No. Each country sets its own requirements: vaccinations, laboratory tests, official certifications and waiting periods that in many cases cannot be shortened under any circumstances. What is valid for one destination may be insufficient or incompatible with another. Before starting any procedure, evaluate your case with us.

Do all airlines accept pets on international flights?

No. Policies vary by airline, route, aircraft type and season, and change frequently. Brachycephalic breeds face additional restrictions on most international carriers. Before purchasing your ticket, check the exact compatibility for your specific route and pet.

How far in advance should I start the international pet travel process?

It depends on the destination. Some protocols include laboratory tests with mandatory waiting periods that cannot be shortened. Starting too late may invalidate already obtained documentation or force a complete trip reschedule. Write to us and we'll calculate your actual timeline together.

What happens if my pet is rejected at customs in the destination country?

The animal may be held at airport facilities, sent to mandatory quarantine at the owner's expense, or repatriated. In destinations with strict biosecurity measures, consequences can be even more severe. Documentary verification before travel is the only way to prevent this scenario. Contact us before you travel.
