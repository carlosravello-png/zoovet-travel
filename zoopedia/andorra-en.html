Travelling with dogs and cats to Andorra: official 2026 requirements guide

How do you bring a dog or cat to Andorra?

Andorra has no international airport or entry point of its own, so your pet physically enters via Spain or France, where the European Union framework applies. Andorra has EU-equivalent status (Decision 1/2005 of the EC-Andorra Joint Committee): the border-to-Andorra leg is intra-Community, with no quarantine if the requirements are met. From Peru (a non-listed country) the path is the full protocol: ISO microchip before the vaccine, rabies vaccine, RNATT ≥ 0.5 IU/mL at an EU-designated laboratory (sample 30 days after the vaccine), a 90-day wait from collection, and a health certificate endorsed by SENASA. From scratch, about 7 months.

The layer no generic guide tells you about: Andorra adds its national regime for potentially dangerous dogs — 15 breeds that require TWO mandatory training courses, a licence, insurance and a muzzle (Decret 285/2021) — and registration of the microchip in the RAC. Zoovet Travel coordinates the entire process from Peru.

Requirements by country of origin — Quick guide

As the pet enters via Spain or France, what decides your path is your country of origin under the EU framework. Identify your case.

CASE 1 — From European Union member countries
Germany, France, Italy, Portugal, Spain and all other member states. Simpler process: ISO microchip 11784/11785 and European pet passport with current rabies vaccination. No RNATT or waiting period. Direct crossing of the Spain/France-Andorra border.
CASE 2 — From EU-listed countries (low risk)
United States, Canada, Mexico, Argentina, Chile, Japan, Australia, United Kingdom, Emirates, Switzerland, Norway, San Marino, Monaco and other listed territories. Exempt from RNATT and waiting period. They require ISO microchip, current rabies vaccination and health certificate. They enter via the Spanish/French PEV and cross into Andorra.
CASE 3 — From non-listed countries (high risk)
Peru, Colombia, Ecuador, Brazil, Bolivia, Venezuela, Paraguay, Uruguay, and most of Africa, Asia and the Middle East. Full protocol mandatory: ISO microchip, rabies vaccine, RNATT ≥ 0.5 IU/mL, 90-day wait from sample collection, and health certificate with official endorsement from the authority of the country of origin (SENASA in Peru). Enters via a Spanish or French PEV and reaches Andorra by road. The rest of this sheet develops Case 3.

What no guide tells you: Andorra has no airport

Andorra is one of the few countries in the world without an international airport, and it does not appear on the list of Travellers' Points of Entry (PEV) of the Spanish MAPA. That is why a pet arriving from a third country does not "land in Andorra": it first enters through a PEV in Spain or France, where the EU veterinary control is carried out, and from there travels by road to the Principality.

  • Via Spain: flight to Barcelona-El Prat (BCN), control at the PEV, and about 3 hours by road to Andorra via La Seu d'Urgell and the Riu Runer / la Farga de Moles border.
  • Via France: flight to Toulouse-Blagnac (TLS), control at the French PEV, and a road journey to the Pas de la Casa border.

Since Andorra has EU-equivalent health status (Decision 1/2005 of the EC-Andorra Joint Committee), that last Spain/France → Andorra leg is considered intra-Community movement: if your pet already passed the PEV control with documentation in order, there is no second import control at the Andorran border.

Requirements for dogs (from a non-listed country, e.g. Peru)

Microchip

— Required standard: ISO 11784 / 11785 (15 digits).
— Must it be implanted before the vaccine? Yes. Identification must precede rabies vaccination for the vaccine to be valid under European regulations.
— If the chip was implanted afterwards: the vaccine loses validity and all deadlines must be restarted.

Rabies vaccine

— Mandatory: Yes.
— Minimum age for vaccination: 12 weeks.
— Minimum post-vaccine period: 21 days for primary vaccination before drawing the RNATT sample.
— Validity: according to the manufacturer's technical datasheet (1 to 3 years), with no immunisation gaps.

Rabies serological titre (RNATT)

— Mandatory: Yes, for animals from Peru and all non-listed Latin American countries.
— Minimum threshold: 0.5 IU/mL.
— Minimum post-vaccine period to draw the sample: 30 days.
— Post-collection waiting period: 90 days (3 months) from the sample collection date until entry via the Spanish or French PEV. Counted from collection, not from the result date.
— Laboratory: must be on the official list of laboratories designated by the European Commission (EU list). There is none in Peru: the sample travels abroad. ⚠ And worth knowing before you pick a laboratory: there is no single list — each authority keeps its own, and they do not always agree. Scientific Reference

The 0.5 IU/mL threshold does not by itself guarantee the success of the file; the kinetics of neutralising antibodies depend on the collection window. We document it in our study on rabies serology (RFFIT/FAVN) — see FAVN technical resource.

Other vaccines required for export

European entry regulations do not require vaccines beyond rabies. But SENASA —Peru's animal health authority— does not issue the International Health Certificate without a complete and current vaccination scheme: for dogs, Distemper, Parvovirus, Infectious canine hepatitis, Parainfluenza and Leptospirosis. The same logic applies to every Latin American animal health authority of origin (see the full table on our Spain sheet).

Health certificate and entry document

— Official harmonised EU model (Regulation (EU) 2026/705), for non-commercial movement from third countries.
— Issuer/endorsement: official veterinarian of the country of origin; in Peru, SENASA signs and stamps.
— Language: at least Spanish (or French, depending on the PEV of entry).
— Validity window: 10 days from signature until control at the first EU entry point.

Quarantine

There is no routine quarantine for pets that meet all requirements. It is conditional: applied only as a corrective measure for documentary or health non-compliance detected at entry control, at the owner's expense.

Breed restrictions — Andorra's national layer (PPP)

Decret 285/2021, of 1 September 2021, is one of the most demanding regimes in Europe. It recognises 15 potentially dangerous breeds (and their crosses): American Akita, Akita Inu, American Bulldog, Bullmastiff, Staffordshire Bull Terrier, Dobermann, Dogo Argentino, Dogue de Bordeaux, Fila Brasileiro, Presa Canario, Neapolitan Mastiff, American Pit Bull Terrier, Rottweiler, American Staffordshire Terrier and Tosa Inu.

What makes Andorra unique: every new owner must pass TWO mandatory training courses before owning the dog — one at an authorised training centre and another on the regulations, delivered by the Cos de Banders, with assessment. In addition: licence, civil liability insurance, muzzle and a non-extendable leash of maximum 1.5 m in public spaces, a ban on minors handling it or on walking more than one PPP at a time, mandatory sterilisation of puppies of these breeds before one year of age and a breeding ban.

Minimum entry age

Approximately 7 months for animals from Peru and non-listed countries: 12 weeks minimum age + 30 days post-vaccine for the sample + 90 days waiting period post-collection.

Registration in Andorra: the RAC

The microchip must be recorded in the Registre d'Animals de Companyia (RAC), managed by the Departament d'Agricultura i Ramaderia. If you settle in the Principality, register your pet (non-native animals are usually registered within a maximum of 4 months). There is a registration fee (Llei 14/2012); check the current amount with the Departament — we do not publish a figure we cannot officially confirm for 2026.

Requirements for cats

Microchip, rabies vaccine and RNATT

Same as dogs. The 0.5 IU/mL threshold and the 90-day wait apply equally to cats from Peru and non-listed countries. Rabies vaccination, optional for a cat that only stays in Andorra, becomes mandatory for international movement.

Other vaccines required for export

Entry regulations do not require vaccines beyond rabies. But SENASA requires a complete scheme to issue the certificate from Peru: for cats, Panleukopenia, Feline viral rhinotracheitis (FHV-1) and Feline calicivirus (FCV).

Breed restrictions

The potentially dangerous dog regime does not apply to cats. All domestic breeds are allowed.

Quarantine and minimum age

Same as dogs — see previous section.

Common errors

ERROR 1: Believing you have to fly direct to Andorra

What happens: the owner searches for flights to Andorra and finds none; Andorra has no airport.
How to prevent it: plan entry via the PEV in Spain (Barcelona) or France (Toulouse) and road transfer. The veterinary control takes place at that PEV, not at the Andorran border.

ERROR 2: Counting the 90 days from the result, not from collection

What happens: the trip is scheduled counting 90 days from when the laboratory result arrives, when the counter starts on the blood collection day.
How to prevent it: calculate the eligible date as day 91 from collection, with an additional margin.

ERROR 3: Arriving with a PPP dog without the two courses

What happens: the owner assumes their country's rules and is unaware that Andorra requires two mandatory prior courses, a licence and insurance for the 15 breeds of Decret 285/2021.
How to prevent it: check whether your breed is on the list before moving and coordinate the training with the Cos de Banders.

ERROR 4: Unreadable microchip or outside the ISO standard

What happens: the chip cannot be read with ISO scanners at the Spanish PEV. Common with non-homologated chips.
How to prevent it: verify the reading with two different scanners before issuing any document. SENASA requires this verification for the endorsement.

🐾 Did you know…? Andorra, its dogs and its corner of the Pyrenees

  • The Gos d'Atura Català is the sheepdog of the Pyrenees — the breed of the border area between Spain, France and Andorra, which tended the flocks during transhumance for centuries.
  • Cobi, the mascot of the Barcelona 1992 Olympic Games, was a Gos d'Atura stylised in a Cubist version by designer Javier Mariscal.
  • Andorra is a co-principality: its heads of state are two co-princes — the President of France and the Bishop of the Seu d'Urgell (Spain).
  • It is the only country in the world with Catalan as its sole official language; that is why your pet's certificate is often requested translated into Catalan or English.
  • Andorra is one of the five microstates without an airport (along with Monaco, San Marino, Liechtenstein and the Vatican), and its capital, Andorra la Vella, is the highest in Europe (1,023 m).
  • 🐕 Its law on potentially dangerous dogs is one of the strictest in Europe: it requires TWO training courses before owning the dog, something almost no country asks for.

Frequently asked questions about your case

Every pet journey is different. These are the most common questions — and why we always recommend consulting your specific situation.

Does Andorra have an airport to arrive with my pet?

No. You enter via a PEV in Spain (Barcelona-El Prat) or France (Toulouse) and reach it by road. The border-to-Andorra leg is intra-Community.

Do all airlines accept pets on international flights?

No. Policies vary by airline, route, aircraft type and season. Brachycephalic breeds face additional restrictions. Before purchasing your ticket, check the exact compatibility for your route and pet.

How far in advance should I start the process?

From a non-listed country like Peru, about 7 months from scratch due to the mandatory vaccine and serology timeframes (a 90-day wait that cannot be compressed). Write to us and we'll calculate your real schedule together.

What happens if my pet is rejected at entry control?

It may be held, sent to isolation at the owner's expense or repatriated. Documentary verification before travel is the only way to avoid it. Contact us before travelling.
