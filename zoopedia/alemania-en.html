Travelling with dogs and cats to Germany: 2026 official requirements guide

To enter Germany from a non-listed country, a dog or a cat needs an ISO 11784/11785 microchip implanted before the rabies vaccination, a rabies vaccination administered from 12 weeks of age, a rabies antibody titration equal to or above 0.5 IU/ml in a designated laboratory, with the blood drawn by an authorised veterinarian no earlier than 30 days after the vaccination, a ninety-day wait counted from that sampling and ending on the date the animal health certificate is issued, and the animal health certificate in Annex III to Implementing Regulation (EU) 2026/705, whose validity is set by Article 18(1) of Delegated Regulation (EU) 2026/131, applicable since 22 April 2026. Germany also adds a national layer that the rest of the Union does not have: the Hundeverbringungs- und -einfuhrbeschränkungsgesetz bans the import of four dog breeds and their crosses, and entry must be made through one of the 24 designated points —20 airports and 4 ports— and within the opening hours of the veterinary office covering each one. The federal authority is the BMLEH, Bundesministerium für Landwirtschaft, Ernährung und Heimat. From a country or territory listed in Annex I or Annex II to Implementing Regulation (EU) 2026/636 no titration is required, and the minimum age stands at 15 weeks on our own count: twelve weeks of age plus the twenty-one days of the vaccination's period of validity. Data verified on 29 August 2026.

Requirements by country of origin — quick guide

CASE 1 · You are leaving Peru or another non-listed third country

Peru appears neither in Annex I nor in Annex II to Implementing Regulation (EU) 2026/636. You are on the long route: microchip, rabies vaccination from 12 weeks, antibody titration ≥ 0.5 IU/ml in a designated laboratory and a ninety-day wait from the sampling. Entry compulsory through a designated point of entry. Real minimum age: 204 days, a little under seven months — our own count: 84 days to 12 weeks, plus 30 to the sampling, plus 90 until the certificate can be issued.

CASE 2 · You are leaving a listed third country (Annex I or Annex II)

The European Commission says it without qualification: the titration is not required of animals arriving from a territory or third country listed in Annex I or Annex II to Implementing Regulation (EU) 2026/636. What remains is the microchip, the rabies vaccination from 12 weeks and the 21-day period of validity. Minimum age: 15 weeks — our own count: 12 weeks of age plus 21 days.

But entry through a designated point remains compulsory, and this asymmetry is the detail most people miss: the titration is waived by Annexes I and II; the point of entry is waived only by Annex I. The only thing we verified here about the composition of the lists is that Implementing Regulation (EU) 2026/1228, in force on 19 June 2026, added Montenegro and Serbia to Annex II. To find out whether your country is there, look it up in our table by country of origin and confirm it in the corresponding annex: that is the only source that decides.

CASE 3 · You are leaving a Member State, or Annex I

From a Member State the intra-Union regime applies, which this factsheet does not develop because its file is a different one. And from a third country or territory in Annex I to Implementing Regulation (EU) 2026/636, or from the United Kingdom (Northern Ireland only), there is no need to enter through a designated point: the European Commission and the BMLEH say the same. Check in the annex whether yours is there, because the exception is narrow and does not match «European country». The ban on the four breeds applies just the same, and also to a movement from another Union country.

IN ALL THREE CASES · The German layer

Four dog breeds and their crosses cannot enter Germany by any ordinary route, wherever you are coming from. And in Baden-Württemberg, Bavaria, Hamburg and Hesse there are additional breeds that also cannot enter if the dog is going to live there. We develop this further down, because it is what sets Germany apart from the rest of the Union.

Global classification of the destination

Germany is a European Union Member State, so the first half of the answer is written by the Union and is the same as for Spain, France or Italy: Delegated Regulation (EU) 2026/131, applicable since 22 April 2026, with the document models of Implementing Regulation (EU) 2026/705 and the third-country lists of 2026/636. What decides your route is your country of departure, not your destination.

The second half is written by Germany, and there it is different. On top of the European layer it adds a federal ban on importing four breeds, carrying criminal liability, a second layer of breeds that depends on the Land where the dog is going to live and a closed list of points of entry whose veterinary offices have very uneven opening hours. It is a predictable destination on the European side and a demanding one on the German side, and anyone who only prepares the European part arrives half ready.

Anti-echinococcus treatment is not a general Union requirement: it is required of dogs —not of cats— before entering a specific destination. Those destinations are Finland, Ireland, Malta, the United Kingdom (Northern Ireland) and Norway. Germany is not among them and does not require it. If your flight has a stopover and the final destination is one of those, the treatment is administered by a veterinarian with praziquantel or an equivalent, between 24 and 120 hours before the intended entry, and is certified by the veterinarian who administers it in the corresponding section of the certificate.

Requirements for dogs

Microchip

Injectable transponder complying with ISO standards 11784 and 11785. Annex IV to Delegated Regulation (EU) 2019/2035, in its consolidated version of 22 April 2026, adds a requirement almost nobody publishes: the transponder «shall be assessed in accordance with ISO standard 24631-1 to ensure compliance with ISO standards 11784 and 11785». German customs also point out that a tattoo only counts if it predates 3 July 2011: for any new identification from that date on, the microchip is compulsory.

The order, and the nuance that changed in 2026

The microchip is implanted before the rabies vaccination. If the order is reversed, the vaccination does not count and the animal has to be revaccinated, which drags the blood sampling and the ninety days with it. This is the sequence that suspends the most journeys.

What is new is how it is written. The repealed framework spoke of «transponder or tattoo»; Annex VII, Part 1, point 2(d), of Delegated Regulation (EU) 2020/688 consolidated on 22 April 2026 says that the date of vaccination «is not earlier than the date of identification or than the date of reading of the means of identification». That addition —the date of reading— matters for an animal identified years ago: it is not enough for the chip to exist, there has to be a record of when it was read.

Rabies vaccination

The animal must be at least twelve weeks old at the time of the primary vaccination. The period of validity of the vaccination begins with the establishment of protective immunity, which cannot be less than twenty-one days from the completion of the manufacturer's protocol, and lasts until the end of the period of immunity set by the marketing authorisation for that vaccine in the country where it was administered. A revaccination outside the period of validity counts as a primary vaccination: you start again.

New in the 2026 framework, and worth reading before choosing a clinic: the vaccination must have been administered by an official veterinarian or an authorised veterinarian within the meaning of Article 2(1) of Delegated Regulation (EU) 2026/131, as the competent authority decides.

Antibody titration

Only from a non-listed third country. The blood is drawn at least thirty days after the primary vaccination and the test is carried out in a designated laboratory and must measure a level of neutralising antibodies equal to or above 0.5 IU/ml. If the result comes in below the threshold the animal is revaccinated and that stretch starts again.

Four details of the procedure that get overlooked and cost journeys. The sample is taken by an official veterinarian or an authorised veterinarian —both count—. The test is carried out without undue delay after the sampling. The method has to be one of those prescribed in the rabies chapter of the WOAH Manual of Diagnostic Tests and Vaccines for Terrestrial Animals: the rule names no specific technique, it refers to that manual. And the laboratory issues an official report of the result, a copy of which has to be attached to the animal health certificate: it is not enough for the result to exist, it has to travel with the animal.

Which laboratory counts, exactly

Annex XXI allows two routes, and only two. The first: an official laboratory designated under Article 37 of Regulation (EU) 2017/625 for this test, whose name and contact details the competent authority has communicated to the Commission. The second: a laboratory located in a territory or third country appearing in Annex VIII to Implementing Regulation (EU) 2021/404, designated by the competent authority of that country as complying with Article 37(4) and (5) of 2017/625, and also communicated to the Commission.

That is worth reading slowly, because it decides where the blood is analysed. That second route depends on a different list from the one that decides whether you need a titration: it is not Annexes I and II to 2026/636, it is Annex VIII to another regulation. Check whether your country of departure is on that list before booking the sampling; if it is not, the sample has to be analysed abroad, and that changes the deadlines and the cost. For Peru it is settled: the list of designated laboratories published by the European Commission covers twenty-three non-EU countries, and Peru is not one of them; in the whole of Latin America only Brazil, Chile and Mexico appear. Checked against that list on 29 August 2026. The list is updated, so look at it again before booking the sampling.

And a requirement with a date on it, for anyone planning long term: reports issued from 1 January 2028 will carry a security device in the form of a code allowing their authenticity to be verified on the designated laboratory's own website.

The good news, and it is written precisely: the test does not have to be repeated in an animal that, after a satisfactory result, has been revaccinated within the period of validity of the primary vaccination and of every successive valid vaccination in the series. The condition is that unbroken chain. If the vaccination lapses, the revaccination counts as a primary vaccination and the whole stretch starts again.

The ninety days: where they start and where they end

They start on the date the sample is taken. And they end —and this is where time gets lost— on the date the animal health certificate is issued, not on the date of the flight. Annex XXI, point 1(a), of Delegated Regulation (EU) 2020/692, in its consolidated version of 22 April 2026, requires the titration to have been carried out «not less than ninety days before the date of issue of the animal health certificate accompanying the consignment of animals to the Union».

Germany reads it this way: in our August 2026 sweep, the BMLEH holds the correct count, ninety days up to the issue of the certificate. But the consular page of the Auswärtiges Amt still says «Die Blutentnahme muss mindestens 30 Tage nach der Impfung und mindestens 3 Monate vor der Einreise erfolgen»: at least three months before entry. Those are two differences, not one. «Three months» can be 89, 90, 91 or 92 days depending on which months they fall in —we develop this with the sources in our article on the ninety-day period—, and «entry» is not «the issue of the certificate». Our recommendation is to count ninety calendar days from the sampling and not to issue the certificate before then: that way both readings are satisfied.

Animal health certificate and declaration

The animal health certificate is completed and issued by an official or authorised veterinarian, and has two different periods that do not replace each other. Issuing window: 10 days from issue to the documentary and identity checks at the point of entry, extendable by the crossing time if the journey is by sea. Validity: 6 months from the date of those checks, or until the rabies vaccination expires if that date comes first, under Article 18(1) of Delegated Regulation (EU) 2026/131.

A step that gets forgotten: signed is not the same as finished. After the veterinarian, the competent authority endorses it. Until that endorsement exists, the certificate is not closed.

A written declaration signed by the owner or the authorised person has to be attached to the certificate confirming that the movement is a non-commercial movement (Article 18(3)), following the model in Part 2 of Annex V to 2026/705. It is not a formality: without it, the movement is not non-commercial, and a different regime applies. The copy of the laboratory report travels with the certificate too.

An exception that matters to anyone coming back: the ninety days do not apply to the re-entry of an animal that habitually resides in the Union and whose passport shows that the titration was carried out, with a favourable result, before it left.

Number of animals and who accompanies them

The maximum is five pet animals per movement, under Article 246(1) of Regulation (EU) 2016/429. It can be exceeded, but the exception is closely defined and the three conditions have to be met at once: that the movement is for the purpose of taking part in competitions, shows or sporting events —or in training for them—, that there is written evidence of the animals' registration, and that they are all over six months old. If one fails, it stops being a non-commercial movement.

If the animal does not travel with its owner but with an authorised person, there is a divergence worth knowing about before buying tickets. Recital 12 of 2026/131 places that movement «within five days of the movement of the owner» —oriented forwards—, while the European Commission itself publicises it as «five days before or after». With that contradiction unresolved, the safe reading is that the animal travels after, never before. The authorisation is attached following the model in Part 1 of Annex V to 2026/705.

Puppies: the European exception is no use to you from Peru

Article 9 of Delegated Regulation (EU) 2026/131 allows the movement of puppies under twelve weeks unvaccinated, or of twelve to sixteen weeks vaccinated without having completed the twenty-one days, but «by way of exception to a Member State from another Member State», and only if the Member State of destination has authorised and published it. It is an intra-Union exception. From a non-listed third country the whole chain has to be completed, and that sets the real minimum age well above sixteen weeks.

Requirements for cats

Identical to the dog's in everything European: microchip before the vaccination, rabies vaccination from twelve weeks, titration ≥ 0.5 IU/ml in a designated laboratory and ninety days if you are leaving a non-listed country, the 2026/705 certificate with its 10-day window and its 6-month validity, declaration of non-commerciality, entry through a designated point and a maximum of five animals per movement, save for the closely defined exception for competitions and shows.

What does not apply to the cat is the national layer. The Hundeverbringungs- und -einfuhrbeschränkungsgesetz is a law about dogs: there are no banned cat breeds and no equivalent Land regime. And the Hundesteuer is, by its own definition, a tax on dogs.

Identifying and registering the cat once inside Germany is a local matter to settle on arrival. We do not close it here: we found no federal rule governing it and we are not going to assume the one that applies in your city.

The four banned breeds, and the Land layer

This is what makes Germany different, and it is the part of the factsheet worth reading twice if your dog could look like any of these breeds.

The federal ban

§ 2(1), first sentence, of the Hundeverbringungs- und -einfuhrbeschränkungsgesetz —law of 12 April 2001— prohibits bringing into or importing into Germany dogs of the breeds Pitbull-Terrier, American Staffordshire-Terrier, Staffordshire-Bullterrier and Bullterrier, as well as their crosses with each other or with other dogs. The law expressly distinguishes two operations and prohibits both: Verbringen, bringing the dog in from another Member State of the Union, and Einfuhr, importing it from a third country. You cannot bring it in from Peru, and not from France either.

Two points that are usually missing. The first: the ban reaches puppies, and customs say so in those words — «Dies sind Hunde (auch Welpen) der Rassen». The second: this article was challenged before the Federal Constitutional Court and declared compatible with the Basic Law on 16 March 2004 —decision 1 BvR 1778/01, recorded as an official footnote to § 2 itself—. It is not a rule under discussion: it is a rule confirmed.

What happens if you try

§ 5 of the same law provides for up to two years' imprisonment or a fine for anyone who brings in or imports a dog contrary to § 2(1). And it adds two things worth reading slowly: «the attempt is punishable» and, where a person acts negligently, the penalty is up to one year or a fine. No intent to infringe is needed. § 7 also allows confiscation of the dog, and § 4 of the HundVerbrEinfVO empowers the authority to order it to be housed until the requirements are met, to seize it, or to order its immediate return to the place of origin.

The exceptions, and the one that does not exist

The Hundeverbringungs- und -einfuhrverordnung, of 3 April 2002, is the rule that opens the exceptions. Its § 2 has four paragraphs; three open workable routes and the fourth deserves a paragraph of its own. The first three:

  • Service dogs (federal, of the Länder, municipal or of foreign armed forces), guide dogs, assistance dogs for people with disabilities and civil protection and rescue dogs. Requires official certification confirming the dog's purpose —Zweckbestimmung—.
  • Dogs returning to a German home where they could already be kept lawfully, after a temporary absence.
  • Temporary stay of a maximum of four weeks, and only if the accompanying person is not domiciled in Germany. The Land authority may extend it, on request, to avoid a disproportionate burden.

And here is the fact that hardly ever gets told. Paragraph 4 of § 2 of the regulation —the only one that allows entry with the intention of permanent residence— refers back to § 2(1), second sentence of the law, which is the one about the Land breeds. It does not refer back to the first sentence, which is the one about the four federal breeds. Put another way: for those four breeds there is no ordinary route of entry to stay and live. Anyone moving to Germany fits none of the three exceptions, because the four-week one requires precisely that you do not live there.

The papers the exception requires

The exception is not automatic. § 3 of the regulation requires the accompanying person to carry documentation establishing the animal's identity, with date of birth, sex, breed and coat colour officially confirmed by the country of origin; if the dog is permanently identified, official confirmation of the chip or tattoo number is enough. For the four-week stay an official certificate that the dog has not been classified as dangerous to date is added, the temporary nature of the stay has to be made plausible, and where it is an import from a third country a customs receipt showing the date of entry, which has to be produced again on leaving, is required. All documents in the original, and those drawn up in another language with an officially certified translation into German (amtlich beglaubigte deutsche Übersetzung).

The second layer: the Land breeds

The second sentence of § 2(1) also prohibits bringing into a particular Land dogs of other breeds that the rules of that Land presume dangerous. Customs themselves say so: the entry ban under Land law «is not an absolute ban», and the local authority of the Land where the dog is going to live may grant an official keeping permit. The federal ban on the four breeds does not work like that. They are two different regimes even though they are applied at the same desk.

They are two different questions and we do not mix them here. The list is dated August 2024 by its issuer; confirm it for your Land before buying the ticket.

Common mistakes

MISTAKE 1 · Counting the ninety days up to the flight

They end at the issue of the certificate, not at landing. Anyone counting up to the flight falls short in practice, because after the ninety days you still have to get the certificate issued and endorsed by the competent authority. Mark the sampling date, add ninety and put the certificate appointment there, not the take-off.

MISTAKE 2 · Trusting «three months before entry»

That is the wording of the repealed framework and it is still alive on the consular page of the Auswärtiges Amt. The figure in force is ninety days and the end point is the issue of the certificate. If your veterinarian or a consular page gives you the old version, do not argue: count ninety calendar days, which always satisfies both readings.

MISTAKE 3 · Vaccinating before implanting the microchip

The microchip comes first, always. A vaccination given before the chip is worthless and forces revaccination, which drags the sampling and the ninety days with it. It is the most expensive mistake because it is not detected until someone checks the dates.

MISTAKE 4 · Choosing the airport on the price of the ticket

Only twenty German airports are a valid point of entry, and each one has its veterinary office with its own hours. Erfurt-Weimar is open on Tuesdays. Hannover-Langenhagen has no office at the airport and requires a prior telephone appointment. A cheap flight that lands on a Sunday afternoon at an airport that closes at weekends is not a cheap flight.

MISTAKE 5 · Thinking a cross «does not count»

The law expressly reaches crosses of those breeds with each other and with other dogs. There is no percentage below which it stops applying, and the person deciding at the border is not the breeder. If your dog could look like any of the four, settle it in writing with the authority of the Land of destination before buying the ticket, not at the desk.

MISTAKE 6 · Confusing the 10-day window with the 6-month validity

They are two different periods and neither replaces the other. Ten days from the signature to the entry checks; six months of validity from those checks, or less if the rabies vaccination expires sooner.

MISTAKE 7 · Sending the animal on ahead

Where an authorised person travels, the two official versions do not say the same thing: recital 12 of 2026/131 counts five days from the owner's journey, and the European Commission publicises it as five days before or after. With that divergence open, the safe play is for the animal to leave after. And no more than five animals per movement, save for the closely defined exception for competitions and shows.

From a non-listed third country, the animal may only enter through a travellers' point of entry designated by the Member State (Article 15(1) of Delegated Regulation (EU) 2026/131). What happens at the check

The BMLEH describes it plainly: you have to contact the competent authority at the point of entry, which checks the documents and the animal's identity; and if the animal does not meet the requirements, «it may be returned to the country of departure or placed in quarantine until it meets EU health rules, or else, as a last resort, put down if the previous measures are not possible». It is worth reading for what it says: quarantine appears described as one of the consequences of non-compliance. We have found no official source stating that the compliant animal is exempt, so we do not state it ourselves.

Our reading of the list was done on the official BMLEH document on 29 August 2026. Opening hours change: confirm them with the office before closing the ticket.

Two documentary lags, and why they affect you

The European framework changed on 22 April 2026. Part of the official documentation still does not reflect it. We put it in writing with a date because a reader who reaches this page in January is entitled to know what we saw, when we saw it and whether it has been corrected since.

Lag 1 · The list of points of entry relies on a rule that no longer applies

The BMLEH document we reproduce above is headed like this: «Liste der deutschen Einreiseorte gemäß Artikel 34 Abs. 3 der VO (EU) Nr. 576/2013». Regulation (EU) 576/2013 stopped applying to these movements on 21 April 2026. The page hosting the document —dated by the ministry itself 26 February 2026— also still offers the certificate model from that rule and cites the consolidated 577/2013 as its legal basis.

What that means in practice, and this is our reading: we understand that the list of airports and ports still holds, because the obligation to enter through a designated point was not created by 576/2013 and is still alive in Article 15(1) of 2026/131. No authority has confirmed it in writing, so if your case is tight, ask the BMLEH. What you must not use is the certificate model hanging off that same page: the valid one is in Annex III to Implementing Regulation (EU) 2026/705.

Lag 2 · «Three months before entry» where the rule says ninety days

The consular page of the Auswärtiges Amt keeps the wording of the previous framework. The rule in force counts ninety days and closes them at the issue of the certificate, not at entry. We develop this, with the two wordings side by side, in our article on the ninety-day period.

Both lags were verified by Zoovet Travel on 29 August 2026, opening each document at source. We do not point them out as a reproach: the updating of explanatory documentation always runs behind that of the official journal, and that is normal. We point them out because anyone planning a journey with those texts arrives late or with the wrong paper. If they are already corrected by the time you read this, so much the better: write to us and we will update it with the new date.

On arrival: municipal registration and the Hundesteuer

If you are staying to live, the dog is registered with the municipality and pays the Hundesteuer, a municipal tax. There is no national figure: each municipal council sets it and it varies a great deal, so we give no general amounts. We give one verified figure, and we give it declared for what it is: Berlin's.

In Berlin the deadline is one month from the purchase of the dog or from your move to the city, and the amounts published by the Senatsverwaltung für Finanzen —as at 23 June 2025— are €120 a year for the first dog and €180 a year for each additional dog, on the basis of the Berliner Hundesteuergesetz. In your city it will be a different figure and perhaps a different deadline: ask at the competent municipal office when you register your address.

🐾 Did you know…? Germany, its dogs and its cats

  • The federal ban on the four breeds reached the Federal Constitutional Court and survived. The decision of 16 March 2004 declared it compatible with the Basic Law, and the reference appears as an official footnote to the article itself. It is not a rule under discussion.
  • In that same law, the attempt is punishable and so is negligence: up to one year's imprisonment or a fine for doing it unintentionally. No intent to infringe is needed.
  • The exceptions regulation leaves an asymmetry that hardly anyone tells you about: the only paragraph that allows entry to stay refers to the Land breeds, not to the four federal ones. And the four-week exception requires the accompanying person not to live in Germany.
  • There are twenty airports and four ports available, and the timetable rules: Erfurt-Weimar is open on Tuesdays, Hannover has no office at the airport, and of the hours we were able to read, only Frankfurt am Main and Berlin Brandenburg cover Saturdays, Sundays and public holidays; Nuremberg's is not recorded.
  • Annex IV to Regulation 2019/2035 is not satisfied with ISO standards 11784 and 11785: it requires the transponder to be assessed in accordance with ISO 24631-1. It is a requirement almost no guide mentions.
  • The ministry changed its name: where it used to read BMEL it now reads BMLEH, Bundesministerium für Landwirtschaft, Ernährung und Heimat. If you search by the old name you will reach archived pages.
  • Anti-echinococcus treatment is not a general Union requirement: it is only required of dogs entering Finland, Ireland, Malta, the United Kingdom (Northern Ireland) and Norway. Germany is not on that list and does not require it.
  • The dog tax is municipal and there is no national figure. In Berlin it is €120 a year for the first dog and €180 for each additional dog, with one month to register from the move.
  • There is an asymmetry hardly anyone explains: the titration is waived by Annexes I and II to Regulation 2026/636, but the compulsory point of entry is waived only by Annex I. Being in Annex II saves you the blood test and the ninety days, not the airport.
  • The laboratory report does not go in a drawer: a copy travels attached to the animal health certificate. And the certificate is not finished when the veterinarian signs it, but when the competent authority endorses it.
  • There is a requirement that has not arrived yet: laboratory reports issued from 1 January 2028 will carry a security code so their authenticity can be verified on the laboratory's website. It is already in the rule, with that date.
  • The rule names no technique for the titration. It refers to the rabies chapter of the WOAH manual. Anyone demanding a specific technique by name is giving you their habit, not the rule.

Frequently asked questions about your case

Every pet journey is different. These are the most common questions — and why we always recommend consulting your specific situation.

Since when has the new European framework for entering Germany with a dog or a cat applied?

Delegated Regulation (EU) 2026/131 has applied since 22 April 2026, with the document models of Implementing Regulation (EU) 2026/705 and the third-country lists of 2026/636, on the basis of Regulation (EU) 2016/429. Regulation 576/2013 was repealed by Article 270(2) of 2016/429 from 21 April 2021, but its Article 277 kept it applying to non-commercial movements until 21 April 2026. Since 22 April 2026 it no longer applies. Warning verified by Zoovet Travel on 29 August 2026, opening each document at source: the official list of German points of entry is still headed by Article 34(3) of 576/2013, and the ministry page hosting it, dated 26 February 2026, still offers the certificate model from that rule. Our reading, and we give it as our reading: the list of airports and ports still holds, because the obligation to enter through a designated point was not created by 576/2013 and is still alive in Article 15(1) of 2026/131. The certificate model hanging off that same page, on the other hand, does not: the valid one is in Annex III to Implementing Regulation (EU) 2026/705.

From what date are the ninety days of the rabies serology wait counted for Germany?

From the date the blood sample was taken, not from the date of the result nor from the laboratory report. And they end on the date the animal health certificate is issued, not on the date of the flight: Annex XXI, point 1(a), of Delegated Regulation (EU) 2020/692 consolidated on 22 April 2026 requires the titration to have been carried out not less than ninety days before the issue of the certificate. The sampling cannot be done earlier than thirty days after the vaccination and the test is carried out in a designated laboratory, on a sample taken by an official or authorised veterinarian, and must measure a level of rabies virus neutralising antibodies equal to or above 0.5 IU/ml by one of the methods prescribed in the rabies chapter of the WOAH manual. A copy of the official laboratory report travels attached to the animal health certificate.

Why does the German consular page say «three months before entry» instead of ninety days?

Because it is the wording of the previous framework, which is still alive on the consular page of the Auswärtiges Amt. There are two differences, not one: «three months» can be 89, 90, 91 or 92 days depending on the month they fall in, and «entry» is not «the issue of the certificate». The BMLEH holds the correct count. Our practical recommendation is to count ninety calendar days from the sampling and not to issue the certificate before then, because that way both readings are satisfied.

Which dog breeds cannot enter Germany?

§ 2(1), first sentence, of the Hundeverbringungs- und -einfuhrbeschränkungsgesetz, of 12 April 2001, prohibits bringing in or importing Pitbull-Terrier, American Staffordshire-Terrier, Staffordshire-Bullterrier and Bullterrier, as well as their crosses with each other or with other dogs, and it reaches puppies as well. The ban applies both to import from a third country and to a movement from another Member State of the Union. In addition, the second sentence of the same article prohibits bringing into a particular Land the breeds that the rules of that Land presume dangerous. According to German customs, with data from August 2024, the Länder that have added breeds for entry purposes are Baden-Württemberg, Bavaria, Hamburg and Hesse. What decides is not the airport of arrival, but where the dog is going to live.

Is there any exception to the ban on the four breeds if I move to Germany?

The exceptions regulation provides for three cases: service, guide, assistance and civil protection and rescue dogs; dogs returning to a German home where they could already be kept lawfully; and stays of a maximum of four weeks with an accompanying person who is not domiciled in Germany. The only paragraph that allows entry with the intention of permanent residence refers back to the second sentence of the article, the one about the Land breeds, and not to the first, the one about the four federal breeds. For those four breeds there is therefore no ordinary route of entry to stay and live. The ban under Land law can be lifted: customs themselves indicate that the local authority of the Land of destination may grant an official keeping permit.

Which German airport do I have to enter through with my pet?

From a non-listed third country, entry can only be made through a designated travellers' point of entry. Germany has twenty-four: twenty airports and four ports, published by the BMLEH. The airports are Stuttgart, Munich, Nuremberg, Berlin Brandenburg, Bremen, Hamburg-Fuhlsbüttel, Frankfurt am Main, Rostock-Laage, Hannover-Langenhagen, Dortmund, Düsseldorf, Cologne, Münster/Osnabrück, Paderborn/Lippstadt, Weeze, Frankfurt-Hahn, Saarbrücken, Dresden, Leipzig/Halle and Erfurt-Weimar. Any other airport is not a valid point of entry. Check the veterinary office's opening hours as well before buying the ticket: if the flight lands outside them, the check cannot be carried out.

Does Germany put my pet in quarantine on arrival?

The BMLEH describes the entry check as a check of the documents and of the animal's identity, for which you have to contact the competent authority at the point of entry. And it adds that, if the animal does not meet the requirements, it may be returned to the country of departure or placed in quarantine until it meets the Union's health rules, or else put down as a last resort if the above is not possible. That is: quarantine appears described as a consequence of non-compliance, not as a step in the procedure. We have found no official source expressly stating that the compliant animal is exempt, so we do not state it ourselves: we describe what is documented.

Can I enter Germany with a puppy under fifteen weeks old?

From a non-listed third country, no. Article 9 of Delegated Regulation (EU) 2026/131 allows very young puppies to be moved by way of exception to a Member State from another Member State, and only if the Member State of destination has authorised and published it: it is an intra-Union exception. From Peru the whole chain has to be completed, and that puts the real minimum age at 204 days, a little under seven months. That figure is our own count and it is worth saying so: the rule does not fix an age, it fixes three periods that produce it — 84 days to twelve weeks, plus thirty to the blood sampling, plus ninety until the certificate can be issued. From a third country or territory listed in Annex I or Annex II no titration is required, and the minimum age stands at fifteen weeks, also on our own count: twelve weeks of age plus the twenty-one days of the vaccination's period of validity.

How long is the animal health certificate valid for entering Germany?

They are two different periods and neither replaces the other. The issuing window is ten days from issue by an official or authorised veterinarian to the documentary and identity checks at the point of entry. If the transport is by sea, those ten days are extended by the time the crossing takes. The validity is six months from the date of those checks, or until the rabies vaccination expires if that date comes first, under Article 18(1) of Delegated Regulation (EU) 2026/131. Two things that get forgotten: the certificate is not finished when the veterinarian signs it, but when the competent authority endorses it; and travelling with it are the written declaration that the movement is non-commercial — model in Part 2 of Annex V to 2026/705 — and a copy of the laboratory report.

What do I have to do on arriving in Germany if I am staying to live?

Register the dog with the municipality and pay the Hundesteuer, the municipal dog tax, at the competent municipal office when you register your address. There is no national amount: each municipal council sets it. The example we do have verified is Berlin, as at 23 June 2025: 120 euros a year for the first dog, 180 euros for each additional dog, and a deadline of one month from the purchase of the dog or from the move. In your city it will be a different figure and perhaps a different deadline. The cat does not pay this tax.
