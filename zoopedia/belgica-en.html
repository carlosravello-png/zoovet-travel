Travelling with dogs and cats to Belgium: 2026 official requirements guide

To enter Belgium from a non-listed country, a dog or a cat needs an ISO 11784/11785 microchip implanted before the rabies vaccination, a rabies vaccination administered from 12 weeks of age, a rabies antibody titration with a result equal to or above 0.5 IU/ml in a laboratory approved by the European Union (blood drawn no earlier than 30 days after vaccination), a 3-month wait —90 days— counted from the date the sample was taken, and the animal health certificate in Annex III, Part 1, of Implementing Regulation (EU) 2026/705, referred to in Article 18(1) of Delegated Regulation (EU) 2026/131, applicable since 22 April 2026. The competent authority in Belgium is the Federal Agency for the Safety of the Food Chain, FAVV/AFSCA. The real minimum age for entry by this route is 7 months. From a country listed in Annex II to Implementing Regulation (EU) 2026/636 no titration is required and the minimum drops to 15 weeks. Data verified on 4 August 2026.

Requirements by country of origin — quick guide

CASE 1 · You are leaving another European Union Member State

Movement within the internal market. Microchip, valid rabies vaccination and EU identification document. There is no serological titration and no three-month wait. European pet passports under Annex III to Regulation 577/2013 remain usable if they were issued before 1 January 2028: that is what Article 6(1) and (2) of Implementing Regulation (EU) 2026/705 says. If you are arriving to stay, what awaits you on landing is regional registration within 8 days, explained further down.

CASE 2 · You are leaving a third country listed in Annex II to 2026/636

Direct entry, no titration. Microchip, rabies vaccination valid from 12 weeks of age plus the 21-day period of validity: minimum 15 weeks. Chile and Argentina are in that annex. Implementing Regulation (EU) 2026/1228 of 11 June 2026, in force on 19 June 2026, added Montenegro and Serbia. The list gets amended; check it on the date of your journey, not on the date of your decision.

CASE 3 · You are leaving a non-listed third country

The full protocol: microchip first, vaccination afterwards, blood sample no earlier than 30 days after the vaccination, a result of ≥ 0.5 IU/ml in an EU-approved laboratory and a 90-day wait from the date of sampling. Bolivia, Brazil, Colombia, Ecuador and Peru belong to this class. Our real Belgian case came from exactly here: Pacha, from Bolivia to Belgium.

Global classification of the destination

Regulatory model

Belgium has no import regulation of its own for dogs and cats. It applies the framework of the European Union: Regulation (EU) 2016/429 —the Animal Health Law, Part VI—, developed by Delegated Regulation (EU) 2026/131, applicable since 22 April 2026, with the document models of Implementing Regulation (EU) 2026/705 and the third-country lists of Implementing Regulation (EU) 2026/636. Regulation (EU) 576/2013 and its Implementing Regulation 577/2013 have been superseded, subject to transitional arrangements. We covered the change of framework in the Journal.

What we found on closer inspection, on 4 August 2026. Three and a half months after the new framework became applicable, official FAVV/AFSCA pages still cite «European Regulation (EU) 2013/577» — the number is inverted on their own page — (Annex II) and Regulation (EU) 2019/1293 for the certificate model. Their English version still cites «Regulation 576/2013, art. 32». This is not an antiquarian detail: if your official veterinarian follows the Belgian page rather than the regulation, they will sign an outdated model. There is a transitional way out, but it has a double lock and we explain it in its own section.

Rabies status

Belgium is a European Union Member State and its entry regime is the one the Union applies to territories that do not require titration between themselves. Our verified sheet does not record a Belgian animal health status declaration regarding rabies issued by the FAVV/AFSCA itself, so we do not assert one here. What is verified is the practical consequence: anyone arriving from a non-listed third country goes through rabies serology and a wait; anyone arriving from a listed country does not.

Classification system for countries of origin

Three boxes. European Union Member States. Third countries and territories listed in Annex II to Implementing Regulation (EU) 2026/636. And everything else, which is the default category: if your country does not appear in that annex, you are on the long protocol even if your country has gone decades without a case of canine rabies. The list is not a measure of your actual risk; it is a list.

Requirements for dogs

Microchip

ISO 11784/11785 transponder. The sequence is what puts journeys on hold: the microchip is implanted before the rabies vaccination. If it was done the other way round, that vaccination does not count for the file and the animal must be revaccinated with the chip already in place, which resets every subsequent deadline.

Watch out for one Belgian detail that concerns not entry but what comes after: in Brussels-Capital, a dog identified by tattoo must be re-identified with a microchip (arrêtés of the Government of the Brussels-Capital Region of 23 May 2024).

Rabies vaccination

Valid from 12 weeks of age. From a listed third country you must add the 21-day period of validity, which is where the 15-week minimum comes from. From a non-listed third country, the vaccination is only the first link: the blood sampling comes next, and then the 90 days.

Rabies antibody titre (RNATT)

Mandatory from a non-listed third country. The blood is drawn no earlier than 30 days after vaccination. The result must be equal to or above 0.5 IU/ml, in a laboratory approved by the European Union. The usual technique is FAVN or RFFIT.

And here is the mistake that costs the most flights: the three months are counted from the date the sample was taken, not from the date of the result nor from the date of the report. Keep the sampling date as if it were the ticket.

Other vaccinations

Our verified sheet records no additional vaccination required by Belgium for the entry of dogs —neither distemper, nor parvovirosis, nor leptospirosis—, so we do not assert that any exist. Unverified point, declared as such. If your airline or your country of departure asks for them on its own account, that is a different file.

Antiparasitic treatments

Nor does our verified sheet record a Belgian requirement for anti-echinococcus treatment or for deworming before entry. We neither affirm nor deny it: we declare it unverified as at 4 August 2026.

Health certificate

The health document that matters is the animal health certificate in Annex III, Part 1, of Implementing Regulation (EU) 2026/705, referred to in Article 18(1) of Delegated Regulation (EU) 2026/131. It is signed by an official veterinarian in the country of departure.

Two different clocks, and they get confused all the time:

  • Issuing window: 10 days from the official veterinarian's signature to the documentary and identity checks at the point of entry. If the journey is by sea, that period is extended by the crossing time.
  • Validity: 6 months from the date of those checks, or until the rabies vaccination expires if that date comes first.
«Het diergezondheidscertificaat is geldig gedurende zes maanden vanaf de datum waarop de documenten- en overeenstemmingscontroles op het punt van binnenkomst voor reizigers worden uitgevoerd of tot en met de datum waarop de geldigheid van de vaccinatie tegen rabiës verstrijkt, indien deze datum eerder valt.» — Article 18(1)

On language: the linguistic requirements are set by the Commission, not by Belgium —Article 245(4) of Regulation (EU) 2016/429, developed in Annex V, Part 6, of 2026/705—. We have not located a Belgian instruction of its own on the language of the certificate. It remains an unresolved matter.

Official entry document

From a third country, the international veterinary certificate described above. From within the Union, the EU identification document.

Transitional arrangements, Article 6 of Implementing Regulation (EU) 2026/705 — a double lock. A certificate under Annex IV, Part 1, of 577/2013 is deemed compliant if it was issued before 1 October 2026 and only until 31 March 2027. Both conditions at once, not one or the other. Passports under Annex III to 577/2013 are valid if they were issued before 1 January 2028. Paragraph 6(4) concerns pet birds: do not mix it up with dogs, cats or ferrets.

Mandatory digital forms

For the non-commercial movement of a private individual with their animal, our verified sheet records no mandatory prior digital form in Belgium. There is one on the commercial route: notification in TRACES.

A paper document that does exist and gets forgotten: where the movement is carried out by an authorised person, Article 4 of Delegated Regulation 2026/131 requires the written authorisation signed by the owner to be attached to the identification document. The model is in Annex V, Part 1, of 2026/705.

Quarantine

If the protocol is met there is no entry quarantine: the 90-day wait is served in the country of origin, before flying. Quarantine appears as a consequence of non-compliance. In the AFSCA's own words: «l'isolement à domicile, la quarantaine, le renvoi de l'animal dans son pays d'origine ou même l'euthanasie peuvent être nécessaires». The FAVV/AFSCA decides and the costs are borne by the owner. We give no amounts: they are not published.

We have no verified quarantine facilities designated by name in Belgium. We do not invent them.

Breed restrictions

This is the part that almost nobody gets right, so we will take it slowly.

Belgium has no national list of banned breeds. There was one: the arrêté ministériel of 21 October 1998 named thirteen breeds. The Council of State annulled it on 31 May 1999, judgment no. 80.521, and did so purely on a procedural defect —it did not rule on the substance—. It was never reinstated. That gap was not filled from above: it was filled from below.

Since the Sixth State Reform, animal welfare and breeds are a regional competence (Flanders, Wallonia, Brussels-Capital), while animal health and importation remain federal. And below the Regions are the municipalities, which regulate on their own account relying on Article 135 §2, 6° of the Nouvelle loi communale —«divagation d'animaux malfaisants ou féroces»— read together with Article 119.

A verified example, so that you can see what this means in practice: Grâce-Hollogne, chapter 13 of its general police regulation, defines twelve breeds as «catégorie 1» and imposes on them a compulsory muzzle in public spaces, declaration to the police, civil liability insurance, a dog club certificate and a ban on breeding.

The list that decides is the one of the municipality where you are going to live. Not the country's, which does not exist; nor the Region's, which does not set one. Before signing a tenancy agreement, read that municipality's règlement général de police or politiereglement. Two neighbouring municipalities may treat the same dog differently.

Minimum age for entry

From a non-listed third country, the arithmetic rules: 12 weeks to vaccinate, at least 30 days until the blood sampling, 90 days of waiting from that sampling. That gives a real minimum age of 7 months. From a listed third country, 15 weeks.

Belgium does not accept the EU derogation for young animals —the one that would allow the movement of vaccinated animals under 15 weeks, or of unvaccinated animals under 12 weeks—. Declared reservation: two of our three verification passes confirm this; the third relied on a verbatim AFSCA quotation that we were unable to reopen on 4 August 2026. We treat it as verified, with this reservation on show. In practice: do not buy a puppy with the idea of flying it out soon.

Mode of transport

Two regimes, and the boundary between them is one of numbers and of company:

  • Non-commercial. A maximum of 5 animals per person on the same journey. There is an exception for a competition, show or sporting event, including training: animals over 6 months old and written proof of registration. Travellers enter through the designated points and the check is carried out by customs.
  • Commercial. From 6 animals upwards, or where there is a transfer of ownership. Transport as cargo with export certification, entry through a border control post and notification in TRACES. The Belgian posts for this route would be Brussels-National and Liège; single-pass data, medium confidence.

The five-day rule, which is the fine trap. Article 245(2) of Regulation (EU) 2016/429 states that, where the movement is carried out by an authorised person, it may only take place within five days following the movement of the owner. We read five official language versions and all five are oriented forwards: «a partir del» (ES), «from» (EN), «suivant» (FR), «nach» (DE), «binnen vijf dagen na» (NL).

But the European Commission, the FAVV/AFSCA and the Spanish ministry all publicise it as «five days before or after». We have not found the rule that supports that version. There is a single operational reading that satisfies both: the animal travels after the owner, never before. Outside that window, the movement ceases to be non-commercial and moves to the import regime. Whether the FAVV also requires a registered establishment of origin and TRACES notification on that switch, we were unable to verify.

Requirements for cats

Microchip

The same ISO 11784/11785 transponder, the same rule of order: before the vaccination. Flanders accepts a foreign microchip if it is readable (Besluit van de Vlaamse Regering of 24 May 2024, in force on 1 July 2024).

Rabies vaccination

From 12 weeks of age, the same as for dogs.

Rabies antibody titre (RNATT)

Identical to the dog's from a non-listed third country: sampling no earlier than 30 days after the vaccination, ≥ 0.5 IU/ml, EU-approved laboratory, 90 days from the sampling.

Other vaccinations

Our verified sheet records no additional feline vaccination required to enter Belgium —not panleukopenia either—. Unverified.

Antiparasitic treatments

No verified requirement. Same criterion as for dogs.

Health certificate

The same certificate under Annex III, Part 1, of 2026/705: a 10-day issuing window, validity of 6 months from the checks or until the rabies vaccination expires.

Official entry document

Animal health certificate from a third country; EU identification document from within the Union. The same double-lock transitional arrangements.

Mandatory digital forms

None verified for the private individual. TRACES only on the commercial route.

Quarantine

There is no entry quarantine where the protocol has been met; there may be one as a measure in response to non-compliance, at the FAVV/AFSCA's discretion.

Breed restrictions

Our verified sheet records no breed restrictions applicable to cats in Belgium. What does exist for cats, and it carries weight: in Flanders, neutering is compulsory before 5 months of age for every cat born after 31 August 2014. That rule reaches an imported cat that arrives already adult. If your cat arrives in Flanders unneutered, that is your next piece of paperwork.

Minimum age for entry

7 months from a non-listed third country; 15 weeks from a listed country. Belgium does not apply the young animal derogation here either.

Mode of transport

The same two regimes as for dogs: up to 5 animals per person non-commercially, from 6 upwards commercially. The five-day rule for the authorised person applies in the same way.

Variations by country of origin

Are requirements differentiated by origin?

Yes, and it is the first thing to settle. The difference between entering at 15 weeks and entering at 7 months depends solely on which box your country is in on the day of the journey.

Risk classification system

It is set by Implementing Regulation (EU) 2026/636. Its Annex II contains the listed third countries and territories. That annex gets amended: Implementing Regulation (EU) 2026/1228 of 11 June 2026, in force on 19 June 2026, added Montenegro and Serbia.

Position of non-listed Latin American countries

Bolivia, Brazil, Colombia, Ecuador and Peru are not listed in 2026/636. Full protocol: titration with a result of ≥ 0.5 IU/ml and a three-month wait from the sampling. Translated into a household calendar: at least four long months pass between the first veterinary appointment and the flight, and that is if nothing goes wrong first time round. That is how Pacha travelled.

Position of low-risk countries

Chile and Argentina are in Annex II: direct entry, no titration, with microchip, rabies vaccination and certificate. The expression «low risk» here means one concrete thing and only one: appearing in that annex.

Transit through third countries

Our verified sheet records no specific Belgian rules on transit through third countries. We do not assert any. What is verified and does affect a long itinerary is the 10-day clock between the signing of the certificate and the checks at the point of entry, with the extension for crossing time if the journey is by sea. Long layovers and missed connections eat into that period.

Common mistakes

MISTAKE 1 · Counting the 90 days from the laboratory result

They are counted from the date the sample was taken. Weeks can pass between sampling and result; whoever counts from the report gives those weeks away and travels later than they needed to. Mark the sampling date on the calendar and add 90.

MISTAKE 2 · Vaccinating before implanting the microchip

The microchip comes first. Always. A vaccination given before the chip is worthless for the file and forces revaccination, which drags the sampling and the 90 days with it. It is the most expensive mistake because it is not detected until someone checks the dates in the passport.

MISTAKE 3 · Looking for «the Belgian list of banned breeds»

It has not existed at national level since the Council of State annulled the 1998 arrêté on 31 May 1999. Anyone looking for a federal list will find forums, not legislation. What has to be read is the police regulation of the municipality of destination.

MISTAKE 4 · Believing the puppy gets in under the European young animal derogation

Belgium does not accept it. Neither vaccinated animals under 15 weeks, nor unvaccinated animals under 12 weeks. The sum is 7 months from a non-listed country and 15 weeks from a listed country, and there is no shortcut.

MISTAKE 5 · Confusing the 10-day window with the 6-month validity

They are two different periods and neither replaces the other. Ten days from the signature to the entry checks; six months of validity from those checks, or less if the rabies vaccination expires sooner. And if someone offers you the old 577/2013 certificate, remember the double lock: issued before 1 October 2026 and used until 31 March 2027.

MISTAKE 6 · Sending the animal on ahead

Where an authorised person travels, the five-day window is counted with reference to the owner's movement. The official language versions say «following»; the official public guidance says «before or after». With that contradiction unresolved, the only safe play is for the animal to leave after. And do not exceed 5 animals per person, save for the exception of a competition, show or sporting event with animals over 6 months old and written proof of registration.

Authorised points of entry

Belgium maintains a designated list of travellers' points of entry. It is published by the FAVV/AFSCA in a document dated 7 March 2024, linked from the European Commission.

We have been unable to open that document. None of our three verification passes, the last on 4 August 2026, managed to access the PDF. That is why this factsheet lists no airport and no port: we prefer a factsheet with a declared gap to a factsheet with an invented list that lands you where you should not be. Before buying the ticket, ask the FAVV/AFSCA for the current list of travellers' points of entry and keep it in writing.

On arrival, the check is carried out by customs at the airport. In the AFSCA's own words: «à votre arrivée à l'aéroport, vous devez immédiatement vous présenter à la douane». It is not a desk that comes looking for you: you report to it.

Do not confuse travellers' points of entry with the border control posts of the commercial regime. They are different lists, for different regimes. An animal travelling as cargo does not enter where its owner enters.

🐾 Did you know…? Belgium, its dogs and its cats

  • Belgium had a list of dangerous breeds for seven months and ten days. The 1998 one named thirteen breeds and fell in 1999 on a defect of form, not on its content. Nobody redrafted it, and today whoever decides which breed wears a muzzle in the street is your municipal council.
  • The Walloon animal-keeping permit is automatic and immaterial: «toute personne détient de plein droit et de manière immatérielle le permis». It is not applied for, it is not printed, it does not exist on paper. It is evidenced by an extract from the Fichier Central issued by the municipality, valid for 30 days, required since 1 July 2022. And it is territorial: it is requested for acquisitions made on Walloon soil. Anyone arriving from abroad with their own animal does not have to apply for anything.
  • Compulsory identification of cats arrived on different dates depending on where you live: 1 November 2017 in Wallonia, 1 March 2023 in Brussels.
  • Several Belgian municipalities levy a dog tax. Each municipal council sets it and some are abolishing it. We give no amounts: they change from one municipality to another and from one year to the next.
  • On the German-speaking Community: we found no transfer of animal welfare, and the enumeration of competences transferred from Wallonia does not include it. Our provisional conclusion, declared as provisional: the Walloon code applies.
  • A cat born after 31 August 2014 must be neutered in Flanders. The rule draws no distinction by origin: it also reaches the cat that lands already adult.

Frequently asked questions about your case

Every pet journey is different. These are the most common questions — and why we always recommend consulting your specific situation.

Since when has the new European framework applied to entering Belgium with a dog or cat?

Delegated Regulation (EU) 2026/131 has applied since 22 April 2026, with the document models of Implementing Regulation (EU) 2026/705 and the third-country lists of 2026/636, on the basis of Regulation (EU) 2016/429. Regulation 576/2013 and its Implementing Regulation 577/2013 have been superseded. Warning verified on 4 August 2026: official FAVV/AFSCA pages were still citing «European Regulation (EU) 2013/577» — with the number inverted on the page itself — and Regulation (EU) 2019/1293, and their English version was citing «Regulation 576/2013, art. 32».

From what date are the 90 days of the rabies serology wait counted?

From the date the blood sample was taken, not from the date of the result nor from the date of the laboratory report. The sampling cannot be done earlier than 30 days after the rabies vaccination and the result must be equal to or above 0.5 IU/ml in a laboratory approved by the European Union.

Which dog breeds are banned in Belgium?

There is no national list. The arrêté ministériel of 21 October 1998, which named thirteen breeds, was annulled by the Council of State on 31 May 1999 (judgment no. 80.521) on a procedural defect and was never reinstated. Each municipality regulates on its own account under Article 135 §2, 6° of the Nouvelle loi communale read together with Article 119. Grâce-Hollogne, for example, defines twelve breeds as «catégorie 1» with a compulsory muzzle in public spaces, declaration to the police, civil liability insurance, a dog club certificate and a ban on breeding. The list that affects you is the one of the municipality where you are going to live.

Which Belgian airport should I enter through with my pet?

Belgium maintains a designated list of travellers' points of entry, published by the FAVV/AFSCA in a document dated 7 March 2024 and linked from the European Commission. We have not managed to open that document in any of our verifications, the last on 4 August 2026, so we list no airports: ask the FAVV/AFSCA for the current list before buying the ticket. On arrival, you must report to customs immediately. Do not confuse travellers' points of entry with the border control posts of the commercial regime.

Can I enter Belgium with a puppy under 15 weeks old?

No. Belgium does not accept the EU derogation for young animals, neither for vaccinated animals under 15 weeks nor for unvaccinated animals under 12 weeks. From a non-listed third country the real minimum age for entry is 7 months; from a listed third country, 15 weeks. Declared reservation: two of our three verifications confirm that the derogation is not accepted and the third relied on an AFSCA quotation that we were unable to reopen.

How long is the animal health certificate valid for entering Belgium?

These are two different periods. The issuing window is 10 days from the official veterinarian's signature to the documentary and identity checks at the point of entry, extendable by the crossing time if the journey is by sea. The validity is 6 months from the date of those checks, or until the rabies vaccination expires if that date comes first, under Article 18(1) of Delegated Regulation (EU) 2026/131.

Can my dog travel with another person and arrive in Belgium before me?

The safe reading is that it cannot: the animal travels after the owner, never before. Article 245(2) of Regulation (EU) 2016/429 places the authorised person's movement within the five days following the owner's, and the five official language versions we read are oriented forwards. The European Commission, the FAVV/AFSCA and the Spanish ministry publicise it as «five days before or after», without our having found the rule that supports it. In addition, Article 4 of Delegated Regulation 2026/131 requires the written authorisation signed by the owner to be attached to the identification document, following the model in Annex V, Part 1, of 2026/705.

What do I have to do on arriving in Belgium if I am staying to live?

Register the animal in the corresponding database, DogID for dogs and CatID for cats, and only a veterinarian can do it. The verified deadline is 8 days: in Brussels-Capital for a dog brought in from abroad, in Flanders and in Wallonia for a cat brought in from abroad, with an exemption in Brussels and Flanders if the stay is shorter than 6 months. In Brussels, a dog identified by tattoo must be re-identified with a microchip. In Flanders, moreover, every cat born after 31 August 2014 must be neutered before 5 months of age, including an imported cat that is already adult.
