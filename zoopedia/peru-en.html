Import your dog or cat to Peru: official requirements guide 2026

What do you need to enter Peru with a dog or cat?

Peru, through SENASA, allows dogs and cats to enter from any country through an accessible procedure: no general quarantine and no rabies serology test (RNATT). You need the official Health Certificate issued by the veterinary authority of the country of origin, a clinical examination performed within the two weeks before travel, an ISO 11784/11785 microchip with its number declared on the certificate, and a current rabies vaccine if the animal is older than three months.

SENASA also requires a complete vaccination schedule: for dogs the DHPPi (distemper, hepatitis, parvovirus, parainfluenza) plus leptospira and rabies; for cats the feline trivalent (panleukopenia, calicivirus and rhinotracheitis) plus rabies. On arrival, the pet undergoes a SENASA inspection at the point of entry —mainly Jorge Chávez International Airport in Lima— with payment of inspection fees. If any requirement is missing, SENASA may deny entry and order the return of the animal to the country of origin.

Requirements by country of origin — Quick guide

Peru is one of the most accessible destinations in the world for pets: no RNATT, no general quarantine, and immediate release at the point of arrival if the paperwork is complete. The core is the same for almost every origin; what changes is the authority that issues the certificate. Identify your case before continuing.

CASE 1 — From developed or low rabies incidence countries
United States, Canada, European Union, United Kingdom, Japan, Australia and similar. The Health Certificate is issued by the country's official authority (USDA-APHIS, the competent authority of the EU Member State, CFIA, etc.) with microchip, current rabies vaccine and a clinical exam within the 14 days before travel. Standard process, no quarantine.
CASE 2 — From Latin American countries
Mexico, Chile, Brazil, Colombia, Ecuador, Argentina, Bolivia and others. Certification corresponds to each country's animal health authority (SENASICA, SAG, MAPA, ICA, AGROCALIDAD, etc.), with the same base requirements: microchip, rabies vaccine and complete vaccination schedule. The rest of this guide develops the procedure common to both cases.

Verify your case directly with SENASA: https://www.gob.pe/senasa

Global destination classification

Regulatory model

Model C: no general quarantine, with retention and inspection authority. Peru allows immediate entry after documentary and physical verification by SENASA at the point of arrival, provided the sanitary requirements are met. With incomplete documentation, an invalid vaccine or a health concern, SENASA may retain the animal, order conditional measures or deny entry with return to the country of origin.

Rabies status of the destination country

Peru maintains active foci of canine rabies in some regions and runs mass vaccination programs. That is why it does not require RNATT for entry —it is not a rabies-free country protecting its status— but it does require a current rabies vaccine as a sanitary requirement.
Source: OIE/WOAH — https://www.woah.org/en/disease/rabies/

Country-of-origin classification system

The base entry requirements are uniform for almost every origin: official health certificate, microchip, rabies vaccine and complete vaccination schedule. What varies is the issuing authority of the certificate depending on the country of departure and, in some cases, the certificate model agreed between that authority and SENASA. Verify the applicable model directly at https://www.gob.pe/senasa before starting the procedure in the country of origin.

Requirements for dogs

Microchip

— Required standard: ISO 11784/11785.
— Mandatory? Yes. Dogs imported to Peru must be identified with a microchip, whose number must be declared on the origin country's Health Certificate.
— Must it be implanted before the vaccine? Recommended, to ensure the sanitary history is traceable to the identified animal.
— Practical tip: verify that the 15-digit ISO number on the certificate matches exactly what the scanner reads. A mismatch between chip and document during the SENASA inspection can prevent entry.

Rabies vaccine

— Mandatory: Yes, for dogs older than 3 months.
— Minimum age to vaccinate: 3 months (90 days).
— Validity: the vaccine must be current at the time of entry, per the manufacturer's declared duration.
— It must appear on the official Health Certificate with the application date and product details.

Rabies serology titer (RNATT)

Not required. SENASA does not require a rabies antibody titration test for the entry of dogs to Peru from any country of origin. This is one of the elements that makes Peru an accessible destination within the region.

✓ No RNATT and no general quarantine. Peru does not require a rabies antibody titration test: a current rabies vaccine recorded on the origin country's certificate is enough.

Other vaccines required for entry

For dogs, SENASA requires a complete and current vaccination schedule beyond rabies: distemper, parvovirus, canine infectious hepatitis, leptospirosis and parainfluenza. All must appear on the official Health Certificate of the country of origin. Verify directly at https://www.gob.pe/senasa.

Antiparasitic treatments

SENASA may require proof of current internal and external deworming on the certificate. Confirm the exact deadline according to the country of origin before the certificate is issued.

Health certificate

— Official name: Health Export Certificate / International Health Certificate, issued by the official veterinary authority of the country of origin.
— Who can issue it: an official veterinarian of the origin country's health authority (USDA-APHIS in the US, the competent authority of the EU Member State, CFIA in Canada, SENASICA in Mexico, SAG in Chile, etc.).
— Clinical exam: within the two weeks (14 days) before travel.
— The certificate must include the microchip number, the rabies vaccination and the current vaccination schedule.

⚠ The Health Certificate must be endorsed by the official authority of the country of origin. A certificate signed only by a private veterinarian, without the official body's endorsement, is not valid before SENASA.

Official entry document

The main document is the official Health Certificate of the country of origin. On arrival, SENASA issues the entry document after the inspection and payment of the corresponding fees. Verify the details at https://www.gob.pe/senasa.

Quarantine

— Mandatory: Not routinely.
— Conditional: only for incomplete documentation, invalid vaccine or health concern. SENASA may retain the animal at the point of entry.

Breed restrictions

At the sanitary entry level, SENASA does not set a list of banned breeds. Ownership of certain breeds deemed potentially dangerous is regulated by municipal rules and the Legal Regime of Dogs law; verify the local ownership rules at your place of residence.

Minimum age of entry

Linked to the rabies vaccination requirement (3 months). Younger puppies may be subject to specific conditions; verify directly with SENASA before travel.

Transport mode

Peru allows cabin, hold and cargo (Manifest Cargo) per airline policy. Check with the carrier the specific conditions for the arrival route to Lima.

Requirements for cats

Microchip

Same as dogs — see the section above. Mandatory ISO 11784/11785 microchip, with the number declared on the origin country's Health Certificate.

Rabies vaccine

Same as dogs — mandatory for cats older than 3 months. It must be current and recorded on the official health certificate.

Serology titer (RNATT)

Not required. SENASA does not require RNATT for cats from any country of origin.

✓ No RNATT and no general quarantine for cats. A current rabies vaccine and complete schedule on the origin country's certificate is enough.

Other vaccines required for entry

For cats, SENASA requires a complete current schedule: feline trivalent (feline panleukopenia, calicivirus and feline viral rhinotracheitis) plus rabies. All must appear on the official Health Certificate of the country of origin.

Antiparasitic treatments

Same as dogs — SENASA may require proof of current deworming on the certificate. Confirm the deadline by country of origin.

Health certificate

Same as dogs — issued and endorsed by the official authority of the country of origin, after a clinical exam within the 14 days before travel.

Official entry document

Same as dogs — origin country's Health Certificate and SENASA inspection on arrival with issuance of the entry document.

Quarantine

Same as dogs — not routine; only conditional for non-compliance or health concern.

Breed restrictions

Not identified in the sanitary entry rules for cats.

Minimum age of entry

Linked to rabies vaccination (3 months). Verify conditions for younger kittens with SENASA.

Transport mode

Same as dogs — cabin, hold or cargo per airline.

Variations by country of origin

Differentiated requirements by origin? The sanitary core is uniform (official certificate + microchip + rabies + complete schedule). What changes is the issuing authority and, in some countries, the certificate model agreed with SENASA.

Authority that certifies in origin

The Health Certificate is issued and endorsed by the official veterinary authority of the country of departure. A private-vet certificate without that endorsement is not valid before SENASA. The Authorities Directory table lists the competent body of each frequent origin.

Rabies-free countries

Animals from countries recognized as rabies-free may, depending on the case, present an official declaration instead of the full rabies vaccination history. This point is coordinated between the origin authority and SENASA; verify it before issuing the certificate.

Where almost all problems occur

Peru requires neither RNATT nor quarantine, so it looks like a simple destination — and it is. But the problems appear in the certificate: a microchip number that doesn't match the document, a rabies vaccine not properly recorded, an incomplete vaccination schedule, or a certificate without the official endorsement of the origin authority. Those four points concentrate almost 100% of rejections at the SENASA inspection.

Common mistakes

MISTAKE 1: Certificate without the endorsement of the origin country's official authority

What happens: the owner arrives with the clinic vet's document without the official body's stamp (USDA-APHIS, the EU competent authority, CFIA, etc.).
Consequence: SENASA does not recognize the document; entry is at risk.
Prevention: obtain the official endorsement in the country of origin before travel.

MISTAKE 2: Microchip number that doesn't match the certificate

What happens: the ISO number read by SENASA's scanner doesn't match the one declared on the certificate.
Consequence: a documentary discrepancy that can prevent entry.
Prevention: verify that the 15-digit number matches exactly before issuing the certificate in origin.

MISTAKE 3: Incomplete vaccination schedule

What happens: the animal has a current rabies vaccine but is missing the full canine schedule (dogs) or the feline trivalent (cats).
Consequence: the certificate does not meet SENASA's requirements.
Prevention: complete and record the whole schedule on the origin country's certificate before travel.

MISTAKE 4: Clinical exam outside the 14-day window

What happens: the certificate is issued with a clinical exam performed more than two weeks before travel.
Consequence: the document loses temporal validity.
Prevention: coordinate the exam and the certificate issuance within the 14 days before departure.

The Clinical File

The clinical signature is only the first link; the official endorsement is what holds the document at the border. We cover how to ensure traceability and avoid certificate invalidation in our technical analysis of the documentary chain.

Minimum timeline from scratch

Scenario A — Animal with microchip and current vaccination

— Day 0 to 14: clinical exam by a vet in the country of origin and issuance of the official Health Certificate with the endorsement of the competent body, within the 14 days before travel.
— Flight day: arrival in Peru and SENASA inspection at the point of entry; immediate release if paperwork is complete.
— Minimum total for Scenario A: a few days, subject to the availability of the official endorsement in origin.

Scenario B — Animal with no prior history, from scratch

— Day 0: ISO microchip implantation + rabies vaccine + complete schedule (canine schedule for dogs, feline trivalent for cats). Minimum age 90 days for rabies.
— Immunization period per manufacturer.
— Last 14 days: clinical exam and issuance of the Health Certificate with official endorsement.
— Minimum total for Scenario B: approximately 30 days from scratch, depending on the vaccination schedule and the origin body's schedule.

Peru looks like a simple destination because it has no RNATT, imposes no general quarantine and does not restrict the airport of entry. And at heart it is. But the problem is not in what Peru demands extra — it is in what the origin country's certificate sometimes brings short: a microchip that doesn't match, an incomplete vaccination schedule, or —most commonly— the missing official endorsement of the competent body. With the certificate correctly issued and endorsed in origin, the SENASA inspection at Jorge Chávez is the last step, not the first.

🐾 Did you know…? Peru, its dogs and its cats

  • The Peruvian Hairless Dog (Viringo) is Heritage of the Nation under Law No. 27537 (2001) and an FCI-recognized breed (No. 310) since 1995.
  • It appears in ceramics of the Vicús, Moche, Chancay and Chimú cultures (between 300 BC and 1460 AD); remains were found alongside the tombs of the Lord of Sipán.
  • It has its own national day (June 12) and, because of its warm skin, has long been credited with a "medicinal" use against rheumatism and asthma.
  • 🐱 The Andean cat, the rarest feline in the Americas (<2,500 adults), is sacred in Andean cultures as a symbol of fertility and the spirit of the mountains; in Peru it is illegal to hunt or keep it.
  • 🐱 Lima's Parque Kennedy (Miraflores) is famous for its 100+ cats: brought in from 1990 to fight a rat plague, they stayed, and today a volunteer association with the municipality feeds, neuters and registers each one.
  • 🐱 One of the Nazca Lines is a giant cat: a ~37-metre feline geoglyph, some 2,000 years old, revealed in 2020.

Frequently asked questions about your case

Every pet trip is unique. These are the most common questions — and why we always recommend reviewing your particular situation.

Are the sanitary requirements the same for every destination country?

No. Each country sets its own requirements: vaccines, laboratory tests, official certifications and waiting periods that in many cases cannot be shortened under any circumstances. What is valid for one destination may be insufficient or even incompatible with another. Before starting any procedure, assess your case with us.

Do all airlines accept pets on international flights?

No. Policies vary by airline, route, aircraft type and season, and change frequently. Brachycephalic breeds face additional restrictions with most international carriers. Before buying your ticket, check the exact compatibility for your route and pet.

How far in advance should I start the international pet travel process?

It depends on the destination. Some protocols include laboratory tests with mandatory waiting periods that cannot be compressed. Starting late can invalidate documents already obtained or force the whole trip to be rescheduled. Write to us and we will work out your real timeline together.

What happens if my pet is rejected at the destination customs?

The animal may be held at airport facilities, sent to mandatory quarantine at the owner's expense, or repatriated. At destinations with strict biosecurity the consequences can be even more severe. Documentary verification before travel is the only way to avoid this scenario. Contact us before traveling.
