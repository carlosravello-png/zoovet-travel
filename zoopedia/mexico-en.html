CDC CLASSIFICATION 2026

Is Mexico High-Risk or Low-Risk for Dog Rabies per the CDC?

Mexico is NOT on the CDC high-risk countries list for dog rabies. The CDC classifies a country as high-risk when it poses a significant risk of importing dog-mediated rabies virus variants into the United States. Countries not on that list — including Mexico — are treated as low-risk or dog-rabies-free for US import purposes.

The CDC's binary rule is explicit: "If a country or political unit is not listed below, it is not considered high risk for importing dog rabies into the United States." Mexico is absent from the high-risk list as of the last published update (May 31, 2024) with no subsequent changes recorded through March 2026.

Practical implication: dogs that have only been in Mexico (and/or other low-risk countries like the USA and Canada) in the 6 months before US entry do not require FAVN rabies titer testing or quarantine reservation. Entry is allowed at any US port of entry, including land borders with Mexico.

Source: https://www.cdc.gov/importation/dogs/high-risk-countries.html

This article covers two scenarios — identify yours

Mexico appears in two distinct contexts for pet travellers. This guide addresses both.

SCENARIO A — Bringing your pet TO Mexico (from Peru, Colombia, Ecuador or other Latin American countries)
SENASICA rules apply. No RNATT required. Process from Peru: approximately 5–10 days with vaccines in order. Health certificate from SENASA (Peru) or equivalent authority required. Up to 3 pets per traveller without import permit.
SCENARIO B — Traveling with your dog FROM Mexico TO the USA
CDC + USDA APHIS rules apply. Mexico = low-risk. No FAVN titer. Required: ISO microchip + CDC Dog Import Form (online, free) + screwworm inspection certificate by a licensed vet within 5 days before departure. No quarantine. Any port of entry valid.

Requirements: Traveling with a Dog from Mexico to the USA (CDC + USDA APHIS 2026)

Mexico's CDC Classification

Low-risk (not listed). Mexico is not included in the CDC's high-risk countries list for dog-mediated rabies. This status has remained unchanged through at least March 2026. Always verify current status immediately before travel at: https://www.cdc.gov/importation/dogs/high-risk-countries.html

CDC Dog Import Form

— Mandatory: Yes, for all dogs entering the USA regardless of country of origin.
— How to obtain it: Complete the form online at the CDC website before travel. The receipt is free, generated immediately, and must be presented at the US port of entry.
— Validity: 6 months for multiple entries from the same country (Mexico), provided the departure country does not change.
— The form system was updated on 5 February 2026 for improved receipt formatting; Mexico's low-risk status is unchanged.
— Official URL: https://www.cdc.gov/importation/dogs/dog-import-form-instructions.html

Microchip

— Required: Yes. ISO-compatible microchip (readable by standard universal scanners).
— Standard: ISO 11784 or ISO 11785 Annex A compatible.
— Must be implanted prior to any documentation required for the trip.

Age requirement

— Minimum age: 6 months at the time of US entry. Dogs under 6 months old are not permitted entry regardless of country of origin or vaccination status.

Health at arrival

— The dog must appear healthy at the US port of entry. Dogs showing signs of illness may be denied entry or referred for further evaluation.

FAVN Rabies Titer Test (RNATT)

— Required: No. Mexico is a low-risk country. FAVN/RNATT titer testing is only required for dogs arriving from CDC high-risk countries. Dogs coming exclusively from Mexico (and other low-risk or dog-rabies-free countries) are exempt from this requirement.

Screwworm (Cochliomyia hominivorax) Inspection — USDA APHIS

— Required: Yes. This is a USDA APHIS requirement separate from CDC rules and applies specifically to dogs traveling from Mexico into the USA.
— What is screwworm? A parasitic fly whose larvae infest living tissue. Mexico is a country affected by this parasite, which is why APHIS requires pre-departure inspection.
— Inspection window: A licensed veterinarian must physically examine the dog within 5 days before the travel date and issue a written certificate confirming the dog is free of screwworm infestation.
— Who can issue the certificate: any licensed private or official veterinarian in Mexico.
— The certificate must accompany the dog at the US port of entry.
— Source: https://www.aphis.usda.gov/pet-travel/another-country-to-us-import/dogs

Quarantine

— Mandatory routine quarantine: No. Dogs from Mexico that meet all requirements (age, microchip, CDC form, screwworm certificate, healthy appearance) are not subject to routine quarantine.
— Conditional quarantine: possible if documents are incomplete or the dog shows signs of illness at entry.

Port of entry restriction

— No restriction. Dogs from Mexico may enter the USA through any port of entry with CBSA/CBP presence, including all land border crossings and international airports.

Rabies vaccination

— CDC federal requirement: Not mandatory for entry from Mexico (low-risk). However, rabies vaccination is strongly recommended by the CDC.
— State-level requirement: Many US states require proof of current rabies vaccination for dogs residing within the state. This is a state regulation, not a federal border requirement. Verify the specific requirements of the destination state.
— Airline requirement: Most US-bound airlines require current rabies vaccination for cargo or cabin transport. Verify with the carrier.

Requirements: Bringing Pets TO Mexico (from Peru and Latin America)

Competent authority

SENASICA — Servicio Nacional de Sanidad, Inocuidad y Calidad Agroalimentaria, under SADER (Secretaría de Agricultura y Desarrollo Rural). Official URL: https://www.gob.mx/senasica

RNATT / FAVN Titer Test

Not required. Mexico does not require rabies serological titer testing (RNATT or FAVN) for pets entering from Peru or other Latin American countries.

Quarantine

— Mandatory routine quarantine: No. Animals that meet all documentary and health requirements enter without quarantine.
— Health inspection: SENASICA inspectors perform a health check at the port of entry. Animals with signs of illness or incomplete documentation may be held pending review.

Requirements for Dogs (entering Mexico)

Microchip

— Recommended standard: ISO 11784 / Annex A of ISO 11785.
— Must be implanted prior to the health certificate examination.

Rabies vaccination

— Mandatory: Yes.
— Must be current at time of travel (within validity period specified on vaccine technical datasheet: 1 or 3 years depending on product).
— If expired: the animal must be revaccinated and the certificate updated prior to issuance.

Other mandatory vaccines (for SENASA certificate issuance in Peru)

SENASICA does not explicitly mandate the full polyvalent vaccination scheme for import, but SENASA (Peru) will not issue the International Health Certificate without a complete and current vaccination scheme. For dogs: Distemper, Parvovirus, Infectious Hepatitis, Parainfluenza and Leptospirosis. The same principle applies in Colombia (ICA), Ecuador (AGROCALIDAD) and other Latin American countries of origin.

Health certificate

— Required: Yes.
— Who issues it: official or accredited veterinarian in the country of origin, endorsed by the national veterinary authority. In Peru: SENASA (https://www.gob.pe/senasa). In Colombia: ICA. In Ecuador: AGROCALIDAD.
— Validity window: the certificate must be issued within 15 days before the travel date.
— Language: Spanish or bilingual Spanish-English accepted by SENASICA.
— Must confirm: microchip number, species, breed, age, vaccination records with lot numbers and validity dates, and veterinary examination confirming clinical health.

Import limit without permit

Up to 3 pets per traveller may enter Mexico without an import permit (for non-commercial, personal travel). More than 3 animals requires a prior import permit issued by SENASICA. Verify current limits at https://www.gob.mx/senasica before each trip.

Port of entry inspection

SENASICA inspectors at Mexican airports and border crossings perform health inspections. The health certificate, vaccination records and microchip must all be available for inspection.

Minimum entry age

SENASICA does not specify a universal minimum age in the primary regulations consulted. Practically, animals must be old enough to have completed their initial vaccination scheme. Verify with SENASICA before travel with puppies under 12 weeks.

Requirements for Cats (entering Mexico)

Microchip

Same as dogs — see previous section.

Rabies vaccination

Same as dogs — current rabies vaccination required.

Other vaccines required for SENASA certificate issuance

SENASA (Peru) requires a complete vaccination scheme to issue the International Health Certificate. For cats: Panleukopenia, Feline viral rhinotracheitis (FHV-1) and Feline calicivirus (FCV). Without a current scheme, SENASA will not certify the animal.

Health certificate

Same as dogs — see previous section.

RNATT titer test

Not required for cats entering Mexico from Peru or Latin America.

Quarantine

Same as dogs — no routine quarantine for compliant animals.

Chronogram: Process from Peru to Mexico

With vaccines in order (microchip and vaccines already valid)

Minimum: approximately 5–10 days. Clinical examination → SENASA health certificate (bilingual) with official endorsement → flight. No waiting periods or titer tests required for Mexico.

Starting from scratch

Approximately 30–45 days: microchip implantation → complete vaccination scheme → 21-day wait for immunity stabilisation (recommended before SENASA examination) → SENASA clinical examination and certificate issuance → flight.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is Mexico a high-risk country for dog rabies according to the CDC in 2026?

No. Mexico is not on the CDC high-risk list. Countries not on that list are treated as low-risk for dog-mediated rabies import into the USA. Source: https://www.cdc.gov/importation/dogs/high-risk-countries.html

Is a FAVN titer test required to bring a dog from Mexico to the USA?

No. FAVN/RNATT titer tests are only required for dogs from CDC high-risk countries. Mexico is low-risk; no titer test is required.

What is the screwworm inspection and why does it apply to Mexico?

USDA APHIS requires that all dogs entering the USA from Mexico must be examined by a licensed veterinarian in Mexico within 5 days before departure and certified free of screwworm (Cochliomyia hominivorax). This requirement is independent of CDC rabies classification and applies regardless of the dog's health status. Source: https://www.aphis.usda.gov/pet-travel/another-country-to-us-import/dogs

Does Mexico require RNATT for pets entering from Peru?

No. SENASICA does not require rabies titer testing for pets entering Mexico from Peru or other Latin American countries. A current rabies vaccination and health certificate are sufficient.

How long does the process take to bring a dog to Mexico from Peru?

If the animal already has a microchip and vaccines in order, the minimum is approximately 5–10 days: clinical examination, SENASA health certificate with official endorsement, and flight. If starting from scratch, approximately 30–45 days including vaccination and waiting period.

Are health requirements the same for all destination countries?

No. Each country sets its own requirements: vaccinations, laboratory tests, official certifications and waiting periods that in many cases cannot be shortened under any circumstances.
