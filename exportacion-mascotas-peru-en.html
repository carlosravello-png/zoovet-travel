Exporting pets from Peru: verified requirements by destination

Exporting a dog or cat from Peru always requires the Zoosanitary Export Certificate (CZE) issued by SENASA — fee S/ 91.10, valid 120 hours (5 days) coordinated to the confirmed flight. The destination determines everything else. European Union and United Kingdom — under EU Regulation 2026/131 in force since April 22, 2026 — require FAVN/RNATT titration ≥ 0.5 IU/mL at an OIE/WOAH-accredited laboratory; we coordinate shipments to the Kansas State Veterinary Diagnostic Laboratory (KSVDL) since 2013. Japan, Australia and New Zealand add mandatory physical quarantine at destination: up to 180 days if the documentary sequence is incomplete. United States classifies Peru as a CDC high-risk country for canine rabies: valid titer or 28-day quarantine at an ACF facility, with entry restricted to 6 airports. China requires prior GACC approval and institutional quarantine. Canada requires only an official CFIA health certificate, microchip and vaccine. South America and Mexico generally do not require FAVN — CZE, vaccines and deworming with deadlines per country. In all cases, the ISO 11784/11785 microchip must be implanted before the rabies vaccine: reversing that sequence invalidates the entire file. We serve the entire country through our network of affiliated clinics that apply our strictest quality standards and comply with international health regulations.

What every destination requires from Peru: the core file

Regardless of where your pet is traveling, every export file from Peru starts with the same four elements. Without any one of them, no destination in the world will accept the animal.

1
ISO 11784/11785 microchip — must be implanted before the vaccine
International electronic identification. Must be placed before any rabies vaccine intended for export use. If implanted after the vaccine, that vaccine is void: the process must start over from scratch. This is the error that most frequently delays files by months.
2
Export-grade rabies vaccine
Applied after the microchip, with a registered export-grade vaccine. Starts the mandatory 30-day count before any serological blood draw. For destinations requiring serology: this vaccine date is the starting point of the entire timeline.
3
Deworming per destination deadlines
Internal and external antiparasitic treatment. The deadline varies: some countries require it days before the flight; others, before the certificate is issued. Confirming the exact deadline per destination is part of the file timeline.
4
SENASA Zoosanitary Export Certificate — the document that enables departure
The Zoosanitary Export Certificate (CZE) is issued by SENASA after a clinical evaluation by a licensed veterinarian. Fee: S/ 91.10. Validity: 120 hours (5 days) from issuance, coordinated to the confirmed flight. It is not processed in advance: issuance date is tied to the departure date. A certificate issued outside that window expires before boarding.

Destinations requiring FAVN/RNATT rabies serology

A group of countries — all classified as rabies-free or in the process of eradication — require the animal to demonstrate, through a blood test, that it has sufficient rabies antibodies. The test is called FAVN (Fluorescent Antibody Virus Neutralization) or RNATT, and the regulatory threshold is ≥ 0.5 IU/mL.

There is no OIE/WOAH-accredited laboratory in Peru to process this test. The sample must be sent abroad. We coordinate shipments to the Rabies Laboratory at Kansas State University (KSVDL) — an OIE/WOAH-accredited laboratory whose results are recognized in all countries requiring titration — since 2013: over 13 years of documented cases, verifiable results, no intermediary laboratory.

Mandatory sequence: microchip → rabies vaccine → minimum 30 days → blood draw → KSVDL shipment → result (7–14 business days) → regulatory waiting period per destination → travel. There are no shortcuts in this sequence: every deadline is regulatory, not optional.

United States: Peru is a CDC high-risk country

The U.S. does not treat all countries equally. Since the CDC reform (August 2024, 42 CFR Part 71), each country is classified as low-risk (direct entry) or high-risk (full protocol). Peru is high-risk. This means:

  • Valid serology titer from a CDC-approved laboratory (KSVDL, Auburn University, U. of Missouri among others), or
  • 28-day quarantine at a CDC-registered ACF facility — prior reservation mandatory, costs borne by owner
  • Entry only through 6 airports with ACF facilities: Atlanta (ATL), Los Angeles (LAX), Miami (MIA), New York JFK, Philadelphia (PHL) and Washington Dulles (IAD)
  • CDC Dog Import Form — free, online, one per dog
  • Screwworm certificate (USDA-APHIS): Peru is an endemic country. Official veterinary inspection certificate within 5 days prior to boarding
Cats are an exception: the CDC high/low-risk classification applies only to dogs. Cats do not require a rabies test to enter the continental U.S. Hawaii and Guam do impose local quarantine for cats.

Japan and Australia/New Zealand: mandatory physical quarantine at destination

These destinations not only require FAVN: they impose mandatory physical quarantine at approved facilities in the destination country. Booking must be made before purchasing the airline ticket.

Japan may require up to 180 days of quarantine at the Animal Quarantine Station if the file arrives with any deadline missed. Quarantine costs are borne by the owner. With a perfect file, quarantine at destination may be reduced, but never fully eliminated when the origin is Peru.

Australia and New Zealand operate with pre-booked approved quarantine facilities. Missing any deadline can result in the animal being returned to the country of origin. These are the destinations requiring the most planning time from Peru: minimum 8–10 months.

China: GACC protocol and prior approval

China regulates pet imports through the General Administration of Customs (GACC). Import approval must be obtained before travel — there is no entry without prior authorization. Requirements include FAVN, institutional quarantine at destination and specific customs documentation. Each province may have additional requirements.

Canada: the most straightforward protocol for dogs from Peru

The Canadian Food Inspection Agency (CFIA) does not classify Peru as a high-risk country for canine rabies. The process is simpler than the U.S.: official health certificate, ISO microchip and current rabies vaccine. No FAVN requirement, no mandatory quarantine at destination. It is one of the most straightforward destinations for pets from Peru.

South America, Mexico and Central America: certificate plus vaccines, no FAVN

Most destinations within South America and Mexico do not require rabies serology. The core file — SENASA certificate, microchip, current vaccines, deworming — is generally sufficient. Deadlines vary by country.

The IATA Live Animal Regulations (LAR) 2026 do not work with generic sizes. The correct kennel is calculated from your pet's actual measurements: length (nose to tail), shoulder height, shoulder width and total height. A kennel that is too small may be rejected at the airport. One that is too large can move dangerously in the hold.

Frequently asked questions about exporting pets from Peru

Is Peru a CDC high-risk country for dogs traveling to the United States?
Yes. Peru is classified as a high-risk country for canine rabies by the CDC (in effect since August 2024, 42 CFR Part 71). Dogs departing Peru to the U.S. need valid serology from a CDC-approved laboratory, or 28 days of mandatory quarantine at an ACF facility. Entry is permitted only through 6 specific airports: Atlanta, Los Angeles, Miami, New York JFK, Philadelphia and Washington Dulles.
Which laboratory accepts samples from Peru for the EU and the U.S.?
The Kansas State Veterinary Diagnostic Laboratory (KSVDL) at Kansas State University is the OIE/WOAH-accredited laboratory that Zoovet Travel has worked with since 2013. Its results are recognized by the EU, UK, Japan, Australia and the U.S. There is no approved laboratory in Peru to process FAVN/RNATT: the sample must leave the country. We coordinate sample collection in Trujillo and international shipping.
Does one FAVN test work for both Europe and the U.S.?
Yes, with conditions. FAVN/RNATT titration ≥ 0.5 IU/mL from an OIE/WOAH-accredited laboratory is valid for both the EU and the CDC, as long as the rabies vaccination protocol remains current. For the EU, a minimum 3-month waiting period from the draw date is required; for the U.S., the titer must be valid at travel time. A single well-coordinated test can cover both destinations — and more.
Can the microchip be placed after the rabies vaccine?
No. The microchip must be implanted before the rabies vaccine. If placed after, that vaccine is invalidated for export: the process must restart from vaccination. This is the most frequent error that delays files by months. Correct, non-negotiable sequence: ISO microchip → rabies vaccine → (minimum 30 days) → serology sample if destination requires it.
What happens if the certificate expires before travel?
The SENASA Zoosanitary Export Certificate (CZE) is valid for 5 days. If the flight is rescheduled beyond that window, the certificate expires and a new one must be issued. That is why we coordinate issuance to the exact confirmed flight date, not in advance. The SENASA fee (S/ 91.10) applies per issuance.
Can everything be managed from Trujillo without traveling to Lima?
The zoosanitary inspection is carried out at SENASA offices authorized for animal inspection in each region or department of Peru. Sample collection is done at authorized veterinary clinics that comply with Zoovet Travel's protocol and quality standards in accordance with international regulations. We serve the entire country through our network of affiliated clinics that apply our strictest standards and comply with international health regulations. See the official directory →
Do cats have the same export process as dogs from Peru?
The SENASA certificate, microchip and rabies vaccine apply equally to dogs and cats. FAVN/RNATT serology is also mandatory for cats going to the EU, UK, Japan and Australia. The key difference: the U.S. does not require a rabies test for cats (the CDC high/low-risk classification applies only to dogs). Hawaii and Guam do impose local quarantine for cats.
How much does it cost to export a pet from Peru?
The SENASA fee for the zoosanitary certificate is S/ 91.10. Additional costs include: microchip (if not yet implanted), export-grade rabies vaccine, FAVN serology at KSVDL (if destination requires it), IATA-approved kennel (from S/ 159.90 at Zoovet Travel, with nationwide shipping to Peru) and vet fees. Destinations without FAVN — Chile, Brazil, Colombia — are the most affordable. Europe or the U.S. add the KSVDL analysis cost. Contact us for a personalized quote by destination and current document status.
What is the difference between pet export, relocation and international transfer?
All three terms describe the same process. "Pet export" is the legal term (SENASA certificate). "Pet relocation" is used in international moves. "International pet transfer" and "traveling abroad with pets" are everyday terms. The health, documentary and kennel requirements are identical regardless of the reason for travel: vacation, permanent move or job relocation.
