CDC and dog importation to the United States: analysis of the 2024 Interim Final Rule and the DogBot verification system

In August 2024, the CDC Interim Final Rule came into force, modernizing the framework for canine importation to the United States. This article analyzes its technical requirements, the DogBot portal, the interaction with USDA APHIS endorsement, and practical implications for dogs exported from Peru.

Structured abstract Background: The CDC Interim Final Rule (August 2024) established new controls for dog importation to the US, replacing the previous system based solely on vaccination certificates. Objective: Describe current technical requirements, document flow, and critical compliance points. Methods: Documentary analysis of Federal Register Vol. 89 No. 89 and USDA APHIS technical documentation. Results: The rule requires ISO 11784/11785 microchip, prior DogBot registration, endorsed health certificate, and for dogs from high-risk countries or vaccinated outside the US, RFFIT rabies serology at an approved laboratory. Conclusions: Exporting dogs from Peru to the US requires 6-12 months of planning and specialized veterinary coordination.

1. Background: why the CDC reformed the rules in 2024

Prior to August 2024, dog importation to the United States was governed primarily by Title 42 of the Code of Federal Regulations (42 CFR Part 71), which required only a rabies vaccination certificate at least 30 days old for animals from countries with canine rabies. The system was vulnerable to document fraud and did not verify the individual identity of the animal.

Between 2021 and 2023, the CDC documented multiple cases of imported dogs with fraudulent documentation, including the 2021 "rescue puppy scandal," which led to a temporary suspension of imports from certain countries. The Interim Final Rule published in the Federal Register on May 8, 2024, effective August 1, 2024, established the current framework.

1.1 Applicable legal framework

The CDC's authority to regulate animal importation derives from the Public Health Service Act (42 U.S.C. § 264). Coordination with USDA APHIS occurs at the port of entry, where both agencies have concurrent jurisdiction over imported animals.

2. Technical requirements under the 2024 rule

2.1 Identification: mandatory microchip

Every dog imported to the US must carry an ISO 11784/11785 microchip implanted before the rabies vaccination used to meet import requirements. This sequence requirement is critical: if the microchip is implanted after vaccination, the vaccination history is not considered valid for import purposes, because it cannot be verifiably linked to the animal.

Critical sequence point Microchip → Rabies vaccination → Wait (if applicable) → RFFIT (if applicable) → Health certificate → USDA APHIS endorsement → DogBot registration → Travel. Any alteration in this order can invalidate the entire process and result in the animal being detained upon arrival.

2.2 The DogBot portal: pre-arrival registration

DogBot is the CDC online registration platform available at dogs.cdc.gov. Registration must be completed before the flight and generates a confirmation number that the owner or agent must present upon arrival. The portal captures microchip number, country of origin, transit countries, vaccination history, RFFIT results if applicable, and flight details.

The system automatically classifies the animal as compliant (meets all requirements, direct entry), conditional (requires inspection at the port of entry), or non-compliant (the animal cannot be imported under those conditions).

3. RFFIT: the rabies serological test

The Rapid Fluorescent Focus Inhibition Test (RFFIT) is the gold standard assay for determining rabies antibody titers in serum. The CDC requires a result of ≥ 0.5 IU/mL analyzed in a USDA-approved laboratory. The blood sample must be taken at least 30 days after the rabies vaccination used for testing, and the result must be obtained at least 28 days before the planned travel date.

4. Health certificate and USDA APHIS endorsement

The health certificate for export to the US must use APHIS Form 7001. The endorsement validates the Peruvian veterinary certificate through USDA APHIS International Services. The endorsement is valid for 10 days of travel, so it must be coordinated with the scheduled flight.

5. Critical errors in Peru–US exports

  • Incorrect sequence: microchip implanted after rabies vaccination, invalidating the documentary history.
  • Non-approved laboratory: RFFIT performed at a Peruvian laboratory not approved by USDA (no Peruvian laboratory currently holds this approval).
  • Incomplete DogBot: registration not finalized or with data inconsistent with the health certificate.
  • Late endorsement: health certificate endorsed more than 10 days before the flight, requiring re-issuance.
  • Animal age: puppies under 6 months cannot be imported to the US from high-risk rabies countries.
