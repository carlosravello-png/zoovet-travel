MPI New Zealand: the world's strictest biosecurity system and quarantine requirements for pets

New Zealand maintains the most demanding pet importation regime in the world. The Ministry for Primary Industries (MPI) applies a country risk classification system, mandatory post-arrival quarantine of up to 10 days, and comprehensive serological and parasitological requirements. This article analyses the current Import Health Standard and the full protocol for exports from Peru.

Jessica Ysabel Camacho Garcia, M.V.Z., CMVP 12434 — Zoovet Travel, Trujillo, Peru · ORCID: 0009-0002-6837-5311
Structured Abstract Objective: To analyse the MPI New Zealand regulatory framework for the importation of dogs and cats, with emphasis on country risk group classification, the complete protocol applicable to Peru (Group 3), and the clinical and operational implications of mandatory post-arrival quarantine. Method: Review of the MPI Import Health Standard (IHS) current as of January 2026, country risk group classification, quarantine requirements at accredited facilities, and Zoovet Travel's experience in Peru–New Zealand exports (2013–2026). Results: Peru is classified in Group 3 (rest of world). The protocol requires: ISO microchip implanted before any relevant antiparasitic treatment, rabies vaccination, RNATT (RFFIT/FAVN) ≥0.5 IU/mL with a 6-month waiting period post-serology for Group 3, specific parasitological treatments within strict time windows, and 10-day post-arrival quarantine at an MPI-approved facility (Levin Animal Quarantine Service, Auckland). The minimum total timeline from Peru is 8–10 months. Conclusion: New Zealand is the world's most technically and logistically demanding destination for pet exports. The most costly error is starting the process without understanding that post-arrival quarantine is unavoidable for Group 3 animals and entails significant economic and animal welfare costs.
Scope of this article Descriptive technical document based on the MPI Import Health Standard current as of January 2026. Always verify at mpi.govt.nz before starting any export process, as IHS documents are periodically updated.

Section 1 — Why New Zealand is the world's most demanding destination

New Zealand is a geographically isolated archipelago in the South Pacific that has remained free of rabies, leptospirosis (several serovars endemic in other countries) and multiple other infectious and parasitic diseases, thanks to one of the world's strictest import biosecurity policies. The Ministry for Primary Industries (MPI) manages this system through Import Health Standards (IHS), legally binding technical documents that establish species- and country-specific requirements.

The underlying philosophy of the New Zealand system differs from other regulatory frameworks such as those of the EU or USA: while the latter allow entry without quarantine when documentary requirements are met, New Zealand assumes that no document is an absolute guarantee and requires mandatory post-arrival quarantine for all animals from higher-risk countries, regardless of the quality of documentation presented.

1.1 The Import Health Standard as a regulatory instrument

IHS documents are the New Zealand equivalent of import requirements in other countries, but with one particularity: they have the force of law under the Biosecurity Act 1993 and are updated by MPI periodically (usually every 3–5 years or when there are significant changes in the global health landscape). The current IHS for dogs and cats exhaustively establishes requirements by risk group, including not only vaccination and serology, but also parasitological treatments with strict time windows, health certificates in specific formats, and post-arrival quarantine procedures.

1.2 The microchip: critical sequence in New Zealand

As with other destinations, the ISO 11784/11785 microchip must be implanted before any treatment or procedure forming part of the export protocol. In the case of New Zealand, this is especially relevant because the first mandatory antiparasitic treatment must also be performed with the animal already identified. A microchip implanted after the first parasitological treatment invalidates that treatment and requires it to be repeated.

Section 2 — Country classification by risk group

MPI classifies countries of origin into four risk groups with progressively more demanding requirements:

Group 1: lowest-risk countries

Includes Australia and a very limited number of Pacific island countries with health status equivalent to New Zealand's. Group 1 animals may enter without post-arrival quarantine if documentary requirements are met, which are significantly less demanding (RFFIT generally not required).

Group 2: moderate-risk countries

Includes most European countries with good rabies control records and without other diseases prioritized by New Zealand. RFFIT with result ≥0.5 IU/mL required, with shorter post-arrival quarantine duration.

Group 3: rest of world (includes Peru)

Peru is classified in Group 3, along with most of Latin America, Africa, Asia and other countries whose health profile is not equivalent to New Zealand's. This is the group with the greatest technical demands and mandatory 10-day post-arrival quarantine at an MPI-approved facility.

Group 3: unavoidable quarantine For animals from Peru (Group 3), the 10-day post-arrival quarantine at an MPI-approved facility is mandatory and unavoidable, even if all documents are perfectly in order. There is no exemption or alternative procedure. The owner must plan quarantine costs (approximately NZD 1,200–1,800 per animal) and the period of separation from the animal from the very start of the process.

Section 3 — Full protocol for Group 3 from Peru

3.1 Mandatory technical sequence

  1. ISO 11784/11785 microchip implanted before any other procedure in the protocol.
  2. Rabies vaccination with authorized inactivated vaccine. The first vaccine valid for the process must be administered when the animal is at least 12 weeks old and the microchip is already implanted.
  3. 30-day wait after primary vaccination before blood collection for RFFIT/FAVN (termed RNATT in MPI nomenclature).
  4. RNATT (RFFIT or FAVN) at an MPI-approved laboratory: result ≥0.5 IU/mL. MPI maintains its own list of approved laboratories, including laboratories in the USA, Australia and Europe. No Peruvian laboratory is MPI-approved.
  5. 6-month wait from the date of the positive RNATT result before traveling to New Zealand. This 6-month wait is the main differentiator of the New Zealand system from Australia (3 months) or the UK (3 months).
  6. Antiparasitic treatments within strict time windows before the flight (against ectoparasites, helminths, and Echinococcus depending on species and group).
  7. Health certificate in MPI format, issued by SENASA-accredited official veterinarian, within the deadlines established before the flight.
  8. 10-day post-arrival quarantine at MPI-approved facility.
Minimum total timeline from Peru (Group 3) The combination of 30 days post-vaccine + RNATT laboratory time + 6-month wait + antiparasitic treatments + health certificate equals a minimum of 7.5–9 months from first vaccination to flight. Adding the 10-day quarantine at destination, the complete process from start until the owner recovers their pet exceeds 8 months under ideal conditions.

3.2 Antiparasitic treatments and their time windows

MPI requires specific treatments against different parasite groups, each with distinct time windows relative to the arrival date in New Zealand:

  • Against ectoparasites (ticks, fleas): treatment with MPI-approved product in a window of 14–21 days before arrival, recorded in the health certificate.
  • Against intestinal helminths: treatment with approved product (praziquantel or combination with fenbendazole) within a specified period before travel.
  • Against Echinococcus (dogs only): praziquantel at minimum dose of 5 mg/kg, within 14 days before arrival (broader window than the UK's 1–5 days).
Mandatory verification of MPI-approved products Not all antiparasitic products available in Peru are on the MPI approved products list. Using a non-listed product, even if pharmacologically equivalent, may invalidate the documented treatment. Always check the updated list of approved products at mpi.govt.nz before selecting the antiparasitic.

Section 4 — Quarantine in New Zealand: logistics and animal welfare

4.1 MPI-approved facilities

Companion animal quarantine facilities in New Zealand are operated by MPI-accredited private companies. The main ones are the Levin Animal Quarantine Service (in Levin, Manawatu region, approximately 100 km from Wellington) and facilities in Auckland. Most international flights arrive in Auckland, from where animals are transported to the designated quarantine facility. The cost of 10-day quarantine ranges from NZD 1,200 to NZD 1,800 per animal (approximately USD 730–1,100), excluding transport from the airport to the facility.

4.2 What happens during quarantine

During the 10 days of quarantine, facility staff perform daily clinical observation of the animal, document verification, and may request additional diagnostic tests if the animal shows clinical signs or if there are documentary discrepancies. Owners may visit their animals at the facility according to each center's internal rules. After 10 days, if the animal shows no clinical or documentary findings justifying retention, it is released to the owner without further procedures.

4.3 Animal welfare impact

The 10-day quarantine at a supervised facility, combined with the stress of long-haul air travel (Lima–Auckland generally involves more than 18 hours of flight with one or two stopovers), has a real impact on animal welfare. Animals with pre-existing chronic conditions (cardiac, hepatic, respiratory disease) or brachycephalic breeds with high travel stress sensitivity are particularly vulnerable. Veterinary assessment prior to starting the process should include an evaluation of fitness for flight and quarantine, not just serological status.

Section 5 — Most frequent errors in Peru–New Zealand exports

5.1 Assuming 3-month rather than 6-month waiting period

The most frequent and most costly error: the owner starts the process assuming the post-RNATT waiting period is 3 months (as in the UK or Australia), and purchases a ticket to travel 3 months after the result. Upon verifying the actual requirements, they discover they must wait 6 months, implying ticket changes and additional costs. The 6-month period for Group 3 is clearly established in the current IHS.

5.2 Non-MPI-approved RNATT laboratory

MPI maintains its own list of approved laboratories for RFFIT/FAVN that is not identical to the USDA or EU approved laboratory lists. An RFFIT result from a USDA-approved laboratory may not be recognized by MPI if that laboratory does not appear on its specific list. Always verify at mpi.govt.nz before sending the sample.

5.3 Non-MPI-approved antiparasitic products

Many antiparasitic products marketed in Peru (including internationally recognized brands) are not on the MPI approved products list. Using a non-listed product invalidates that documented treatment and may require repeating it with an approved product, with the associated timeline implications.

5.4 Health certificate in incorrect format

MPI requires its own health certificate format, different from the US APHIS Form 7001 or the UK AHC. The certificate must be downloaded from the MPI portal, completed in English, and endorsed by SENASA before travel. A certificate in a format other than required is rejected at the port of entry.

Clinical evidence — Zoovet Travel 2013–2026 New Zealand is the destination with the highest number of pre-process consultations abandoned after the first information session. 67% of owners contacting Zoovet Travel to export to New Zealand withdraw upon learning the total timeline (7–9 months), quarantine costs (NZD 1,200–1,800) and the inevitability of the 10-day post-arrival quarantine. The 33% who decide to continue generally do so with the advantage of having planned sufficiently in advance, significantly reducing the risk of error in the process.
