MPI New Zealand: the world's strictest biosecurity system and quarantine requirements for dogs and cats

New Zealand maintains the most stringent biosecurity regime for pet importation in the world. The Ministry for Primary Industries (MPI) classifies countries of origin into risk categories that determine the applicable protocol, including mandatory quarantine of up to 10 days for animals from high-risk countries such as Peru.

Structured abstract Background: New Zealand is free of rabies and multiple zoonoses, requiring mandatory quarantine for all imported pets. Objective: Describe the MPI country classification system and technical requirements for dogs and cats from Peru. Results: Peru is in Group 3 (high risk); dogs must complete a 6-9 month pre-travel protocol and 10 days of mandatory quarantine in New Zealand. Conclusions: Peru–New Zealand export is technically possible but requires minimum 9 months planning.

1. Why New Zealand is the most demanding destination

New Zealand is a geographically isolated archipelago that has maintained rabies-free, leptospirosis-free (several serovars), and Brucella canis-free status for decades. The Ministry for Primary Industries (MPI) manages pet importation through a country group classification framework.

2. Country group classification

Peru is in Group 3 (rest of world). The complete protocol applies: ISO microchip, rabies vaccination, 30-day wait, RFFIT ≥ 0.5 IU/mL at an IAEA-approved laboratory, 6-month wait from the sample date (not the result date), specific anti-parasitic treatments per MPI protocols, export health certificate, and 10 days of mandatory quarantine in New Zealand at the owner's expense.

The 6-month wait from RFFIT is the critical factor Unlike other countries requiring 3 months from a positive RFFIT result, New Zealand requires 6 months from the sample date (not the result). This distinction can add weeks to the process depending on laboratory processing time.

3. Quarantine in New Zealand

Quarantine facilities for pets are located primarily in Auckland and operated by MPI-accredited private companies. The animal is received directly from the plane and remains in quarantine for 10 days, during which MPI performs veterinary inspections. If documentary non-compliance is detected, the quarantine may be extended or the animal returned to the country of origin at the owner's expense.

Quarantine is not a formality: the MPI has deported animals to their country of origin when documentation was incomplete. The cost of return and additional quarantine falls on the owner.
