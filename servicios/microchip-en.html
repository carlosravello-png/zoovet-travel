What it is

A microchip is a small transponder the size of a grain of rice that is placed under your pet's skin, between the shoulder blades. It has no battery and emits nothing on its own: when a reader is brought close, it returns a unique 15-digit code. It is a permanent identification, for life, and it is the international standard for any country to recognize your animal.

What we do

We implant the microchip with sterile material, register it and —this is key— we verify it on the spot with the reader to confirm that it was placed correctly and reads without any problems. A chip that cannot be read is as good as not having one.

Why it is the first step of every journey

The microchip is the anchor of the entire file: every subsequent document —the rabies vaccine, the blood test, the certificate— is issued in the name of that number. That is why the European Union requires the microchip to be placed before or on the same day as the rabies vaccine: if the vaccine predates the chip, it simply does not count, and the process has to start over.2 It must also comply with the ISO 11784/11785 standard; if it does not, the owner would have to bring their own reader to the border.

The microchip on the inside (which standard and what it means)

  • ISO 11784 — the international standard that defines the structure of the code: 15 digits, where the first ones identify the country or the manufacturer.1
  • ISO 11785 — defines how the reader activates and reads the transponder, so that the same scanner works at any airport or border.1
  • FDX-B (full-duplex type B, 134,2 kHz) — the technology that the world's standard scanners read; it is what you see on the reader's screen.
  • Permanent and painless — it is placed in seconds with a fine needle, requires no anesthesia and lasts the animal's whole life.

A microchip that does not comply with the international standard, or that cannot be read, can hold up the journey at the control point. That is why we always verify the reading before discharging you.

Frequently asked questions

What microchip does my pet need to travel?

One that complies with the international ISO 11784/11785 standard, with a unique 15-digit code. If the microchip does not comply with that standard, the owner must bring their own compatible reader to the border control point. We developed this with evidence, in three languages: our technical series on the identification microchip and the guide what the microchip is and how to get it done.

Does the microchip go before or after the rabies vaccine?

Before, or on the same day. The European Union and other destinations require that the rabies vaccination not predate the placement of the microchip; if it does, the vaccine is not considered valid for travel and the process must be repeated.
