What it is

It is a blood test that measures whether your pet's rabies vaccine really produced enough defenses. Being vaccinated is not enough: several countries require proof that the vaccine worked, measured as a titer of neutralizing antibodies (the amount of defenses against the virus present in the blood). It is the difference between "my pet is vaccinated" and "it is proven to be protected".

What we do

We take your pet's blood sample at the clinic and send it to an approved laboratory for this test. When the result arrives, that is the point where the official travel clock starts ticking.

Why it matters, and why timing rules

Destinations such as the European Union, the United Kingdom or Japan will not admit your pet without this result. To enter the European Union from a country like ours (a non-listed third country), the sequence is strict and has no shortcuts: microchip → rabies vaccine → the blood sample must be taken at least 30 days after the vaccine, with a result of 0,5 UI/ml or more, at an approved laboratory → and then a 3-month wait (90 days) before the pet can enter.2 That calendar is non-negotiable: starting late can cost months. It all begins with a rabies vaccine properly administered and recorded.

Inside the test (which test is used and what it means)

  • FAVN (fluorescent antibody virus neutralisation) — one of only two tests that WOAH accepts for the international movement of animals.1
  • RFFIT (rapid fluorescent focus inhibition test) — the other internationally accepted test.1
  • The threshold: a titer equal to or greater than 0,5 UI/ml (international units per milliliter) — the minimum level of defenses required by high-standard destinations.
  • The laboratory matters: the test must be done at an approved laboratory; the European Union maintains an official list. A result from an unrecognized laboratory is not valid for the procedure.

Something few explain: ELISA-type tests are useful for internal surveillance, but they are not accepted for the international movement of pets. That is why we work only with FAVN or RFFIT.

When to start

As soon as you are clear about the destination country. The countdown —vaccine, 30-day wait, sample, result, 3-month wait— can add up to several months. Starting early is the only thing that guarantees the travel date. Write to us with the destination and the approximate date and we'll put together the exact schedule for you.

Frequently asked questions

What is rabies serology or the rabies blood test?

It is a test that measures the level of neutralizing antibodies against rabies in the blood, to prove that the rabies vaccine produced enough protection. High-standard destinations require a titer equal to or greater than 0,5 UI/ml, measured with the FAVN or RFFIT tests at an approved laboratory. To dig deeper with evidence, we publish in three languages the technical basis of the RFFIT and FAVN tests and the guide what the rabies serological titer is.

How long does the serology process take to travel to the European Union?

From a non-listed country like ours, after the rabies vaccine you must wait at least 30 days to take the blood sample, and then a wait of 3 months (90 days) from the sampling before the pet can enter the EU. In total it is several months, which is why it is best to start as soon as the destination is defined.
