Requirements to enter Australia with your pet (2026): the rule changes depending on your country

Australia is one of the strictest destinations in the world and only admits dogs and cats from approved countries, sorted by the DAFF into three groups. From a Group 1 country (New Zealand and territories) the process is minimal. From Group 2 (rabies-free countries: Japan, Singapore, the Pacific islands, Iceland, Hawaii, Mauritius…) you need an import permit, tests and quarantine. From Group 3 (rabies controlled: United States, United Kingdom, most of the European Union, Canada, Chile, Argentina…) the protocol is full: microchip, rabies vaccine, rabies blood test (RNATT) with a 180-day wait, permit and quarantine. If your country is not approved —Brazil, Colombia, China, India, Mexico, Romania, Russia, almost all of Africa, Latin America and much of Asia—, you cannot export directly: you must move your pet to an approved country and complete its entire process there (the fast transit route ended on 1 March 2023). Every pet undergoes quarantine at a single facility: Mickleham (Victoria), with a minimum of 30 days that drops to 10 only if you verify your pet's identity before the blood test.

First step: find your country

Australia does not treat all countries the same. Find yours: the table tells you which DAFF group you fall into —and therefore whether you face a minimal process, a permit with quarantine, the full protocol with a blood test, or the long road through an approved third country.

The rule that stops the most trips: if your country is not in Group 1, 2 or 3, it is not approved and you cannot fly directly to Australia. You will have to take your pet to an approved country and complete the entire process there. Start by verifying your group.

Australia's four categories

The DAFF system classifies the country of origin according to its rabies situation. The whole process depends on this:

  • Group 1 — minimal process. New Zealand, Norfolk Island and Cocos (Keeling) Islands. From New Zealand and Norfolk Island you don't even need an import permit.
  • Group 2 — approved rabies-free countries. They need an import permit, but being rabies-free they avoid the 180-day wait of the blood test. Here you find Japan, Singapore, Iceland, Mauritius, Hawaii, Guam and the Pacific islands.
  • Group 3 — rabies controlled, approved. United States, United Kingdom, almost all of the European Union, Canada, Chile, Argentina, Uruguay, the Emirates, South Africa, South Korea, etc. This is the full protocol: it includes the rabies blood test and the 180-day wait.
  • Not approved. Any country that does not appear on the previous lists. It cannot export directly; it must route through an approved country.

A verified fact that surprises: unlike the United Kingdom, for Australia Mexico, Romania, Russia and Malaysia are NOT approved countries. Romania is, in fact, the only European Union country missing from the DAFF list. Always verify yours in the table, don't assume it.

If your country is NOT approved

This applies to Brazil, Colombia, Peru, China, India, Mexico, Romania, Russia, almost all of Latin America, Africa and much of Asia. The path is:

  1. Move your pet to a Group 2 or 3 country (for example, an EU country, the United States, Canada, Chile, Japan or Singapore).
  2. Complete the entire process there of that approved country following its DAFF step-by-step guide, as if your pet originated in that country.
  3. Export to Australia from that approved country.

⚠ Key change of 1 March 2023: since that date the fast transit route through an approved country no longer exists. The animal must genuinely complete the entire process in the approved country, which in practice takes around six months (due to the vaccine, the blood test and the 180-day wait). It is not a layover formality: it is residing and preparing there.

The full protocol (Group 3)

If you come from the United States, United Kingdom, the European Union, Canada, Chile, Argentina and other Group 3 countries, this is the path:

  1. ISO microchip (11784/11785), which is scanned at every step of the process.
  2. Valid rabies vaccine, administered after the microchip.
  3. Rabies blood test (RNATT): after the vaccine a sample is taken that must give a valid result at an approved laboratory.
  4. Wait 180 days before entry into Australia, counted according to the test rule. This clock is the most common cause of delayed trips.
  5. Apply for the import permit from the DAFF (paid separately from quarantine).
  6. Book the quarantine at Mickleham before travelling.
  7. Official veterinary certificate and the antiparasitic treatments indicated by your country's guide.

Laboratories and tests

The rabies blood test must be processed at an approved laboratory; always confirm that the laboratory is current before sending the sample. The DAFF's country guides may also require additional tests and treatments against internal and external parasites, and in dogs tests for tick-borne diseases. The exact list varies by country and species: confirm it in the official guide for your country before starting.

Quarantine in Mickleham and real costs

Every approved pet undergoes quarantine at the single official facility: the post-entry quarantine centre of Mickleham, in Victoria. The booking is made through the official DAFF system before travelling and slots are limited. The minimum stay is 30 days, reduced to a minimum of 10 days only if an official veterinarian verifies your pet's identity BEFORE the blood sample (RNATT) is taken; it may be extended for biosecurity reasons. And the cats?

The group system and the Mickleham quarantine apply equally to dogs and cats. The rabies blood test and the 180 days apply to both when the origin is Group 3. The additional antiparasitic tests and treatments depend on the guide by country and species; in dogs tests for tick-borne diseases are usually added. Review the official guide for your country before planning dates.

⚠ Hybrid cats banned. Australia does NOT allow hybrid cats crossed from domestic cats with wild species: Bengal, Savannah, Safari and Chausie. For the Bengal, exemptions have ended and from 1 March 2026 no Bengal may enter. This ban is under environmental law (Department of Climate Change, Energy, the Environment and Water — DCCEEW, exotic.species@dcceew.gov.au), not DAFF biosecurity.

Frequently asked questions

Is my country approved to import my pet into Australia?
Australia only admits dogs and cats from approved countries (Group 1, 2 or 3). If your country does not appear on any list, it is not approved and you cannot export directly. Look for it in the table.
What happens if my country is not approved?
You must take your pet to a Group 2 or 3 country and complete the entire process there before exporting it to Australia. Since 1 March 2023 the fast transit route no longer exists: it takes around six months.
What is the rabies blood test (RNATT)?
It is the titration of rabies antibodies. For Group 3, after vaccinating a sample is taken that must give a valid result at an approved laboratory, followed by a 180-day wait before entering Australia.
How much does quarantine in Australia cost?
Quarantine is at Mickleham (Victoria): minimum 30 days, dropping to 10 only if you verify your pet's identity before the RNATT. DAFF fees: booking $269, import $1.078, 10 days $530, 30 days $1.590, inspection/assessment $80 per 30 min. The permit is paid separately; the DAFF estimates ~$4.100 AUD in total. Minimum cost ~AUD $1,877; DAFF estimates ~$4,100 total. Confirm with DAFF.
Are the rules the same for dogs and cats?
The group system and quarantine apply to both. The country guides may require additional antiparasitic tests and treatments, and in dogs tests for tick-borne diseases. Confirm in your country's guide.
