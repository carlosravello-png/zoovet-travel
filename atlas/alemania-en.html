Entering Germany with your pet (2026): the rule changes depending on your country — and Germany bans the import of 4 breeds

To enter Germany with your dog, cat or ferret, the European Union regime comes first: depending on your country of origin, entry is direct (microchip, rabies vaccine and certificate) or it also requires a rabies blood test (RNATT) ≥0,5 UI/ml and a 90-day wait. But Germany has the strictest national layer in Europe: a federal ban on importing four breeds (Pitbull-Terrier, American Staffordshire-Terrier, Staffordshire-Bullterrier, Bullterrier) and their crosses — the Zoll (customs) seizes them and there is criminal liability. In addition, each state (Bundesland) has its own breed list, and on arrival you must register the dog and pay the Hundesteuer tax. First step: locate your country of origin

To enter Germany from outside the EU, the first thing that decides your path is your country of origin. Find yours: the table tells you whether you get direct entry (EU/EEA or listed country) or the protocol with blood test and 3-month wait (non-listed country, like Peru).

A verified fact that surprises: being "rich" or nearby does not guarantee being listed. Russia and Belarus are no longer on the lists (they need a blood test); and neither are South Korea or Uruguay. By contrast, Mexico, Argentina, Chile and —since June 2026— Montenegro and Serbia are. Check yours, don't assume it.

What sets Germany apart: the strictest national layer

The European regime is only half the story. Germany adds a layer of its own that can ruin a move if it is not known in advance.

🚫 Germany bans IMPORTING four breeds (it is not a licence: it is a ban)

The federal law HundVerbrEinfG (§2) bans bringing into the country dogs of the breeds Pitbull-Terrier, American Staffordshire-Terrier, Staffordshire-Bullterrier and Bullterrier, as well as their crosses. And note: the ban applies both to import from outside the EU (Einfuhr) and to movement from another EU country (Verbringen) — you cannot even bring one from France or Spain.

If you try, the Zoll (customs) can seize the animal at the airport, and there is criminal liability (up to 2 years in prison or a fine). It is the toughest regime of the three major European destinations: Spain requires a licence, Italy has no list, and Germany directly bans entry.

And in addition, each state (Bundesland) has its own list (Listenhunde): four states —Lower Saxony, Schleswig-Holstein, Mecklenburg-Vorpommern and Thuringia— no longer have a breed list; the other twelve do, sometimes with two categories and requirements of permit, insurance or test. Since the rule depends on the destination state, always verify it for your specific Land.

Entry and authority in Germany

From outside the EU, your pet must enter through an authorised point of entry (Einreiseort); the list is published by the BMLEH (not required for those arriving from Andorra, Switzerland, Norway, Iceland, Liechtenstein, Monaco, San Marino, the Faroe Islands, Greenland, Gibraltar or the Vatican). At the border, the Zoll does the initial document check and the Amtstierarzt (official veterinarian) the veterinary check. The federal authority is the BMLEH (Federal Ministry of Agriculture, Food and Homeland, formerly BMEL) and, at local level, the Veterinärämter.

The entry or transit of puppies under 15 weeks is not authorised (12 weeks to vaccinate + 21 days of validity). To enter from a third country, the animal health certificate is valid for 6 months, and if the owner is not travelling a written authorisation (Vollmacht) is required.

Registering your pet and the dog tax (Hundesteuer)

When you settle in Germany, you must register your dog in the municipality (Bürgeramt or Ordnungsamt) within about 2 to 4 weeks, and pay the Hundesteuer (annual dog tax). The amount is set by each municipality: around €90-150 for a normal dog, but it can rise to several hundred euros a year if the dog is a Listenhund. Apart from the tax registration, the microchip can be registered for free in registries such as TASSO e.V. or FINDEFIX, which help recover lost pets.

If your country is NOT listed (protocol with blood test)

This applies to Peru, Brazil, Colombia, China, Russia, Belarus and almost all of Africa and Asia. For your dog or cat to be able to enter Germany, the path is:

  1. ISO microchip (11784/11785, 15 digits), implanted before the rabies vaccine. If the order is reversed, the vaccine does not count.
  2. Rabies vaccine, with the animal at least 12 weeks old; wait 21 days after the primary vaccination.
  3. Rabies blood test (RNATT). The sample is taken at least 30 days after the vaccine and at most 3 months before entry, at an EU-designated laboratory; result ≥0,5 UI/ml.
  4. Wait 90 days (3 months) counted from the sampling before entering. (Does not apply to the re-entry of a pet that already had a valid RNATT before leaving the EU.)
  5. Official animal health certificate (model of Regulation (EU) 2026/705), signed by an official veterinarian.

The clock for non-listed countries. Microchip → vaccine (from 12 weeks) → wait 30 days → RNATT → wait 90 days → entry. From scratch it is about 7 months, and the minimum age of the animal will be 15 weeks at least. The good news: with a favourable result, the RNATT is valid for life as long as the vaccine is kept up to date.

If you come from the EU/EEA or from a listed country (direct entry)

If your pet comes from an EU member state (or from Norway, Iceland, Liechtenstein, Switzerland), it travels with the European pet passport, without a blood test. If it comes from a listed country (United States, Canada, Mexico, Argentina, Chile, Japan, Australia, United Kingdom —except Northern Ireland—, the Emirates, Hong Kong, Taiwan, Singapore, Montenegro, Serbia and others): microchip, rabies vaccine (wait 21 days) and animal health certificate. Without a blood test and without the 3-month wait. (The ban on the four breeds applies the same, wherever you come from.)

Authority and procedure in Germany

Federal authority: BMLEH (formerly BMEL), with the local Veterinärämter and the Amtstierarzt (official veterinarian). The border control is carried out by the Zoll. To export a pet from Germany to a third country, the certificate is issued by the Amtstierarzt of the local Veterinäramt (the exact requirements depend on the destination country). The current European framework is the Delegated Regulation (EU) 2026/131 and the Implementing Regulations (EU) 2026/636 (country lists) and 2026/705 (certificate), all under Regulation (EU) 2016/429. The general EU regime for entering from outside is explained in our European Union pillar page.

Frequently asked questions

Does my country need the rabies blood test to enter Germany?
It depends on your origin, because Germany applies the EU regime. EU/EEA or listed country (USA, Canada, Mexico, Argentina, Chile, Japan, Australia, United Kingdom, the Emirates, and since June 2026 Montenegro and Serbia): no. Non-listed country (Peru, Brazil, Colombia, China, Russia…): yes, RNATT ≥0,5 UI/ml + 90 days. Look it up in the table.
Which breeds are banned in Germany?
Under the federal HundVerbrEinfG law it is banned to import (and to move from the EU) Pitbull-Terrier, American Staffordshire-Terrier, Staffordshire-Bullterrier and Bullterrier, and their crosses. The Zoll can seize the animal and there is criminal liability. In addition, each Bundesland has its list (4 states no longer have it). Verify your destination state.
Who controls entry and what is the authority?
Federal: BMLEH (formerly BMEL). Local: Veterinärämter with the Amtstierarzt. Border: the Zoll (customs). Entry from a third country must be made through an authorised Einreiseort.
Do I have to register my pet and pay tax?
Yes: registration in the municipality (Bürgeramt/Ordnungsamt) within 2-4 weeks + Hundesteuer (~€90-150 normal, several hundred for a Listenhund). The microchip can be registered for free in TASSO or FINDEFIX.
How long does it take from a non-listed country like Peru?
About 7 months from scratch: microchip → vaccine (from 12 weeks) → 30 days → RNATT → 90-day wait → entry. Minimum age 15 weeks. A favourable RNATT is valid for life if the vaccine is kept current.
