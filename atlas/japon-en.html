Requirements to enter Japan with your pet (2026): the destination of the 180-day clock

⚠ Standing rule · Japan · 11/07/2026

Japan does not accept certain microchips, nor RNA vaccines. Those beginning with “900 202” have been rejected since 2023, duplicates exist in seven other series, and the first vaccination must be given at 91 days — not 84. Read the full rule →

To enter Japan with your pet, Japan sorts every country into two groups. If you come from one of the 6 designated regions (Iceland, Australia, New Zealand, Fiji, Hawaii and Guam), entry is simplified: microchip, official certificate and inspection, with no antibody test and no 180 days —but the pet must have resided ≥180 days in that region—. If you come from any other country —Peru, Brazil, the continental United States, continental Europe and almost the whole world—, the path is the full protocol: microchip, two rabies vaccines, an antibody titration (RNATT ≥0.5 IU/ml) at a MAFF-designated laboratory and a 180-day wait from the blood draw, plus notice to the AQS at least 40 days in advance. The same protocol applies to dogs and cats. Failing on paperwork triggers up to 180 days of quarantine at your expense.

First step: find your country

Japan does not treat every country the same. Look for yours: the table tells you whether you get the simplified entry (designated region) or the full protocol with 180 days (non-designated country).

Two details that change everything: first, some territories are designated even when their country is not —Hawaii and Guam are designated regions, but the continental United States is not—. Second, coming from a designated region is not enough: the pet must have resided at least 180 days there (or since birth). Check your exact territory and the residence time.

If your country is FULL PROTOCOL (non-designated)

This applies to the vast majority of the world —Peru, Brazil, Colombia, Mexico, the continental United States, continental Europe, India and the rest—. It is the longest process of all the fact sheets. For your dog or cat to avoid quarantine, the path is:

  1. ISO microchip (11784/11785, 15 digits), implanted before the first rabies vaccine.
  2. Two rabies vaccines: the first from 91 days of age (vaccinating earlier doesn't count), the second at least 30 days after the first. RNA vaccines are not accepted —only inactivated or recombinant ones—.
  3. Rabies antibody titration (RNATT) with a result of ≥0.5 IU/ml at a MAFF-designated laboratory (the list is different from the EU or UK one; the micro-RFFIT is not accepted). The blood is drawn after the second vaccine. Result valid for 2 years from the draw.
  4. 180-day wait counted from the date the blood is drawn (not from the vaccine). Arrival must fall within the validity of the vaccine and the test.
  5. Notice to the AQS at least 40 days before arrival, via the NACCS system or by email. Notice with fewer than 40 days is, in principle, not accepted.
  6. Clinical inspection by a veterinarian within the 10 days before boarding (free of signs of rabies; in dogs, also of leptospirosis).
  7. Official health certificate (Form AC) from the country of origin, issued and endorsed by the official authority (in Peru, SENASA; in Colombia, ICA; in Bolivia, SENASAG; in Ecuador, AGROCALIDAD).
  8. Entry inspection by the AQS on arrival. If everything is in order, quarantine is about 12 hours; if not, up to 180 days of detention at your expense or return.

MAFF-designated laboratories (serology)

The blood sample must be processed at a laboratory on the current official MAFF list, which is different from the EU or UK one. In Peru and almost all of Latin America there is no designated laboratory: the sample leaves the country (typically to Kansas State University in the U.S.). The only exceptions in the region are Mexico (CENASA) and Brazil (TECSA). Recent notices that can invalidate a certificate: the Biobest laboratory (United Kingdom) was revoked on 25 June 2025 —certificates issued between November 2024 and June 2025 may not be valid—; the FLI in Greifswald (Germany) was cancelled in August 2023. Always confirm the current official list before sending the sample.

If your country is a DESIGNATED REGION (simplified entry)

If you come from one of the 6 designated regions —Iceland, Australia, New Zealand, Fiji, Hawaii and Guam—, the path is much shorter, but with one key condition:

  • Residence rule: the pet must have resided continuously for ≥180 days in the designated region (or since birth, or since it was imported directly from Japan). If it has resided less, quarantine applies for the remaining days.
  • Valid ISO microchip.
  • Clinical inspection within the 10 days before boarding.
  • Official certificate (Form AB) declaring that there have been no rabies cases in the country for at least 2 years.
  • Direct flight or ship to Japan. If there is transit through a non-designated country, the crate must travel sealed + an ANNEX transport certificate.

No antibody titration and no 180-day wait. Notice to the AQS 40 days in advance and entry through the authorized ports still apply.

Two hard rules that stop trips

⚠ The 180-day clock. The wait is counted from the date the blood is drawn for the RNATT, not from the vaccine. In practice, start the process about 7 months before the trip. If you already have a flight booked and haven't started, start today.
⚠ The mistake that resets everything. If the blood for the RNATT is drawn before completing the two vaccines with the correct interval, Japan rejects the result and the protocol goes back to starting from scratch —months are lost—.

Authorized ports of entry

The dog can only enter through one of these points. For cats through another port, you must contact the AQS beforehand.

Airports

And cats?

Unlike the United States, in Japan the protocol is the same for dogs and cats: microchip, two rabies vaccines and, if you come from a non-designated country, the antibody titration and the 180-day wait. Operational note: the quarantine facility for cats at Haneda airport is full; if your cat might require detention, confirm it with the AQS before choosing the port of entry.

After customs: municipal registration

After passing inspection, the dog must be registered in the municipality where it will live within 30 days of import, and be re-vaccinated against rabies once a year in accordance with Japan's Rabies Prevention Act. The ISO microchip can be registered nationally with the import quarantine certificate.

Frequently asked questions

Does my country qualify for the simplified entry or the full protocol to Japan?
Japan classifies the world into designated regions (simplified) and non-designated ones (full protocol). The 6 designated: Iceland, Australia, New Zealand, Fiji, Hawaii and Guam. The rest is non-designated. Look it up in the table.
How long does the process take?
From a non-designated country, at least 180 days from the RNATT blood draw, plus the lead time for the two vaccines: roughly 6 to 7 months. If you already have a flight booked and haven't started, start today.
When are the 180 days counted from?
From the date the blood is drawn for the RNATT, not from the vaccine. Arrival must be within the validity of the vaccine and the result (2 years from the draw).
Is there a Japan-approved laboratory in my country?
The MAFF list is different from the EU or UK one. In Peru and almost all of Latin America there isn't one; the regional exceptions are Mexico (CENASA) and Brazil (TECSA). The sample usually goes to Kansas State University (U.S.). Notice: Biobest (United Kingdom) was revoked in June 2025.
Is the protocol the same for cats?
Yes, the same as for dogs: microchip, two vaccines and, if you come from a non-designated country, the antibody titration and the 180-day wait.
I'm coming from Hawaii or Guam, is entry easy?
Only if the pet resided continuously for ≥180 days in the designated region (or since birth, or since its direct import from Japan). If it resided less, quarantine for the remaining days.
