Enter Andorra with your pet (2026): the rule changes depending on your country — and Andorra has no airport

To enter Andorra with your dog, cat or ferret there are two layers. First: Andorra has no international airport nor its own point of entry, so your pet physically enters through Spain or France and there the European Union regime applies — depending on your country of origin, entry is direct (microchip, rabies vaccine and certificate) or also requires a rabies blood test (RNATT) ≥0,5 UI/ml and a 90-day wait. Second: Andorra has EU-equivalent status (the border-to-Andorra stretch is intra-Community) and adds its national layer: 15 potentially dangerous breeds (PPP) that require two mandatory courses, a licence, insurance and a muzzle (Decret 285/2021), and registration of the microchip in the RAC. What no guide tells you: Andorra has no airport

This is the detail that confuses most and that almost nobody explains. Andorra has no international airport with a veterinary control post, nor does it appear on the MAPA's list of Travellers' Points of Entry (PEV). That is why a pet arriving from a third country (such as Peru) does not "land in Andorra": it first enters through a PEV in Spain or France, where the European Union veterinary check is carried out, and from there it travels by road to the Principality.

  • Via Spain: flight to Barcelona-El Prat (BCN), check at the PEV, and about 3 hours by road to Andorra via La Seu d'Urgell and the Riu Runer / Farga de Moles border.
  • Via France: flight to Toulouse-Blagnac (TLS), check at the French PEV, and a road journey to the Pas de la Casa border.

As Andorra has EU-equivalent animal health status, that last Spain/France → Andorra stretch is considered intra-Community movement: if your pet has already passed the PEV check with the documentation in order, there is no second import check at the Andorran border. The key is to comply with the EU regime at the Spanish or French point of entry.

First step: locate your country of origin

Since the pet enters through Spain or France, what decides your path is your country of origin under the EU regime. Find yours: the table tells you whether you get direct entry (EU/EEA or listed country) or the protocol with blood test and 3-month wait (non-listed country, like Peru).

A verified fact that surprises: being "rich" or nearby does not guarantee being listed. Russia and Belarus are no longer on the lists (they need a blood test); and neither are South Korea or Uruguay. By contrast, Mexico, Argentina, Chile and —since June 2026— Montenegro and Serbia are. Check yours, don't assume it.

Andorra's national layer: potentially dangerous dogs (PPP)

The European entry regime is only half the story. Andorra has its own rules on dangerous and potentially dangerous dogs, and they are among the most demanding in Europe.

⚠️ 15 breeds and two mandatory courses (Decret 285/2021)

The Decret 285/2021, of 1 September 2021, distinguishes the gos perillós (by behaviour) from the gos potencialment perillós (by breed). The 15 potentially dangerous breeds (and their crosses) are:

American Akita · Akita Inu · American Bulldog · Bullmastiff · Staffordshire Bull Terrier · Dobermann · Dogo Argentino · Dogue de Bordeaux · Fila Brasileiro · Presa Canario · Neapolitan Mastiff · American Pit Bull Terrier · Rottweiler · American Staffordshire Terrier · Tosa Inu.

What makes Andorra unique: every new owner must pass TWO mandatory training courses before owning the dog — one at an authorised training centre (on the animal's behaviour and the assessment of the owner's environment) and another on the regulations in force, given by the Cos de Banders, with an evaluation. In addition: a licence, civil liability insurance, in public a muzzle and a non-extendable leash of a maximum of 1.5 m, a ban on minors handling it or on walking more than one PPP at a time, mandatory sterilisation of puppies of these breeds before one year of age, and a ban on breeding and crossing.

Andorra's status: equivalent to the EU

Andorra is not an EU member state or part of the EEA, but for pet movement it has an equivalent animal health status, recognised by Decision 1/2005 of the EC-Andorra Joint Committee, which required the Principality to transpose the Community regime on the movement of companion animals (Reglament del 20-2-2019). That is why Andorra appears alongside Switzerland, San Marino, Monaco, Norway or the Faroe Islands as an equivalent territory, and movement between Andorra and Spain or France is treated as intra-Community.

Honest update note: in April 2026 the EU launched a new package of regulations (2026/131, 2026/636 and 2026/705). Andorra remains recognised as an equivalent territory under that framework, but as of the date of this page we have not located in the BOPA the formal Andorran transposition of the 2026 package (the last recorded amendment is Decret 219/2022). In practice this does not change the traveller's path: the requirements that are really checked are those of the Spanish or French PEV through which the pet enters. Always confirm with the Departament d'Agricultura before starting the procedure.

If your country is NOT listed (protocol with blood test)

This applies to Peru, Brazil, Colombia, China, Russia, Belarus and almost all of Africa and Asia. For your dog or cat to be able to enter (through the Spanish or French PEV and from there to Andorra), the path is:

  1. ISO microchip (11784/11785, 15 digits), implanted before the rabies vaccine. If the order is reversed, the vaccine does not count.
  2. Rabies vaccine, with the animal at least 12 weeks old; wait 21 days after the primary vaccination.
  3. Rabies blood test (RNATT). The sample is taken at least 30 days after the vaccine, at an EU-designated laboratory; result ≥0,5 UI/ml.
  4. Wait 90 days (3 months) counted from the sampling before entering.
  5. Official animal health certificate (model of Regulation (EU) 2026/705), signed by an official veterinarian from the country of origin and presented at least in Spanish (or French, depending on the PEV of entry).

The clock for non-listed countries. Microchip → vaccine (from 12 weeks) → wait 30 days → RNATT → wait 90 days → entry through the Spanish/French PEV → road to Andorra. From scratch it is about 7 months, and the minimum age of the animal will be 7 months. The good news: with a favourable result, the RNATT is valid for life as long as the vaccine is kept up to date.

If you come from the EU/EEA or a listed country (direct entry)

If your pet comes from an EU member state (or from Norway, Iceland, Liechtenstein, Switzerland), it travels with the European pet passport, without a blood test, and crosses the Spain/France-Andorra border directly. If it comes from a listed country (United States, Canada, Mexico, Argentina, Chile, Japan, Australia, United Kingdom, the Emirates, Hong Kong, Singapore, Montenegro, Serbia and others): microchip, rabies vaccine (wait 21 days) and animal health certificate. Without a blood test and without the 3-month wait.

Registering your pet in Andorra: the RAC

In Andorra, the microchip must be recorded in the Registre d'Animals de Companyia (RAC), managed by the Departament d'Agricultura i Ramaderia. If you settle in the Principality, register your pet (non-native animals are usually registered within a maximum of 4 months from entry); your veterinarian in Andorra usually handles it. There is a registration fee (Llei 14/2012 de salut animal i seguretat alimentària); check the amount in force with the Departament, because we do not publish a figure we cannot officially confirm for 2026.

Authority and procedure in Andorra

Competent authority: Departament d'Agricultura i Ramaderia, of the Ministeri de Medi Ambient, Agricultura i Ramaderia of the Govern d'Andorra (veterinary service). Oversight of potentially dangerous dogs falls to the Cos de Banders. Since the actual entry takes place through Spain or France, the European framework checked at the border is Regulation (EU) 2016/429 with Regulations (EU) 2026/131 (non-commercial movement), 2026/636 (country lists) and 2026/705 (certificate). The general EU regime is explained in our pillar page on the European Union, and entry through Spain in the Spain page.

Frequently asked questions

Does Andorra have an airport to arrive with my pet?
No. Andorra has no international airport nor its own PEV. The pet enters through a PEV in Spain (e.g. Barcelona-El Prat) or France (Toulouse) and then arrives by road. The border-to-Andorra stretch is intra-Community.
Does my country need the rabies blood test?
It depends on your origin, because you enter through Spain/France and the EU regime applies. EU/EEA or a listed country (USA, Canada, Mexico, Argentina, Chile, Japan, Australia, United Kingdom, the Emirates, and since June 2026 Montenegro and Serbia): no. Non-listed country (Peru, Brazil, Colombia, China, Russia…): yes, RNATT ≥0,5 UI/ml + 90 days. Look it up in the table.
Can I take a PPP dog to Andorra?
Yes, but Decret 285/2021 applies: 15 breeds (American Akita, Akita Inu, American Bulldog, Bullmastiff, Staffordshire Bull Terrier, Dobermann, Dogo Argentino, Dogue de Bordeaux, Fila Brasileiro, Presa Canario, Neapolitan Mastiff, American Pit Bull Terrier, Rottweiler, American Staffordshire Terrier, Tosa Inu). It requires two mandatory courses (one authorised training + one on regulations with the Cos de Banders), a licence, insurance, muzzle and leash ≤1.5 m; sterilisation of puppies and breeding prohibited.
Is Andorra part of the EU?
No, but it has equivalent animal health status (Decision 1/2005 of the EC-Andorra Joint Committee); movement from Spain/France is intra-Community, without quarantine if the requirements are met.
How long does it take from a non-listed country like Peru?
About 7 months from scratch: microchip → vaccine (from 12 weeks) → 30 days → RNATT → 90-day wait → entry through the Spanish/French PEV → road to Andorra. The favourable RNATT is valid for life if the vaccine is kept current.
