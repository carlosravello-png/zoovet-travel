Requirements to enter Russia with your pet (2026): the destination that does NOT require a blood test

To enter Russia with your dog or cat, the authority is Rosselkhoznadzor, under EAEU Decision No. 317. And here is what makes it different from almost the entire world: Russia does NOT require a rabies blood test (RNATT) from any country of origin —neither the European Union's 90-day wait, nor the 180 days of Japan or Australia—. The process is uniform: the same procedure for a pet from Peru as for one from the United States. You need: identification, a valid rabies vaccine (valid 1 year for Russia even if the leaflet says 3, and with the certificate issued at least 20 days after vaccinating), the EAEU's additional vaccines (dogs: distemper, hepatitis, parvovirus, adenovirus, leptospirosis; cats: panleukopenia), a clinical examination within the previous 14 days, and the corresponding document: with an owner and up to 2 animals, the international veterinary passport is enough; in other cases, Form No. 15. No quarantine if everything is in order. EAEU countries (Armenia, Belarus, Kazakhstan, Kyrgyzstan) have an even simpler procedure.

First step: find your country

Russia is the most egalitarian destination in the Atlas: there are no "good" and "bad" countries, no rabies list, no blood test. Look for yours in the table: unless you come from an EAEU country (simplified procedure), you get the standard process —the same for everyone—.

The most surprising contrast: while the EU asks you for a blood test and 3 months of waiting if you come from an unlisted country, Russia does not ask you for a blood test no matter where your pet comes from. What it does scrutinize closely: that the vaccine is valid according to ITS criterion (1 year, not 3) and that you carry all the vaccines, not just rabies.

The standard process (for almost the whole world)

If you do not come from an EAEU country, this is the path —the same for Peru, Brazil, Mexico, the United States, the EU or Australia—:

  1. Identification. The animal must be identified (microchip or tattoo). Russia does not require an ISO microchip as strictly as the EU, but the international veterinary passport normally requires it; use an ISO microchip to avoid problems.
  2. Valid rabies vaccine. Valid 1 year for Russia even if the manufacturer indicates 3. The certificate cannot be issued before 20 days after vaccination (counted up to the day of landing).
  3. Additional vaccines. Dogs: distemper, hepatitis, parvovirus, adenovirus and leptospirosis. Cats: panleukopenia.
  4. Clinical examination by the competent authority, with the mark in the passport/certificate, within the 14 days before the trip.
  5. Document: with an owner and up to 2 animals, the international veterinary passport (serves as the certificate). Without an owner, more than 2 animals, or an unvaccinated puppy/kitten under 3 months: Form No. 15 issued by the veterinary authority of the country of origin.

If you come from an EAEU country (simplified)

From Armenia, Belarus, Kazakhstan and Kyrgyzstan, the pet moves throughout the entire territory of the Eurasian Economic Union with the EAEU veterinary passport and the clinical inspection mark, without an additional certificate and without a permit, for personal use and up to 2 animals.

Quarantine, entry and number of animals

No quarantine if the documentation is in order and there are up to 2 animals with their owner. Entry must be made through a point with veterinary control; in Moscow these are Sheremetyevo, Domodedovo and Vnukovo. Up to 2 animals travel without a Rosselkhoznadzor permit; from the third onward, or if the pet travels without its owner, a Rosselkhoznadzor permit is needed (for non-commercial use, an annual permit for up to 5 animals may be issued).

⚠ The three mistakes that leave your pet at the airport: (1) arriving with a "3-year" vaccine that for Russia has already expired (only 12 months count); (2) issuing the certificate before 20 days post-vaccine; (3) carrying only rabies without the additional vaccines (distemper/parvo… or panleukopenia).

What about cats?

Same system as dogs: identification, rabies vaccine (1 year, 20-day window), 14-day clinical examination and the document depending on the case. The difference is the additional vaccine: in cats, panleukopenia (in dogs, the distemper/hepatitis/parvovirus/adenovirus/leptospirosis group). No blood test is required either.

Frequently asked questions

Does my country need the rabies blood test for Russia?
No. Russia does not require RNATT from any country of origin, unlike the EU, Japan or Australia. The same process applies whether your pet comes from Peru, Brazil, the US or Australia. Only EAEU countries have an even simpler procedure.
Passport or Form No. 15?
With an owner and up to 2 animals: international veterinary passport with the 14-day clinical inspection. Without an owner, more than 2 animals, or an unvaccinated puppy under 3 months: Form No. 15 from the authority of the country of origin.
Is the 3-year vaccine valid for Russia?
No. Rosselkhoznadzor applies a 1-year validity even if the leaflet says 3. And the certificate cannot be issued before 20 days after the vaccine.
Do I only need the rabies vaccine?
No. Dogs: also distemper, hepatitis, parvovirus, adenovirus and leptospirosis. Cats: panleukopenia. A certificate with only rabies may be rejected.
Is there quarantine?
No, if the papers are in order and there are up to 2 animals with their owner. Enter through a point with veterinary control (Moscow: Sheremetyevo, Domodedovo, Vnukovo).
