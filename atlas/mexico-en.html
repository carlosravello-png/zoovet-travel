Requirements to enter Mexico with your pet (2026): the rule changes depending on where you're coming from

Mexico is one of the easiest destinations in the world to enter with your dog or cat, and the authority is SENASICA (with inspection by the OISA at airports, ports and borders). The big difference is where your pet is coming from: if it comes from the United States or Canada, on arrival there is only a physical inspection —you don't need a health certificate or a vaccination record—. If it comes from any other country, you need a Good Health Certificate valid for a maximum of 15 days that includes the rabies vaccine (animals under 3 months are exempt), confirms the animal is healthy and dewormed (internally and externally, within the previous 6 months). In no case does Mexico require a rabies blood test, and there is no quarantine. The import certificate (CZI) is issued by SENASICA on your arrival, free for up to 3 pets.

First step: locate your country

In Mexico, what defines your procedure is where you're coming from. Find your country: if you come from the USA or Canada, it's inspection only; from the rest of the world, the 15-day Good Health Certificate.

The contrast that surprises people: while Australia requires quarantine in Mickleham and the EU asks for a blood test and a 3-month wait, Mexico asks no one for a blood test and has no quarantine. From the USA or Canada, you don't even need paperwork: the inspection is enough.

Country of departureEntry to Mexico

If you come from the United States or Canada (inspection only)

If your pet originates in Mexico, the USA or Canada and comes from the USA or Canada, on arrival it undergoes a physical inspection to verify there are no health risks. You don't need to present a Good Health Certificate or a vaccination record. (The same procedure applies if you enter through the border zone or on a cruise and only stay for the duration of the trip.) Even so, keeping the rabies vaccine up to date is recommended.

If you come from any other country (with certificate)

From the rest of the world, at your point of entry the SENASICA staff carries out a documentary and physical review. You must present a Good Health Certificate (original and a plain copy) valid for a maximum of 15 days from its issuance, meeting the following:

  1. Issued by an official veterinarian or, if private, on letterhead with a professional license number (or photocopy).
  2. Name and address of the exporter (country of origin) and of the importer (destination in Mexico).
  3. Date and validity of the rabies vaccine (animals under 3 months are exempt).
  4. That the animal was found clinically healthy in the inspection prior to travel.
  5. That it was dewormed internally and externally within the previous 6 months and is free of ectoparasites.

If you don't have the certificate, a private veterinarian in Mexico must apply the treatments and issue it, at your cost.

The inspection, the carrier and quarantine

There is no quarantine if everything is in order. The carrier must arrive clean and receives a preventive spraying by official staff. The entry of beds, diapers, newspaper, sawdust, rags, toys or treats made with ingredients of ruminant origin is not allowed: they are removed and destroyed. Your pet may enter with its collar and leash.

If the inspection detects parasites: if there are ectoparasites —and especially ticks— you must contact a veterinarian for treatment (and the removal of all specimens) before release, with the costs borne by you. If the pet arrives without documents, it is held at the OISA until a veterinarian reviews, vaccinates and deworms it.

Number of pets and cost

The procedure is free for travelers' pets. If you travel with more than three animals, a cost for the Import Animal Health Certificate applies. And if you enter Mexico more than three times a year, register for free in the Frequent Traveler Pet Program (PMVF) so you don't have to repeat the full procedure on each entry.

What about cats?

Same system as dogs: from the USA/Canada, inspection only; from the rest, the 15-day Good Health Certificate with rabies vaccine, deworming and a clinically healthy status. No blood test is required either. Animals under 3 months are exempt from the rabies vaccine.

Official contact (SENASICA / OISA)

Authority: SENASICA (Servicio Nacional de Sanidad, Inocuidad y Calidad Agroalimentaria), a body of SADER. Portal: gob.mx/senasica. The procedure is carried out by the network of OISA (Agricultural Health Inspection Offices) at all points of entry; the main one is Mexico City International Airport (AICM). The phone numbers and addresses of each office are in the official OISA directory. The CZI application form and the help to fill it in (Spanish and English) can be downloaded from the official page. For frequent travelers, the PMVF registration is done online.

Frequently asked questions

Does my country need a health certificate for Mexico?
If it comes from the USA or Canada, no: just a physical inspection. From any other country, yes: a Good Health Certificate valid for a maximum of 15 days. Mexico asks no one for a rabies blood test.
What must the Good Health Certificate include?
A licensed veterinarian, details of the exporter and importer, the date and validity of the rabies vaccine (animals under 3 months exempt), the animal clinically healthy and dewormed internally/externally within the previous 6 months, free of ectoparasites. Validity: 15 days.
Do I need a permit in advance?
No. The CZI is issued by SENASICA (OISA) on your arrival, after the review. There is no prior procedure from the country of origin for personal use.
How many pets and how much does it cost?
Free for travelers' pets; with more than 3 animals, the CZI has a cost. If you enter Mexico more than 3 times a year, register for free in the Frequent Traveler Pet Program.
Is there quarantine?
No, if the paperwork is in order and the inspection is normal. The carrier must arrive clean (it receives a spraying); without documents or with ectoparasites, there is treatment at your cost before release.
