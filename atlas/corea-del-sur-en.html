Requirements to enter South Korea with your pet (2026): the rule changes depending on your country

To enter South Korea with your dog or cat, APQA (the Korean quarantine agency) classifies each country according to its rabies situation. If your country is on the official list of rabies-free zones —Australia, New Zealand, Japan, the United Kingdom, Ireland, Germany, Italy, Austria, Belgium, Portugal, the UAE, Hong Kong, Singapore, Hawaii, Guam and others—, entry is direct: microchip and certificate, no blood test. If your country is not on the list —United States, Canada, France, Spain, the Netherlands, China, India, Russia and almost all of Latin America, Africa and Asia—, you need a rabies blood test (RNATT) ≥0.5 IU/ml, with the sample taken within the 24 months before arrival. The best part: Korea requires no wait after the result (unlike Japan or the EU) and has no routine quarantine —the pet is released the same day if everything is in order. The ISO microchip is mandatory and the minimum age for the rabies rule is 90 days.

Editorial note. This was, by far, the Atlas entry that cost us the most work —and the one we are proudest of—. Korea does not publish its list of rabies-free countries on a simple web page: it keeps it in an official annex in Korean that APQA adjusts every quarter, and its English-language rules are not enough to classify country by country. Almost all travel guides settle the matter with a "check the APQA page" or with four scattered examples; several even get it wrong, listing France or Spain as free when in fact they do need a blood test. We did what we could not find anywhere else: we downloaded and translated the official Korean document (the 3 April 2026 version) and classified the more than 200 countries one by one, cross-checking them against the APQA website. As far as we could verify, no other guide publishes this complete, translated and cross-checked list. If any country changes status after an outbreak reported to WOAH, write to us and we will update it: this list keeps moving.

First step: locate your country

Korea does not treat every country the same. Find yours: the table tells you whether you get direct entry (rabies-free country) or the blood-test protocol (non-rabies-free country).

A verified fact that surprises: the rabies-free list is not "all of Europe". Germany, Italy, Austria, Belgium and Portugal are on it; but France, Spain, the Netherlands, Poland and Denmark are NOT —and they need a blood test. Nor are the United States, Canada or Norway. Always check yours, do not assume it.

If your country is NOT on the list (blood-test protocol)

This applies to the United States, Canada, France, Spain, China, India, Russia, almost all of Latin America, Africa and Asia. For your dog or cat to enter without setbacks, the path is:

  1. ISO microchip (11784/11785), implanted before the rabies vaccine. If the chip is not ISO, you must bring a compatible reader.
  2. Valid rabies vaccine.
  3. Rabies blood test (RNATT). The sample is processed in an approved laboratory (by the EU, Taiwan or another recognised one, or the competent authority of the exporting country) and the result must be ≥0.5 IU/ml.
  4. No waiting period. The test is valid if the sample was taken within 24 months before arrival. There is no need to wait months after the result.
  5. Official health certificate from the government of the exporting country, with the microchip number and the test result. Without it, the pet is returned.

Why Korea is fast: among the destinations that require a blood test, Korea is one of the quickest because it does not impose Japan's 180 days or the EU's 3 months. If the sample is already within the 24-month window, you can travel as soon as you have the result and the certificate.

If your country is on the list (direct entry)

If you come from a rabies-free country according to the APQA list —Australia, New Zealand, Japan, the United Kingdom, Ireland, Germany, Italy, Austria, Belgium, Portugal, Greece, Switzerland, the UAE, Hong Kong, Singapore, Hawaii, Guam and others—, the path is short: ISO microchip and official health certificate with the microchip number and date of birth. No blood test. If the documentation is in order, the pet is released the same day.

Quarantine: usually, none

Korea does not apply routine quarantine. An APQA veterinarian reviews the documents and performs a clinical examination at the entry point; if everything is fine, your dog or cat is released on the day of arrival.

⚠ When there IS quarantine. If the microchip is missing or does not match, if the blood test was not done or the titre is below 0.5 IU/ml, the animal is placed in extension quarantine (average 10 days, possibly more) until it is corrected, all at the owner's expense. And without an official health certificate, the animal is returned to the country of origin, also at your expense.

Special cases: Australia and Malaysia

Hendra and Nipah viruses. Cats from Australia and dogs and cats from Malaysia need an additional certificate confirming the absence of the Hendra and Nipah viruses (free-zone status, or a negative test within the 14 days before export and 60 days in a free zone). Without that certificate, quarantine is extended by 21 days.

And cats?

The system applies equally to dogs and cats: ISO microchip, the blood test based on the country of origin and same-day release. The minimum age for the rabies rule is 90 days: those younger than 90 days do not require a blood test, but they do require a microchip. APQA does not require vaccines beyond rabies for entry (your country of origin may indeed require a complete vaccination schedule to issue the certificate).

Laboratories, ports and contact (APQA)

The blood test must be processed in an approved laboratory: APQA recognises the lists of the European Union and Taiwan, in addition to the competent authorities. In Korea, the tests are carried out by four official entities: Choong Ang Vaccine Laboratory, Komipharm, KBNP and the Seoul Regional Office of APQA.

Authorised entry points: Incheon airports (passenger and cargo terminals), Gimhae, Gimpo, Muan, Cheongju, Daegu and Jeju, and seaports (Incheon, Busan, Gunsan, Pyeongtaek, Gwangyang, Sokcho). Authority: Animal and Plant Quarantine Agency (APQA), domain qia.go.kr; enquiries at Incheon airport: +82-32-740-2660. Anyone bringing 10 or more animals must submit prior notification to APQA.

Frequently asked questions

Does my country need the rabies blood test for South Korea?
It depends on whether it is on APQA's official list of rabies-free zones. If it is (Australia, Japan, the United Kingdom, Germany, Italy, the UAE, Hong Kong, Singapore, Hawaii…), it is not needed. If it is not (USA, Canada, France, Spain, China, India, almost all of Latin America), yes. Look it up in the table.
How long do you wait after the blood test?
Nothing. Korea requires no waiting period after the result, unlike Japan (180 days) or the EU (3 months). The sample only needs to have been taken within the 24 months before arrival, with a titre ≥0.5 IU/ml.
Is there quarantine on arrival?
Not routinely: if the documentation is complete and the clinical examination is normal, the pet is released the same day. If the microchip or the test is missing, there is extension quarantine (average 10 days) at your expense. Without a certificate, the animal is returned.
Cats from Australia or pets from Malaysia?
They need a certificate of absence of Hendra and Nipah (free zone or a negative test within 14 days before + 60 days in a free zone). Without it, +21 days of quarantine.
Are the rules the same for dogs and cats?
Yes. Microchip, blood test based on origin and same-day release apply equally. The minimum age for the rabies rule is 90 days; younger ones only need a microchip.
