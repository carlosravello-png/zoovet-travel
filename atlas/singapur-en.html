Bringing your pet to Singapore (2026): which Schedule is your country in, and how much quarantine?

📅 Updated 2 July 2026. Singapore places each country of origin in Schedule I, II or III by rabies risk. The AVS veterinary conditions are dated 1 April 2026 and the official guide was updated 18 June 2026. Verified live against AVS/NParks on 2 July 2026.

To import your dog or cat into Singapore, the first thing is knowing which Schedule your country is in. The Animal & Veterinary Service (AVS), part of the National Parks Board (NParks), ranks the origin in three tiers: Schedule I (only Australia, New Zealand, Ireland and the United Kingdom: no quarantine and no rabies test), Schedule II (32 countries/regions such as the USA, Canada, Japan, Spain, Germany, France and Italy: a rabies antibody blood test (RNATT) and, depending on residence and travel, possible 10-day quarantine) and Schedule III (everyone else, including Peru and almost all of Latin America: RNATT + mandatory 30-day quarantine at the Animal Quarantine Centre and a rabies vaccination on arrival). There are also banned breeds and, since 1 April 2026, you must use an AVS-recognised agent. Find your country in the table.

First step: which Schedule is your country of origin in?

Singapore does not split origins into approved / not approved: it ranks the country of origin into three Schedules by rabies risk, and that decides how much quarantine you face. The table shows each country's Schedule.

Key fact: only Australia, New Zealand, Ireland and the United Kingdom are in Schedule I (no quarantine). Almost all of the EU, the USA, Canada and Japan are Schedule II. All of Latin America — Peru, Brazil, Mexico, Colombia and also Argentina, Chile and Uruguay — is Schedule III: mandatory 30-day quarantine. Don't assume it: look it up.

How the AVS Schedules work

  • Schedule I — Australia, New Zealand, Ireland and the United Kingdom: the simplest. No rabies vaccine or blood test (RNATT) and no quarantine if all conditions are met (ISO microchip, core vaccines, veterinary certificate, import licence).
  • Schedule II — 32 countries/regions (Austria, Belgium, Bermuda, Canada, Cayman Islands, Cyprus, Czech Republic, Denmark, Estonia, Finland, France, Germany, Greece, Hong Kong, Iceland, Italy, Japan, Jersey, Latvia, Liechtenstein, Luxembourg, Malta, New Caledonia, Norway, Portugal, Slovakia, Slovenia, Spain —except Ceuta and Melilla—, Sweden, Switzerland, the Netherlands and the USA): mandatory RNATT and, if the pet arrives within 5 days of the owner and has lived with them for over 6 months, no quarantine; otherwise 10 days (home quarantine with a personal licence or at the AQC with a commercial licence).
  • Schedule III — every other country (including Peru and almost all of Latin America, Africa and much of Asia): mandatory RNATT + minimum 30-day quarantine at the Animal Quarantine Centre (AQC) and a rabies vaccination on arrival.

From Schedule III (Peru and most of Latin America): the longest route

If your country is Schedule III — the case for Peru and almost all of Latin America, including Argentina, Chile and Uruguay — your pet can enter, but by the most demanding route: microchip, rabies vaccine, a rabies antibody blood test (RNATT) with the sample taken at least 28 days after a valid vaccination and between 90 days and 12 months before travel, parasite treatments, an import licence and, on arrival, a mandatory minimum 30-day quarantine at the AQC (booked in advance) plus a rabies vaccination on entry. It is a process planned many months ahead.

What changed in 2026

The AVS veterinary conditions in force are dated 1 April 2026 and the official guide was updated 18 June 2026. The most important operational change: since 1 April 2026, the owner must appoint an AVS-recognised pet agent to handle import clearance at the Changi Animal & Plant Quarantine Station (self-clearance is no longer allowed). Always confirm the current version before starting.

🚫 Banned and restricted breeds

Under the Animals and Birds (Licensing and Control of Cats and Dogs) Rules 2024 (First Schedule, Part 1), Singapore bans the entry of Pit Bull (including American Pit Bull Terrier, American Staffordshire Terrier, Staffordshire Bull Terrier and American Bulldog), Akita, Neapolitan Mastiff, Tosa, Dogo Argentino, Fila Brasileiro, Boerboel and Perro de Presa Canario, plus their crosses. Part 2 breeds (Bull Terrier, Doberman, Rottweiler, German Shepherd and related, Mastiffs) are allowed as specified dogs with conditions (muzzle, insurance, banker's guarantee, sterilisation). For cats, Bengal or Savannah crosses must be fifth generation or later.

Authority and points of entry

Authority: Animal & Veterinary Service (AVS), part of the National Parks Board (NParks). Air arrivals are inspected at the Changi Animal & Plant Quarantine Station (CAPQ) in the Changi Airfreight Centre; land arrivals at Tuas Checkpoint. Quarantine, when it applies, is served at the Animal Quarantine Centre (AQC), booked in advance via the Quarantine Management System. The Animal Quarantine Centre (AQC) is at 2 Jalan Lekar, Singapore 698919 (near Choa Chu Kang). Minimum age to import: 12 weeks.

Frequently asked questions

Can I bring my pet straight from Peru?
Yes — Peru is Schedule III: the pet can enter directly by completing the full protocol (microchip, vaccine, RNATT, treatments, licence) and a mandatory minimum 30-day quarantine at the AQC plus a rabies vaccination on arrival. Almost all of Latin America — including Argentina, Chile and Uruguay — is also Schedule III.
What are Schedules I, II and III?
The AVS system by rabies risk of the origin. Schedule I (Australia, New Zealand, Ireland, UK): no RNATT or quarantine. Schedule II (USA, Canada, Japan, listed EU…): RNATT and possible 10-day quarantine. Schedule III (the rest, incl. Peru): RNATT + 30-day AQC quarantine.
How much quarantine is there?
Schedule I: none if conditions are met. Schedule II: none if the pet arrives within 5 days of the owner and lived with them over 6 months; otherwise 10 days. Schedule III: minimum 30 days at the Animal Quarantine Centre (AQC).
Which breeds are banned?
Pit Bull (and related types), Akita, Neapolitan Mastiff, Tosa, Dogo Argentino, Fila Brasileiro, Boerboel and Perro de Presa Canario, plus crosses (Rules 2024, First Schedule Part 1). Others are allowed as specified dogs with conditions. Bengal/Savannah: fifth generation onwards only.
Where does the pet enter and what changed in 2026?
Inspection at Changi (CAPQ) by air or Tuas by land; quarantine at the AQC. Since 1 April 2026 you must appoint an AVS-recognised pet agent for clearance (self-clearance is no longer allowed).
