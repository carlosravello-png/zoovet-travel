Taking your pet to New Zealand (2026): does your country enter directly? And what changed on 1 July

⚠ Regulatory alert · 11 July 2026

New Zealand's new standard came into force on 1 July 2026. It requires an official identity check six months before export. Anyone travelling from 1 April 2027 must start by 1 October 2026. Read the full alert →

📅 Updated on 2 July 2026. New Zealand brought its new Import Health Standard "Cats and Dogs 2026" into force on 1 July 2026, replacing the 2021 one, with a 9-month transition period (until April 2027). Verified live against the MPI on 2 July 2026.

To import your dog or cat into New Zealand, the first thing is not what you need — it is whether your country is approved. The Ministry for Primary Industries (MPI) only allows imports from approved countries: Australia (Category 1, the simplest) and Categories 2 and 3, which enter directly with an import permit, a rabies blood test (RNATT) in Category 3 and a minimum 10-day quarantine. If your country is not on the list —and that is where Peru, Brazil, Colombia, Mexico and almost all of Latin America, Africa and Asia fall— you cannot import directly: you have to transit, that is, move the pet to an approved country, have it reside there for 6 months and export it from there. In addition, entry is only through Auckland or Christchurch and there are banned breeds. Look for your country in the table.

First step: is your country of origin approved?

New Zealand is one of the strictest destinations in the world. The table tells you whether your country enters directly (approved by the MPI) or whether you cannot import directly and must transit through an approved country.

A verified fact that surprises: from Peru, Brazil, Colombia, Mexico, Ecuador, Bolivia, Venezuela and Paraguay you CANNOT import directly — they are not approved. In Latin America only Argentina, Chile and Uruguay are. And there are even European countries off the list (for example Romania, Andorra, Liechtenstein, San Marino). Don't assume it: look it up.

The rule almost nobody tells you: from Peru you cannot import directly

🚫 Peru (and almost all of Latin America) is a "non-approved country"

The MPI only authorises imports from approved countries (Categories 1, 2 and 3). Peru is not in any of them — nor are Brazil, Colombia, Mexico, Ecuador, Bolivia, Venezuela or Paraguay. That means you cannot fly your pet directly from Peru to New Zealand.

The only legal route is to transit: take the pet to an approved country (for example the United States, Spain, Chile or another Category 3 country), where it must reside for at least 6 months immediately before shipment, complete the whole protocol there (vaccine, RNATT, treatments, permit) and only then travel to New Zealand. It is the toughest regime of all destinations — tougher than Australia. In Latin America, the only ones that enter directly are Argentina, Chile and Uruguay.

What changed on 1 July 2026

On 1 July 2026 the MPI's new Import Health Standard "Cats and Dogs 2026" came into force, replacing the 2021 IHS. There is a 9-month transition period (until April 2027) during which compliance with either of the two standards is accepted. Among the adjustments in the new IHS is a verification of the identification (microchip) at least 6 months before export. Since any process starting today may ship under one or the other standard depending on the date, it is advisable to confirm with the MPI which IHS applies to your case before starting.

How the MPI categories work

  • Category 1 — Australia (and Norfolk Island): the simplest. No import permit and no post-arrival quarantine (inspection only) for animals that comply.
  • Category 2 — rabies-free countries (e.g. Japan, Singapore, Hawaii, Iceland, Fiji and several Pacific islands): import permit + minimum 10-day quarantine. They do not require RNATT.
  • Category 3 — countries with rabies absent or well controlled (the whole EU, United Kingdom, USA, Canada, Argentina, Chile, Uruguay, South Africa, South Korea, the Emirates, etc.): permit + RNATT ≥0.5 IU/ml + minimum 10-day quarantine.
  • Non-approved (Peru and most of the world): cannot import directly → transit.

🚫 Breeds banned from import

Under the Dog Control Act 1996, New Zealand bans the importation of the American Pit Bull Terrier (any type), Fila Brasileiro, Dogo Argentino, Tosa Inu (Japanese Tosa) and Perro de Presa Canario, as well as their hybrids. The ban applies at the border, whatever country you come from.

Protocol from an approved country (Category 3)

If your pet departs from an approved Category 3 country (or you have already transited and it has resided there for its 6 months), the path is:

  1. ISO microchip implanted before the rabies vaccine and the sampling; it is recorded on all certificates. (Under the 2026 IHS, verification of the identity ≥6 months before shipment.)
  2. Current rabies vaccine.
  3. Rabies blood test (RNATT) ≥0.5 IU/ml at an approved laboratory; the sample is taken at least 3 weeks after the vaccine and its date must be at least 3 months (90 days) and no more than 24 months before entry.
  4. Internal and external parasite treatments within the required windows and a pre-shipment veterinary examination.
  5. MPI import permit (all except Australia): apply for it in advance (min. ~20 working days; 6 weeks recommended).
  6. Entry through Auckland or Christchurch and minimum 10-day quarantine at an MPI-approved facility.

Minimum age: the IHS certificate requires the animal to be more than 8 weeks old at shipment. Quarantine may be extended if there are non-compliances, parasites or health problems at the time of release.

Authority and points of entry

Authority: Ministry for Primary Industries (MPI) — Biosecurity New Zealand, under the Biosecurity Act 1993, via the Import Health Standard "Cats and Dogs" (CATDOG.GEN). The entry of dogs and cats is only allowed through Auckland (AKL) or Christchurch (CHC) airports. Quarantine facilities are private, approved and supervised by the MPI. For specific queries from non-approved countries, the MPI can be reached at animal.imports@mpi.govt.nz.

Frequently asked questions

Can I take my pet directly from Peru?
No. Peru is not approved by the MPI. You have to transit: move the pet to an approved country (USA, Spain, Chile…), have it reside for 6 months and export from there. This also applies to Brazil, Colombia, Mexico, Ecuador, Bolivia, Venezuela and Paraguay. Argentina, Chile and Uruguay are approved.
What changed on 1 July 2026?
The new Import Health Standard "Cats and Dogs 2026" came into force, replacing the 2021 one, with a 9-month transition (until April 2027) during which both are valid. It adds, among other things, verification of the identification (microchip) at least 6 months before export.
Does my country enter directly?
Only those approved by the MPI: Category 1 (Australia), Category 2 (rabies-free) and Category 3 (rabies absent/controlled). Peru and most of the world are not approved. Look it up in the table.
Which breeds are banned?
American Pit Bull Terrier, Fila Brasileiro, Dogo Argentino, Tosa Inu and Perro de Presa Canario (Dog Control Act 1996), plus hybrids. Import ban at the border.
Where does it enter and how much quarantine?
Only through Auckland or Christchurch. Minimum 10-day quarantine at an MPI-approved facility (except from Australia, which requires no quarantine).
