Enter Italy with your pet (2026): the rule changes depending on your country — and Italy bans no breed

To enter Italy with your dog, cat or ferret, the European Union regime comes first: depending on your country of origin, entry is direct (microchip, rabies vaccine and certificate) or it also requires a rabies blood test (RNATT) ≥0,5 UI/ml and a 90-day wait. Italy's own layer has a key difference compared with Spain: Italy has no list of dangerous breeds (it abolished it in 2009) — no dog is banned for its breed. Entry must be made through an airport designated by the Ministero della Salute (a broad list, with control by the Agenzia delle Dogane), and the microchip is registered in the regional anagrafe degli animali d'affezione. First step: locate your country of origin

To enter Italy from outside the EU, the first thing that decides your path is your country of origin. Find yours: the table tells you whether you get direct entry (EU/EEA or listed country) or the protocol with blood test and a 3-month wait (non-listed country, like Peru).

A verified fact that surprises: being "rich" or nearby does not guarantee being listed. Russia and Belarus are no longer on the lists (they need a blood test); and neither are South Korea or Uruguay. By contrast, Mexico, Argentina, Chile and —since June 2026— Montenegro and Serbia are. Check yours, don't assume it.

What sets Italy apart: its national layer

The European regime is only half the story. Italy has its own rules, and one of them is among the most misunderstood in Europe.

✅ Italy has no list of dangerous breeds (the opposite of Spain)

In 2009, the Ordinanza Martini eliminated the "blacklist" of dangerous breeds, and the regulation in force —the Ordinanza of 6 August 2013, extended— does not distinguish by breed. No dog is banned in Italy for its breed: not Pit Bull, not Rottweiler, not Dogo. This contrasts with Spain, which does keep its list of 8 PPP breeds.

Instead, Italy applies an individual and behaviour-based approach: the owner is civilly liable for any damage their dog causes, whatever the breed (art. 2052 of the Codice Civile). The reinforced obligations —civil liability insurance, muzzle and leash— apply only to dogs that the ASL enters, case by case, in the register of dogs with an elevated risk of aggressiveness (after a bite or assessment), not for belonging to a breed.

Base rule for all dogs: in public places and on transport you must keep the dog on a leash and have a muzzle available to put on at the request of the authority (Veterinary Police Regulation, DPR 320/1954).

Designated points of entry (airports)

From outside the EU, your pet must enter through an airport designated by the Ministero della Salute as a point of entry for travellers with animals. The official list is broad and alphabetical (it is not four airports, as is sometimes said): it includes, among others,

  • Alghero (Fertilia)
  • Ancona (Falconara)
  • Bari (Palese)
  • Bergamo (Orio al Serio)
  • Bologna
  • Cagliari (Elmas)
  • Catania (Fontanarossa)
  • Milano Malpensa
  • Napoli (Capodichino)
  • Palermo (Punta Raisi)
  • Roma Fiumicino
  • Torino (Caselle)
  • Venezia (Marco Polo)
  • Verona (Villafranca)

The border control is carried out by the Agenzia delle Dogane (customs) at the point of entry, with support from the veterinary service: document check and microchip reading. The designated list changes, so always confirm the current version at the Ministero della Salute before buying the flight. (Birds have stricter restrictions than dogs, cats and ferrets.)

Registering your pet on arrival in Italy

Your pet's microchip must appear in an official register. In Italy the registers are regional (the anagrafe degli animali d'affezione of each region), integrated into the Banca Dati Nazionale (BDN) of the Ministero della Salute, being unified into the SINAC system since May 2025. If you settle in Italy, register your pet in the anagrafe of your region; it is handled by the veterinary service of the ASL or your veterinarian.

If your country is NOT listed (protocol with blood test)

This applies to Peru, Brazil, Colombia, China, Russia, Belarus and almost all of Africa and Asia. For your dog or cat to be able to enter Italy, the path is:

  1. ISO microchip (11784/11785, 15 digits), implanted before the rabies vaccine. If the order is reversed, the vaccine does not count.
  2. Rabies vaccine, with the animal at least 12 weeks old; wait 21 days after the primary vaccination.
  3. Rabies blood test (RNATT). The sample is taken at least 30 days after the vaccine, at an EU-designated laboratory; result ≥0,5 UI/ml.
  4. Wait 90 days (3 months) counted from the sampling before entering. (Does not apply to the re-entry of a pet that already had a valid RNATT before leaving the EU.)
  5. Official animal health certificate (model of Regulation (EU) 2026/705), signed by an official veterinarian.

The clock for non-listed countries. Microchip → vaccine (from 12 weeks) → wait 30 days → RNATT → wait 90 days → entry. From scratch it is about 7 months, and the minimum age of the animal will be 7 months. The good news: with a favourable result, the RNATT is valid for life as long as the vaccine is kept up to date.

If you come from the EU/EEA or a listed country (direct entry)

If your pet comes from an EU member state (or from Norway, Iceland, Liechtenstein, Switzerland), it travels with the European pet passport, without a blood test. If it comes from a listed country (United States, Canada, Mexico, Argentina, Chile, Japan, Australia, United Kingdom —except Northern Ireland—, the Emirates, Hong Kong, Taiwan, Singapore, Montenegro, Serbia and others): microchip, rabies vaccine (wait 21 days) and animal health certificate. Without a blood test and without the 3-month wait.

Authority and procedure in Italy

Health authority: Ministero della Salute (Direzione Generale della Sanità Animale), with the veterinary services of the ASL (Aziende Sanitarie Locali) at the local level; the border control is carried out by the Agenzia delle Dogane. To export a pet from Italy to a third country, the certificato di espatrio is issued by appointment by the ASL veterinarian (its validity depends on the destination country). The European framework in force is Delegated Regulation (EU) 2026/131 and the Implementing Regulations (EU) 2026/636 (country lists) and 2026/705 (certificate, with new models mandatory from 1 October 2026), all under Regulation (EU) 2016/429. The general EU regime for entering from outside is explained in our pillar guide to the European Union.

Frequently asked questions

Does my country need the rabies blood test to enter Italy?
It depends on your origin, because Italy applies the EU regime. EU/EEA or listed country (USA, Canada, Mexico, Argentina, Chile, Japan, Australia, United Kingdom, the Emirates, and since June 2026 Montenegro and Serbia): no. Non-listed country (Peru, Brazil, Colombia, China, Russia…): yes, RNATT ≥0,5 UI/ml + 90 days. Look it up in the table.
Does Italy ban any dog breed?
No. Italy abolished its list of dangerous breeds in 2009 (Ordinanza Martini); the regulation in force (Ordinanza 6 agosto 2013) does not distinguish by breed. The owner is civilly liable for the damage whatever the breed (art. 2052 Codice Civile). The reinforced obligations (insurance, muzzle, leash) apply individually to dogs entered by the ASL in the elevated-risk register, not by breed.
Through which airports can I enter from outside the EU?
Through an airport designated by the Ministero della Salute. The list is broad and alphabetical: Alghero, Ancona, Bari, Bergamo Orio al Serio, Bologna, Cagliari, Catania, Milano Malpensa, Napoli, Palermo, Roma Fiumicino, Torino, Venezia, Verona and others. The control is carried out by the Agenzia delle Dogane. Confirm the current list at salute.gov.it.
Do I have to register my pet on arrival?
The microchip must appear in an official register: in Italy they are regional (anagrafe degli animali d'affezione), integrated into the Banca Dati Nazionale (BDN), being unified into the SINAC system since May 2025.
How long does it take from a non-listed country like Peru?
About 7 months from scratch: microchip → vaccine (from 12 weeks) → 30 days → RNATT → 90-day wait → entry. A favourable RNATT is valid for life if the vaccine is kept current.
