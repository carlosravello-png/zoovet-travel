Requirements to enter China with your pet (2026): the most binary destination in the world

To enter China with your pet, China classifies each country into two groups. If you come from a designated country (19 in total: New Zealand, Australia, Japan, United Kingdom, Ireland, Singapore, Portugal, Sweden, Switzerland and others), entry is direct: ISO microchip, a current rabies vaccine and an official certificate, with no antibody test and no quarantine. If you come from a non-designated country —such as Peru, Brazil, the continental United States or continental Europe—, the path is the full protocol: microchip, two rabies vaccines and an antibody titer (RNATT ≥0.5 IU/mL) from a laboratory accredited by China Customs; without it, 30 days of mandatory quarantine. China requires no waiting period after the result. Hard rules: only one pet per passport and entry only through ports with a quarantine facility. Dogs and cats follow the same protocol.

First step: find your country

China does not treat every country the same. Look for yours: the table tells you whether you face direct entry (designated country) or the full protocol (non-designated country).

Subnational nuance: some territories enter as designated even when their country is not. Hawaii and Guam are designated, but the continental United States is not. French Polynesia is designated, but France is not. Verify your exact territory, not just the country.

If your country is FULL PROTOCOL (non-designated)

This applies to the vast majority of the world —Peru, Brazil, Colombia, Mexico, the continental United States, continental Europe, India and the rest—. For your dog or cat to avoid the 30-day quarantine, the path is:

  1. ISO microchip (11784/11785, 15 digits), implanted before the rabies vaccine. If done the other way around, the vaccine does not count.
  2. Two doses of documented rabies vaccine (primary vaccination + booster), with their dates and batch numbers. Minimum age to vaccinate: 90 days. China requires the record of two doses, not just the last one.
  3. Rabies antibody titer (RNATT) with a result ≥0.5 IU/mL from a laboratory accredited by China Customs (GACC) —different from the EU or UK list—. China requires no waiting period after the result: the animal can travel as soon as the laboratory delivers it (valid for 12 months). Without a valid titer → 30 days of quarantine.
  4. Official health certificate from the country of origin, with a clinical examination performed within 14 days before arrival, documenting the microchip, the two vaccines and the RNATT result. It is issued and endorsed by the official authority (in Peru, SENASA; in Colombia, ICA; in Bolivia, SENASAG; in Ecuador, AGROCALIDAD).
  5. Customs declaration in the official GACC app within the 24 hours before landing.
  6. Only one pet per passport and entry only through one of the ports with a quarantine facility (see below).

The authorized entry ports (with a quarantine facility)

The pet can only enter through ports with an official quarantine facility. According to the official Customs Q&A:

  • Beijing-Capital Airport · Beijing-West railway station · Shanghai-Hongqiao · Shanghai-Pudong · Shanghai railway station · Shanghai International Passenger Center · Wusongkou cruise terminal · Urumqi Airport · Alashankou (land port) · Guangzhou-Baiyun Airport.

Laboratories accredited by China Customs

The blood sample must be processed in a laboratory on the current official GACC list, which is different from the EU or UK one: confirm it before sending. In Peru there is no GACC-accredited laboratory, so the sample leaves the country (for example Kansas State University in the USA or Laboklin in Germany). Sending to a laboratory off the GACC list, even one with EU accreditation, leads to 30 days of quarantine.

If your country is DIRECT ENTRY (designated country)

If you come from one of the 19 designated countries or territories —New Zealand, Australia, Japan, United Kingdom, Ireland, Liechtenstein, Cyprus, Portugal, Sweden, Switzerland, Iceland, Singapore, Fiji, Jamaica, French Polynesia, Hawaii, Guam, Hong Kong and Macao—, the path is much shorter, but it is not "nothing":

  • Valid ISO microchip.
  • Current rabies vaccine + official certificate from the country of origin.
  • Clinical examination within 14 days before arrival, documented in the certificate.
  • No antibody titer and no quarantine if the documentation is complete and passes inspection.

The one-pet-per-passport rule, the GACC app declaration and entry through the authorized ports still apply.

Two hard rules that stop trips

⚠ One pet per passport. GACC Announcement No. 5 strictly allows one pet (dog or cat) per passenger. A second pet under the same passport is confiscated or returned, no exceptions. If you travel with two animals, the second must go with another passenger.
⚠ 30-day quarantine. Any documentary non-compliance —an unreadable microchip, a missing titer from a GACC lab, an expired vaccine or a port without a facility— triggers 30 days of official quarantine at the owner's expense. It is the longest quarantine in this collection of profiles.

What about cats?

Unlike the United States, in China the protocol is the same for dogs and cats: microchip, two rabies vaccines and, if you come from a non-designated country, the antibody titer to avoid quarantine. The one-pet-per-passport rule applies to both.

After customs: municipal regulations

Clearing customs without quarantine does not guarantee you can live with the animal in your district. Each Chinese city has its own breed and size regulations: Beijing bans dogs taller than 35 cm in the central districts and prohibits certain breeds; Shanghai and Guangzhou have different rules. Check the regulations of your destination district before starting the process, not on arrival.

Frequently asked questions

Does my country enter China directly or under the full protocol?
China classifies the world into designated (direct entry) and non-designated (full protocol). The 19 designated: New Zealand, Australia, Fiji, French Polynesia, Hawaii, Guam, Jamaica, Iceland, United Kingdom, Ireland, Liechtenstein, Cyprus, Portugal, Sweden, Switzerland, Japan, Singapore, Hong Kong and Macao. The rest are non-designated. Look yours up in the table.
What happens if I come from a non-designated country?
To avoid 30 days of quarantine: microchip, two rabies vaccines and RNATT ≥0.5 IU/mL from a GACC-accredited laboratory. Without that, mandatory quarantine at your expense.
Does China require a waiting period after the antibody test?
No. Unlike the EU (90 days) or Australia (180 days), China requires no waiting period: the animal travels as soon as the laboratory delivers the result (valid for 12 months).
How many pets can I bring?
One per passport. The second is confiscated or returned, no exceptions (GACC Announcement No. 5).
Through which port must I enter?
Only through ports with a quarantine facility: the airports of Beijing-Capital, Shanghai-Hongqiao and Pudong, Urumqi and Guangzhou-Baiyun, plus Beijing-West, the Shanghai railway station and passenger center, Wusongkou and Alashankou.
Is there a GACC-accredited laboratory in my country?
The GACC list is different from the EU/UK one. In Peru there is none; the sample goes abroad (Kansas State, Laboklin). Check the current official GACC list.
Do these rules apply to cats?
Yes, the same as for dogs: microchip, two vaccines and, if you come from a non-designated country, the antibody titer.
