Enter Spain with your pet (2026): the rule changes depending on your country — and Spain adds its own

To enter Spain with your dog, cat or ferret, the European Union regime comes first: depending on your country of origin, entry is direct (microchip, rabies vaccine and certificate) or also requires a rabies blood test (RNATT) ≥0,5 UI/ml and a 90-day wait. But Spain adds its national layer that no generic EU guide tells you: you can only enter through an authorised Travellers' Point of Entry (PEV) (23 airports), potentially dangerous breeds (PPP) need a licence, insurance and muzzle (Law 50/1999 and Royal Decree 287/2002, which Law 7/2023 did not repeal), and the microchip must be recorded in the register of your autonomous community (REIAC). Look up your country in the table and then check the Spanish layer.

First step: locate your country of origin

To enter Spain from outside the EU, the first thing that decides your path is your country of origin. Find yours: the table tells you whether you get direct entry (EU/EEA or listed country) or the protocol with blood test and 3-month wait (non-listed country, like Peru).

A verified fact that surprises: being "rich" or nearby does not guarantee being listed. Russia and Belarus are no longer on the lists (they need a blood test); and neither are South Korea or Uruguay. By contrast, Mexico, Argentina, Chile and —since June 2026— Montenegro and Serbia are. Check yours, don't assume it.

What no EU guide tells you: Spain's national layer

The European regime is only half the story. Spain has its own rules that apply on arrival and while living with your pet. Here are the three that prevent the most problems.

⚠️ Potentially dangerous breeds (PPP): the point that surprises most

Law 7/2023 on animal welfare did not repeal the regime for Potentially Dangerous Dogs: Law 50/1999 and Royal Decree 287/2002 remain in force. The 8 breeds of Annex I (and their crosses) are:

Pit Bull Terrier · Staffordshire Bull Terrier · American Staffordshire Terrier · Rottweiler · Dogo Argentino · Fila Brasileiro · Tosa Inu · Akita Inu.

If you bring one of these breeds (or a dog whose physical characteristics match the criterion), the keeper needs: an administrative licence from the town council, civil liability insurance for damage to third parties (the minimum cover is set by each municipality or autonomous community; frequently €120,000), and in public a muzzle and a non-extendable leash under 2 metres, plus the certificate of physical capacity and psychological aptitude. Some autonomous communities extend the list, so check the one for your destination.

New development (don't assume it already applies): Law 7/2023 provides in its article 30.3 that civil liability insurance be mandatory for all dogs, not just PPP. But the state regulation that develops it was still in draft and not in force at the national level as of the date of this page. In the meantime, the firm insurance obligation is the one for PPP.

At the PEV you must locate the control point and declare to the Tax Guard of the Guardia Civil or to the authorised customs staff that you are travelling with a companion animal, presenting its documentation for the documentary and identity check (they will read the microchip). Always confirm the current list of PEV with the MAPA before buying your flight, because it is updated.

Registering your pet on arrival in Spain

Your pet's microchip must be recorded in an official register. In Spain the registers are regional (one per autonomous community) and are federated in the REIAC (Spanish Network for the Identification of Companion Animals). Law 7/2023 provides for a central national system of registers (SICERPA), but it is still not fully operational. If you settle in Spain, register your pet in the register of your autonomous community; your usual veterinarian usually handles it.

If your country is NOT listed (protocol with blood test)

This applies to Peru, Brazil, Colombia, China, Russia, Belarus and almost all of Africa and Asia. For your dog or cat to be able to enter Spain, the path is:

  1. ISO microchip (11784/11785, 15 digits), implanted before the rabies vaccine. If the order is reversed, the vaccine does not count.
  2. Rabies vaccine, with the animal at least 12 weeks old; wait 21 days after the primary vaccination.
  3. Rabies blood test (RNATT). The sample is taken at least 30 days after the vaccine, at an EU-designated laboratory; result ≥0,5 UI/ml.
  4. Wait 90 days (3 months) counted from the sampling before entering. (Does not apply to the re-entry of a pet that already had a valid RNATT before leaving the EU.)
  5. Official animal health certificate (model of Regulation (EU) 2026/705), signed by an official veterinarian and presented at least in Spanish, plus the corresponding declaration.

The clock for non-listed countries. Microchip → vaccine (from 12 weeks) → wait 30 days → RNATT → wait 90 days → entry. From scratch it is about 7 months, and the minimum age of the animal will be 7 months. The good news: with a favourable result, the RNATT is valid for life as long as the vaccine is kept up to date.

If you come from the EU/EEA or a listed country (direct entry)

If your pet comes from an EU member state (or from Norway, Iceland, Liechtenstein, Switzerland), it travels with the European pet passport, without a blood test. If it comes from a listed country (United States, Canada, Mexico, Argentina, Chile, Japan, Australia, United Kingdom —except Northern Ireland—, the Emirates, Hong Kong, Taiwan, Singapore, Montenegro, Serbia and others): microchip, rabies vaccine (wait 21 days) and animal health certificate. Without a blood test and without the 3-month wait.

Authority and procedure in Spain

National authority: Ministry of Agriculture, Fisheries and Food (MAPA). To export a pet from Spain to a third country, the certificate is requested by your veterinarian through the MAPA's CEXGAN application, and it is issued by the Agriculture Areas of the Government Delegations and Sub-delegations (Áreas de Agricultura de las Delegaciones y Subdelegaciones del Gobierno). The European framework in force is Delegated Regulation (EU) 2026/131 and the Implementing Regulations (EU) 2026/636 (country lists) and 2026/705 (certificate), all under Regulation (EU) 2016/429 (Animal Health Law). The general EU regime for entering from outside is explained in our pillar page on the European Union.

Frequently asked questions

Does my country need the rabies blood test to enter Spain?
It depends on your origin, because Spain applies the EU regime. EU/EEA or a listed country (USA, Canada, Mexico, Argentina, Chile, Japan, Australia, United Kingdom, the Emirates, and since June 2026 Montenegro and Serbia): no. Non-listed country (Peru, Brazil, Colombia, China, Russia…): yes, RNATT ≥0,5 UI/ml + 90 days. Look it up in the table.
Can I enter with a dangerous breed dog (PPP)?
Yes, but the PPP regime applies (Law 50/1999 + Royal Decree 287/2002): 8 breeds (Pit Bull, Staffordshire Bull Terrier, American Staffordshire, Rottweiler, Dogo Argentino, Fila Brasileiro, Tosa Inu, Akita Inu) that require a municipal licence, civil liability insurance and, in public, a muzzle + leash ≤2 m. Some autonomous communities extend the list.
Through which airports can I enter from outside the EU?
Only through an authorised Travellers' Point of Entry (PEV): Madrid-Barajas, Barcelona-El Prat, Málaga, Valencia, Sevilla, Palma, Alicante, Gran Canaria, Tenerife N/S, Lanzarote, Fuerteventura, Santiago, A Coruña, Vigo, Santander, Vitoria, Girona, Reus, Jerez, Almería, Castellón and La Palma. Declare to the Tax Guard of the Guardia Civil.
Do I have to register my pet on arrival?
The microchip must be recorded in an official register. In Spain these are regional registers federated in the REIAC; the central national system (SICERPA) provided for by Law 7/2023 is not yet fully operational.
How long does it take from a non-listed country like Peru?
About 7 months from scratch: microchip → vaccine (from 12 weeks) → 30 days → RNATT → 90-day wait → entry. The favourable RNATT is valid for life if the vaccine is kept current.
