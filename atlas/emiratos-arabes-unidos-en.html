Requirements to enter the United Arab Emirates with your pet (2026): MOCCAE permit and country classification

To enter the United Arab Emirates with your dog or cat, the authority is MOCCAE (Ministry of Climate Change & Environment) and there are two things that apply to everyone: you need a prior import permit (obtained online before traveling, valid for 30 days, 200 AED) and your pet must travel as manifested cargo, never in the cabin. After that, the rule changes depending on your country: MOCCAE has its own list of about 50 low-risk countries that enter without a blood test; the rest of the world is high risk and needs a rabies antibody titration test (FAVN) ≥0.5 IU/ml. Bahrain and Qatar are the exception: they appear as low risk, but still require the blood test. In addition, there are banned dog breeds and a limit of 2 pets per person per year. There is no quarantine if the paperwork is in order.

First of all: the MOCCAE permit

Unlike many destinations, in the Emirates the import permit is mandatory for everyone and is processed BEFORE traveling, online (eservices.moccae.gov.ae). It is valid for 30 days from issue, so don't apply for it until you have a confirmed flight date: if it expires, your pet won't be allowed in. It costs 200 AED. This applies equally whether your country is low or high risk.

First step: locate your country

Look for yours in the table. It tells you whether you enter without a blood test (low risk) or with a FAVN antibody titration test (high risk), plus the special case of Bahrain and Qatar.

A verified fact that surprises: MOCCAE's low-risk list does not match that of the EU or that of rabies-free countries. Countries you'd expect to be "easy" —such as France, Poland, Norway or Canada— do not appear on the published low-risk list, so by MOCCAE's rule they would enter with a blood test. And the other way around, Bahrain and Qatar are on the low-risk list, but still require the titer.

If your country is NOT on the low-risk list (with a blood test)

This applies to the United States, almost all of Latin America, much of Asia and Africa, and several European countries that MOCCAE does not include on its list. For your dog or cat:

  1. ISO microchip (11784/11785), implanted before the rabies vaccine; the number must match across all documentation.
  2. Rabies vaccine with the pet at least 12 weeks old when vaccinated and a minimum of 21 days before travel. Dogs: also distemper, parvovirus, infectious hepatitis and leptospirosis. Cats: panleukopenia, rhinotracheitis and calicivirus.
  3. Rabies antibody titration test (FAVN), result ≥0.5 IU/ml, at a laboratory accredited by the authority of the exporting country. The sample: taken no more than 12 months before travel and, if it is a first vaccination or it expired, at least 21 days after vaccinating (with a valid booster, it can be taken the same day or after).
  4. Minimum age at import: 15 weeks (from a high-risk country).
  5. Internal and external antiparasitic treatment within the previous 14 days, recorded on the certificate.
  6. Official veterinary certificate endorsed by the competent authority of the country of origin.

If your country is low risk (without a blood test)

The roughly 50 countries on MOCCAE's low-risk list —among them the United Kingdom, Germany, Spain, Italy, Japan, Singapore, Australia and New Zealand— enter without the antibody titration test. They still need a permit, microchip, valid rabies vaccine, veterinary certificate and transport as cargo. The minimum age drops to 12 weeks.

Important about the list: MOCCAE's list of approved countries is its own and may be updated without notice. If your country does not appear as low risk, MOCCAE treats it as high risk (with a blood test). Before starting any procedure, confirm the exact status of your country directly with MOCCAE (moccae.gov.ae) — especially in borderline European cases.

The exception: Bahrain and Qatar

It is the most counterintuitive case in the entire classification: Bahrain and Qatar appear on MOCCAE's low-risk list, but still require the rabies antibody titration test to import into the country. If you come from there, prepare the titer even though your country is "approved".

Banned dog breeds

⚠ Breeds banned from import (MOCCAE). The following cannot enter: Pit Bulls (Staffordshire Bull Terrier, American Pit Bull Terrier, American Staffordshire Terrier, American Bully); mastiffs (Fila, Dogo Argentino, Tibetan Mastiff, Neapolitan Mastiff, Dogue de Bordeaux, Boerboel, Bullmastiff, Cane Corso, Bully Kutta, Presa Canario); and the Japanese Tosa — as well as any cross or hybrid of those breeds. Identification is phenotypic: if the MOCCAE inspector considers that the dog resembles a banned breed, it can be rejected even if the paperwork says otherwise. Only exception: service or assistance dogs with documentation from an accredited center.

The Rottweiler does not appear in MOCCAE's federal import ban; the restrictions you'll see on Rottweilers are usually about local ownership (for example, rules of the Emirate of Dubai), which is a procedure separate from the federal import permit. Confirm it according to the destination emirate.

Permit, costs and transport

  • Import permit: 200 AED, valid for 30 days, online.
  • Inspection fee on arrival: 500 AED per dog and 250 AED per cat.
  • Transport: only manifested cargo (IATA conditions), never cabin or baggage. Exception: certified service dogs.
  • Limit: maximum 2 pets per person per year (2 dogs, 2 cats, or 1 and 1), except resident pets returning.
  • Entry: in practice, most arrive through the airports of Dubai or Abu Dhabi.

What about cats?

Same system by country (low risk without titer / high risk with titer). There are no banned cat breeds as such, but hybrids and wild felines (Bengal, Serval) require a fifth-generation pedigree certificate in order to be imported. Confirm the case of the Savannah or other hybrids directly with MOCCAE.

Quarantine, authority and contact

There is no quarantine for pets that arrive with everything in order. If a pet from a high-risk country arrives without a valid blood test, it may be held in government quarantine at the owner's expense. Authority: MOCCAE — Ministry of Climate Change & Environment (moccae.gov.ae / eservices.moccae.gov.ae; call center 8003050, email info@moccae.gov.ae). The local ownership license (e.g. Dubai Municipality) is a procedure separate from the federal import permit.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need a permit to enter with my pet?
Yes, the MOCCAE permit is mandatory for everyone, obtained online before traveling, valid for 30 days and costs 200 AED. Don't apply for it until you have the flight date: if it expires, your pet won't be allowed in.
Does my country need a blood test?
It depends. MOCCAE has its own list of ~50 low-risk countries (no titer). If your country is not on it, it is high risk and needs FAVN ≥0.5 IU/ml. The list does not match the EU's; look for it in the table and confirm it with MOCCAE.
Why do Bahrain and Qatar require a titer if they are low risk?
It is a documented exception: both are on MOCCAE's low-risk list but require the antibody titration test anyway.
Can my dog breed enter?
Pit Bulls, mastiffs (Fila, Dogo Argentino, Tibetan, Neapolitan, Dogue de Bordeaux, Boerboel, Bullmastiff, Cane Corso, Bully Kutta, Presa Canario) and the Japanese Tosa are banned, plus their crosses. The review is phenotypic. Only service dogs with accredited documentation are allowed.
Can it travel in the cabin?
No. It must enter as manifested cargo. Exception: certified service dogs. Emotional support animals do not qualify.
Is there quarantine?
No, if the documentation is in order. Without a blood test (coming from high risk), the pet may be held in quarantine at the owner's expense.
