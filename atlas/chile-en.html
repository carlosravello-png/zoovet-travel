Requirements to enter Chile with your pet (2026): the antiparasitic window almost no one gets right

To enter Chile with your dog or cat, the authority is the SAG (Servicio Agrícola y Ganadero) under Resolución N° 6.508/2021. The rule is the same for all countries: you need a Certificado Zoosanitario de Importación (CZI) in Spanish, with a current rabies vaccine (or, as an alternative, the RNATT antibody test). The detail that is most often missed: the internal and external antiparasitic treatment must be applied between 5 and 30 days before entry —neither earlier nor later—. On arrival there is a 10-day home confinement (in your home, not in a facility). The good news: no blood test is required from any country, there is no facility quarantine, you do not need a prior permit and the microchip is not mandatory for entry.

The document: the CZI (Resolución 6.508/2021)

Chile requires a Certificado Zoosanitario de Importación (CZI) issued by the official health authority of the country of origin, in Spanish (and in the official language of the origin). For pets from the European Union, the EU Passport is valid; with Argentina the CZI may be issued digitally. There is no prior import permit: the procedure is done on arrival before the SAG at the border control.

CZI validity: 10 calendar days, extendable by up to 5 more days depending on travel conditions. Issue it close to the travel date so it does not expire in transit.

The rule that fails most: the 5 to 30 day antiparasitic

⚠ Two-sided window. The antiparasitic treatment (internal and external, broad-spectrum) must be applied at least 5 days and at most 30 days before entering Chile. Applying it the day before the flight —the most common mistake— invalidates it; and applying it more than 30 days before does too. The active ingredient, dose and date must be recorded on the CZI.

What your pet needs

  1. Current rabies vaccine. If it is the first vaccination, it must be applied at least 21 days before entry; if the pet already has a current vaccination plan, that additional wait is not needed. Minimum age to vaccinate: 3 months.
  2. Antiparasitic, internal and external, between 5 and 30 days before (see above).
  3. Veterinary clinical examination within the previous 10 days: healthy animal, with no signs of infectious-contagious or parasitic diseases.
  4. CZI in Spanish, with the owner's details, the pet's details and the destination address in Chile.
  5. Microchip: not required for entry (yes to leave Chile). Recommended for identification.

Alternative to the vaccine (RNATT): instead of the rabies vaccine, an antibody titration test ≥0.5 IU/ml carried out between 3 and 24 months before travel is accepted. It is not mandatory except in the case of puppies (below).

10-day home confinement

Unlike Australia or the United Arab Emirates, Chile does not admit the pet into a facility. What applies —to all entries— is a 10-day confinement at the private destination address declared on the certificate. It is carried out in your home, without official inspection during those days.

Puppies under 60 days

Puppies under 60 days that travel without a rabies vaccine can only enter if they present an antibody titration certificate (RNATT ≥0.5 IU/ml) duly accredited on the CZI. Declaring the age is not enough: the laboratory result must appear on the certificate. It is the only situation in which the SAG requires the blood test.

Hybrid cats: prohibited

⚠ Hybrids do not enter. Chile prohibits the entry of hybrid animals resulting from crossing a domestic species with a wild one. The Bengal (domestic cat × Asian leopard) and the Savannah (domestic × serval) fall into this category — and, unlike other countries, there is no fifth-generation pedigree exception.

Are you Chilean and returning with your pet?

The regime changes depending on the time away: if you were out less than 21 days, a copy of the CZE issued by the SAG on departure plus the current rabies vaccine certificate is enough. If you were out more than 21 days, you must meet all the CZI requirements of the country of origin. (For Argentina, the SAG's CZE is valid for up to 60 days for a round trip.)

Entry, costs and contact

The entry procedure before the SAG has no cost; you only pay for the steps at origin (vaccine, antiparasitic, examination, CZI issuance). On arrival, the SAG verifies the CZI at the border control. The main point of entry is the Aeropuerto Arturo Merino Benítez (SCL, Santiago). Authority: SAG (sag.gob.cl), phone +56 2 2345 1100, inquiries via ChileAtiende (101). The Registro Nacional de Mascotas applies to residents in Chile, not to the entry of travelers.

And cats?

Same process as dogs (except the hybrid ban): CZI in Spanish, current rabies vaccine or RNATT, antiparasitic within the 5 to 30 day window, clinical examination and 10-day home confinement. No mandatory titer and no microchip for entry.

Frequently asked questions

When do I apply the antiparasitic?
Between 5 and 30 days before entry. Two-sided window: neither the day before (the most common case and a mistake), nor more than 30 days before. Internal and external, with active ingredient, dose and date on the CZI.
Does Chile require a blood test (titer)?
Not as standard. A current rabies vaccine is enough for all countries. The RNATT (≥0.5 IU/ml, 3-24 months before) is an alternative to the vaccine and only mandatory for puppies under 60 days without a vaccine.
Is the microchip mandatory to enter?
Not to enter. It is mandatory to leave Chile (Registro Nacional de Mascotas). A foreign pet can enter without a microchip.
Is there quarantine?
Not in an official facility. There is a 10-day home confinement, at the destination address, for all entries.
Can my Bengal or Savannah cat enter?
No. Chile prohibits the entry of hybrids of a domestic species with a wild one, with no pedigree exception.
Do I need a prior permit?
No. The procedure is done on arrival at the border control, with the valid CZI.
