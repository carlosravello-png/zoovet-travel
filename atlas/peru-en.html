Requirements to enter Peru with your pet (2026): the SENASA process, the same for everyone

To enter Peru with your dog or cat, the authority is SENASA (National Agrarian Health Service), and the process is uniform for all countries of origin: the same procedure whether your pet comes from the United States, Spain or Argentina. No rabies blood test (RNATT) is required and there is no quarantine. You need: full and valid vaccination (dogs: parvovirus, distemper, hepatitis, leptospirosis and rabies; cats: panleukopenia and rabies —mandatory from 3 months—), a health certificate from a licensed veterinarian stating the health status and the dewormings, and internal and external deworming within the 30 days before travel. On arrival (the main point is Jorge Chávez Airport), SENASA carries out an inspection with payment of fees and issues the animal health entry certificate.

First step: find your country

In Peru the country of origin does not change the process: it is the same for everyone. Look up your country in the table to confirm it —for all of them you will see the standard SENASA process, with no blood test—. What stops most trips is not the country, but arriving with an incomplete vaccine schedule or with the deworming outside the 30-day window.

The standard process (the same for all countries)

  1. Full and valid vaccination. Dogs: parvovirus, distemper, hepatitis, leptospirosis and rabies. Cats: panleukopenia and rabies. Rabies is mandatory from 3 months of age.
  2. Valid health certificate, issued by a licensed veterinarian, stating the animal's health status and the dewormings.
  3. Internal and external deworming carried out within the 30 days before travel.
  4. Valid vaccination certificate with the rabies shot recorded.
  5. On arrival: SENASA inspection after payment of fees (inspection + entry document). With SENASA's approval, the pet enters the country.

The mistake that costs the most: believing that "rabies is enough". Peru requires the complete schedule (for dogs, five vaccines) and the deworming within the 30 days. A certificate missing a vaccine or with expired deworming can stop entry at the airport.

Inspection and costs on arrival

The main entry point is Jorge Chávez International Airport (Lima/Callao); in addition, pet procedures are handled by SENASA's 16 Executive Directorates across the country. On arrival, SENASA carries out the physical inspection of the pet and charges the inspection and entry document fees (the current fee is in SENASA's TUPA). There is no quarantine if the paperwork is in order.

What about cats?

The process is the same (health certificate, up-to-date vaccination, 30-day deworming and inspection). The only difference is the vaccine schedule: cats need panleukopenia and rabies (dogs, the group of five). No blood test is required either.

Official contact (SENASA)

Authority: SENASA — National Agrarian Health Service. Head office: Av. La Molina 1915, La Molina (Lima). Phones: (01) 313-3300 / 313-3306. Website: senasa.gob.pe. Pet procedures are carried out at the 16 regional Executive Directorates, and the main entry/exit point is Jorge Chávez International Airport.

Frequently asked questions

Does my country need a rabies blood test for Peru?
No. SENASA does not require RNATT from any country of origin. The process is uniform: full vaccination, health certificate, deworming and inspection. There is also no quarantine if the paperwork is in order.
What vaccines does my pet need?
Dogs: parvovirus, distemper, hepatitis, leptospirosis and rabies. Cats: panleukopenia and rabies. Rabies is mandatory from 3 months. Rabies alone is not enough: the schedule must be complete and valid.
What documents does SENASA require?
A valid health certificate from a licensed veterinarian (with dewormings), a valid vaccination certificate with rabies, and internal and external deworming within the 30 days before travel. On arrival, SENASA issues the animal health certificate after the inspection and the payment of fees.
Is there a quarantine or inspection on arrival?
There is no quarantine if everything is in order. SENASA carries out an inspection at the entry point (main one: Jorge Chávez), after payment of the inspection fees and the entry document.
Are the rules the same for dogs and cats?
The process is the same; the vaccine schedule changes (dogs: five; cats: panleukopenia and rabies). In both, rabies from 3 months and no blood test.
