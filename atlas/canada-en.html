Requirements to enter Canada with your pet (2026): the rule changes depending on your country

To enter Canada with your dog or cat, the authority is the CFIA (Canadian Food Inspection Agency) and the inspection is done by the CBSA (customs) at the border. The CFIA recognizes as rabies-free only 9 countries or territories: Australia, Fiji, Finland, Iceland, Ireland, Japan, New Zealand, Sweden and the United Kingdom. From them, a certificate of rabies-free origin (no vaccine) is enough. From any other country —including the United States, Mexico and almost all of Latin America—, you need a valid rabies vaccination certificate, in English or French (a certificate only in Spanish may be rejected). There is no quarantine, no blood test is required (the RNATT is an alternative, not an obligation) and no import permit is needed for personal pets. Those under 3 months of age are exempt from the vaccine.

A surprising verified fact: the CFIA list is very short —only 9—. Norway is NOT on it (unlike the EU), and neither is the United States: a dog arriving from the U.S. needs its vaccination certificate. And watch the language: the certificate must be in English or French, not in Spanish.

If your country is NOT rabies-free (with vaccination certificate)

This applies to the United States, Mexico, Brazil, Peru, almost all of Latin America, Asia, Africa and most of Europe. For your dog or cat aged 3 months or older:

  1. Valid rabies vaccination certificate, issued by a licensed veterinarian, that clearly identifies the animal and shows it is up to date with rabies (the vaccine is not valid if administered before 12 weeks of age).
  2. In English or French. A certificate only in Spanish may be rejected by the CBSA at the border.
  3. No mandatory blood test. The RNATT exists as an alternative to the vaccination certificate, but it is not the traveler's standard route.
  4. On arrival: the CBSA carries out a documentary and visual check of the animal. Remember you are required by law to declare your pet.

Under 3 months: exempt from the rabies vaccine. But dogs under 8 months traveling without their owner have additional requirements (veterinary exam and distemper, hepatitis, parvovirus and parainfluenza vaccines). Service dogs accompanied by their user are exempt from many restrictions.

If your country is rabies-free (entry with certificate of origin)

From the 9 rabies-free countries on the CFIA list —Australia, Fiji, Finland, Iceland, Ireland, Japan, New Zealand, Sweden and the United Kingdom— you do not need the rabies vaccine: a veterinary certificate is enough, proving that rabies has not existed in that country in the previous 6 months and that the animal lived there during that period (or since birth). The certificate must also be in English or French.

Quarantine, costs and commercial dogs

There is no quarantine for personal pets that meet the requirements. On arrival, the CBSA charges an inspection fee (roughly around $36.95 CAD for the first animal and about $6.16 for each additional one; more if a vaccination must be ordered at the border); confirm it in the CBSA's current tariff.

⚠ Commercial dogs from countries at high risk for canine rabies. Since September 28, 2022, their entry is prohibited and the CFIA does not issue permits. This does not affect the personal pet traveling with its owner, but it does affect commercial or rescue imports from those countries.

What about cats?

The system is the same: from a rabies-free country, certificate of origin; from the rest, rabies vaccination certificate in English or French. Cats under 3 months are not subject to import restrictions. No blood test is required either.

Authority and contact (CFIA / CBSA)

Regulatory authority: CFIA — Canadian Food Inspection Agency (portal inspection.canada.ca; check requirements by country in the AIRS tool). Border inspection: CBSA — Canada Border Services Agency. For import inquiries, the CFIA answers at 1-800-835-4486 and 1-289-247-4099 (email cfia.nisc-csni.acia@inspection.gc.ca).

Frequently asked questions

Is my country rabies-free for Canada or do I need the vaccine?
Rabies-free for the CFIA are only 9: Australia, Fiji, Finland, Iceland, Ireland, Japan, New Zealand, Sweden and the United Kingdom (certificate of origin, no vaccine). The rest (U.S., Mexico, Latin America…) need a rabies vaccination certificate. Look it up in the table.
Can the certificate be in Spanish?
No. It must be in English or French, signed by a licensed veterinarian, and identify the animal. A certificate only in Spanish may be rejected by the CBSA. Bring a bilingual one or one in English.
Is there quarantine?
No for personal pets with documents in order. The CBSA carries out a documentary and visual check on arrival. Without documents, it may order vaccination at your expense or refuse entry.
Does my puppy under 3 months need the vaccine?
No. Those under 3 months are exempt from rabies vaccination. Dogs under 8 months traveling without an owner have extra requirements (exam and core vaccines).
What about commercial or rescue dogs?
Stricter: since September 28, 2022, commercial dogs from countries at high risk for canine rabies are banned from entry and the CFIA does not issue permits. This guide is for personal pets.
