Requirements to Enter Turkey with Your Pet (2026): The Country with No List and No Shortcuts

⚠ Rule in force · Turkey · 07/23/2026

Turkey requires a rabies antibody titration with a 3-month wait — even though it is the only European country with canine rabies. The laboratory must be approved by the European Union, not by Turkey. Many commercial aggregators claim the opposite and mislead travelers. Read about the serology laboratories →

To enter Turkey with your dog or cat, forget about checking whether your country is on a "good list": Turkey has no list of countries of origin. Wherever your pet comes from —Peru, Germany, the United States, India or South Africa—, the path is the same third-country protocol: an ISO microchip before the vaccine, an inactivated rabies vaccine, a rabies antibody titration ≥0.5 IU/ml at a European Union-approved laboratory (not one approved by Turkey), with the sample taken at least 30 days after the vaccine, and —the point almost nobody sees— entry is only allowed 3 months after that draw. A maximum of two animals per passenger, and 6 dog breeds are banned from entry. The only thing that changes according to your country is not whether you meet the protocol, but where the blood test is done: whether your country has an EU-approved laboratory or whether the sample has to travel.

First step: locate your country

Turkey treats all origins the same, but there is one detail that changes your timeline: where the titer test is done. Turkey only recognizes laboratories approved by the European Union. Find your country in the table: it tells you whether the serology can be done in your own country (it has an EU-approved laboratory) or whether the blood sample has to travel to an approved laboratory in another country —which adds weeks—.

Careful, this is not a "list of easy countries." The protocol —microchip, vaccine, titer test and 3-month wait— is identical for everyone. The table only tells you where your blood test is processed, not whether you get to skip a step. Nobody gets to skip one.

Does your country show up as "no local EU lab"? It is not an insurmountable obstacle: it means the serum sample is sent to an EU-approved laboratory in another country (with a cold chain and traceability to the microchip). You just have to account for it in the timeline. The list of approved laboratories is published by the European Union and can change: confirm it before drawing the sample.

The protocol, step by step (the same for everyone)

This is the complete path for any third country, including all of Latin America, Asia, Africa and the European Union itself. So that your dog or cat is not held up at the border:

  1. ISO microchip (11784/11785), implanted before the rabies vaccine. A vaccine dated earlier than the chip does not count.
  2. Inactivated rabies vaccine (modified live virus vaccines are not accepted). The vaccination date counts as day 0.
  3. Rabies antibody titration (FAVN/RNATT) ≥0.5 IU/ml at a European Union-approved laboratory —not one approved by Turkey—. The sample is taken at least 30 days after the vaccine.
  4. 3-month wait counted from the date the sample was drawn (not from the vaccine or from the result). Entry can take place 3 months later at the earliest. Example from the official document itself: vaccine 04/01 → earliest sample 05/01 → earliest entry 08/01.
  5. EK-2 veterinary certificate, bilingual Turkish-English, issued and endorsed by the official veterinary authority of your country of origin.
  6. At the border, three original documents are presented: the animal's passport or identity document, the ORIGINAL of the titration report and the EK-2 certificate. A maximum of 2 animals per passenger.
⚠ The mistake that costs months. The most common one is not a badly filled-in form: it is believing the commercial search engines that say "Turkey does not require a titer test." The official Turkish document requires it, with its 3-month wait. Anyone who arrives without the test cannot enter.

Turkey's trap: the laboratory has to be an EU one

This is where most people fall. Turkey does not recognize "any reference laboratory": the titer test has to come from a laboratory on the European Union's list. If your country does not have a designated one (the case for much of Latin America, Africa and Asia), the serum sample must travel to an approved laboratory in another country. This is not optional and it is the number one cause of rejections and broken timelines. We develop this in our analysis of the four lists of serology laboratories, which do not agree with one another.

Turkey's national layer: 6 banned dog breeds

In addition to the health protocol, Turkey bans the ENTRY into the country of six dog breeds considered dangerous, regardless of the documentation: American Pitbull Terrier, Dogo Argentino, Fila Brasileiro, Tosa Inu, American Staffordshire Terrier and American Bully. Legal basis: Law 7332 (Official Gazette 07/14/2021, no. 31541) and the Ministry's circular of 12/07/2021. Any source that lists only four breeds is out of date: the American Staffordshire Terrier and the American Bully were added in December 2021.

The only exceptions (they do not depend on the country)

Turkey does not exempt anyone based on their country of origin, but it does based on the animal's history:

  • Animal with a valid prior titer: if the passport certifies an acceptable rabies titer and a vaccination kept up without lapses, the official document does not require the test again.
  • Animal of Turkish origin returning with a titer obtained before departure: the 3-month wait does not apply to it.

What about cats?

The Turkish regime is the same for cats and dogs: the official document and the EK-2 certificate treat cats, dogs and ferrets together. The microchip before the vaccine, the inactivated vaccine, the titer test (0.5 IU/ml + 30 days + 3-month wait) and the maximum of 2 animals per passenger apply equally. There is no Turkish list of banned cat breeds of entry; the breed ban materialized only over six canine breeds.

Frequently asked questions

Does Turkey have a list of countries of origin with easy entry?
No. Unlike Japan or the EU regime, Turkey does not publish a list of countries. The same protocol —microchip, vaccine, rabies antibody titration at an EU-approved laboratory and a 3-month wait— applies equally to every third country. The only thing that changes according to your country is where the blood test is done.
Where do I have to do the titer test for Turkey?
At a European Union-approved laboratory, not one approved by Turkey. If your country has a designated one (EU/EEA and a specific list of non-EU countries), it is done locally; if not, the sample travels to an approved laboratory in another country. Look it up in the table above.
How long does the process take?
From scratch, about four months: microchip and vaccine, sample at least 30 days after the vaccine, and entry only 3 months after that draw. If your country has no EU-approved laboratory, you must add the shipping of the sample. If you already have a flight and have not started, start today.
Which dog breeds are banned from entry?
Six: American Pitbull Terrier, Dogo Argentino, Fila Brasileiro, Tosa Inu, American Staffordshire Terrier and American Bully (Law 7332 + circular of 12/07/2021). The four-breed lists are out of date.
Is the European pet passport valid?
Not on its own. Turkey requires its own EK-2 certificate, bilingual Turkish-English, issued by the official veterinary authority of the country of origin. The titer test remains required, with its 3-month wait, even from the European Union.
