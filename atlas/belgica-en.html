Enter Belgium with your pet (2026): the rule changes depending on your country — and here there is no national breed list: your municipality decides

To enter Belgium with your dog, cat or ferret, the European Union regime comes first: depending on your country of origin, entry is direct (microchip, rabies vaccine and certificate) or it also requires a rabies blood test (titrage) ≥0,5 UI/ml and a 90-day wait. Belgium's own layer is not a breed list: there is no national list since the Council of State annulled the 1998 one, and the one who decides which dog wears a muzzle is your municipality. What is closed is the door: only 7 points of entry, and one of them is a railway station. On arrival, if you stay, you have 8 days to register the animal in DogID or CatID, and the animal welfare regime changes depending on whether you live in Flanders, Brussels or Wallonia. Look up your country in the table and then review the Belgian layer.

First step: locate your country of origin

To enter Belgium from outside the EU, the first thing that decides your path is your country of origin. Find yours: the table tells you whether you get direct entry (EU/EEA or listed country) or the protocol with blood test and a 90-day wait (non-listed country, like Peru).

A verified fact that surprises: being "rich" or nearby does not guarantee being listed. Russia and Belarus are no longer on the lists (they need a blood test); and neither are South Korea or Uruguay. By contrast, Mexico, Argentina, Chile and —since June 2026— Montenegro and Serbia are. Check yours, don't assume it.

What sets Belgium apart: there is no national breed list

Here is the difference with its neighbours. Germany bans the import of four breeds by federal law and France classifies dogs into two categories. Belgium has neither, and not by political decision but because of a procedural defect.

⚖️ The Belgian list lasted seven months and ten days

The arrêté ministériel of 21 October 1998 named thirteen breeds. The Council of State annulled it on 31 May 1999 —judgment no. 80.521— for a procedural defect, not for its content. Nobody redid it. Since then there has been no national list of dangerous breeds in Belgium.

But that does not mean there are no rules: it means your municipality writes them. Municipalities regulate on their own under article 135 §2, 6.º of the Nouvelle loi communale, read with article 119. Grâce-Hollogne, for example, defines twelve breeds as «catégorie 1» with a compulsory muzzle in public space, a declaration to the police, civil liability insurance, a dog-club certificate and a ban on breeding. The list that affects you is not Belgium's: it is that of the municipality where you are going to live, and you have to read it before signing a lease.

Belgium's 7 points of entry

From a third country not listed in Annex I of Implementing Regulation (EU) 2026/636, entry can only be made through a designated travellers' point of entry, where the checks are carried out. The AFSCA says so bluntly, and adds that the owner or the authorised person must report to customs with the animal.

This is the full official list, from the AFSCA document in its version 4, dated 13 May 2026:

  1. Brussels Airport — airport
  2. Brussel-Zuid / Bruxelles-Midi — railway station
  3. Internationale Luchthaven Antwerpen (Antwerp) — airport
  4. Brussels South Charleroi Airport — airport
  5. Liège Airport — airport
  6. Internationale Luchthaven Oostende-Brugge — airport
  7. Port of Zeebrugge — port

Two things about that list that almost nobody publishes. The first: Bruxelles-Midi is a point of entry, and it is the Eurostar and Thalys terminal. You can enter the Union with your animal by train, not only by air or sea; it is the route for anyone arriving from the United Kingdom. The second: the document has two columns, and for pet birds the list is two, not seven — only Brussels Airport and Liège Airport. Anyone travelling with a bird does not have the other five. Any Belgian airport not on this list is not a valid point of entry.

Register your pet on arrival in Belgium

If you settle here, the animal is registered in the database that applies to it —DogID for dogs and CatID for cats— and only a veterinarian can do it. The verified deadline is 8 days from arrival.

From there on, animal welfare is a regional competence, and the three regions do not ask for the same things:

  1. Flanders. A cat born after 31 August 2014 has to be sterilised. The rule does not distinguish by origin: it also reaches the cat that lands already adult.
  2. Brussels-Capital. Cat identification has been compulsory since 1 March 2023. And a dog identified by tattoo must be re-identified by microchip.
  3. Wallonia. Cat identification has been compulsory since 1 November 2017. There is a permis de détention that is automatic and immaterial: it is not applied for, not printed, it does not exist on paper. It is evidenced with an extract from the Fichier Central issued by the municipality, valid for 30 days, required since 1 July 2022. And it is territorial, for acquisitions made on Walloon soil: anyone arriving from abroad with their own animal has nothing to request.

Several Belgian municipalities charge a dog tax. Each municipality sets it and some are abolishing it. We give no amounts: they change from one municipality to another and from one year to another.

If your country is NOT listed (protocol with blood test)

This applies to Peru, Brazil, Colombia, China, Russia, Belarus and almost all of Africa and Asia. For your dog or cat to be able to enter Belgium, the path is:

  1. ISO microchip (11784/11785), implanted before the rabies vaccine. If the order is reversed, the vaccine does not count.
  2. Rabies vaccine, with the animal at least 12 weeks old; wait 21 days after the primary vaccination.
  3. Rabies blood test (titrage). The sample is taken by an official or authorised veterinarian, at least 30 days after the vaccine or during a valid vaccination series in progress, and it is analysed without waiting. Result positive from 0,5 UI/ml.
  4. Wait 90 days. There is a divergence here worth knowing, and we develop it below.
  5. Official animal health certificate (model of Implementing Regulation (EU) 2026/705), signed by an official or authorised veterinarian and endorsed by the competent authority.

Where the blood is analysed, and why it matters from Peru. The AFSCA refers to «a laboratory in a third country listed in Annex VIII of Regulation (EU) 2021/404». The list of designated laboratories published by the European Commission contains twenty-three third countries, and Peru is not one of them; from all of Latin America only Brazil, Chile and Mexico are there. Checked on 29 August 2026. A sample taken in Peru has to be analysed abroad, and that shifts timelines and cost.

The good news and the 2028 requirement. A positive result remains valid as long as the vaccination schedule is respected, following the manufacturer's instructions: the test does not have to be repeated for every trip. And a dated fact that almost nobody publishes: from 1 January 2028, the laboratory report will have to include a code that allows its authenticity to be verified on the designated laboratory's website, and it will have to be attached to the health certificate.

The 90 days: the AFSCA counts them differently

This is the point where most trips go wrong, and in Belgium it has a twist of its own. The AFSCA writes, on its page for professionals:

«Si le titrage est effectué dans le pays tiers, le prélèvement sanguin doit être effectué au moins 90 jours avant le mouvement depuis ce pays tiers.» — read directly on favv-afsca.be on 29 August 2026.

That is: the AFSCA anchors the ninety days to the movement from the third country. Annex XXI of Delegated Regulation (EU) 2020/692, in its consolidated version of 22 April 2026, anchors them somewhere else: «not less than ninety days before the date of issue of the animal health certificate». They are not the same date, and the certificate's is always earlier than the flight's.

Our recommendation, and it is our own reading: count ninety calendar days from the sampling and do not issue the certificate before that. That way you meet both readings at once, the regulation's and that of the authority that will receive you. We develop it with the sources set against each other in our article on the ninety-day rule.

Two exceptions that are actually written down. No titration is required for mere transit through a territory or third country not listed in Annexes I and II of 2026/636, provided the owner supplies a signed declaration that during that transit the animal was not in contact with species susceptible to rabies and stayed confined in the means of transport. And the animal that resides in the Union and returns: if the titration was done before leaving and section VI of the passport is filled in by an authorised veterinarian, the test is valid immediately and there is no waiting time, neither on the way out nor on the way back.

If you come from the EU/EEA or a listed country (direct entry)

If your pet comes from an EU member state (or from Norway, Iceland, Liechtenstein, Switzerland), it travels with the European pet passport, without a blood test. If it comes from a listed third country in Annexes I or II of Implementing Regulation (EU) 2026/636: microchip, rabies vaccine (wait 21 days) and animal health certificate, without a blood test and without the 90 days.

Watch the asymmetry. The titration is waived by Annexes I and II; the mandatory point of entry is waived only by Annex I. Being in Annex II saves you the blood test and the wait, not the counter. And Belgium does not accept the EU exception for young animals: neither vaccinated animals under 15 weeks, nor unvaccinated animals under 12 weeks. From a non-listed third country the real minimum age is 7 months; from a listed one, 15 weeks.

Belgium is not on the list of destinations that require the treatment against Echinococcus multilocularis —those are Finland, Ireland, Malta, the United Kingdom (Northern Ireland) and Norway—, so it does not require it.

Authority and procedure in Belgium

Animal health is federal and is run by the AFSCA — Agence fédérale pour la sécurité de la chaîne alimentaire in French, Federaal Agentschap voor de veiligheid van de voedselketen (FAVV) in Dutch. It is the body that publishes the list of points of entry and that sets the ninety-day criterion. The check at the point of entry is carried out by customs, and the traveller must report to them with the animal. Animal welfare, by contrast, is a regional competence: Flanders, Brussels-Capital and Wallonia legislate separately, and the breed regime is municipal. To export from Belgium to a third country, the certificate is signed by an official veterinarian and the requirements depend on the destination. The European framework in force is Delegated Regulation (EU) 2026/131 and Implementing Regulations (EU) 2026/705 (certificate) and (EU) 2026/636 (country lists), under Regulation (EU) 2016/429. The general EU regime is explained in our European Union pillar page.

On the German-speaking Community we found no transfer of animal welfare, and the list of competences transferred from Wallonia does not include it. Our provisional conclusion, declared as provisional: the Walloon code applies.

Frequently asked questions

Does my country need the rabies blood test to enter Belgium?
It depends on your origin, because Belgium applies the EU regime. EU/EEA or country listed in Annexes I or II of Regulation (EU) 2026/636 (USA, Canada, Mexico, Argentina, Chile, Japan, Australia, United Kingdom, the Emirates, and since June 2026 Montenegro and Serbia): no. Non-listed country (Peru, Brazil, Colombia, China, Russia…): yes, titrage ≥0,5 UI/ml and a 90-day wait. Look it up in the table.
Which dog breeds are banned in Belgium?
None at national level. The arrêté ministériel of 21 October 1998, which named thirteen breeds, was annulled by the Council of State on 31 May 1999 (judgment no. 80.521) for a procedural defect, and it was never reinstated. Each municipality regulates on its own under article 135 §2, 6.º of the Nouvelle loi communale read with article 119. The list that affects you is that of the municipality where you are going to live.
Through which points can I enter Belgium from outside the EU?
Through seven, according to the official AFSCA list in its version 4 of 13 May 2026: Brussels Airport, Brussel-Zuid/Bruxelles-Midi (railway station), Internationale Luchthaven Antwerpen, Brussels South Charleroi Airport, Liège Airport, Internationale Luchthaven Oostende-Brugge and the port of Zeebrugge. For pet birds the list is reduced to two: Brussels Airport and Liège Airport. On arrival you have to report to customs with the animal.
From when are the 90 days of waiting counted in Belgium?
From the date the sample is taken, but there is a divergence about where they end. The AFSCA writes that the prélèvement must be done at least 90 days before the movement from the third country; Annex XXI of Delegated Regulation (EU) 2020/692, consolidated on 22 April 2026, says ninety days before the date of issue of the animal health certificate. The safe play is to count ninety calendar days from the sampling and not issue the certificate before that: that way both readings are met.
What do I have to do on arrival if I stay to live in Belgium?
Register the animal in DogID if it is a dog or in CatID if it is a cat, within 8 days, and only a veterinarian can do it. After that, what you are asked for depends on the region: in Flanders a cat born after 31 August 2014 must be sterilised; in Brussels cat identification has been compulsory since 1 March 2023 and a tattooed dog must be re-identified by microchip; in Wallonia cat identification has been compulsory since 1 November 2017. Several municipalities also charge a dog tax.
