Requirements to enter the European Union with your pet (2026): the rule changes depending on your country

To enter the European Union with your dog, cat or ferret, the European Commission (DG SANTE) classifies each country of origin. If you come from an EU/EEA country or a listed country (Annex I and II: United States, Canada, Mexico, Argentina, Chile, Japan, Malaysia, Australia, New Zealand, United Kingdom, the Emirates, Hong Kong, Taiwan, Singapore and, since June 2026, also Montenegro and Serbia), entry is direct: microchip, rabies vaccine and certificate, without a blood test. If you come from a NON-listed country —Brazil, Colombia, China, India, Russia, Belarus and almost all of Africa and Asia—, you also need a rabies blood test (RNATT) ≥0,5 UI/ml, with the sample taken at least 30 days after the vaccine and a 90-day (3-month) wait from the sample before travelling. From scratch, that path takes about 7 months. In all cases: microchip before the vaccine and, for dogs entering Finland, Ireland, Malta, Northern Ireland or Norway, tapeworm treatment 24-120 h beforehand.

Update note (Jun 2026). The EU pet regime changed recently: Delegated Regulation (EU) 2026/131 replaced the old 576/2013, and the country list (Regulation 2026/636) has already been amended in its first month in force: 2026/1228 (11 June 2026) added Montenegro and Serbia to Annex II. This page is classified with the current list; as the lists are adjusted, always confirm the consolidated version on EUR-Lex before travelling.

First step: locate your country

The EU does not treat every country the same. Find yours: the table tells you whether you get direct entry (EU/EEA or listed country) or the protocol with blood test and 3-month wait (non-listed country).

A verified fact that surprises: being "rich" or nearby does not guarantee being listed. Russia and Belarus are no longer on the lists (they need a blood test); and neither are South Korea, Uruguay or Kuwait. By contrast, Mexico, Argentina, Chile, Malaysia and —since June 2026— Montenegro and Serbia are. Check yours, don't assume it.

If your country is NOT listed (protocol with blood test)

This applies to Brazil, Colombia, Peru, China, India, Russia, Belarus, almost all of Africa and Asia. For your dog or cat to be able to enter, the path is:

  1. ISO microchip (11784/11785, 15 digits), implanted before the rabies vaccine. If the order is reversed, the vaccine does not count and the clock starts from zero.
  2. Rabies vaccine, with the animal at least 12 weeks old. You must wait 21 days after completing the primary vaccination.
  3. Rabies blood test (RNATT). The sample is taken at least 30 days after the vaccine and processed at an EU-designated laboratory; the result must be ≥0,5 UI/ml.
  4. Wait 90 days (3 months) counted from the sampling before entering the EU. (Does not apply to the re-entry of a pet that already had a valid RNATT before leaving the EU.)
  5. Official animal health certificate (model of Regulation 2026/705), signed by an official veterinarian.

The clock for non-listed countries. Microchip → vaccine (from 12 weeks) → wait 30 days → RNATT → wait 90 days → entry. From scratch it is about 7 months. The good news: once you have a favourable result, the RNATT is valid for life as long as the vaccine is kept up to date without expiring. In many origin countries there is no designated laboratory and the sample travels abroad; the EU does not set a fee (as a guide the test runs around USD 90 at some designated laboratories, confirm it).

If your country is in the EU/EEA or is listed (direct entry)

If your pet comes from an EU member state (or from Norway, Iceland, Liechtenstein, Switzerland), it travels with the European pet passport, without a blood test. If it comes from a listed country (Annex I or II: United States, Canada, Mexico, Argentina, Chile, Japan, Malaysia, Australia, New Zealand, United Kingdom —except Northern Ireland—, the Emirates, Hong Kong, Taiwan, Singapore, Mauritius, Montenegro, Serbia and others), the path is: microchip, rabies vaccine (wait 21 days) and animal health certificate. Without a blood test and without the 3-month wait.

Dogs: tapeworm treatment

Dogs only (not cats or ferrets). To enter Finland, Ireland, Malta, Northern Ireland and Norway, dogs need a treatment against the Echinococcus multilocularis tapeworm (praziquantel) administered by a veterinarian between 24 and 120 hours before entry, certified in the travel document. Without it, refusal at the border.

Document and point of entry

The pet travels with the animal health certificate (valid for 10 days from its issue until the point of entry, and then up to 6 months to move within the EU) or, from an Annex I territory, with a passport. Entry must be made through a Travellers' Point of Entry (TPE) designated by each country, where documents and identity are checked. The maximum is 5 animals per non-commercial movement. If you enter through a non-designated airport or port, the pet may be refused.

What about cats and ferrets?

The regime applies equally to dogs, cats and ferrets: microchip, rabies vaccine and, if the country is not listed, the blood test and the 3 months. The tapeworm treatment is only for dogs. The minimum age to vaccinate is 12 weeks, so a puppy from a non-listed country will not be able to enter until it completes the whole clock (~7 months).

Authority and regulations

Authority: European Commission — Directorate-General for Health and Food Safety (DG SANTE), portal food.ec.europa.eu. Regulations in force: Delegated Regulation (EU) 2026/131 (framework), Implementing Regulation (EU) 2026/636 (country lists, amended by 2026/1228) and 2026/705 (certificate model). The list of designated laboratories and the points of entry by country are published on the DG SANTE portal.

Frequently asked questions

Does my country need the rabies blood test for the EU?
If you come from the EU/EEA or a listed country (USA, Canada, Mexico, Argentina, Chile, Japan, Malaysia, Australia, NZ, United Kingdom, the Emirates, Hong Kong, Taiwan, Singapore, and since June 2026 Montenegro and Serbia), no. If you come from a non-listed country (Brazil, Colombia, Peru, China, India, Russia, Belarus…), yes. Look it up in the table.
How long does it take from a non-listed country?
About 7 months from scratch: microchip → vaccine (from 12 weeks) → 30 days → RNATT → 90-day wait → entry. The RNATT result is valid for life if the vaccine is kept current.
How much does the RNATT cost and where is it done?
At an EU-designated laboratory (the list changes; often the sample travels abroad). The EU does not set a fee; as a guide ~USD 90 at some laboratories. Confirm it before sending the sample.
Do dogs need extra treatment for any country?
Yes: for Finland, Ireland, Malta, Northern Ireland and Norway, dogs need tapeworm treatment (praziquantel) 24-120 h before entering. Does not apply to cats or ferrets.
Does it apply to cats and ferrets?
Yes, with the same requirements; the tapeworm treatment is only for dogs. Maximum 5 animals per non-commercial movement.
