Enter France with your pet (2026): the rule changes depending on your country — and France classifies dogs into 2 categories

To enter France with your dog, cat or ferret, the European Union regime comes first: depending on your country of origin, entry is direct (microchip, rabies vaccine and certificate) or it also requires a rabies blood test (titrage) ≥0,5 UI/ml and a 90-day wait. France's own layer is a two-category system of dangerous dogs: catégorie 1 (pit-bull type, Mastiff and Tosa without an LOF pedigree) is BANNED from import; catégorie 2 (the same types with LOF, plus the Rottweiler) can enter but with a permit, insurance, evaluation and muzzle. The microchip is registered in the I-CAD. Look up your country in the table and then review the French layer.

First step: locate your country of origin

To enter France from outside the EU, the first thing that decides your path is your country of origin. Find yours: the table tells you whether you get direct entry (EU/EEA or listed country) or the protocol with blood test and 3-month wait (non-listed country, like Peru).

A verified fact that surprises: being "rich" or nearby does not guarantee being listed. Russia and Belarus are no longer on the lists (they need a blood test); and neither are South Korea or Uruguay. By contrast, Mexico, Argentina, Chile and —since June 2026— Montenegro and Serbia are. Check yours, don't assume it.

What sets France apart: the two-category system

The European regime is only half the story. France does not ban or let everything through: it classifies certain dogs into two categories, and the difference between one and the other can be just the pedigree.

🚫 Catégorie 1 (chiens d'attaque): BANNED from import

These are dogs without a pedigree (without LOF) of the pit-bull type (American Staffordshire Terrier without LOF), Mastiff/Boerbull without LOF and Tosa without LOF. Since 6 January 1999 it has been prohibited to import, buy, sell or transfer a catégorie 1 dog; acquisition or import is punishable by 6 months in prison and a €15,000 fine (art. L.215-2 of the Code rural). They must be sterilised (veterinary certificate) and, in addition to the permis de détention, they are barred from public transport and most public places — they can only move on the voie publique, with a muzzle and leash.

Catégorie 2 (chiens de garde et de défense): CAN enter, but with conditions. These are the American Staffordshire with LOF, the Rottweiler (always, with or without LOF) and the Tosa with LOF. To keep one in France: permis de détention, évaluation comportementale, civil liability insurance, attestation d'aptitude (training) and, in public places, muzzle and leash. The key: the LOF pedigree decides whether your American Staffordshire (or your Tosa) is catégorie 1 (banned) or catégorie 2 (allowed).

Airports of entry in France

From outside the EU, your pet must enter through a designated travellers' point of entry. The main airports for receiving pets coming from third countries are:

  • Paris Charles de Gaulle / Roissy (CDG)
  • Paris Orly (ORY)
  • Nice Côte d'Azur (NCE)
  • Lyon Saint-Exupéry (LYS)

The veterinary border control is carried out by the SIVEP (Service d'inspection vétérinaire et phytosanitaire aux frontières, under the DGAL), and the Douanes do the document check: the traveller must spontaneously present their animal to the Douanes on arrival. Always confirm the current list of points of entry on the official websites (DGAL / Douanes) before buying the flight.

Register your pet on arrival in France

Your pet's microchip must be recorded in the I-CAD (Identification des Carnivores Domestiques), the French national file for the identification of dogs, cats and ferrets. If you settle in France, register or regularise your pet's identification in the I-CAD; several procedures (regularisation, import, supporting documents) can be done online.

If your country is NOT listed (protocol with blood test)

This applies to Peru, Brazil, Colombia, China, Russia, Belarus and almost all of Africa and Asia. For your dog or cat to be able to enter France, the path is:

  1. ISO microchip (11784/11785, 15 digits), implanted before the rabies vaccine. If the order is reversed, the vaccine does not count.
  2. Rabies vaccine, with the animal at least 12 weeks old; wait 21 days after the primary vaccination.
  3. Rabies blood test (titrage). The sample is taken at least 30 days after the vaccine and at most 3 months before entry, at an EU-designated laboratory; result ≥0,5 UI/ml.
  4. Wait 90 days (3 months) counted from the sampling before entering. (Does not apply to the re-entry of a pet that already had a valid titrage before leaving the EU.)
  5. Official animal health certificate (model of Regulation (EU) 2026/705), signed by an official veterinarian.

The clock for non-listed countries. Microchip → vaccine (from 12 weeks) → wait 30 days → titrage → wait 90 days → entry. From scratch it is about 7 months, and the minimum age of the animal will be 15 weeks at the very least (7 months if it comes from a country with rabies risk). The good news: with a favourable result, the titrage is valid for life as long as the vaccine is kept up to date.

If you come from the EU/EEA or a listed country (direct entry)

If your pet comes from an EU member state (or from Norway, Iceland, Liechtenstein, Switzerland), it travels with the European pet passport, without a blood test. If it comes from a listed country (United States, Canada, Mexico, Argentina, Chile, Japan, Australia, United Kingdom —except Northern Ireland—, the Emirates, Hong Kong, Taiwan, Singapore, Montenegro, Serbia and others): microchip, rabies vaccine (wait 21 days) and animal health certificate. Without a blood test and without the 3-month wait. (The catégorie 1 and 2 rules apply equally, wherever you come from.)

Authority and procedure in France

Authority: DGAL (Direction générale de l'alimentation) with the departmental DD(ec)PP; the veterinary border control is carried out by the SIVEP and the Douanes do the document check. To export a pet from France to a third country, the certificate is signed by an official veterinarian of the DD(ec)PP; the exact requirements depend on the destination country. The European framework in force is Delegated Regulation (EU) 2026/131 and Implementing Regulations (EU) 2026/705 (certificate) and (EU) 2026/636 (country lists), all under Regulation (EU) 2016/429. The general EU regime for entering from outside is explained in our European Union pillar page.

Frequently asked questions

Does my country need the rabies blood test to enter France?
It depends on your origin, because France applies the EU regime. EU/EEA or listed country (USA, Canada, Mexico, Argentina, Chile, Japan, Australia, United Kingdom, the Emirates, and since June 2026 Montenegro and Serbia): no. Non-listed country (Peru, Brazil, Colombia, China, Russia…): yes, titrage ≥0,5 UI/ml + 90 days. Look it up in the table.
Can I enter with a dangerous-breed dog?
It depends on the category. Catégorie 1 (pit-bull/Mastiff/Tosa WITHOUT LOF): banned from import (penalty 6 months + €15,000), mandatory sterilisation and barred access to transport and public places. Catégorie 2 (American Staffordshire WITH LOF, Rottweiler always, Tosa with LOF): allowed with permis de détention, evaluation, insurance, training and muzzle+leash. The LOF pedigree decides the category.
Through which airports can I enter from outside the EU?
Through a designated travellers' point of entry: mainly Paris Charles de Gaulle (CDG), Paris Orly (ORY), Nice (NCE) and Lyon Saint-Exupéry (LYS). The veterinary control is done by the SIVEP (DGAL) and the Douanes the document check; present your animal spontaneously to the Douanes.
Do I have to register my pet on arrival?
The microchip must be recorded in the I-CAD (Identification des Carnivores Domestiques), the French national file for dogs, cats and ferrets. Several procedures can be done online.
How long does it take from a non-listed country like Peru?
About 7 months from scratch: microchip → vaccine (from 12 weeks) → 30 days → titrage → 90-day wait → entry. Minimum age 15 weeks. A favourable titrage is valid for life if the vaccine is kept current.
