Requirements to enter the United Kingdom with your pet (2026): the rule changes depending on your country

To enter Great Britain (England, Wales and Scotland) with your pet, the United Kingdom places every country into one of two groups. If you come from a listed country —the entire European Union, the United States, Canada, Mexico, Argentina, Chile, Australia, New Zealand, Japan, Singapore, Switzerland, Norway, Iceland and others—, entry is simplified: microchip, rabies vaccine and a certificate, no blood test. If you come from an unlisted country —Brazil, Colombia, China, India, the Philippines, almost all of Africa and most of Asia—, you also need a rabies blood test (≥0.5 IU/ml) at an EU-approved laboratory and you must wait 3 months from the blood draw before travelling. In every case: microchip before the vaccine, wait 21 days after vaccinating, travel by an approved route, and, for dogs, tapeworm treatment between 24 hours and 5 days before arrival. Breaking the rules triggers up to 4 months of quarantine at your own cost. Northern Ireland is a separate scheme.

If your country is UNLISTED (blood-test protocol)

This applies to Brazil, Colombia, China, India, the Philippines, most of Asia and almost all of Africa. For your dog or cat to avoid quarantine, the path is:

  1. ISO microchip (11784/11785), implanted before the rabies vaccine.
  2. Rabies vaccine. You must wait at least 21 full days after the first vaccination before travelling.
  3. Rabies blood test. The sample is taken at least 30 days after the vaccine and sent to a laboratory approved by the European Union (the same official EU list). The result must be ≥0.5 IU/ml.
  4. Wait 3 months counted from the date the blood was drawn before travelling. The test stays valid as long as the vaccine is kept up to date.
  5. Tapeworm treatment (praziquantel) in dogs, between 24 hours and 5 days (120 h) before arriving in Great Britain.
  6. Travel document: the Great Britain pet health certificate (or an AHC issued in GB), with the microchip, vaccine and blood test details.
  7. Approved route: travel with an authorised carrier and via a route approved for pets.

Approved laboratories (serology)

The sample must be processed at a laboratory approved by the European Union (the official European Commission list, inside and outside the EU; the same one used by the UK and Japan). Always confirm that your chosen laboratory is on the current list before sending the sample.

If your country is LISTED (simplified entry)

If you come from a listed country —the entire EU, the United States, Canada, Mexico, Argentina, Chile, Australia, New Zealand, Japan, Singapore, Hong Kong, Taiwan, Switzerland, Norway, Iceland, United Arab Emirates and others—, the path is shorter: microchip, rabies vaccine (wait 21 days), tapeworm treatment in dogs, approved route and the appropriate document (EU pet passport, AHC or Great Britain pet health certificate). No blood test and no 3-month wait.

Special cases: cats from Australia also need a certificate from the Australian Department of Agriculture regarding the Hendra virus; dogs and cats from Peninsular Malaysia require an official certificate regarding the Nipah virus.

Two hard rules that stop trips

⚠ Quarantine of up to 4 months. If the rules are not met, the pet can be placed in rabies quarantine for up to 4 months —or refused entry if it arrived by sea—, with all costs borne by the owner.
⚠ The clock for unlisted countries. Between the vaccine, the blood test (30 days later) and the 3-month wait, the process from an unlisted country takes several months. If you already have your flight booked, start today.

Arrival and routes

The pet must enter via an approved route with an approved carrier. No approved route is needed if you travel within the United Kingdom or from Ireland. There are additional routes for guide and assistance dogs.

What about cats?

The scheme applies equally to dogs, cats and ferrets: microchip, rabies vaccine and, if you come from an unlisted country, the blood test and the 3 months. The tapeworm treatment is only for dogs. Remember the Hendra certificate for cats from Australia.

Frequently asked questions

Is my country listed or unlisted for the United Kingdom?
Listed countries include the entire EU, the USA, Canada, Mexico, Argentina, Chile, Australia, New Zealand, Japan, Singapore, Switzerland, Norway, Iceland and others. Unlisted ones include Brazil, Colombia, China, India, the Philippines, almost all of Africa and most of Asia. Find yours in the table.
What happens if I come from an unlisted country?
Microchip, rabies vaccine and a rabies blood test (≥0.5 IU/ml) at an EU-approved laboratory; the sample is taken ≥30 days after the vaccine and you wait 3 months from the blood draw. Without this, up to 4 months of quarantine or refusal of entry.
How long do you wait after the blood test?
3 months from the date the sample was drawn. The test stays valid as long as the rabies vaccine is kept up to date.
Do dogs need parasite treatment?
Yes, against tapeworm (praziquantel), between 24 hours and 5 days (120 h) before arrival. It does not apply to cats.
Does Northern Ireland have the same rules?
No. This guide covers Great Britain (England, Wales, Scotland). Northern Ireland is governed by a different scheme (DAERA).
Do these rules apply to cats?
Yes, the same as dogs and ferrets; tapeworm is only for dogs. Cats from Australia need a certificate regarding the Hendra virus.
