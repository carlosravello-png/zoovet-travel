Regulatory alert · SAG Chile

Chile changed the rules for bringing in your pet: identification is now mandatory, and two vaccine deadlines are in print

The new rule has applied since 27 July and repeals the 2021 one. Seven weeks later, WHO validated Chile for eliminating dog-transmitted rabies. These are two different stories, and only the first one changes your paperwork.

By Carlos Ravello · Veterinary review: Dr. Jessica Camacho García (DVM · CMVP 12434) · Published 6 October 2026 · Rules read in their official text the same day

⚠ What has applied since 27 July 2026

To enter Chile with a dog, a cat or a ferret, the rule is SAG Exempt Resolution No. 3.343 of 2026, amended by No. 5.444. The 2021 resolution (No. 6.508) has been repealed.

Your pet needs individual, permanent identification: an ISO microchip or a tattoo. The number must be the same one shown on the certificate.

The essentials: Chile has a new rule for bringing in pets. Four things change: permanent identification becomes mandatory; the clinical examination and the antiparasitic treatment are counted back from the issue of the certificate, not from arrival; and anyone leaving Chile with a pet can return on the export certificate for 60 calendar days instead of 21. The rabies vaccine is still mandatory. The WHO validation of 14 September changed none of these requirements.

What changed, exactly

Repealed rule (2021)Rule in force (2026)
IdentificationThe certificate had to show the “microchip number or any other verifiable method of identification”“Individually and permanently, by tattoo or microchip (in accordance with ISO standards 11784/11785)”
Clinical examination“Within the 10 days prior to entry bound for Chile”“Within the 10 days prior to the date of issue of the international veterinary certificate (CVI)”
Antiparasitic“Between 5 and 30 days prior to entry into Chile”“Between 5 and 30 days prior to the issue of the CVI”
Return to ChileThe resolution did not regulate it. For those who left before 27 July, the new rule speaks of “21 calendar days”“Within 60 calendar days” of the issue of the export certificate, with a valid vaccine and permanent identification. Does not apply to ferrets

Quotations in this article are our translation from the official Spanish text.

What did not change: the certificate —SAG now calls it the International Veterinary Certificate, CVI— is valid for 10 days from issue, extendable by 5. It must be in Spanish and in the language of the country of origin or, failing that, in English.

The change of reference point looks minor and is not. You used to count the examination and the antiparasitic from the day of arrival. Now you count from the day your country's authority signs the certificate, and since the treatment goes at least five days before that signature, it can no longer be left for the last week.

Two vaccine deadlines, both in print

This part needs slow reading. On the rabies vaccine there are two official Chilean texts in force, and they do not say the same thing.

The SAG resolution:

“First-time vaccinated animals, regardless of their age, must be vaccinated at least 21 days prior to entry into Chile.” — SAG Exempt Resolution No. 3.343 of 2026, point 1.3.2. Read on 6 October 2026. Our translation.

The Ministry of Health's rabies regulation:

“Vaccines administered a minimum of 30 days and a maximum of 12 months before the date of entry shall be considered valid.” — Decree No. 1 of the Ministry of Health, article 6. Enacted on 22 January 2013 and published on 29 January 2014. Read on leychile.cl on 6 October 2026; the record shows a single version. Our translation.

Twenty-one days in one, thirty in the other. The decree also sets a twelve-month ceiling that the resolution does not mention, and allows an antibody titration as an alternative that the resolution does not include either.

We are not going to tell you which one prevails. It is not for us to say, and we have not seen it settled in writing. What can be said is arithmetic: a vaccine given at least 30 days and no more than 12 months before arrival satisfies both texts at once. At Zoovet Travel, pets travel with all their vaccines up to date and within the required deadlines.

The SAG resolution adds a detail worth knowing: if your pet is not a first-time vaccinate, the certificate must report the last two doses. If it does not, the animal is treated as first-time vaccinated. And a vaccine given after the previous one had expired counts as a first dose.

What the 2026 rule does not contain

We read both resolutions in full and searched them word by word. Neither contains:

  • A 10-day home confinement on arrival. The word does not appear in the text.
  • A separate regime for puppies and kittens. The rule sets no minimum age of entry, and that does not mean any puppy gets in: the vaccine is required “regardless of their age”, with the same 21 days.

No fixed confinement does not mean nothing can happen at the border. If the animal arrives without meeting the requirements, SAG “may apply the sanitary measures established in the legislation in force, at any authorised point of entry into Chile […] at the cost of the person responsible for bringing in the animals”, in addition to any applicable penalties.

We say this because the confinement is still in circulation. The procedure page on SAG's website, consulted on 6 October 2026, still describes 10 days of confinement and gives Resolution No. 6.508 of 2021 as its legal basis. That resolution was repealed by point 7 of Resolution 3.343: “Exempt Resolution No. 6.508 of 2021 is hereby repealed”. The ChileAtiende page for the same procedure, updated on 27 July 2026, cites the new rule and does not mention confinement.

And the WHO validation: what does it change?

On 14 September 2026 WHO announced that Chile “has become the first country in South America to receive the World Health Organization (WHO) validation for eliminating dog-transmitted rabies as a public health problem”. Mexico was the first in the world, in 2019. Bhutan received its validation on 4 September 2026. According to the PAHO/WHO representative in Chile, Chile is the third.

The wording is worth reading in full, because the headlines shortened it. WHO itself explains that “dog-transmitted” refers to human rabies cases caused by the bite or scratch of an infected dog. The criterion is the absence of human deaths by that route for at least two years, plus proof that the country can prevent transmission from re-emerging. Chile's last reported case of this kind dates from 1972.

It does not say “rabies-free country”. Chile's Ministry of Health pointed it out two weeks later: bats “remain a reservoir that can infect dogs and cats” (our translation). That is why Chile keeps vaccinating, including in border areas.

For anyone travelling with a pet, as of today:

If you are going…What the validation changedVerified
To ChileNothing. The 2026 SAG resolutions apply, and they predate the announcement. We found no rule issued after 14 September that amends them6 October 2026
From Chile to the United StatesNothing. Chile is not on the CDC list of high-risk countries for dog rabies, dated 15 April 2026. Peru is6 October 2026
From Chile to CanadaNothing. Chile is not on the CFIA high-risk list. Peru is6 October 2026
From Chile to the European UnionNothing. Chile was already on the third-country list of Implementing Regulation (EU) 2026/636. Peru is not6 October 2026

Chile was already well classified before the validation. Each destination's lists are decided by its own authority on its own criteria, and none of the ones we read has moved.

What to do if Chile is in your plans

  • Identification first. ISO 11784/11785 microchip or tattoo, with the number matching the certificate.
  • A valid rabies vaccine, given at least 30 days and no more than 12 months before arrival: that satisfies both the resolution and the decree.
  • Internal and external antiparasitic between 5 and 30 days before the certificate is issued, not before the flight.
  • Clinical examination within the 10 days before that issue. The certificate is valid for 10 days, extendable by 5.
  • On arrival, the SAG Sworn Declaration. Every person over 18 entering Chile fills it in, and with animals the answer is “yes”. SAG states that the digital form can be accessed “up to one day before your arrival in Chile” (our translation), at www.ingresoachile.cl or through the QR codes at points of entry.
  • If you leave Chile and come back, you have 60 calendar days on the export certificate, with a valid vaccine and permanent identification.

Not verified by us

The resolution exempts from the vaccine those animals that have lived since birth, or for at least six continuous months, in a country free of dog-transmitted rabies, provided that this status “is recognised by Chile”. We did not find a published list of the countries Chile recognises. Until one appears, we will not guess who is on it.
