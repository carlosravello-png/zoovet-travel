Rule in force · European Union

Ninety days from the blood draw: how the EU rabies deadline is actually counted

Three months are not ninety days. Depending on the month, they are 89, 90, 91 or 92. For eleven years that difference did not matter, because three official documents counted to three different dates. Since 22 April 2026 there is one single count, and it is worth knowing which one before you buy the ticket.

By Carlos Ravello · Veterinary review: Dr Jessica Camacho García (DVM · CMVP 12434) · Published 28 August 2026 · Every regulation read on EUR-Lex and every official page read that same day

⚠ What changed on 22 April 2026

The rabies antibody test must now be taken at least ninety days before the date the certificate is signed — the certificate the animal enters the European Union with.

The old wording said «three months», and depending on which official paper you read, those three months were counted to the movement, to the signing of the certificate, or to the arrival. Three different answers to the same rule.

The short answer. Blood is drawn from your dog on a given day. From that day you count ninety days. Once the ninety are complete, the veterinarian may sign the certificate; not before. The flight comes after the signature, within the ten days the certificate is valid for.

The previous rule said «three months». And three calendar months are not ninety days: they are 89, 90, 91 or 92, depending on the month the blood was drawn. In fourteen of the twenty-four months of 2026 and 2027 they are 92. In February they are 89: one day short of what the rule now requires.

That is the whole difference. And it is why two equally honest veterinarians could count differently without either of them lying.

Start with the calendar, which is what people actually hold in their hands

Picture a dog leaving Lima for Madrid. His story is the usual one: microchip, rabies vaccination, and thirty days later the blood test that measures whether the vaccine worked. That test is the antibody titration — FAVN — and not just any laboratory can run it: it has to be one the European Union has designated.

Say the blood is drawn on 1 March 2026. Under today's rule, the count is a single line:

  • 1 March — the sample is taken. Day zero.
  • 30 May — ninety days are complete. From that day, the official veterinarian may sign the certificate.
  • 30 May to 9 June — the signed certificate is valid for ten days until the documentary checks at the point of entry into the Union. The flight has to fit inside that window.

Now the same blood under the previous rule. Three months from 1 March is 1 June: 92 days, because March has 31, April 30 and May 31. Two days more than ninety.

Who cares about two days? Anyone who has already bought the ticket. Because for eleven years, while the regulation said the three months were counted to the movement, a veterinarian could sign the certificate on 28 May — day 88 — for a flight on 1 June, and be in compliance. Under today's wording, that same certificate signed on day 88 is not valid: the count ends at the signature, not at take-off.

And the reverse case, which surprises people more. For a dog whose blood is drawn on 1 February 2027, three months lands on 1 May — only 89 days. Today that is not enough: his certificate cannot be signed until 2 May. «Three months» is not always more generous than ninety days. In February it is one day shorter.

There used to be three answers, and all three were signed

This is the part we have not seen told anywhere, and it is the reason half a continent still counts in different ways without anyone being entirely wrong.

Until 21 April 2026, the same rule was written in three official documents. With three different endings.

One. The regulation. Regulation (EU) 576/2013, Annex IV, in its 2024 consolidated version:

«must be carried out on a sample collected at least 30 days after the date of vaccination and: (i) not less than three months before the date of: — the non-commercial movement from a territory or a third country other than those listed…»Regulation (EU) 576/2013, Annex IV, point 2(a)(i)

Three months to the journey.

Two. The box on the certificate the veterinarian signed, in the model set by Regulation (EU) 2019/1293:

«…a minimum of 30 days after the previous vaccination and at least three months prior to the date of issue of this certificate»Certificate model, box II.3.1

Three months to the signature.

Three. The footnote on that same certificate, about the same test, two paragraphs further down:

«…at least 30 days after the date of vaccination and three months before the date of import»Certificate model, footnote 8

Three months to the arrival.

The same form counted itself in two different ways. And the regulation that mandated it, in a third.

And this is not ancient history: it downloads today

The Colombian agricultural institute (ICA) publishes right now, at an address literally called anexo-iv-vigente-union-europea — «annex IV in force» — the model SANTE/7013/2016-EN ANNEX Rev. 1. We downloaded it and counted: twenty-eight mentions of 576/2013, fifteen of 577/2013 and not one 2026 regulation. Inside it carries both of the sentences that disagree with each other.

You do not need EUR-Lex to see the contradiction. It is in the paper an exporter downloads today.

Before we go on: this may not apply to you

The blood test is not required of everyone. Article 17 of Delegated Regulation (EU) 2026/131 exempts the animal if its country is in Annex I or Annex II to Implementing Regulation (EU) 2026/636.

  • Annex I — ten entries, none of them American: Andorra, Switzerland, the Faroe Islands, Gibraltar, Greenland, Iceland, Liechtenstein, Monaco, San Marino and the Vatican.
  • Annex II — forty-seven. From the Americas: Argentina, Chile, Mexico, the United States with Puerto Rico, the US Virgin Islands, Guam, American Samoa and the Northern Mariana Islands, and much of the Caribbean.

Peru, Colombia, Ecuador, Bolivia and Brazil are on neither list. The test applies to them, which is why this article exists.

But being on the list is not enough either. The same Article 17 sets a routing condition: the animal must come directly, or have stayed exclusively in listed countries; and if it passed through one that is not listed, a signed owner's declaration is required stating that it had no contact with susceptible animals there. A Chilean dog that spent two months in Peru is not covered by the exemption, even though Chile is in Annex II.

And so nobody panics: the list did not move when the framework changed. We compared it code by code against the last consolidated version of the previous regulation and they are the same countries, plus two that joined on 11 June 2026 — Montenegro and Serbia. Nobody was taken off.

What it says today, word for word

The sentence in force lives in point 1(a) of Annex XXI to Delegated Regulation (EU) 2020/692:

«be carried out without undue delay after sample collection on a sample collected by an official veterinarian or an authorised veterinarian as defined in Article 2(1) of Delegated Regulation (EU) 2026/131 at least 30 days after the date of the primary vaccination, or within a current valid vaccination series, and not less than 90 days before the date of issue of the animal health certificate accompanying the consignment of animals to the Union»Point 1(a) of Annex XXI to Delegated Regulation (EU) 2020/692, consolidated version of 22 April 2026

Four things changed, and it is worth separating them because they get mixed up:

  • The number. From «three months» to «ninety days». A calendar interval becomes a fixed number.
  • The end of the count. Of the three previous answers, the strictest one survived: to the issue of the certificate, not to the journey.
  • Who draws the blood. It used to say «authorised veterinarian». It now says «an official veterinarian or an authorised veterinarian».
  • Which vaccination starts the clock. Before, «the date of vaccination». Now, «the date of the primary vaccination, or within a current valid vaccination series». It is more precise, and it settles a frequent argument: an animal revaccinated on time does not start over.

Two more things from the same annex worth knowing. First: the test does not have to be repeated if the animal is revaccinated within the validity period of the previous vaccination. That was already in the footnote of the old certificate; what changed is that it moved up from a footnote to the body of the regulation. Second: from 1 January 2028, laboratory reports will carry a code for verifying their authenticity on the laboratory's own website.

Why this sentence is so hard to find

Here is the part that explains why so many serious people get it wrong. If you open the pet regulation — 2026/131 — looking for the deadline, it is not there. What you find is Article 14(c), which says the test is carried out «in accordance with point 1 of Annex XXI to Delegated Regulation (EU) 2020/692».

In other words: the regulation governing pets travelling with their owners does not legislate this deadline. It inherits it, by cross-reference, from an annex to the regulation on commercial consignments. And that annex is not the one that used to be there either: Delegated Regulation (EU) 2026/135 replaced it in full, in a single line — «Annex XXI is replaced by the text in the Annex to this Regulation». And in May, a third regulation amended it again.

Four document hops to reach one sentence. Anyone can fall off along the way.

The detail that clinches it

In the whole of Regulation 2026/131, the words «90 days» appear exactly once. And it is in Article 20(b)(ii), to say this:

«The 90-day period required for the validity of the titration test as provided for in point 1 of Annex XXI to Delegated Regulation (EU) 2020/692 shall not apply in this case.»Delegated Regulation (EU) 2026/131, Article 20(b)(ii)

The only time the pet regulation names the deadline is to say who it does not apply to. It never states it.

And we fell for it too

While preparing this article we took it as verified that there were no later amendments… by looking at the April consolidated version, which by construction cannot contain anything from May. Delegated Regulation (EU) 2026/1052 of 12 May then turned up, amending that same Annex XXI — point 2, on Echinococcus treatment, not point 1 with the deadline — and as of today EUR-Lex has still not folded it into the consolidated text.

The number did not move. But we got it right by luck, not by method, and we say so because anyone can repeat the check and find the same thing.

Of fourteen administrations, two write both the figure and the anchor the way the rule writes them today. Germany and Sweden. We did not read the other thirteen Member States; we say so plainly so that nobody reads more into this than there is.

Sweden goes further and puts it in a section heading: «90 dagars väntetid innan hälsointyget får utfärdas» — ninety days of waiting before the health certificate may be issued — and adds a sentence nobody else does: «and that we cannot grant an exemption from».

And here is the finding that dismantles the easy explanation. Belgium and Austria are the two that cite the new framework most — twenty-five and thirty-five mentions — and both keep the old anchor. It is not that they did not read it. It is that the preposition is invisible even when it is right in front of you.

The Danish case, which is the one to watch

Denmark is the only one that gets the end of the count right — the issue of the certificate — while keeping the old figure, three months. Which in most months is more than ninety days, so anyone following Denmark will not fall short. Except in February.

We mention it because it illustrates the one piece of practical advice in this whole article: when two official sources disagree, follow the longer one. Nobody rejects an animal for having waited two extra days.

Costa Rica explains the whole mechanism of this lag, which is why we single it out. The certificates it hands out are the literal pages of the EU Official Journal, with «COSTA RICA» typed into the boxes: the new model, with «ninety days». But the two guides explaining how to use them are its own, from 2019 and 2020, and they do not even agree with each other: one says «3 months» and the other «90 days to the date of departure».

The form updates itself, because it is copied. The guide does not, because it is written. That, in one line, is why half the world still publishes the old figure.

What is not settled

We are not going to pretend this is closed.

  • The Union itself does not speak with one voice. Twelve of the fourteen administrations we read publish something today that differs from the text in force. These are not abandoned pages: they are the official pages a citizen lands on.
  • Nobody corrects backwards. Authorities publish the update and move on; what came before stays online for whoever looks. That is not negligence, it is how public portals work, and you have to plan around it.
  • We did not read thirteen Member States, and we could not read Guatemala. What we did not check, we do not assert.
  • During the transition two legal papers coexist with two wordings of the same deadline. The old certificate model may be issued until 1 October 2026 and used until 31 March 2027.

So, and this is the only thing we give as advice: count ninety full days from the sampling, and have the certificate signed after that. If your destination authority says three months, count three months. Always take the higher of the two numbers. It costs a few days of waiting and it avoids the one outcome that cannot be fixed, which is an animal held at the border.

Questions we get every week

From which day are the ninety days counted?
From the day the blood sample was taken. Not from the vaccination date, and not from the day the laboratory report arrived. The date that governs is the date of sampling, and it is the one written on the report.
Do the ninety days end on the flight date or on the date the certificate is signed?
On the date the certificate is signed. The rule says «not less than 90 days before the date of issue of the animal health certificate». The flight comes afterwards, within the ten days the certificate remains valid until the entry checks.
Are three months and ninety days not the same thing?
Not always. Three calendar months are 89, 90, 91 or 92 days depending on the month they start in. In fourteen of the twenty-four months of 2026 and 2027 they are 92. In February they are 89, one day short of the ninety the rule now requires.
Does this test apply to my country?
Not if it is listed in Annex I or Annex II to Implementing Regulation (EU) 2026/636 and the animal comes directly or has stayed exclusively in listed countries. From the Americas the list includes Argentina, Chile, Mexico, the United States with its listed territories and much of the Caribbean. Peru, Colombia, Ecuador, Bolivia and Brazil are not listed: the test does apply to them.
Does the test have to be repeated every year?
No, provided the animal is revaccinated against rabies within the validity period of the previous vaccination. If the vaccination is allowed to lapse, the count starts again: the test must be repeated and another ninety days must pass.

The fact we close on

On 13 April 2026, nine days before all of this took effect, the European Commission Representation in Spain published a piece to clarify the change. To the question «will the rules change?» it answered: «yes, but not radically». It mentions the new regulation once. It does not mention the deadline. It does not use the word «rabies» a single time.

When the same point escapes a ministerial note updated on the day of the change, the clarification the Commission publishes that week, twelve of the fourteen European administrations we could read and — as we said — ourselves for a week, the problem stops being with whoever is telling it.

The old rule gave three answers. The new one gives one. What is missing is for it to reach the papers where people actually read it. And that, for now, moves slower than the law.

Notice and method. This article explains what the rule in force says and how it differs from the previous one. It does not replace the official source, it is not legal advice and it does not tell anyone what to do in their own case: the specific requirements are set by the health authority of the country of departure and by that of the Member State of entry.
