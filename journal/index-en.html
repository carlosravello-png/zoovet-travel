Regulatory alerts and analysis

When a rule changes, your trip changes. Here we follow the authorities that decide whether your dog or cat may cross a border —CDC, MPI, DG SANTE, APHA, SENASA and the rest— and explain what each change means for the people who actually have to travel. Every alert is verified against the official source and carries the date on which it was checked.

Standing rule · AQS Japan

Japan does not accept certain microchips, nor RNA vaccines. It has not accepted them for years

It is not a new rule. It is a standing rule that nobody explains. Microchips beginning with “900 202” have not been accepted since April 2023; duplicates have been found in seven other series; RNA rabies vaccines are rejected; and the first vaccination must be given at 91 days, not 84.

Regulatory alert · AQS Japan · CDC

Japan revoked a laboratory for breaching its standards. The CDC still accepts it

The AQS removed Biobest Laboratories from its list of designated laboratories for violations of the designation standards. The same laboratory appears today on the CDC's approved list. Both lists are official. They do not agree.

11 July 2026 · By Carlos Ravello · Veterinary review: Dr. Jessica Camacho · Read the alert →

Regulatory alert · CDC

Your dog's rabies titre may stop counting: the CDC removed laboratories and the deadline is 12 July

The CDC dropped several laboratories from its approved list in January. Behind that date sits a permanent rule the agency states but never explains: a rabies titre is valid for life… only as long as the laboratory stays approved.

11 July 2026 · By Carlos Ravello · Veterinary review: Dr. Jessica Camacho · Read the alert →
Regulatory alert · MPI New Zealand

New Zealand's new standard: if you travel after April 2027, your countdown starts on 1 October

The new Import Health Standard came into force on 1 July. It introduces an official identity check that must be done six months before export. Anyone planning to travel from April 2027 has to start this October.
