Regulatory alert · CDC

Your dog's rabies titre may stop counting: the CDC removed laboratories and the deadline is 12 July

Behind a date almost nobody noticed sits a permanent rule that the CDC states but never explains: your rabies titre is valid for life… only as long as your laboratory stays approved.

By Carlos Ravello · Veterinary review: Dr. Jessica Camacho García (DVM · CMVP 12434) · Published 11 July 2026 · Sources verified the same day

⚠ Urgent notice — expires tomorrow

If your dog's rabies blood test was performed at one of the laboratories the CDC removed from its list on 12 January 2026, that result is only accepted if the dog enters the United States on or before 12 July 2026.

From 13 July 2026, that test no longer counts. It must be repeated at an approved laboratory, or the dog will have to serve a 28-day quarantine at a CDC-registered animal care facility.

The rule, in one sentence: a rabies antibody titre accepted by the CDC is valid for the life of the dog —as long as vaccination coverage never lapses— and as long as the laboratory that performed it remains approved by the CDC. If the CDC drops that laboratory from its list, the test stops counting, even if it is correct and fully paid for.

What happened

On 12 January 2026, the CDC updated its list of laboratories approved to perform the rabies antibody test (the serology titre) for dogs entering the United States from countries at high risk for dog rabies. In that update it removed several laboratories from the list.

The CDC did publish it. But it published it as a footnote to a table, in English, without explaining the consequence. And the consequence is serious: anyone whose dog was tested at one of those laboratories is holding a piece of paper that, from tomorrow, is worthless.

Note the Japanese case: the laboratory removed is the one belonging to Japan's own government animal quarantine service. A laboratory being official in its own country does not mean the CDC accepts it.

The CDC's exact condition

The CDC will still accept results from the removed laboratories only if both conditions are met at once:

  • The blood sample was drawn on or before 12 January 2026, and
  • The dog is imported into the United States on or before 12 July 2026.

If either fails, a new result must be obtained from a laboratory that is currently approved.

“Test results are valid for the life of the dog as long as vaccination coverage does not lapse and as long as the laboratory remains approved.” — Centers for Disease Control and Prevention, Approved Rabies Serology Laboratories for Testing Dogs

Why this matters beyond tomorrow

The 12 July date is only the episode. What really matters is the rule that episode exposes — a rule that will still be alive tomorrow, next month and next year:

A rabies titre is not yours forever. It depends on a third party —the CDC— continuing to consider the laboratory that produced it valid. And the CDC updates that list whenever it decides to, without prior notice, and without telling you.

That has a practical consequence worth keeping in mind when planning a trip: choosing the laboratory is not a minor formality. A laboratory with a long, stable record on the CDC list is safer than a recently added one, even if the price is better. And if your test was done a while ago, it is worth checking —before buying the ticket— that the laboratory is still on the current list.

How to know whether your test still counts

  • Look at who ran the test. The laboratory's name is on the results report.
  • Check it against the CDC's current list (Table 1 on the official page, linked below). If your laboratory is there, your result counts.
  • If it isn't, and your dog did not enter the U.S. before 12 July 2026, the test is no longer valid: it must be repeated at an approved laboratory, or the dog enters with a 28-day quarantine at a CDC-registered animal care facility (reservation required in advance, and all costs borne by the owner).

The U.S. laboratories that remain approved

Verified against the CDC's official list on 11 July 2026: Kansas State University (KSVDL), Auburn University (College of Veterinary Medicine) and the University of Missouri Veterinary Medical Diagnostic Laboratory. There is also a Department of Defense laboratory, available only to U.S. military personnel and eligible beneficiaries.

The deadlines that did not change

  • The microchip must be implanted before the rabies vaccination.
  • The blood sample must be drawn at least 30 days after the first valid rabies vaccination.
  • And at least 28 days before the date of entry into the United States.
  • The CDC itself recommends drawing the sample 60 days or more before travel, because laboratory turnaround times vary.
  • Results must be in English or accompanied by a certified translation, and must come directly from the approved laboratory, not from a third party forwarding them.
