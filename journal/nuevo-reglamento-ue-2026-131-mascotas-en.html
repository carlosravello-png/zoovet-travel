Regulatory alert · European Union

The EU pet regulation changed on 22 April: your deadlines stand, your paperwork doesn't

Among the things that changed there is a new document: the authorisation for someone else to bring the animal now has an official European template. Read closely, it exposes an old rule that almost nobody applies correctly — and that can end with the animal entering as goods.

By Carlos Ravello · Veterinary review: Dr. Jessica Camacho García (DVM · CMVP 12434) · Published 4 August 2026 · Legislation checked in EUR-Lex and the Spanish BOE that same day

⚠ What changed on 22 April 2026

From that date, Commission Delegated Regulation (EU) 2026/131 applies. The owner's written authorisation — what in Latin America is called a carta poder — now has an official European template: Part 1 of Annex V to Implementing Regulation (EU) 2026/705.

A generic power of attorney drafted by a lawyer is not automatically that document.

The rule, in one sentence: the written authorisation lets a third party carry the animal, but it does not replace the owner's own journey. If the owner does not travel within a window of five days, the movement stops being a «non-commercial movement» and falls under the import regime. That brings customs clearance, and import VAT is due unless the relief for transfer of residence applies. It affects every origin, and especially the countries the European Union does not list: Peru, Bolivia, Colombia, Ecuador and Brazil, among others.

The regulation was published on 27 March and applies from 22 April. We are writing about it now, with real cases behind us: what a regulation says and how it behaves at a counter are two different things.

What changed, exactly

The framework that governed this since 2013 — Regulation (EU) 576/2013 — stopped applying on 21 April 2026. It was not a surprise repeal. It had been written ten years earlier into Article 277 of Regulation (EU) 2016/429, which said that 576/2013 «shall continue to apply until 21 April 2026». The handover was exact: the old one until the 21st, the new one from the 22nd. Not a single day of gap.

What almost nobody has reported is that the new framework is not one regulation. It is four.

Delegated Regulation 2026/131 also repealed, in its Article 32, Delegated Regulation (EU) 2018/772 — the one that governed the treatment against Echinococcus multilocularis. The treatment window did not change: still no more than 120 hours and no less than 24 before entry. What changed is the rule that orders it, not the deadline.

Four months, one corrigendum

On 27 July 2026 the Official Journal published a corrigendum to 2026/131 (OJ L 2026/90625). It corrects Article 33, first paragraph: where it said the Regulation would enter into force «on the twentieth day following that of its publication», it should read «on the day following that of its publication».

It does not touch the date of application. The second paragraph of the same Article 33 — «it shall apply from 22 April 2026» — stands, and with it the repeal of 2018/772 and the treatment window. What changed is when the regulation existed, not from when it binds.

And a list that has already moved

Implementing Regulation 2026/636, the one with the country lists, was published on 27 March and already has a consolidated version dated 19 June. Three months. Anyone copying that list without recording the date they consulted it is publishing something with an invisible expiry date.

As of today, verified against that consolidated version: Annex II includes Chile and Argentina. It does not include Peru, Bolivia, Colombia, Ecuador or Brazil. For those, the rabies antibody titration (rabies serology) in an EU-designated laboratory and the three-month waiting period are still required.

The five-day rule

Here is the problem, and it is not new: it comes from Article 245(2) of Regulation (EU) 2016/429, which reads, verbatim:

«Where the non-commercial movement of a pet animal is carried out by an authorised person, that movement shall only take place within a period of five days from the movement of the owner of the pet animal.» — Regulation (EU) 2016/429, Article 245(2). Consulted 4 August 2026.

Paragraph 3 of the same article further empowers the Commission to regulate «the granting of derogations in relation to the time period». The window admits exceptions by design; it is not a wall.

The Spanish Ministry of Agriculture puts it without any room left:

«The animals may be moved in a means of transport separate from the owner's if this occurs within a period of no more than five days relative to the owner's movement. That is, in all cases the owner must make the same journey as the animal within those five days before or after.» — Ministerio de Agricultura, Pesca y Alimentación, Viajar con la mascota. Perros, gatos, hurones. Consulted 4 August 2026. Our translation.

And the sentence that shuts the door, on the same page: «If the movement of your animal or animals does not correspond to that of pet animals, then it is a commercial movement».

It is worth noting that the two wordings do not say quite the same thing. The Regulation says «from the movement of the owner». The Ministry, explaining it, says «before or after». One is a symmetric window and the other is not. We come back to this at the end.

Read it again. Neither version says the animal has to travel accompanied. Both say the owner has to travel. The authorised person settles who sits next to the crate. It does not settle the owner's absence.

The case we see at the counter is always the same, and always with the best intentions: someone emigrated eight months ago, is settled, and asks a sister to send the dog over. They sign a power of attorney before a notary, get it apostilled, do everything right. And that impeccable paperwork does not fit the category, because they are not travelling again.

Why it is then taxed

Reclassified as a commercial movement, the animal stops entering through a travellers' point of entry and moves into the import circuit: registered establishment of origin, commercial animal health certificate, prior notification in TRACES, entry through a Border Control Post and customs clearance.

At customs, an animal is goods. And Article 17 of the Spanish VAT Act (Ley 37/1992) leaves no margin: «imports of goods shall be subject to the tax, whatever their purpose and whatever the status of the importer». The general rate is 21% (Article 90.One). The base is the customs value; absent an invoice, the administration estimates it.

One thing here is worth saying, because it usually is not: this rule was not written to trap anyone. The five-day window was born as a concession, not a restriction. Recital 10 of Regulation (EU) 576/2013 justified it like this: «experience shows that during such movements it is not always possible for the pet animal to be with its owner or the authorised person at all times». That is why it allows, verbatim, «duly justified and documented reasons». It was written in 2013 and survived the 2026 change of framework untouched.

The relief that does exist, and why it probably does not reach you

There is an exemption designed for exactly this, and it is a good one. Council Regulation (EC) 1186/2009, in Article 2(1)(c), states verbatim that personal property includes «domestic animals and saddle animals». Your dog is, in customs law, personal property just as your sofa is.

But that relief hangs on a transfer of residence, not on an animal. The conditions are cumulative:

  • That you are the one transferring your normal residence to the Union (Art. 3).
  • Having had your residence outside the EU for at least 12 consecutive months (Art. 5(1), and Art. 28.Two.1 of Ley 37/1992).
  • Having lived with the animal for at least six months before leaving the previous residence (Art. 4(a)).
  • Declaring it for free circulation within 12 months of establishing residence (Art. 7(1)).

Here is the fine detail worth knowing before arguing at a desk: the European customs regulation names domestic animals. Article 29 of the Spanish VAT Act does not. It uses a general formula — goods «normally intended for the personal use of the person concerned or of those living with them» — which a pet fits, but by generic definition. In the customs relief the animal is named; in the VAT exemption it is merely covered.

Whoever sends the dog ahead without travelling themselves falls outside both doors at once. Outside the sanitary one, because there is no owner's movement from which to count five days. And outside the relief, because they did not transfer their residence: they transferred it eight months ago, and arrived without the dog.

What did not change

  • Non-commercial movement still allows a maximum of five animals, with documented exceptions for competitions, exhibitions and sporting events in animals over six months old.
  • There still cannot be a transfer of ownership. An international adoption is commercial by definition, even if nothing is paid for the animal.
  • The microchip before the vaccine, the vaccine before the blood draw, and the three-month wait for unlisted countries: unchanged.
  • The anti-Echinococcus treatment window: 24 to 120 hours, same as before.

How it is avoided

Two paths, and only two.

The owner travels. This is the clean route. It is enough for the owner to make their own journey within the five-day window — before or after the animal — and for the written authorisation, using the Annex V template, to accompany the identification document. They do not need to be on the same flight or even the same day.

Or fit the entry into a transfer of residence. If the owner has already emigrated, the only route to no taxation is the exemption in Article 28 of Ley 37/1992, and the documentation has to be prepared beforehand, not at the airport: consular deregistration, municipal registration, and something proving the animal lived with you six months before you left. A veterinary record with dates works. The twelve-month deadline runs from when you settled, not from when you decided to bring the animal.

If neither fits, it is better to know before buying the ticket, not after.

With the truth up front

After walking several of these cases through, what we suspect is that the rule does not prevent a clean solution. What prevents it is arriving at the desk with a version that does not hold up.

If the animal is travelling for commercial purposes, do not dress it up. And if your pet is your pet, say so too — and bring the papers that prove it. The law works the same for both cases.

And it is worth knowing where the real wall is: the commercial channel requires a registered establishment of origin and notification in TRACES. A private individual has neither. It is not that it costs more: it is that they cannot. A shortcut to skip a procedure ends in a wait nobody planned, or in an animal held at a post that was not prepared to receive it. No saving pays for that.

The useful question, then, is not how much it costs. It is which of the two channels your case falls into, and what can be proven about the dates that have already happened.

What we still do not know

This is not closed and we are not going to pretend otherwise.

  • There is no binding ruling from the Spanish Directorate-General for Taxation on applying Article 28 to a pet animal brought in by an authorised person. The published rulings deal with removals in general.
  • We have not located public files of specific assessments issued by Spanish customs on pets brought in by third parties. The conclusion rests on the legal framework, not on documented cases. Criteria may differ between customs offices.
  • The enacting text says one thing and the EU's own guidance says another. The five official language versions we have read agree on a one-directional window: «from» in English, «a partir del» in Spanish, «suivant» in French, «nach» in German and «binnen vijf dagen na» in Dutch. The European Commission's own travel page says something else: «the owner must travel either five days before or after the pet animal». The Spanish ministry says the same, and so does the Belgian federal agency. This is not one ministry's oversight: it is the enacting text on one side and the guidance on the other, and we have not found the provision that would support the second. Until it is settled, only one reading satisfies both: the animal travels after the owner, never before.
  • We have not confirmed whether Implementing Regulation (EU) 2018/878 — the list of Member States with Echinococcus-free status — is still in force or was replaced. Being an implementing act, Article 32 of 2026/131 does not touch it.
