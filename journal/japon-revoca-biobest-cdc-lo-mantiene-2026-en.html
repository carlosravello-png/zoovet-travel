Regulatory alert · AQS Japan · CDC

Japan revoked a laboratory for breaching its standards. The CDC still accepts it

The Animal Quarantine Service removed Biobest Laboratories Ltd. from its list of designated laboratories due to violations of the designation standards. The same laboratory is on the CDC's approved list today. Both lists are official. Both are published. They do not agree.

By Carlos Ravello · Veterinary review: Dr. Jessica Camacho García (DVM · CMVP 12434) · Published 11 July 2026 · Both sources verified that same day

⚠ If your rabies titre was done at Biobest

Japan's Animal Quarantine Service (AQS) removed Biobest Laboratories Ltd. (United Kingdom) from its list of designated laboratories due to violations of the designation standards.

Holders of certificates issued between 27 November 2024 and 25 June 2025 must contact the AQS office at their port of entry. The MAFF states that it is working with the United Kingdom authorities to confirm the situation.

The fact, in one sentence: the same laboratory Japan revoked for breaching its standards appeared, on 11 July 2026, on the list of laboratories approved by the United States CDC. Both lists are official. Both are published. They do not agree.

What each authority says

This is not an interpretation. These are two official pages, consulted on the same day, saying incompatible things about the same laboratory.

Why this matters even if your dog is not going to Japan

There is a CDC rule we have already explained in this Journal, and here it returns wearing a different face. A rabies titre accepted by the CDC is valid for the life of the dog —as long as vaccination coverage never lapses— and as long as the laboratory that performed it remains approved.

That condition is not theoretical. On 12 January 2026, the CDC removed laboratories in Brazil, China, Japan, Russia, South Korea and Tunisia from its list. With them, tests that were correct, paid for and properly done stopped counting.

So the question is not whether Biobest is a good laboratory. The question is what happens to the tests that came out of it if the CDC decides tomorrow what Japan decided yesterday. That risk exists today, it is asymmetric, and it falls entirely on the dog's owner.

What to do, depending on your case

  • If you are travelling to Japan and your titre report carries the Biobest name: contact the AQS office at your port of entry. That is what the agency itself asks for. The AQS has stated that it will inform individually those who have already submitted the import notification.
  • If you are travelling to the United States and your report carries that name: as of today the laboratory remains approved by the CDC and the result is valid. But check Table 1 before you buy the ticket, not after.
  • If you have not had the test done yet: choose the laboratory by looking at its record on the list, not at its price. A stable laboratory protects the investment. A newly added one —or one just questioned by another authority— does not.

What this case exposes

The rabies titration system rests on a premise nobody states out loud: that health authorities assess the same laboratories against the same criteria. They do not.

Japan, the United States and the European Union keep three independent lists, with their own procedures, their own reviews and their own timetables. A laboratory can be on one and not on another. It can be revoked by one and remain approved by the rest. And the dog's owner —who paid, waited and complied— finds out when there is nothing left to do.

We are not claiming the CDC should remove Biobest. We do not hold the files of either agency, and we are not going to assume what we cannot verify. We are stating something simpler and more uncomfortable: one health authority revoked this laboratory for breaching its standards and another keeps it approved, and neither of them explains why.

We publish both sources below. Anyone can check them in five minutes.

Update — 13 July 2026. We have now checked the European list, which this alert flagged as pending: Biobest's designation in the European Union expired on 9 February 2026. And the United Kingdom, the country where the laboratory sits, does not list it either: its official register, updated on 3 February 2026, contains a single laboratory. Four authorities, four answers. The full story is in There is no single list of rabies serology laboratories.
