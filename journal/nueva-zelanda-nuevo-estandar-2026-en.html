New Zealand's new standard: if you travel after April 2027, your countdown starts on 1 October

The new Import Health Standard came into force on 1 July. It brings an official identity check that must be done six months before export — and that moves the clock much further back than people realise.

By Carlos Ravello · Veterinary review: Dr. Jessica Camacho García (DVM · CMVP 12434) · Published 11 July 2026 · Source verified the same day

⚠ Countdown — under three months left

If you plan to take your dog or cat to New Zealand on or after 1 April 2027, your animal will need an official identity check performed at least six months before departure.

That means the clock starts on 1 October 2026. Anyone who finds out in March 2027 will not make it.

The essentials: the Ministry for Primary Industries (MPI) issued a new Import Health Standard for cats and dogs (CATSDOGS.GEN) on 12 May 2026, which came into force on 1 July 2026 with a nine-month transition period. During that transition you may travel under the old (2021) or the new (2026) standard. But from 1 April 2027 the new one is mandatory — and it requires an official identity check carried out at least six months before export. That is why MPI warns that from 1 October 2026 those checks already become necessary.

The official timeline

DateWhat happens
12 May 2026MPI issues the new Import Health Standard (CATSDOGS.GEN).
1 July 2026It comes into force, with a nine-month transition period. Either the 2021 or the 2026 standard may be used.
1 October 2026Pre-export identity checks become necessary in order to be eligible for import from 1 April 2027.
1 April 2027All cats and dogs must meet the new standard. No exceptions.

What the identity check is, and why it changes your planning

This is the point to grasp. The new standard requires the animal's identity to be officially verified and recorded before everything else: a government-approved veterinarian in the country of origin must scan the microchip and officially record the number, at least six months before the departure date.

In other words: the countdown for a trip to New Zealand no longer begins when you buy the ticket. It begins half a year earlier, with a procedure that does not even depend on you but on your country's animal health authority.

And if you are coming from a country New Zealand does not classify as approved —as is the case for Peru and much of Latin America— those six months are added on top of the timelines you already had: the prior residence in an approved country, the rabies titre and the quarantine on arrival. This is where planning stops being a detail and becomes the difference between travelling and not travelling.

During the transition, you must know which standard you are under

Until 1 April 2027 both systems coexist, and they are not interchangeable mid-way: a file is prepared under one or the other. MPI says so plainly — before starting, you must ask the animal health authority in the country of origin, or your pet transport company, which set of requirements applies to your case.

It is an administrative detail with expensive consequences: preparing a file under the wrong standard means doing it all over again.

What to do if New Zealand is in your plans

  • If you travel before 1 April 2027, you can still use the 2021 standard — but confirm which one applies to you before starting any paperwork.
  • If you travel on or after 1 April 2027, count backwards from your date: the identity check must be done six months earlier. If your flight is in April 2027, that check belongs in October 2026.
  • If you do not yet know your travel date, do the identity check anyway. It is the step that blocks the most time and the one money cannot speed up.
  • For any doubt, MPI answers enquiries at animal.imports@mpi.govt.nz.
