Interview · Public health

«In Peru there is rabies»

Víctor Camacho Paz has coordinated the rabies campaigns of La Libertad since 1993. Here he explains what the virus does inside a body, where it is moving now —from the bat to cattle, in the highland provinces— and why vaccinating once a year leaves a whole litter out.

Interview by Carlos Ravello Joo · Published on 1 September 2026

We are aware that we run this site almost as an encyclopaedic portal of pet relocation around the world. Before publishing this interview we counted, across the 788 pages online that day: the word that names this disease —«rabia», «rabies», «rage», depending on the language— appears on 568 of them, seven out of ten, more than six thousand times, not counting the three thousand four hundred from the «anti-rabies» family. Our veterinarians have written scientific articles about it and we keep an open public repository of antibody titration results.

And even so we made an omission: we had not taken the reader by the hand to a professional who has worked precisely where the problem is. We are also proud that he is one of our own.

We are not going to lie, and we do not want to advertise ourselves. We have believed, since the genesis of our practice, that he was the person who could help us fulfil the step. But the step must not only be fulfilled: it must be understood why it has to be.

We have seen that it is not a whim of countries nor a discrimination based on cultural idiosyncrasy. It is a defence mechanism, sometimes insular: the survival of entire ecosystems, such as those of Australia, Japan or Hawaii.

Our job is not to judge the rules. It is to abide by them and to protect, from our own trench, global wellbeing. We do little, that is true, but it is the grain of sand we are called to contribute.

Víctor Jesús Camacho Paz Veterinarian · CMVP 3103 · ORCID 0009-0005-8096-9317
Regional Zoonoses Coordinator, Regional Health Authority of La Libertad, since 1993.
Administrator of the Rabies Centre of the La Libertad Region until 2014. Graduate of the Universidad Nacional de Cajamarca. Researcher on the prevalence of Fasciola hepatica in the highlands of La Libertad; has worked outbreaks of bubonic plague and urban rabies.

We talk about the disease: what the virus does inside a body, where it is moving right now in Peru, what a cattle-farming family should do when a cow wakes up strange, and why the reproductive calendar of the dog does not match the calendar of the vaccination campaigns.

The interview was conducted in Spanish. The translation of his answers is ours; the original wording is preserved on the Spanish version. Cuts are marked […].

I · Thirty-three years

«Rabies, since 1993, has been increasing»

Before your current post as Regional Zoonoses Coordinator, where were you working with rabies specifically? Clinic, laboratory, field?

From 1993 to date I have been Regional Zoonoses Coordinator, and until 2014, administrator of the Rabies Centre of the La Libertad Region. Uninterrupted work, carrying out both activities simultaneously, which are closely related in terms of the prevention and control measures for this harm, rabies. […]

That is thirty-three years. How has rabies changed in La Libertad? Are the scenario of 1993 and today's the same problem?

Rabies, since 1993, has been increasing in terms of cases and in terms of provinces or districts affected by the virus. In the La Libertad Region we had a case in 1996, in the province of Virú, in the month of March, in a child of two years and eight months: it was the last recorded case.

Until we had four cases of rabies in cattle in Bolívar, transmitted by a wild reservoir, and this year in the province of Sánchez Carrión, in Marcabal Grande, which are adjoining provinces. So, as we can see, the transmission reservoir is not only urban rabies but wild rabies, and we see that cases are increasing.

Basically, I could point out that this is due to the lack of appropriate active surveillance, which must be carried out continuously. The risk factors and the determinants that can favour the presentation of rabies are being neglected. This is also attributed to the lack of inter-institutional work between SENASA, the Ministry of Health and, logically, the population, which is basically the one that can give us real information about any case they may observe within their animal population.

II · What rabies is from the inside

«It is a disease that has no cure»

What happens to a body when the virus enters? Why is there no turning back once the symptoms appear?

What happens first is the replication of the virus at the site of the bite, in the peripheral nervous system. Then it travels to the central nervous system, where it replicates again […], and the Negri bodies are formed, which are the ones that determine in the laboratory the confirmation of a positive rabies case.

There is no way back because the central nervous system deteriorates, and the damage produced by the rabies virus is irreversible. It is a disease that has no cure. It is a disease that when it presents itself, when the symptoms begin, in one hundred per cent of cases is fatal. Hence the existence of the vaccine for post-exposure treatment.

How much time can pass between the bite and the disease?

The time that passes depends basically on the place where the bite occurred and on the amount of inoculum the bite leaves. It can be between fifty days and three hundred and sixty-five days. There are reports of cases in which it has taken up to twenty years for the disease to appear, but basically it is up to one year.

[…] Basically it is three hundred and sixty-five days: hence vaccination is begun. If there is no appearance of symptoms before the year, after the year it is no longer considered, according to Technical Standard 131 of the Ministry of Health.

And in humans? How does it act, what happens, how long does it last?

In people it is seen that, once the symptoms have begun, the patient generally does not go beyond ten days until death. It begins with symptoms such as fear of water, fear of air, difficulty swallowing. Then there is respiratory paralysis, and then death comes. But before that, the incubation period lasts from fifty days onwards, up to three hundred and sixty days.

It is said that there have been cases that last years. Is that a myth or a reality?

Once the symptoms begin, there are no reports of patients having lasted years with the symptomatology. That they may be exposed to developing rabies at any moment is another matter. If the patient has been bitten by a rabid animal, there are reports that it can take up to a year for the symptomatology to appear.

That is why Technical Standard 131, on the clinical management of rabies cases, indicates that a person exposed to an injury by an unknown animal, or one that implies classifying it as a serious bite, must be vaccinated before one year has passed. After the year, the patient is said to be free of contracting rabies and no longer warrants any vaccine.

III · Where rabies is right now

«In Peru there is rabies»

How close is rabies to us right now? Is there rabies in the region, in Peru?

In Peru there is rabies, transmission of rabies by both types of reservoir. Through the urban reservoir we have it in Madre de Dios, we have it in Arequipa, we have it in Cerro de Pasco. And being here in the La Libertad Region, close by we have foci of transmission by wild rabies in Huamachuco, in the province of Bolívar. Recently, this year, two cases in Huamachuco […] in this area of Marcabal Grande. In Bolívar we have also had cases; they have been controlled within the animal population itself, in coordination with SENASA staff, since we basically dedicate ourselves to the treatment of people's health.

But in Peru we have no certain way of saying that there is no circulation of the rabies virus, since samples are not taken from dogs found dead in public streets to determine the circulation of the rabies virus. […] So, we do not know whether there really is rabies or not, but should circulation exist, it does exist.

IV · The Marcabal Grande focus

«We were notified by SENASA»

The Marcabal Grande cases: how many were there, in what species, in what month were they confirmed, and were there exposed people who required treatment?

In the month of April two cases occurred in Marcabal Grande, by wild rabies transmission, in cattle. We were notified by SENASA; the health personnel of Sánchez Carrión travelled to the area to give treatment to all those contacts.

However, we have to insist on the matter of health education for the population, since the population tends to become alarmed and often demands that people who have had no contact be vaccinated. However, the health personnel have the instruction, according to the management protocol, […] to apply the three doses only to those people who have had direct contact with the sick animals or those infected with rabies.

Vaccination has also been taken into account for all SENASA personnel working in the area, so that they are exempt from contracting rabies in the event of having any direct contact with these sick animals.

What counts as direct contact and what does not? If a sick animal licks my hand, am I exposed? If I was only nearby? If I drink that animal's milk?

Direct contact is the name given to any person who has handled the sick animal, provided that person had an open wound and the contact with the animal's saliva was direct. As long as there is no lesion, wound or exposure of the person that can be attributed as contact, there is no transmission of rabies.

Let us not forget that the transmission of rabies is through saliva, and it must have a point of entry. Often it is not only through a lesion that the person handling these animals may have, but it can also enter through the cornea, by some direct contact with the saliva of the sick animal.

V · The cattle, the milk and the farmer

«Once the symptoms have begun, no treatment is of any use»

In Marcabal Grande there are families who milk. If a cow develops rabies, does the milk transmit it? And the fresh cheese, the butter?

Once the symptoms have begun in a cow, the entire milk production, the wellbeing and the quality of life of this animal deteriorate completely, to such an extent that it stops producing milk. The animal moves away from the group of animals, enters paralysis of the hindquarters, of what are called the hind limbs, and, as I repeat, milk production is completely cancelled.

And if the milker, in the event that he had to milk this animal and it produces milk, there is no contagion from the consumption of milk. However, for hygiene and safety measures, the consumption of these by-products must be avoided.

What is the first thing one notices as strange in that animal? And what does the farmer do when he finds his cow like that: whom does he call?

The presentation of the symptoms, I repeat, is due basically to the area where the animal was bitten or where it suffered exposure to the rabies virus. In this case, it can be not only by bats, but also by a dog that has been bitten by a bat, which may be developing the symptomatology and which may attack the cow. That is, both types of transmission can occur, wild and urban.

The incubation period of rabies depends basically on the area where the bite was inflicted and on the amount of inoculum. […] Once the symptoms have begun, no treatment is of any use.

The information, in the case of animals, basically reaches SENASA. SENASA takes charge of going to see the animal side and notifies us.

What SENASA states

Box added by Zoovet Travel, with the source at the foot. It is not part of the interview.

Faced with the suspicion of rabies in an animal, the National Agrarian Health Service states two things:

It is not slaughtered and it is not consumed. «Meat affected by rabies must not be slaughtered or consumed.»

Notice is given immediately. Livestock producers in the affected areas «must inform the nearest Senasa office in their locality immediately when they suspect».

The virus is transmitted through saliva and needs a point of entry, so processing a sick animal means handling saliva and nervous tissue without protection.

Source: SENASA, «La Rabia: una enfermedad común a varias especies», 1 December 2016. Translation ours.

VI · Vaccinating the dog

«What should be done is to vaccinate twice a year»

How often should a dog be vaccinated against rabies?

What is recommended to people here in Peru, and in any area or zone where rabies exists epidemiologically and there is no adequate control or preventive measures, is to vaccinate their pets. What should be done is to vaccinate twice a year against rabies, in areas endemic for rabies. Not once, but twice. However, the Ministry of Health schedules it only once, because of the economic situation.

And this is related to the animals that are born. We know that the oestrous cycle of dogs is twice a year, one in summer and the other in spring. So, the Ministry of Health vaccinates in the month of August, September; the campaigns are carried out, and all the little animals born in the period comprising January, February, March and April manage to be vaccinated and protected against rabies. But those born in September, October are exempt from protection through the biological product, and are more exposed to contracting the disease and rabies in the following months, until the next vaccination campaign takes place. And those are the summer months, when the transmission of this virus increases the most, above all in the coastal areas.

Hence it is advisable to vaccinate the animals twice a year, and to vaccinate respecting the endemic channel of rabies: to check in which months the number of cases rises and to vaccinate one period before.

And is there a difference between campaign vaccination and taking the dog to a veterinarian?

The most appropriate would be to take them to be vaccinated in private practice, where the little animal will undergo, before vaccination, verification of whether it has been dewormed. Because we also have to bear in mind that parasitosis inhibits the production of antibodies in the dog. And the Ministry of Health, when vaccinating, limits itself only to canine vaccination without taking into account the condition of the animal it vaccinates.

So it is not only a question of having good faith and vaccinating because we have resources, but of having a study behind why and when one should vaccinate.

Logically. To vaccinate a little animal we have to take some factors into consideration. One of them is the animal's health status: I refer again to the fact that it must be dewormed, in good health, it must be a little animal that is not pregnant, in order to avoid health problems, and that is well fed, so that the vaccine takes effect and does not cause any harm.

And on the Ministry's part, the Ministry has to determine reliably in which months there is a greater increase in bites and also greater exposure to these animals being vulnerable to becoming infected with rabies.

So it is, on the one hand, institutions such as the Ministry of Health, and on the other hand, us as owners of our pet, to see what best corresponds to our little animal, for the protection of ourselves within our home as well as of our surroundings.

VII · Why the world watches us

«Peru is not a rabies-free country»

Every country that receives an animal from Peru requires a rabies vaccination certificate, and many also require antibody titration. From your experience at the Rabies Centre: what are those countries trying to prevent?

The countries that request these vaccinations or this type of quarantine are countries that are rabies-free. So, it is logical that they do not allow animals from Peru to enter their country. And why does this happen? Because they know, they are aware, that Peru is not a rabies-free country. So, they do not want to have to compromise the health of the animal population of their country.

Where herbivore rabies vaccination takes place

Each year SENASA publishes a resolution that formalises the vaccination campaign against herbivore rabies and states in which departments it is carried out. In La Libertad, the 2024 campaign was formalised for the province of Bolívar; those of 2022 and 2025, for the department without specifying a province.

Who does what when a sick animal appears

The division of responsibilities described by the interviewee is set out in SENASA's own coordination materials: «SENASA provides information to MINSA from the level of suspicion of rabies and when the outbreak is confirmed, so that MINSA carries out the actions according to its remit». Coordination between sectors is channelled through the Standing Multisectoral Commission for the Prevention and Control of Zoonotic Diseases (CMPPCEZ).

The standard cited in the interview

The «Technical Standard 131» mentioned by the interviewee is NTS N° 131-MINSA/2017/DGIESP, «Health Technical Standard for the Surveillance, Prevention and Control of Human Rabies in Peru», approved by Ministerial Resolution N° 024-2017/MINSA and published on 19 January 2017. Ministerial Resolution N° 1176-2023/MINSA, dated 29 December 2023, amended its clauses 6.2.1 and 6.2.4 by means of an annex.

The specific clinical schedule of post-exposure vaccination —days of administration, routes, serum and immunoglobulin— lives in that annex, which is not published in the official viewer. That is why we do not reproduce it here: anyone who has suffered an exposure must go to a health facility, which is where the current schedule is applied.
