Lola had to travel to Spain. By the time Jorge came in, the deadline had already passed.

The story of a mixed-breed dog that flew from Lima to Madrid — and the owner who never once thought of leaving her behind.

Told by Carlos Ravello · Case coordinated and verified by Dr. Jessica Camacho (DVM · CMVP 12434) · July 9, 2026

Trip file

Pet
Lola — mixed-breed dog
Route
Lima, Peru (Jorge Chávez · LIM) → Madrid, Spain (Adolfo Suárez Madrid-Barajas · MAD)
Starting point
Arrived ~1½ months past the deadline; rabies vaccination not current
Process
Microchip · rabies vaccination + booster · FAVN test at KSVDL (Kansas State University) · SENASA Export Zoosanitary Certificate
Outcome
Travelled alongside her owner ~4 months after the first consultation

Jorge Paredes L. came to Zoovet looking for answers, and I think that deep down he already sensed them. His were questions whose answer was already a no: the time for Lola —his mixed-breed dog— to travel on the date he needed simply no longer existed.

In cases like this, the easy thing would be to measure whether the sale is won or lost. We prefer to do the one thing we know how to do well: tell the truth. We told him plainly that against the clock there was nothing we could do, and that no protocol would be broken. But he still had options: send Lola as cargo, have a relative or a friend take her, or find an alternative within the regulatory window.

When we reviewed the case, the other half of the news appeared: Lola's vaccination wasn't up to date. That squeezed the window between what was left to do and the flight date even tighter.

You could see the disappointment on his face.

“Pucha, no sabía… no, no la puedo dejar. ¿No puedo hacer más? No quiero dejarla.” — Jorge, the day of the bad news (“Man, I didn't know… no, I can't leave her. Isn't there anything else I can do? I don't want to leave her.”)

And that's the point: we see cases like this often, and precisely because of that we know what not to do. You don't behave like a salesman with a shark's mindset, or push someone in the middle of the frustration of an expectation the regulations have just broken. Jorge didn't want to leave Lola. Nor did he want anyone else to take her.

With all the information on the table, he left, thought it over, and made the best possible decision: postpone his trip and do it with us.

From there, time was everything. There was no room to lose a single day, and no way to skip a single rule. Everything within the regulations, but with the accelerator floored: chip, vaccines, booster, and the FAVN test at Kansas State University (KSVDL). That wait is always tense.

Until the email arrived. I delivered it myself, in person. I like to give news that way: the bad news out of responsibility, and the good news for the pleasure of hearing, on the other end, an expectation finally met.

Then came the normal nerves of the trip. We took care of the heavy lifting: updating the vaccination and health certificates, assembling Lola's complete file —copies included—, giving Jorge the SENASA account numbers and telling him exactly what to pay and at which bank. So the owner only has to do what's theirs to do, nothing more.

And what was Jorge's to do was the good part: go to SENASA with Lola, present the paperwork, let them inspect her, and receive her export card. Four months after that first conversation, Lola and Jorge flew together: they left Jorge Chávez International Airport in Lima and landed at Adolfo Suárez Madrid-Barajas. Just as he'd wanted from the start.

Jorge has the peace of mind of knowing that, even though the clock was against him from day one, he never abandoned his companion. And that, more than any paperwork, is the part of the job we like to tell.
