Scarlett, Nala and Campanita: three cats from Colombia to the United States (via Peru)

Three cats their owner sent from Colombia to Peru just so they could travel with us to the United States. And, along the way, why cats enter the US easily.

Told by Carlos Ravello · Case coordinated and verified by Dr. Jessica Camacho (DVM · CMVP 12434) · 9 July 2026

Trip file

Pets
Scarlett, Nala and Campanita — three cats
Route
Colombia → Trujillo (Peru) → Lima (Jorge Chávez · LIM) → Los Angeles (LAX) → Salt Lake City, Utah (US)
Mode
Cargo — arrival through the MasAir terminal in the United States
Context
Cat entry to the US is simple: the CDC requires no rabies-vaccination proof and no dog form; the cat only needs to arrive healthy
What is required
Health certificate endorsed by SENASA · vaccinations and microchip up to date · documentation properly translated and in the US format
Handling
Owner in Utah — the cats reached Peru with a relative, to travel with Zoovet
Outcome
Two days of care in Trujillo (Nala got her appetite back) and a safe arrival in the United States

Jennifer wrote to us from Utah. Colombian, far from home, with a simple question: how to get her cats to the United States. We told her what we knew, no calculator involved —a trip that started in Colombia, passed through Peru and ended in North America wasn't, to be frank, much of a deal—: with cats, into the United States, it's easy. No blood test, no permits, none of the form they do demand for dogs. Get the cat ready, papers in order, and onto the plane. Times three.

What she said stuck with us: that in Colombia no one had treated her with that assurance, that kindness. And then she did something that still turns over in my head: she sent her three cats —Scarlett, Nala and Campanita— with a relative who was coming to Peru, so that we would be the ones to put them on the plane bound for the United States. We said yes. That same afternoon we started moving paperwork.

The three of them arrived in Trujillo. First thing —and here Jessica is in charge, she hunts cats the way the rest of us breathe— was to open the carrier the moment they came through the door and let them loose in the big room, the one with the cat gyms, with water and food. One at a time at first. Then, seeing how gentle and affectionate they were, all three loose at once.

Nala had us on edge. The relative who brought them warned us she wasn't eating or drinking. We kept her two days in Trujillo, and it took space, calm and a couple of churus for her appetite to come back. Jessica checked all three cats' vitals, a hug for each one, and cleared them for Lima.

The Trujillo–Lima leg fell to me, behind the wheel, because —again— there was no room for pets on any domestic flight. Hours of road and not a single meow. Not a complaint. Nothing. Three cats travelling as if they did it every day.

At TALMA, the cargo terminal, Migdonio was waiting for them again, our travel agent. And he surprised me: he closed the office, stepped out to the curb and took them out of their carriers with the same care he gives the dogs —that man loves animals in a way you can't fake— this time for three affectionate cats. I'm not going to inflate this into an epic. It was good work, done with heart, and that's it.

And here's the part that takes the fear out of a lot of people: sending a cat by cargo to the United States is, as they say, plug and play. Next to a dog —with its serology, its ISO microchip, the CDC form— a cat has it easy. But easy isn't magic: SENASA, health certificate, vaccines and microchip up to date, everything translated and in the format they use over there. With the papers spotless, the rest rolls on its own. They flew from Lima to Los Angeles, and from there to Salt Lake City. Jennifer was waiting on the other side.

It was one of the loveliest cases we've had, and one of the ones that best explains what we do: someone who didn't know us trusted us from another country, just for the way we treated her over the phone. Three cats crossed half a continent and none of them had a bad time of it. For us, that's having it all.
