International Pet Export Requirements: Rabies Serology FAVN/RNATT by Destination

Four independent regulatory systems — the US, European Union, United Kingdom and Japan — converged on the same requirement: a verified rabies antibody titer ≥0.5 IU/mL before entry. This guide sets out the current legal frameworks, mandatory sequences, approved laboratories and consequences for non-compliance, based exclusively on primary official sources as of April 2026.

Scientific basis

Why antibody titer testing replaced vaccination records

Four distinct jurisdictions with independent regulatory systems reached the same conclusion: a vaccination record is not sufficient evidence of active immunity against rabies. The consensus is not political or bureaucratic — it has a documented epidemiological basis.

The Wallace et al. (2017) study analysed 8,011 vaccinated dogs and found that a significant proportion did not develop protective serological titers after primary vaccination. Key variables included the animal's age, breed, vaccine type used, cold chain failures, and administration technique. A dog with a formally valid vaccination record may have an immune response insufficient to travel safely to a rabies-free country.

The regulatory threshold of ≥0.5 IU/mL was established by OIE/WOAH as an indicator of verifiable active protection. It is not an arbitrary number: it corresponds to the level of neutralising antibodies at which the probability of active rabies virus spread is considered acceptably low for the biosecurity systems of recipient countries.

The common denominator: same threshold, four distinct legal frameworks

The fact that the US (42 CFR § 71.51), EU (Regulation 576/2013), UK (DEFRA post-Brexit guidance) and Japan (Rabies Prevention Law) all adopt the 0.5 IU/mL threshold is not coincidental. All cite the OIE/WOAH standard as their reference. The difference between jurisdictions lies in post-result waiting times, approved laboratories and consequences of non-compliance — not in the criterion for animal approval.

There is active scientific debate about whether this threshold, established in 1977, remains optimal. McElhinney et al. (2026) published a workshop report in Vaccine on the need to re-evaluate the standardisation of rabies serology methods and inter-laboratory variability. However, no regulatory jurisdiction has modified the threshold as of the publication date of this guide.

FAVN vs. RNATT: Different names for the same family of tests. FAVN (Fluorescent Antibody Virus Neutralization) is the technique used by KSVDL (Kansas State University Veterinary Diagnostic Laboratory) and other OIE/WOAH-accredited laboratories. RNATT (Rabies Neutralizing Antibody Titre Test) is the generic name used by DAFF (Australia) and AQS (Japan). The threshold and acceptance criterion are identical: ≥0.5 IU/mL.
United States
🇺🇸

United States — CDC Final Rule 2024

Legal basis Public Health Service Act, 42 U.S.C. § 264 · 42 CFR § 71.51 · Final Rule 89 FR 38450 (August 2024)
Competent authority Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC) · Division of Global Migration and Quarantine
Peru's classification High-Risk Country — Dog-Maintained Rabies Virus Variant (DMRVV). List updated April 15, 2026. Other high-risk Latin American countries: Bolivia, Brazil, Colombia, Cuba, Ecuador, El Salvador, Guatemala, Guyana, Haiti, Honduras, Suriname, Venezuela. Mexico does not appear on the high-risk list.

Complete chronological sequence

  • 1
    ISO 11784/11785 microchip — implanted before first rabies vaccine Must be detectable by universal scanner. The number must match exactly across all documents.
  • 2
    Valid rabies vaccine Administered from 12 weeks of age. Must not expire during travel. Under Zoovet Travel's D1/D15/D30 protocol, Novibac Rabies is used with two doses (D1 and D30) before blood draw.
  • 3
    Blood draw — minimum 30 days post-vaccination The sample must be taken at least 30 days after the valid vaccine. The CDC recommends that the same veterinarian who starts the process also completes it — changing veterinarians mid-process is not recommended.
  • 4
    Shipment and processing at CDC-approved laboratory There are no CDC-approved laboratories within Peru. The sample must be shipped to the US: Kansas State University Veterinary Diagnostic Laboratory (KSVDL), Auburn University, or University of Missouri. Result time: 10–14 business days.
  • 5
    Result ≥0.5 IU/mL + SENASA endorsement With a passing result, SENASA issues the Export Zoosanitary Certificate (CZE) through the VUCE platform. Processing time: 24–48 business hours before the flight. The CDC vaccination certificate must be endorsed by an official SENASA veterinarian.
  • 6
    CDC Dog Import Form + ACF reservation Complete the online form (CDC Dog Import Form) with a recent photo of the animal. Reserve at one of the 6 approved ACFs, specifying the arrival airport. The form is valid for a single trip and must match exactly with the reserved ACF and purchased flight.
  • 7
    Arrival at approved airport + CDC/CBP inspection Air arrival only at the 6 airports with ACFs. The dog is examined by a USDA-accredited veterinarian, microchip, age and vaccination are verified. If everything is in order: direct admission.

The 6 CDC-approved Animal Care Facilities (ACF)

ACF reservation is mandatory for dogs from high-risk countries. Only 6 airports and their approved facilities exist across the entire US:

⚠ Arriving in the US without a valid FAVN titer

Mandatory 28-day quarantine at the reserved ACF. Full cost borne by the owner (USD 1,400–5,000+ depending on airport). The dog receives re-vaccination with a USDA-licensed vaccine during quarantine.

Without prior ACF reservation: the airline will deny boarding at origin. Without a CDC Dog Import Form: CBP may order immediate return on the next available flight at the owner's or airline's expense.

Historical documented case: a suspected rabies importation cost over USD 400,000 in public health response. The 2024 Final Rule (89 FR 38450) tightened controls precisely to eliminate that risk.

Most common errors in cases from Peru

Documented in specialist forums (Facebook group "U.S. CDC Dog Import Rules Help", Reddit r/dogs, 2024–2026) and in Zoovet Travel's operational experience:

Blood draw timing: Blood taken before 30 days post-vaccination. The result is invalid and requires repeating the process from blood draw. Microchip implanted after vaccination: Invalidates the process for most destinations — the microchip must precede the vaccine. Missing SENASA endorsement: The CDC vaccination certificate must be signed and endorsed by an official SENASA veterinarian — the private vet alone is not sufficient. Wrong airport on the CDC form: The form is for a single trip and must match exactly with the reserved ACF and purchased flight. A last-minute airport change results in rejection.

European Union
🇪🇺

European Union — Regulation (EU) 576/2013

Legal basis Regulation (EU) 576/2013 of the European Parliament and of the Council · Commission Implementing Regulation (EU) 577/2013 · Commission Delegated Regulation (EU) 2020/692 · Delegated Regulation 2024/822 (latest amendment)
Competent authority DG SANTE (European Commission) at regulatory level · Competent authority of each Member State at execution level · Designated border inspection posts (BIP)
Peru's classification Non-listed third country: Peru does not appear in Annex II of Implementing Regulation 577/2013 (countries with equivalent requirements). The strictest protocol applies for non-commercial movement from third countries.

Chronological sequence

  • 1
    Microchip (ISO 11784/11785) — before vaccination Prerequisite for any valid rabies vaccination under the EU protocol.
  • 2
    Valid rabies vaccine — from 12 weeks of age Validity begins no earlier than 21 days from completion of the primary vaccination schedule.
  • 3
    Blood draw — minimum 30 days post-vaccination Performed by an authorised veterinarian. The sample must be sent to a designated laboratory under Council Decision 2000/258/EC. There are no EU-designated laboratories within Peru — the sample is sent abroad (KSVDL or other approved European laboratories).
  • 4
    3-month waiting period from blood draw The animal cannot enter the EU before 3 months have elapsed from the date of the blood draw with a result ≥0.5 IU/mL. This period is not reduced even if the result arrives earlier.
  • 5
    Animal Health Certificate + SENASA CZE Official health certificate according to the Implementing Regulation 577/2013 template, issued by an authorised official veterinarian. SENASA issues the CZE aligned with the EU destination requirements. SENASA processing time: 24–48 business hours before the flight.
  • 6
    Entry via designated BIP (Border Inspection Post) The animal must enter through a designated EU entry point. Documentary and sanitary checks are carried out at the BIP of the first-entry Member State.

Variations between Member States

Regulation 576/2013 has direct and uniform application across all 27 Member States. However, practical execution may vary: some countries require additional border controls, and documentary processing times at each BIP differ. Spain, France, Germany and the Netherlands are the most commonly used first-entry BIPs from Peru.

⚠ Arriving in the EU without a valid FAVN titer — Article 35, Regulation 576/2013

Article 35 of the Regulation establishes three possible measures, at the discretion of the competent authority of the Member State: return to the country of origin (on the next available flight, at the owner's expense), official quarantine until entry requirements are met (at the owner's expense), or euthanasia as a last resort if neither of the foregoing is viable.

Euthanasia as a legal consequence is explicitly provided for in the Regulation — it is not an interpretation. It is the option that applies when return is impossible and quarantine is not feasible or poses an unacceptable sanitary risk.

United Kingdom
🇬🇧

United Kingdom — DEFRA/APHA post-Brexit

Legal basis The Rabies (Importation of Dogs, Cats and Other Mammals) Order 1974 (SI 1974/2211) · Retained EU law post-Brexit · DEFRA/APHA current guidance (GOV.UK)
Competent authority Department for Environment, Food & Rural Affairs (DEFRA) · Animal and Plant Health Agency (APHA) · Contact: imports@apha.gov.uk
Peru's classification Unlisted country: Peru does not appear on DEFRA's approved country list. The highest-requirement protocol applies, with a blood test (titer ≥0.5 IU/mL) and a 3-month waiting period.

Chronological sequence

  • 1
    ISO microchip — before the rabies vaccine Prerequisite for vaccination so that the protocol is valid.
  • 2
    Valid rabies vaccine — from 12 weeks of age The vaccine must be administered according to the manufacturer's instructions. Minimum 21 days from completion of the primary vaccination schedule for the vaccine to be valid.
  • 3
    Blood test — minimum 30 days post-vaccination Titer ≥0.5 IU/mL at an APHA/DEFRA-approved laboratory. The sample is processed at recognised laboratories outside Peru (includes KSVDL and European laboratories approved by DEFRA).
  • 4
    3-month waiting period from blood draw The animal cannot enter Great Britain before 3 months from the date of the blood draw with an approved result.
  • 5
    GB Pet Health Certificate + APHA endorsement Issued by an official veterinarian. In Peru, SENASA issues the CZE with documents required by DEFRA. The APHA endorsement is managed through the British consulate or embassy in Peru.
  • 6
    Arrival via approved route + documentary inspection Animals from unlisted countries must arrive by an approved route. APHA conducts documentary and physical checks at the border inspection point. Approved routes updated on GOV.UK/bring-pet-to-great-britain.

⚠ Arriving in the UK without a valid blood test result

The animal is held in approved quarantine at the owner's expense, for up to 4 months, until requirements are met. If entry is refused, the animal is returned on the next available transport. The legal basis (The Rabies (Importation of Dogs, Cats and Other Mammals) Order 1974) gives APHA the authority to detain or refuse entry to any animal that does not meet the requirements for an unlisted country.

Japan
🇯🇵

Japan — Animal Quarantine Service (AQS)

Legal basis Rabies Prevention Law (狂犬病予防法) · Domestic Animal Infectious Diseases Control Law (家畜伝染病予防法) · Official AQS guidelines for Non-Designated Regions
Competent authority Animal Quarantine Service (AQS) · Ministry of Agriculture, Forestry and Fisheries (MAFF) · URL: maff.go.jp/aqs/english
Peru's classification Non-designated region: Peru is not among the designated regions (the only ones exempt from the full protocol are Iceland, Australia, New Zealand, Fiji Islands, Hawaii and Guam). This is the longest and strictest protocol of the four destinations covered in this guide.

Chronological sequence — 180-day minimum

  • 1
    ISO microchip — mandatorily before the first vaccine AQS is the strictest destination on this point: the microchip must be implanted and registered before the first rabies vaccine dose. If the vaccine precedes the microchip, the entire protocol must be restarted from scratch.
  • 2
    Two doses of rabies vaccine — spaced per protocol AQS requires a minimum of two rabies vaccine doses administered before blood draw. Both doses must be within their validity period on the blood draw date.
  • 3
    Antibody test at MAFF-designated laboratory Result ≥0.5 IU/mL. The result is valid for 2 years from the blood draw date. Note: Biobest Laboratories Ltd. (UK) was revoked from the AQS list of designated laboratories for failing to meet standards. Verify the updated list at maff.go.jp before selecting a laboratory.
  • 4
    Mandatory 180-day wait from blood draw The longest period of all destinations covered. The animal cannot enter Japan before 180 full days from the date of the blood draw with an approved result. There are no exceptions to this wait.
  • 5
    AQS notification — minimum 40 days before arrival Mandatory prior notice to the AQS at the destination airport. Without this notice, the animal may be held in quarantine.
  • 6
    Clinical inspection by official veterinarian — within 10 days before departure In Peru, SENASA issues the CZE with the documents required by AQS (including Form A/C recommended by Japan). Inspection for rabies and leptospirosis.
  • 7
    Quarantine inspection at destination port (AQS) Documentary verification and physical examination by AQS staff at the destination airport. If everything is compliant: immediate release. If there are deficiencies: quarantine.

⚠ Arriving in Japan without a valid FAVN titer

The animal is subject to quarantine and isolation under the Rabies Prevention Law. Quarantine at AQS facilities can extend up to 180 days, at the full cost of the importer.

Japan requires the longest planning lead time of all destinations covered in this guide. The most frequently documented error: the microchip implanted after the first vaccine, which requires restarting the entire protocol and delaying travel by up to 9 months.

Peru — Exporting authority

SENASA's role as the hub of the export process

For all destinations covered in this guide, the Export Zoosanitary Certificate (CZE — Certificado Zoosanitario de Exportación) issued by SENASA (Peru's National Agricultural Health Service) is the central document that articulates Peru's sanitary requirements with those required by the destination country.

The CZE — process and timelines

The CZE application is submitted through the VUCE platform (Single Foreign Trade Window). The registered veterinarian submits complete documentation: animal health certificate, current vaccination records (rabies, distemper, parvovirus, leptospira, hepatitis), internal and external deworming certificate valid within the past 15 days, and the original laboratory result from the approved FAVN/RNATT laboratory where applicable. The in-person procedure for CZE issuance takes between 24 and 48 business hours before the flight.

Documented causes of CZE rejection or delay

Identity inconsistency: The microchip number on the FAVN test does not match exactly with the health certificate or animal passport. This is the most frequent error and the one that requires restarting the entire process. Premature vaccination: Vaccinating against rabies before microchip implantation invalidates the process for most destinations. Expired antiparasitic treatments: Using products not approved by the destination country, or applied outside the required time window, generates rejection. Incomplete laboratory documentation: The laboratory report must include the exact numerical result, blood draw date, and the animal's microchip number — any omission can cause delays.

SENASA · Contact and verification: senasa.gob.pe · SENASA Jorge Chávez Airport Office (Lima) for international dispatches. Country-specific requirements must be verified directly with SENASA before starting any protocol, as they may be updated without prior notice.

Frequently asked questions

Questions from owners and veterinarians

Why do all strict destination countries require FAVN serology rather than just a vaccination record?

Because a vaccination record documents that the biological was administered, but does not verify the animal's actual immune response. The Wallace et al. (2017) study of 8,011 dogs found that factors such as age, breed, cold chain failures or technical errors produce insufficient serological responses in apparently vaccinated animals. The US (CDC), EU (Reg. 576/2013), UK (DEFRA) and Japan (AQS) all require a titer ≥0.5 IU/mL as the only verifiable proof of active immunity.

How far in advance do I need to plan to export my pet with a valid FAVN titer?

The most demanding destination in terms of time is Japan: 180 days from blood draw. The US requires at least 30 days post-vaccination for the blood draw plus 10–14 business days of lab processing. The EU and UK require 3 months from blood draw. With the D1/D15/D30 protocol, the realistic minimum total time is 60–90 days for the US/EU/UK, and 6–9 months for Japan.

What is the difference between FAVN and RNATT?

They are different names for the same family of tests. FAVN (Fluorescent Antibody Virus Neutralization) is the technique used by KSVDL (Kansas State) and other OIE/WOAH-accredited laboratories. RNATT (Rabies Neutralizing Antibody Titre Test) is the generic name used by DAFF (Australia) and AQS (Japan). The threshold is identical across all destinations: ≥0.5 IU/mL, as established by OIE/WOAH.

What happens if a pet arrives at the destination without a valid antibody titer?

Consequences vary by destination but none are favorable. US: mandatory 28-day quarantine, USD 1,400–5,000+. EU: return to country of origin, quarantine until compliant, or euthanasia as a last resort (Art. 35, Reg. 576/2013). UK: quarantine up to 4 months or refusal of entry. Japan: quarantine at AQS facility up to 180 days. All costs are borne by the owner or importer.

Is the 0.5 IU/mL threshold the same across all destinations?

Yes. The ≥0.5 IU/mL threshold is set by OIE/WOAH and adopted by the US (CDC), EU (Reg. 576/2013), UK (DEFRA) and Japan (AQS). There is ongoing scientific debate about whether this threshold, established in 1977, remains optimal — McElhinney et al. (2026) published a workshop report on re-evaluating methods — but the regulatory threshold has not been modified.

Can the process be managed from anywhere in Peru?

Yes. Zoovet Travel coordinates the full process from any city in Peru through a network of partner veterinary centers selected for technical rigour and document traceability. Blood collection, shipment to KSVDL and SENASA CZE management are coordinated nationally.
