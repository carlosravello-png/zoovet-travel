Why this calculator exists and how to use it

The IATA Live Animals Regulations (LAR) 2026 edition requires that flight kennels have minimum dimensions calculated from your pet's actual body measurements — weight and "breed standard size" are not enough. A kennel that is too small may be rejected at the airport. One that is too large can move dangerously in the cargo hold. This tool applies the IATA A+B formula to calculate the exact size.

How to measure your dog

  • A — Nose to tail length: from the tip of the muzzle to the base of the tail (not the tail itself). Vet term: labial plane → first coccygeal vertebra (Cc1). Typical medium dog: ~78 cm (30.7 in).
  • B — Floor to elbow: dog standing, floor to elbow joint (humerus-radius). Determines kennel interior height. Typical: ~28 cm (11 in).
  • C — Shoulder width: maximum width from glenohumeral joint to glenohumeral joint. Determines kennel interior width. Typical: ~25 cm (9.8 in).
  • D — Floor to head: dog standing, floor to highest point of head or erect ears. Typical: ~78 cm (30.7 in).

Tip for your veterinarian

If you take your pet to the pre-travel consultation, you can ask the veterinarian to record these measurements in centimeters for the export certificate:

  • • Body length (atlas C1 → tail base Cc1)
  • • Elbow height (floor → humero-radial joint)
  • • Chest width (left scapula → right scapula)
  • • Total height (floor → cranial apex in natural position)

These measurements are also required by many airlines (LATAM, Iberia, Air France) as part of the documentation package for live animal cargo.

IATA LAR formula: Kennel length ≥ A + ½B  |  Kennel height ≥ D  |  Kennel width ≥ C × 2. If you already know your dog's exact measurements, enter the values manually. Otherwise, select the breed and average values will load automatically.

Aluminum Kennels for Powerful Breeds: When Are They Necessary?

The vast majority of pet travel is done in hard plastic kennels (Petmate Sky, SportPet), which comply with IATA LAR regulations and are accepted by nearly all airlines. However, there is a category of dogs for which plastic is simply not enough: breeds with exceptionally powerful jaws and a tendency to escape under stress.

Which breeds need an aluminum kennel?

Airlines with stricter transport policies may require structurally stronger containers for the following breeds or their crosses:

  • Pitbull Terrier and American Staffordshire Terrier — exceptional bite pressure; can deform standard plastic under stress
  • Rottweiler — raw strength combined with high body weight (88–143 lbs); requires L100–L120
  • Doberman Pinscher — combination of size (62–99 lbs) and agility; frequent escape attempts in cargo hold
  • Cane Corso and Presa Canario — among the most powerful jaws in the canine world; risk of breaking plastic latches
  • Bullmastiff and Dogo Argentino — very bulky body (99–154 lbs); few plastic options with sufficient size

Important: IATA LAR regulations do not distinguish between plastic and aluminum — both are "hard-sided" and technically valid. The aluminum requirement comes from each airline's internal policy or the veterinarian responsible for the clearance.

Hard Plastic Kennel

  • ✅ IATA LAR approved for the vast majority of breeds
  • ✅ Lighter (7–18 lbs) → lower excess baggage costs
  • ✅ Affordable price (USD 80–250)
  • ✅ Available in sizes L50 to L90 with 4-sided ventilation
  • ⚠️ Not recommended for breeds with destructive bite
  • ⚠️ Practical limit at L90 (~40" exterior) in standard retail

Aluminum Kennel

  • ✅ Welded construction; resistant to bites and brute force
  • ✅ Compatible with additional security padlock
  • ✅ Perforated ventilation on side walls and roof
  • ✅ Greater long-term durability (does not crack)
  • ⚠️ Heavier (26–55 lbs empty) → may increase cargo cost
  • ⚠️ Premium price (USD 350–900)

K9 Kennel Boss: the benchmark in aluminum flight kennels

The most widely used aluminum kennel for international flights with powerful breeds is the K9 Kennel Boss, manufactured in the United States from anodized aluminum with a powder-coat finish. Key specifications:

  • Interior: 41.6" L × 28" W × 30" H (105 × 71 × 76 cm) — covers dogs up to ~132 lbs
  • Front aluminum door with heavy-duty lever latch
  • 4 ventilation points on side panels and top grille
  • Compliant with IATA LAR Live Animals Regulations as a rigid animal container
  • Available in black and grey — colors that absorb less heat than white on sun-exposed tarmacs

⚠️ Note on L100 and L120 sizes: In the retail market there are no hard plastic kennels larger than 48" exterior (~122 cm) that comply with IATA. For dogs that calculate as L100 or L120 (Rottweiler, Dogo Argentino, Great Dane over 121 lbs), the aluminum option like the K9 Kennel Boss at 41.6" covers dogs up to ~132 lbs as long as the body measurements (length + height) fit inside. If your dog exceeds these dimensions, contact us — some airlines accept custom-built plywood containers with metal reinforcements, under prior airline approval.

What should an IATA kennel have?

Rigid construction with metal screws

Metal door with double lock (top and bottom)

Ventilation on all 4 sides

Two bowls attached to the door, refillable from the outside without opening
