What this licence covers

All the text and tables on this site are released under CC BY 4.0:

  • The regulatory glossary — all 49 definitions, their tables, timelines and FAQs, in Spanish, English and French.
  • The Atlas — the classification of all 203 countries and the tables by country of origin.
  • The Zoopedia — the country requirement sheets.
  • The Zoovet Journal — regulatory alerts and standing rules.
  • The technical articles and the guides.
  • Our own scientific figures, diagrams and illustrations.

How to cite

A single credit line next to the reused material is enough. This is the format:

Zoovet Travel (C. E. Ravello Joo & J. Y. Camacho García, CMVP 12434), licensed CC BY 4.0. Source: https://zoovettravel.com

If the material comes from a specific page, link to that page. If it comes from an article with a DOI, cite the DOI. You do not need to ask us for permission — attribution is enough.

What is NOT licensed

Facts and regulatory data belong to no one. That the CDC removed laboratories from its list on 12 January 2026, or that Japan requires the first rabies vaccination at 91 days, is public information, verifiable at the official source. Anyone may publish it without citing us, and rightly so. What this licence covers is our expression: how we wrote it, how we ordered it and how we explained it.

The Zoovet Travel name, brand and logo are not covered by the licence and may not be used in any way that suggests endorsement or affiliation.

Why we do it this way

Because the standard rises for everyone or it does not rise at all. A colleague who documents well is an animal that does not get held at a border. We publish openly knowing full well that our competitors will use it too — and that is fine by us. All we ask in return is that the source be stated.
