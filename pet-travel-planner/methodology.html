Pet Travel Planner Methodology

This page documents how the Planner computes the Dynamic Coherence Index (ICD-Travel), what assumptions it makes, what official sources it consults and why it applies the Dynamic Coherence Model (MCD) to the international pet export zoosanitary system.

1. Theoretical framework

This planner is a field application of the Dynamic Coherence Model (MCD), an interdisciplinary framework developed by Carlos Eduardo Ravello Joo.

The MCD postulates that the functional persistence of complex systems — human, organisational or artificial — is sustained by the ability to maintain coherence between three interdependent domains: energy (physiology), information (cognition) and purpose (meaning). The framework integrates contributions from autopoiesis (Maturana, Varela), thermodynamics of systems far from equilibrium (Prigogine), general systems theory (Bertalanffy, Morin), observer cybernetics (Shannon, Wiener, Bateson, von Foerster) and cognitive neuroscience (Damasio, Kahneman, Dehaene).

Two of the MCD's own formulations are particularly relevant to this application:

Ravello Paradox: "A system reaches its maximum coherence when it accepts its own incoherence as part of the dynamic flow that sustains it."
Ravello's Law: "Every system seeks coherence between what it knows, what it feels and what it does. When one of those vectors shifts, the system compensates with energy, time or identity."

2. Application to the zoosanitary system

In the "pet + owner + authorities + destination" system, dynamic coherence is evaluated across three operationalised axes:

Axis E · Physiological

Biological age, ISO microchip, valid rabies vaccine, FAVN/titer test, general clinical status.

Axis C · Information

Health certificate, file, SENASA regulations, destination country requirements, import permits.

Axis P · Directional

Specific destination, target travel date, available time, global temporal feasibility.

The ICD-Travel is the numerical synthesis (0-100) of the synchrony between the three axes. When all three are coherent in time, the traffic light is green. When one falls out of sync, amber (dynamic compensation possible). When two or more collapse, red (structural incoherence — no travel).

3. Traffic light rules

🔴 RED — structural incoherence

  • Pet's age on travel day < destination's absolute minimum age
  • Rabies vaccine < 21 days before travel (impossible threshold)
  • FAVN not done and < 90 days to travel (180 for Japan/Australia)
  • Mandatory quarantine not booked and tight deadline
  • Mandatory import permit not obtained and insufficient time
  • Microchip absent AND destination requires it as blocker AND vaccine already applied (re-vaccination needed) AND tight deadline

🟡 AMBER — dynamic compensation

  • Rabies vaccine between 21 and 29 days before travel (legal grey zone)
  • Between 30 and 90 days to travel without serious blockers
  • Microchip absent but time available to implant + re-vaccinate if destination requires

Important: amber is not a green light. It means viability is subject to the destination country sanitary officer's discretion. In Zoovet Travel's field practice (2013-2026), amber almost always equates to NO.

🟢 GREEN — confirmed coherence

  • More than 90 days to travel
  • All destination requirements met with correct order and deadlines
  • Minimum age met with margin

4. Model assumptions

  • Vaccine as booster: the model assumes the rabies vaccine is a booster. Initial vaccination is not considered valid for export; the deadline count starts from the second dose or last booster.
  • Reference date: all deadlines count against the travel date (ETD) declared by the user.
  • Microchip → vaccine → FAVN order: universal. If the destination requires this order and it was not followed, the owner is required to re-vaccinate — no exceptions.
  • Health certificate validity: 5 real days (SENASA Peru rule), regardless of what the destination country guide states.
  • SENASA endorsement: 1 day (next-day, in regular practice).
  • Vaccine 30 days vs 21 days: legally most destinations accept 21 days, but SENASA Peru and variable officer criteria make 30 days the recommended minimum.
  • FAVN/titer test — wait: 90 days general, 180 days for Japan and Australia, counted from sample collection to entry into the destination country.

5. What this planner does NOT calculate

  • Airlines, cabin/hold restrictions, shipping costs
  • Breed-specific restrictions (beyond minimum ages)
  • Temporary customs situations
  • Paperwork costs
  • FAVN or quarantine pricing

7. Documentary rigour

Each generated PDF includes: generation timestamp, version of rules.json used, citation of official sources and full legal disclaimer. The rules.json file is versioned (semver), has a last-updated date, next review date, changelog and declared maintainer.

This tool is strictly informational and advisory. It does not constitute veterinary advice, does not replace consultation with a licensed veterinarian, and does not substitute direct verification with SENASA Peru and the destination country sanitary authority.
