World Directory of Pet Export Authorities

Which official authority issues my pet's export certificate in each country?

To legally take a dog or cat out of a country you need an export certificate issued or endorsed by its official veterinary authority. This directory brings together, country by country, which body it is (SENASA, SAG, ICA, USDA APHIS, CFIA, SENASICA, AQS, DALRRD…), what the certificate is called, where it is processed (a physical office or a digital system) and its official government source, with a verification date. It is the first directory to gather this in one place, using official sources only.

Last general verification: 10 June 2026 · 49 export authorities across 8 world regions · Information drawn exclusively from each government's official sources.

The directory in numbers: of the 49 authorities, 20 process the certificate at a physical office, 13 through a 100% digital system and 16 in a hybrid way (online application + in-person collection or endorsement). Every record cites its official government source and verification date.

Notice: Information drawn exclusively from the official sources of each country and body. No external sources (blogs, agencies or third parties) were used. Each entry cites its official URL and verification date. Official data may change without notice: always confirm directly with the relevant authority before starting any procedure or trip.

🌍 Europe

In the European Union the framework is harmonised (Regulation (EU) 576/2013, updated by Regulations 2026/636 and 2026/705): to move within the EU the European passport + microchip + valid rabies vaccination is enough; to export to a third country, each Member State issues or endorses the certificate through its official veterinarian (often via TRACES). Verification methodology

This directory is built on a single criterion: official government sources only. Every record —authority, certificate, place of processing and contact— comes from the competent body's official website or document and includes its source URL and verification date. No blogs, agencies or intermediaries are used. Where the official source does not publish a datum (opening hours, a named officer), it is marked as not published rather than filled in from third parties. The directory is edited, not deleted: when a datum changes it is updated and re-dated, and it is reviewed periodically against regulatory changes.

This resource gathers information that, until its publication, did not exist together in a single source. Each country's entry requirements are widely documented by destination governments; but which authority in the country of origin issues the exit certificate and where it is processed remained scattered. This directory concentrates it for 49 countries, using official sources only.
