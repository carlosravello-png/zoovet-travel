1. Why dogs fail the FAVN — and why it matters

The FAVN (Fluorescent Antibody Virus Neutralization) test is the gold standard for verifying rabies immune status in dogs and cats intended for international movement to rabies-free destinations — the European Union, the United Kingdom, Australia, New Zealand, Japan, and others. The regulatory threshold of ≥0.50 IU/mL, adopted by WOAH (Chapter 8.14) and EU Regulation 576/2013, is a minimum documentary standard, not a clinical protection threshold.

Primary FAVN failure — a result below 0.50 IU/mL — is more common than owners expect. Belanger et al. (2025) found that approximately 48% of imported dogs had rVNA titres below threshold at the time of arrival at destination. The most consistently documented cause is primary vaccination without a confirmed booster, or blood collection before adequate anamnestic response has developed (Wallace et al., 2017; McElhinney et al., 2026).

ATON's case was textbook: a vaccination in Sweden with no documented brand, no verified date, and no booster record — producing 0.22 IU/mL, 44% of the minimum required value.

3. The D1/D15/D30 Protocol — step by step

The protocol is based on the humoral immune response kinetics of inactivated rabies vaccines in dogs. After primary vaccination (D1), naive B lymphocytes process the antigen and begin differentiating into IgM-producing plasma cells. Class switching to IgG — the isotype with the highest neutralizing capacity — occurs progressively over weeks 2–4.

The booster on Day 15 (14 calendar days after D1) hits the optimal immunological window: memory B cells are fully primed but the primary response has not yet declined. This generates a high-magnitude anamnestic response that elevates rVNA titres to FAVN-detectable levels by the Day 30 blood draw — 16 days after the booster.

  2. D1 — Primary vaccination: Novibac Rabies (March 5, 2026 in ATON's case)Administer Novibac Rabies. Record the exact date. Microchip must precede this vaccination.
  3. D15 — Booster (March 19, 2026 — 14 days after D1)Administer the Novibac Rabies booster on Day 15. This timing is the protocol's critical element.
  4. D30 — Blood draw for KSVDL FAVN (April 4, 2026 — 30 days after D1)Collect serum exactly 30 days after D1. This meets EU Reg. 576/2013 minimum sampling window precisely.

5. Regulatory compliance — EU Regulation 576/2013

EU Regulation 576/2013 requires, for dogs from third countries not on the authorised list — a category that includes Peru — that the VNT sample be collected no earlier than 30 days after primary vaccination. The D30 blood draw on April 4, 2026 is exactly 30 calendar days after D1 on March 5, 2026 — the minimum regulatory interval met exactly. After a passing result, dogs from non-listed third countries must also complete a mandatory 3-month waiting period before EU entry.

6. Frequently asked questions

What does it mean if my dog failed the FAVN?
A result below 0.50 IU/mL means the current rabies antibody titre does not meet the international regulatory threshold for travel to rabies-free countries. The most common cause is primary vaccination without a documented booster, or premature blood collection. A reconversion protocol can correct this — as the ATON case demonstrates.
How long does the D1/D15/D30 protocol take?
30 days from first vaccination to blood draw, plus approximately 5–7 business days for KSVDL processing. Total: approximately 37–40 days from starting the protocol to receiving results.
Does this protocol meet EU Regulation 576/2013 requirements?
Yes. The D30 blood draw is exactly 30 calendar days after D1, meeting the minimum sampling window precisely. After a PASS result, a 3-month waiting period still applies for dogs from non-listed third countries like Peru before EU entry.
Is this the first published FAVN reconversion case report from Latin America?
Yes. As of June 2026, this is the first openly published FAVN failure/reconversion case report with verified KSVDL laboratory identifiers from a Latin American veterinary operator. DOI: 10.5281/zenodo.20533499.
