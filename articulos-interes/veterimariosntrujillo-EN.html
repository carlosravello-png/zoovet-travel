Section 1 The difference between general practice and export medicine

The veterinarian dedicated to conventional practice prioritises diagnosis and treatment of disease, but export medicine requires specialisation in international health law and biological logistics. A clinician without specific training often does not know that rabies vaccination compliant with international movement regulations loses legal validity if administered one minute before microchip reading or implantation (ISO 11784/11785). This lack of documentary rigour invalidates the entire file before SENASA, forcing protocol repetition and indefinite postponement of the family move.

Veterinarians specialised in international travel in Trujillo, Peru operate under an error taxonomy based on documentary reversibility. Each antiparasitic treatment must be recorded with brand, batch and exact date, ensuring the application window matches the requirements of countries such as Chile or the United Kingdom. The structure of these files is set out technically in The pet export file: complete documentary chain, error taxonomy and reversibility, which explains why a misplaced stamp leads to border rejection.

Section 2 Serology management and international laboratories

Handling samples for rabies neutralising antibody titration (RNATT) requires cold-chain logistics and customs handling that most local practices cannot execute. Serum must reach accredited laboratories in the United States or Europe without haemolysis and with documentation that unequivocally links the sample to the patient’s microchip. Veterinarians specialised in international travel in Trujillo, Peru know processing times and the 90- or 180-day waiting periods imposed by high-surveillance destinations.

A result below 0.5 IU/mL does not always indicate biological failure of the animal; it is often due to an incorrect sampling interval or poor sample handling in the practice. Specialisation allows identification of the optimal time for drawing blood after primary vaccination, reducing the risk of negative results that force restarting the month count. At our Trujillo office we audit the prior immune response to ensure international shipment of the sample is a procedure with the highest chance of regulatory success.

Section 3 Carrier audit and fit-to-fly certification

The animal’s safety in the aircraft hold depends on a physiological assessment that goes beyond a general health check. Only veterinarians specialised in international travel in Trujillo, Peru have the judgement to assess whether a brachycephalic or geriatric patient can tolerate the mild hypoxia of the cabin and the metabolic stress of transport. Issuing a health certificate without considering the flight’s equivalent altitude or runway temperature restrictions is negligence that endangers the animal’s life in transit.

The technical audit includes verification of the container against IATA LAR standards, ensuring the space allows thermoregulation and natural posture. Sedation, often suggested in non-specialised clinics, is contraindicated due to the risk of respiratory collapse at altitude. Correct preparation is based on ethological conditioning and stabilisation of the intestinal microbiota to mitigate cortisol impact, a protocol only implemented in centres dedicated exclusively to international animal movement.

Section 4 What to resolve before starting

Verification of import requirements must be done directly with the primary sources of each country, as regulations vary drastically according to Peru’s risk classification. In August 2024 the CDC changed dog entry into the United States, an update that invalidated processes for those without up-to-date specialised advice. Veterinarians specialised in international travel in Trujillo, Peru consult official portals such as MAPA, APHIS or DAFF daily to align health calendars with current regulation.

Payment of fees to SENASA and handling via the Single Window for Foreign Trade (VUCE) require administrative precision to avoid discrepancies between the registered owner and the travelling passenger. The La Libertad regional office requires the Official Veterinary Export Certificate (CZE) to be processed within very narrow time windows before the flight. Having professional support in Trujillo ensures the mandatory physical inspection is a smooth step rather than a conflict point over misfiled documents or out-of-window treatments.

Full traceability is achieved when the veterinarian assumes responsibility for the documentary chain from day one until official endorsement. Improvisation in deworming records or missing original microchip labels on the record are failures that customs inspectors do not overlook. Specialisation ensures every datum in the file has a verifiable technical basis, allowing the family to focus on their move while the professional secures the legal viability of the animal’s transport.

Inconsistency in a single datum in the health file will lead to your pet's deportation or immediate rejection at the airport.
