What it is for

A deadline is almost never written as a date. It is written as a period: “within thirty days”, “valid for six months”, “to be delivered before ninety days have passed since signing”. This page turns that period into a specific day on the calendar. And it does the opposite too: if you already have both dates, it tells you exactly how far apart they are.

Typical uses

  • Expiry dates. Documents, certificates, policies, warranties, laboratory results: the last day they remain valid.
  • Paperwork and administrative deadlines. When the period to file, reply, correct or appeal runs out.
  • Contracts and employment. End of a probation period, notice periods, automatic renewal dates.
  • Logistics and deliveries. Shipping date, transit and the delivery date you committed to.
  • Personal counts. How long is left or how long it has been, and an exact age in years, months and days.

What this calculator does not do

It counts plain calendar days. It does not skip weekends or public holidays. If your deadline is written in working days, the real date will fall later than the one shown here, because the non-working days of whichever calendar applies to you have to be skipped. Nor does it interpret the rule: it tells you which day it lands on, not whether your period starts counting on the day of notification or the day after. That is defined by the text you are reading, and it is worth checking before you trust the number.

Why we publish it

At Zoovet Travel we work every day with deadlines that do not forgive: tests with a limited validity, treatments that have to be given inside an exact window of days before a flight, certificates that expire. Miscounting a single day there means missing a flight. We needed a calculator that counted properly, so we built one. And since anyone dealing with paperwork has the same problem, we left it open: free, no sign-up, no advertising and nothing collected, for whatever you need to work out.

Frequently asked questions

How are the days between two dates counted?

By default the plain difference is counted: from 1 to 10 March there are 9 days, because the start day is not counted. If you need both ends counted — as in many administrative deadlines, where the day of filing counts too — tick “Include the end date” and the result goes up to 10.

Why does the years, months and days breakdown not match the total divided by 30?

Because calendar months are not all the same length. A month is counted from date to date: from 31 January to 28 February is “1 month”, and from 31 January to 1 March is “1 month and 1 day”, even though in days they are 28 and 29. When the target month has no such day number, it is clamped to the last day of that month. The total in days shown above is always exact; the breakdown is the calendar reading of that same total.

Are the dates I type stored or sent anywhere?

No. The whole calculation happens inside your browser, in the page’s own code. There is no calculation server, no cookies, no advertising and no third-party scripts. If you copy the result link, the dates travel inside that address only to wherever you paste it.

Does it count working days or calendar days?

Calendar days. It counts every day, including Saturdays, Sundays and public holidays. If the deadline you are working out is written in working days, the real date will fall later than the one shown here: the non-working days of whichever calendar applies to you have to be skipped, and that calendar changes from country to country and sometimes from region to region.

How are leap years and daylight saving changes handled?

Leap years are taken into account: 29 February counts as an extra day when it applies. Daylight saving changes do not affect the result, because the calculation does not work with hours: it operates on whole days in universal time, so a night when the clock moves forward or back is still exactly one day.
