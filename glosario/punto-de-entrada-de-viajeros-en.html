Travellers' point of entry (TPE) — European Union

The only place through which a pet may enter the EU from a third country

Author: Carlos Eduardo Ravello Joo Clinical review: Dra. Jessica Ysabel Camacho García, CMVP 12434 Published: 11 July 2026

Definition based on documentary practice 2013-2026 · Zoovet Travel, Trujillo, Peru

Quick definition

The only legal way in

A dog arriving in the European Union from Peru cannot enter through just any airport. It must do so through a travellers' point of entry designated by the country of destination, and there it must be presented to the competent authorities.

Those authorities carry out two checks: the animal's documentation and its identity — that is, they scan the microchip and confirm that the number matches the one on the paperwork.

If the animal does not pass the check, European rules provide for three outcomes, and they are worth reading unsweetened: return to the country of departure, quarantine until it meets the requirements, or —as a last resort— euthanasia. This is not a rhetorical warning: it is written into the rules.

Formal technical definition

A travellers' point of entry is not the same thing as a commercial border control post. It is a specific figure for the non-commercial movement of pet animals: it exists precisely because these animals travel with their owner and not as goods.

Each Member State designates its own and publishes the list. Not every international airport in a country is a point of entry, and not every point of entry is at an airport. The list is maintained by the European Commission and must be checked before buying the ticket, not afterwards.

The obligation to present the animal is active, not passive: it is not enough to pass through the right airport and wait for nobody to ask. The owner must contact the competent authority present at the point of entry. An animal that crosses the border without that check has not "got through on luck": it is in an irregular situation.

Regulatory context in export

The point of entry is where everything else converges. It is the moment an official scans the microchip and compares that number with the one on the certificate, the one on the titration and the one on the vaccination record. If any of them fails to match —a digit mistyped months earlier, on another continent— the entire file collapses right there.

That is the real reason behind all the sequencing rules that look bureaucratic: the microchip before the vaccine, the identical number on every document, the titration tied to that chip. It all exists so that a three-second scan adds up.

For an animal leaving Peru, the chain ends here: SENASA endorses, the airline carries, and the authority at the point of entry decides. It is the only link Zoovet Travel does not control — and that is why it is the one to armour most carefully.

Typical timeline

  • The point of entry is chosenBefore buying the ticket. Not every airport is one. The list is published by the European Commission.
  • Health certificate endorsedWith the microchip number identical to the one on every other document.
  • Arrival at the point of entryThe owner must actively contact the competent authority. This is not a passive check.
  • Microchip scanThree seconds that validate or bring down months of paperwork.
  • EntryIf everything adds up, the animal enters with its owner.

Common errors

  • Flying to an airport that is not a designated point of entry. The mistake is made when buying the ticket, months earlier, and discovered on landing.
  • Not presenting the animal to the authority. The obligation is active. Walking past is not luck: it is an irregular situation.
  • A single mistyped microchip digit. One is enough. That is why the number must be copied and verified, never recalled from memory.
  • Forgetting the non-commercial movement declaration. Without it, the animal may shift to the commercial regime, with different requirements and different costs.
  • Assuming the check is a formality. The three possible outcomes of non-compliance are written into the rules, and one of them is irreversible.

Frequently asked questions

Can I enter the EU with my dog through any airport?
No. An animal arriving from a third country may only enter through a travellers' point of entry designated by the country of destination. Not every international airport is one. The list is published by the European Commission and must be checked before buying the ticket.
Do I have to do something on arrival, or will they call me?
You have to present yourself. The obligation is active: the owner must contact the competent authorities present at the point of entry, who will check the documentation and the identity of the animal. Walking past because nobody asked is not a stroke of luck — it is an irregular entry.
What happens if my pet does not pass the check?
European rules provide for three outcomes: return to the country of departure, quarantine until the animal meets the health requirements, or —as a last resort— euthanasia. This is not a rhetorical warning: it is written into the rules. That is why the file is reviewed before flying, not after.
What exactly do they check?
The documentation and the identity. Identity is verified by scanning the microchip and matching that number against the one on the certificate, the vaccination record and the titration. If a single digit does not match, the whole file ceases to refer to that animal.
Do I need the non-commercial movement declaration?
Yes, if you are travelling from a third country. A written declaration must be attached to the health certificate stating that the movement has no commercial purpose. It is also required if the animal travels under the responsibility of a person authorised by you — and in that case the animal's journey must take place within 5 days of your own.
