Rabies Serology — Neutralizing Antibody Measurement Against Rabies

Veterinary immunology · Antibody test to certify rabies immunity for international pet export

Author: Carlos Eduardo Ravello Joo Clinical review: Dra. Jessica Ysabel Camacho García, CMVP 12434 Published: 12 May 2026

Definition based on documentary practice 2013-2026 · Zoovet Travel, Trujillo, Peru

Quick definition

≥ 0.5 IU/mL · OIE/WOAH

Rabies serology is the blood test that measures the concentration of rabies-neutralizing antibodies in a dog or cat. In the context of international pet export, the term rabies serology is used synonymously with FAVN (Fluorescent Antibody Virus Neutralization), the specific test recognized by the OIE/WOAH and required by rabies-free countries.

The international minimum threshold is ≥ 0.5 IU/mL. A result below this threshold means the animal does not certify sufficient immunity and cannot travel to those destinations until completing a new protocol.

Formal technical definition

Technically, "rabies serology" is a generic term encompassing all methods for measuring rabies virus antibodies. The main methods are: FAVN (Fluorescent Antibody Virus Neutralization) and RFFIT (Rapid Fluorescent Focus Inhibition Test). Both are viral neutralization tests in which the animal's serum is exposed to live rabies virus.

For pet export regulatory purposes, FAVN is the method required by the EU (Regulation 576/2013), Japan (AQS), Australia (DAFF) and most rabies-free countries. RFFIT is not accepted as equivalent by these regulators.

The ≥ 0.5 IU/mL threshold is established in the OIE/WOAH Terrestrial Manual. The sample must be taken at least 30 days after valid rabies vaccination, with the microchip already in place before that vaccination.

Regulatory context in export

Rabies serology serves a specific function in the export documentary chain: it certifies that the animal's rabies vaccination generated a sufficient immune response. It does not replace the vaccine — both are necessary — but complements and validates it before destination country authorities.

Post-serology waiting periods vary significantly by country: for the EU, the animal must wait 3 months from the date of ≥ 0.5 IU/mL result. For Japan, the wait is 180 days. For Hawaii, there is no post-serology waiting period if other conditions are met.

Common errors

  • Blood draw before 30 days post-vaccination. The result may be technically correct as an analysis, but is regulatorily invalid if obtained before day 30.
  • Not waiting the post-result period. A ≥ 0.5 IU/mL result does not immediately authorize travel. EU: 3 months. Japan: 180 days. Skipping this period is the most costly error cause.
  • Confusing RFFIT with FAVN. Requesting a serology test at a laboratory that performs RFFIT instead of FAVN is an error: the result is not accepted by EU, Japan or Australia.
  • Non-OIE/WOAH certified laboratory. From Peru, the only operational laboratory for FAVN is KSVDL. A domestic laboratory result is not accepted internationally.
  • Losing vaccination continuity between serology and travel. If the rabies vaccine expires before the trip without renewal, the previous FAVN result loses validity and the complete protocol must restart.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between rabies serology and the FAVN test?
Rabies serology is the generic term for any analysis measuring rabies virus antibodies. The FAVN (Fluorescent Antibody Virus Neutralization) is the specific rabies serology method required for international pet export by the OIE/WOAH, the EU, Japan, Australia and most rabies-free countries. In export practice, the two terms are used synonymously because FAVN is the only accepted method.
How long must you wait between serology and travel?
Depends on the destination. For the EU (Spain, France, Italy, etc.): 3 months from the date of ≥ 0.5 IU/mL result. For Japan: 180 days from that date. For Hawaii: no waiting period if other conditions are met. For Australia: no post-serology waiting period, but mandatory 10-day quarantine on arrival.
Can serology be repeated if the result is negative?
Yes. If the result is < 0.5 IU/mL, standard protocol is to revaccinate and wait 30 days before repeating sampling. The post-result waiting period (3 months for EU, 180 days for Japan) only starts from the date of the first positive result (≥ 0.5 IU/mL).
How long does it take to receive the serology result from Peru?
From Peru, the sample is sent to KSVDL in the USA. Total time includes: 1-2 days preparation and shipping, 3-5 days transit with cold chain, and 10-14 business days processing at KSVDL. Total approximate time: 3-4 weeks from blood draw to result receipt.
Does a positive serology result have an expiration date?
The result itself does not expire as a document, but its regulatory validity is conditional on the animal maintaining current rabies vaccination. If the rabies vaccine expires without renewal before the trip, the previous serology loses its value and the entire protocol must restart from scratch.
