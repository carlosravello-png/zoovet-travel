Three-month waiting period — European Union

Counted from the date of the blood draw, not from the result

Author: Carlos Eduardo Ravello Joo Clinical review: Dr. Jessica Ysabel Camacho García, CMVP 12434 Published: 11 July 2026

Definition based on documentary practice 2013-2026 · Zoovet Travel, Trujillo, Peru

Quick definition

3 months from the blood draw

This is the deadline that delays the most journeys and the one most people count wrong. The European Union requires a wait of three months after the rabies antibody titration test before the animal may enter. So far, everybody knows that.

What almost nobody is clear about is when the count starts. Not when the laboratory result arrives: on the day the blood was drawn. That distinction is worth weeks. If the sample was taken on 1 August and the result came back on the 20th, the animal can fly on 1 November, not on 20 November.

And there is one exception almost nobody uses, because almost nobody knows it: the three months are not required if the animal re-enters the EU after a short stay abroad, provided that before leaving it was vaccinated, had a satisfactory titration result, and both facts were recorded in its European pet passport.

Formal technical definition

The waiting period applies to animals arriving from a non-listed third country — the category into which Peru and most of Latin America fall. Countries on the EU list need no titration test and therefore no waiting period either.

The full sequence, with its three chained clocks: the microchip goes in before the vaccine. The rabies vaccine is administered. Thirty days must pass from the primary vaccination before the blood can be drawn. It is drawn. And from that day the 3 months begin to run.

Added up: between the first vaccine and the flight there is a minimum of four months, and that in the best of cases. This is not a procedure that can be hurried along by paying more.

If the animal was already vaccinated and the vaccine is still valid, the blood can be drawn immediately: there is no need to wait the 30 days. But the 3 months that follow apply just the same.

The titration test is not repeated. A satisfactory result is good for life — as long as the animal is revaccinated within the validity period of the previous vaccine. If there is a lapse, the entire clock goes back to zero.

Regulatory context in export

The waiting period is the heart of the European calendar and the reason a trip to Spain or France cannot be improvised. Take the Peruvian circuit as the working example; the order runs like this:

The accredited veterinarian draws the sample and sends it to a laboratory designated by the European Commission — which is not the same list as the CDC's. The result is attached to the health certificate. And SENASA endorses the international veterinary certificate only once the three months have run their course.

One detail worth internalising: the European health certificate has a short validity (days), while the waiting period lasts months. They are two different clocks and they must be synchronised, not confused. The certificate is issued at the end, when the three months are already behind you.

Typical timeline from a non-listed third country

  • MicrochipAlways before the vaccine that will be used for the count.
  • Rabies vaccinationPrimary vaccination by an authorised veterinarian.
  • Blood drawMinimum 30 days after the primary vaccination. If the animal was already vaccinated and the vaccine is still valid, it can be done immediately.
  • End of the waiting periodThree months counted from the blood draw. This is the day from which the animal may enter the EU.
  • Health certificateIssued at the end, not at the beginning. Its validity is measured in days.
  • Travellers' point of entryDocumentary and identity check by the competent authority.

Common errors

  • Counting the 3 months from the result. The costliest mistake in the European calendar. They are counted from the blood draw. Whoever gets it wrong loses weeks of useful life from the file — or buys a ticket for an impossible date.
  • Adding the laboratory days on top of the 3 months. They are not added on: they run inside.
  • Drawing the blood before 30 days have passed since the primary vaccination. The result will be numerically valid and regulatorily useless.
  • Issuing the health certificate too early. It expires in days. It is done at the end of the process, not at the start.
  • Letting the vaccine lapse during the 3 months. A lapse turns the next vaccine into a primary vaccination: another 30 days, a new titration test and 3 months all over again.

Frequently asked questions

Are the 3 months counted from the result or from the blood draw?
From the blood draw. The European rule is explicit: you must wait three months from the date on which the blood sample was taken. The time the laboratory spends analysing it runs inside those three months, not after them. Getting this wrong can cost weeks of needless waiting — or lead someone to buy a ticket for a date on which the animal still cannot enter.
How long does it take in total to bring a dog into the European Union?
A minimum of four months from the first rabies vaccination: 30 days of waiting before the blood can be drawn, plus the 3 months counted from that draw. It is not a deadline that can be shortened by paying more: it is a legal sequence.
My dog is already vaccinated and the vaccine is still valid. Do I still have to wait 30 days?
No. If the animal was already vaccinated against rabies and that vaccination is still valid, the titration test can be carried out immediately, without waiting the 30 days. What cannot be skipped is the 3-month waiting period after the blood draw.
Is there any case in which the 3 months do not apply?
Yes, one: when the animal re-enters the EU after a short stay in a third country. To qualify for the exception, before leaving the EU the animal had to be vaccinated against rabies, have a satisfactory titration result, and both had to be recorded by the veterinarian in its European pet passport.
Does the titration test have to be repeated for every trip?
No. A satisfactory result is not repeated, provided the animal is revaccinated within the validity period of the previous vaccine. If the vaccine expires before the booster, the next dose counts as a primary vaccination and the whole calendar starts again: 30 days, a new titration test and another 3 months of waiting.
