Laboratory designated by the European Union

The European list of titration laboratories is not the same as the CDC's

Author: Carlos Eduardo Ravello Joo Clinical review: Dra. Jessica Ysabel Camacho García, CMVP 12434 Published: 11 July 2026

Definition based on documentary practice 2013-2026 · Zoovet Travel, Trujillo, Peru

Quick definition

Its own list · different from the CDC's

This is the trap almost nobody sees coming: there are two lists of laboratories, not one. The European Union keeps its own and the United States CDC keeps its own, and they do not match.

A rabies titration result issued by an impeccable laboratory, with a perfect number on it, may be valid for one bloc and worth nothing for the other. The assay is the same. The signature backing it is not.

To enter the EU, the sample must be drawn by an authorised veterinarian and analysed by a laboratory designated by the European Commission. Full stop. The list is published by the Commission itself, and it has to be checked before the blood is shipped — not after.

Formal technical definition

Designation is an administrative act of the European Commission, with its own procedure and its own review cycles. A laboratory may be on the European list and not on the American one, or the other way round, and the two lists are updated separately.

The practical consequence for anyone planning a two-destination move —or for anyone who still does not know whether the final destination will be Europe or the United States— is uncomfortable but clear: the destination has to be decided before the sample is sent, or you accept that it may have to be repeated.

The condition attached to the sample. The blood must be drawn by an authorised veterinarian. In Peru, that means a licensed veterinarian with a CMVP registration number.

And one rule that is common to both blocs: a satisfactory result does not have to be repeated, provided the animal is revaccinated within the validity period of the previous vaccine. A lapse voids it on both sides of the Atlantic.

Regulatory context in export

In the European file, the designated laboratory is what turns a number into evidence. The result is attached to the health certificate endorsed by SENASA, and it is from the date the sample was drawn —not the date of the result— that the three-month waiting period starts running.

That chain has a silent point of failure: if the laboratory is not on the European list, everything that comes after it —the three months, the certificate, the flight— is built on nothing. And it does not surface until the entry point.

The operational lesson is the one taught by the CDC's laboratory purge of January 2026: the laboratory is not a supplier, it is part of the file. Choosing it badly, or choosing it late, costs months.

Typical timeline

  • The destination is decidedBefore the blood is drawn. Europe and the United States require laboratories from different lists.
  • Sample drawn by an authorised veterinarianAt least 30 days after the primary vaccination.
  • Shipment to the designated laboratoryFrom the European list, if the destination is the EU.
  • The three monthsCounted from the date the sample was drawn, not from the result.
  • Health certificateWith the result attached and endorsed by the national authority (SENASA, in Peru).

Common errors

  • Using the CDC laboratory for a trip to Europe. The lists do not match. A result that is valid in one bloc may be worth nothing in the other.
  • Sending the sample before deciding the destination. The destination determines the list, and the list determines the laboratory.
  • Not checking the list currently in force. Designations are reviewed. What was valid two years ago may not be valid today.
  • Having the blood drawn by a veterinarian who is not authorised. The rule requires an authorised veterinarian, not just anyone.
  • Assuming that because the laboratory "is a good one" it will do. Technical quality is irrelevant if it is not on the list. What validates the result is the designation, not the prestige.

Frequently asked questions

Can I use the same laboratory for Europe and for the United States?
Only if that laboratory appears on both lists — and it cannot be taken for granted. The European Commission and the CDC maintain independent lists, each with its own procedures and its own updates. A result that is valid for one bloc may be worth nothing for the other, even if the assay is identical.
Who is allowed to draw the blood?
An authorised veterinarian. In Peru, that means a licensed veterinarian with a CMVP registration number. This is not a step just anyone can carry out.
When does the three-month wait start counting?
From the date the sample was drawn, not from the date of the result. The days the laboratory takes to process the sample run inside those three months; they are not added on top of them.
Does the titration have to be repeated for every trip?
No, provided the animal is revaccinated within the validity period of the previous vaccine. If there is a lapse, the titration is void — and that rule applies equally in the European Union and in the United States.
What happens if the laboratory is dropped from the list after my test has been run?
That is exactly what happened with the CDC's laboratory purge in January 2026: the result stops being valid, even if it is correct and fully paid for. This is why it pays to choose laboratories with a stable track record on the list, and to check that they are still on it before buying the plane ticket.
