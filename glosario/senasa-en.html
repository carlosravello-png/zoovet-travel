SENASA — National Agrarian Health Service of Peru

Zoosanitary certification · Competent authority for companion animal export from Peru

Author: Carlos Eduardo Ravello Joo Clinical review: Dra. Jessica Ysabel Camacho García, CMVP 12434 Published: 12 May 2026

Definition based on documentary practice 2013-2026 · Zoovet Travel, Trujillo, Peru

Quick definition

DS 051-2000-AG · TUPA CA07

SENASA (Servicio Nacional de Sanidad Agraria) is the Peruvian state technical body attached to the Ministry of Agriculture and Irrigation, with competence over zoosanitary certification of companion animals for export. In practical terms, SENASA issues and endorses the Export Health Certificate (CSE) that destination countries recognize as the official Peruvian document.

Without the SENASA certificate, no animal can be legally exported from Peru, regardless of the quality of the rest of the file.

Formal technical definition

The legal framework for SENASA's action in companion animal export is established primarily in Supreme Decree 051-2000-AG and its complementary regulations. The certification procedure is codified in TUPA CA07 (Single Text of Administrative Procedures).

The SENASA inspection process involves: review of the complete documentary file (microchip, vaccines, laboratory results, treatments), physical examination of the animal to verify chip number concordance, and confirmation that the animal is clinically fit for travel. The certificate is issued within the 10 days before embarkation for EU destinations, and 5-7 days for most other destinations.

Regulatory context in export

The SENASA certificate is the convergence point of the entire export file. A SENASA-authorized veterinarian (with active CMVP registration) examines the animal, completes the official form for the destination country, and SENASA endorses it with institutional seal and signature.

Typical timeline from Peru

  • Documentation completeness checkVerify microchip, vaccines, FAVN (if applicable) and treatments are in order before scheduling SENASA appointment.
  • SENASA veterinary inspectionAuthorized veterinarian examines animal, verifies chip and completes official destination country form.
  • SENASA institutional endorsementSENASA stamps and signs certificate. This is the document that travels with the animal.
  • Airline coordination and cargo reservationSENASA certificate must accompany animal boarding documentation.
  • Export control presentationCustoms and SENASA verify file at airport before boarding.

Common errors

  • Certificate issued outside validity period. The CSE for EU destinations is valid for 10 days from inspection. A certificate issued 11 days before is not valid at entry control.
  • Incorrect form for destination. Each country has its own certificate model. A generic certificate or one with another country's form is not accepted.
  • Incorrect microchip data in certificate. If the chip number in the certificate does not match the animal's chip, the document is invalid.
  • Vaccines unreadable or with questionable dates in booklet. SENASA may refuse endorsement if the booklet is unclear or dates raise doubts about the chronology.
  • Veterinarian without active SENASA authorization. Only veterinarians with active SENASA registration can issue internationally recognized export certificates.

Frequently asked questions

Who can issue the SENASA certificate for pet export?
Only veterinarians with active registration in the SENASA National Registry of Veterinary Professionals. The veterinarian must have a current CMVP (Peruvian Veterinary Medical College) registration and be authorized by SENASA to issue zoosanitary export certificates. Zoovet Travel has operated with SENASA-authorized veterinarians since 2013.
How long before the flight is the SENASA certificate issued?
For EU destinations, the certificate must be issued within the 10 days before the embarkation date. For most other destinations the window is also 5-10 days. The certificate cannot be issued too far in advance because it loses validity.
What happens if the SENASA certificate has an error?
An error in the SENASA certificate (incorrect chip number, wrong vaccination date, misspelled animal name) invalidates the document. If detected before the flight, a new certificate can be issued. If detected at the destination country entry control, the animal may be held or returned. Meticulous certificate review before travel is part of Zoovet Travel's protocol.
Does SENASA certify for all countries in the world?
SENASA issues zoosanitary certificates for all destinations. However, the certificate format varies by destination country: some countries have their own official forms that must be completed instead of the generic Peruvian certificate. SENASA knows the models recognized by each country and adapts documentation accordingly.
How much does the SENASA certification process cost?
SENASA fees are established in TUPA CA07 and vary by certificate type and destination. Contact Zoovet Travel directly for the total process cost including veterinary fees and SENASA fees for your specific destination.
