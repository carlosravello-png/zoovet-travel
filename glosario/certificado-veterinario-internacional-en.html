International Veterinary Certificate (CVI): the MERCOSUR model

The CVI is the official health document certifying compliance with the requirements for the international transit of dogs and cats. Within MERCOSUR it follows a harmonized model, distinct from the US CVI.

Author: Carlos Eduardo Ravello Joo Clinical review: Dra. Jessica Ysabel Camacho García, CMVP 12434 Published: 25 June 2026

Definition based on documentary practice 2013-2026 · Zoovet Travel, Trujillo, Peru

Quick definition

CVI · official health document · international transit of dogs and cats · MERCOSUR model

The International Veterinary Certificate (CVI) is the official health document, issued or endorsed by the veterinary authority of the country of origin, certifying compliance with the requirements for the international transit of dogs and cats. Within MERCOSUR it follows a harmonized model. IMPORTANT: it must not be confused with the US CVI (Certificate of Veterinary Inspection / USDA VS Form 7001), which is a distinct, US-specific document.

Formal technical definition

The CVI identifies the animal (species, breed, age, microchip where applicable), certifies the current rabies vaccination, the antiparasitic treatment, and the clinical examination, and is endorsed by the official authority of the country of origin (for example, MAPA/VIGIAGRO in Brazil). As for validity: within MERCOSUR it is 60 calendar days from issuance; from third countries, according to the agreed model (frequently around 10 days). In Brazil, the model in force is set by Portaria MAPA No. 741/2024, mandatory since 6 September 2025.

Regulatory context in export

The CVI is the international transit instrument within MERCOSUR for dogs and cats: its harmonized model allows a single format to be recognized among the State parties. The difference in validity is operationally critical: 60 calendar days within MERCOSUR provide a wide margin, while the shorter deadline usually applied by third countries forces issuance to be coordinated close to the travel date. The endorsement by the official authority of the country of origin — such as MAPA through VIGIAGRO in Brazil — is the step that gives the document validity. The most common confusion is over the acronym: the MERCOSUR CVI and the US CVI (USDA VS Form 7001) share the letters but are separate legal instruments, and using one where the other applies is grounds for rejection.

Typical timeline

  • Complete the health fileMicrochip identification where applicable, current rabies vaccination, and antiparasitic treatment. The CVI reflects what is already done.
  • Clinical examinationThe veterinarian examines the animal and prepares the data: species, breed, age, microchip, vaccination, and antiparasitic treatment.
  • CVI issuance and endorsementThe official authority of the country of origin (for example, MAPA/VIGIAGRO in Brazil) issues or endorses the CVI.
  • Validity window60 calendar days within MERCOSUR; from third countries, according to the agreed model (frequently ~10 days).
  • Presentation in transitThe CVI is presented at the destination country's border veterinary control.

Common errors

  • Confusing the MERCOSUR CVI with the US CVI. They share the acronym, but the USDA VS Form 7001 is a distinct, US-specific document with its own legal framework.
  • Miscalculating validity. The 60 calendar days apply within MERCOSUR; from third countries the deadline is usually much shorter (frequently ~10 days). Always verify the destination's deadline.
  • Omitting the endorsement by the official authority. The CVI must be issued or endorsed by the veterinary authority of the country of origin — in Brazil, MAPA/VIGIAGRO. Without that endorsement it is not valid.
  • Using an outdated model in Brazil. Since 6 September 2025, the model from Portaria MAPA No. 741/2024 is mandatory.
  • Not recording the microchip correctly. Where applicable, the microchip number must match the reader at the border; an incorrect entry can detain the animal.

Frequently asked questions

Is the Brazilian CVI the same as the US CVI?
No. The MERCOSUR International Veterinary Certificate (CVI) is an international transit document issued or endorsed by the veterinary authority of the country of origin. The US CVI (Certificate of Veterinary Inspection / USDA VS Form 7001) is a distinct, US-specific document. They share the acronym, but they are different instruments with separate legal frameworks.
How long is the MERCOSUR CVI valid?
Within MERCOSUR, the CVI is valid for 60 calendar days from issuance. From third countries, validity follows the agreed model, which is frequently around 10 days. Always verify the deadline applicable to the destination before issuing the document.
Who issues the CVI?
The CVI is issued or endorsed by the official veterinary authority of the country of origin. In Brazil, for example, it is endorsed by MAPA through VIGIAGRO. The document records the animal's identification, the current rabies vaccination, the antiparasitic treatment, and the clinical examination.
Since when has the new CVI model been in force in Brazil?
In Brazil, the model in force is set by Portaria MAPA No. 741/2024 and has been mandatory since 6 September 2025. At the regional level, the harmonized model is based on MERCOSUR GMC Resolution No. 20/24.
What information does the CVI contain?
The CVI identifies the animal (species, breed, age, and microchip where applicable), certifies the current rabies vaccination, the antiparasitic treatment, and the clinical examination, and is endorsed by the official authority of the country of origin.
