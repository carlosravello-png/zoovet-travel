Sanitary traceability · Universal requirement for international pet movement

Author: Carlos Eduardo Ravello Joo Clinical review: Dra. Jessica Ysabel Camacho García, CMVP 12434 Published: 12 May 2026

Definition based on documentary practice 2013-2026 · Zoovet Travel, Trujillo, Peru

Quick definition

ISO 11784/11785 · FDX-B · 134,2 kHz

The animal identification microchip is a passive RFID transponder the size of a grain of rice, implanted subcutaneously in the neck. Its regulatory function is to irrevocably link the animal's identity to its health documentary file. The current international standard is ISO 11784/11785, FDX-B technology, 134.2 kHz frequency.

The most important rule in export: the microchip must be implanted before the rabies vaccination that will be used for timeline calculations. A microchip placed after the vaccination invalidates that vaccination for export purposes.

Formal technical definition

The microchip is governed by two complementary ISO standards: ISO 11784 defines the identification code structure (15 numeric digits, with country code in the first 3), and ISO 11785 defines the transponder technical parameters (FDX-B frequency 134.2 kHz, communication protocol, encoding technique).

For documentary traceability, the microchip number must match exactly in: the vaccination booklet, the export health certificate, the FAVN result and the animal's travel document. Any discrepancy in the number creates a traceability error that invalidates the entire file.

Regulatory context in export

EU Regulation 576/2013, Article 17, requires that pet animals be identified by a microchip transponder complying with ISO 11784/11785 before any international movement. The same regulation specifies that if rabies vaccination was administered before microchip placement, that vaccination will not be considered valid for movement purposes.

Common errors

  • Microchip implanted after rabies vaccination. This is the most common cause of invalid files. Pre-chip vaccination does not count; must revaccinate post-chip and restart all timelines.
  • 125 kHz chip (non-ISO). Older technology chips are not read by ISO standard readers used at European, Japanese and Australian border controls.
  • Chip number incorrectly transcribed in any document. A single incorrect digit in the certificate or FAVN result invalidates the full traceability of the file.
  • Migrated or undetectable chip. If the chip is not readable at SENASA certification, the file cannot be completed. Verify reading before starting any procedure.
  • Chip not registered in database. In destinations with mandatory registration (UK, Australia), the chip must appear in the national database before travel.

Frequently asked questions

What happens if the microchip was placed after the rabies vaccination?
Rabies vaccination administered before the microchip is not recognized as valid by the European Union, Japan, Australia or most destinations. The file must restart: confirm chip presence, then revaccinate with rabies vaccine after chip placement, wait 30 days and perform FAVN sampling. The error has a time cost, not just a financial one.
Does a 125 kHz chip work for international travel?
Not for the main destinations. 125 kHz chips use pre-ISO technology and are not read by ISO 11784/11785 readers used at European, Australian and Japanese border controls. If the animal has a 125 kHz chip, standard practice is to implant a second ISO chip and use the new chip number in all export documents.
How many digits does an ISO microchip number have?
ISO 11784 defines a 15-digit numeric code. The first 3 digits correspond to the country code (Peru: 604, though this may vary by chip manufacturer). The number must be identical in all file documents: booklet, SENASA certificate, FAVN result and travel document.
Does the microchip need to be registered in a database?
Depends on the destination. In the UK and Australia, national database registration is mandatory before travel. In the EU and most destinations it is not a regulatory import requirement, though recommended. The key requirement is that the number is readable and matches all documents.
Can a chip migrate and become unreadable?
Yes, though infrequent. The chip may migrate from its original position (dorsal neck) to other body areas, or in rare cases the transponder may fail. It is therefore recommended to verify chip reading at each veterinary visit and especially before starting the export process.
