Foreign-vaccinated dog

United States · The regulatory category that decides almost the entire file of a dog travelling from a high-risk country

Author: Carlos Eduardo Ravello Joo Clinical review: Dra. Jessica Ysabel Camacho García, CMVP 12434 Published: 11 July 2026

Definition based on documentary practice 2013-2026 · Zoovet Travel, Trujillo, Peru

Quick definition

The heaviest file of all

For the CDC, the question that changes everything is not the dog's breed or weight: it is where the rabies vaccine was given. A dog vaccinated in the United States and a dog vaccinated abroad do not carry the same file, even if they board the same flight.

A foreign-vaccinated dog is one whose rabies vaccine was administered outside the United States and is recorded on a foreign certificate. If it has also been in a high-risk country, the most demanding regime in the system applies.

The logic is simple and not in the least arbitrary: the United States trusts its own vaccination chain and distrusts — by default, not by country — the chains it cannot audit. Everything required afterwards exists to replace that trust with evidence.

Formal technical definition

The category is defined by two crossed axes: where the dog was vaccinated and where the dog has been over the past 6 months. That cross produces three distinct situations, with very different requirements:

1. Dog vaccinated in the United States that has been in a high-risk country. Intermediate requirements: it may return by proving its US vaccination, with no titre and no ACF.

2. Dog vaccinated abroad that has been in a high-risk country. The full regime.

3. Dog that has only been in rabies-free or low-risk countries. Minimum requirements: the CDC recommends the vaccine but imposes no titre, no ACF and no quarantine.

And a fourth situation that is not a regime but a closed door: an unvaccinated dog that has been in a high-risk country in the past 6 months cannot enter the United States. No quarantine and no fee will open it.

There is also an age requirement that is routinely overlooked: the dog must be at least 6 months old on entry into the United States. A five-month-old puppy does not travel, however flawless its file.

Regulatory context in export

The complete file of a foreign-vaccinated dog coming from a high-risk country has eight pieces, and the absence of any one of them stops the journey:

1. Microchip readable with a universal scanner, implanted before the rabies vaccine. The number must appear on every form. 2. Valid rabies vaccination, given at 12 weeks (84 days) of age or later and at least 28 days before entry. 3. Minimum age: 6 months on entry. 4. Certification of Foreign Rabies Vaccination and Microchip, completed by the veterinarian and endorsed by an official government veterinarian (SENASA in Peru). 5. Rabies titre report from a CDC-approved laboratory — or, failing that, 28 days of quarantine. 6. Reservation at an ACF, with a direct flight to that facility's airport. 7. CDC Dog Import Form receipt. 8. Healthy appearance on arrival — a dog that arrives sick may be refused.

Every document must be in English or accompanied by a certified translation: a signed statement on letterhead from a licensed translator, with their details, stamp and licence number.

Typical timeline from a high-risk country

  • The regime is settledWhere the dog was vaccinated and where it has been over the past 6 months. Those two answers fix everything else.
  • Microchip, then vaccineIn that order. Reversing it voids the vaccine as far as the CDC is concerned.
  • Blood drawAt least 30 days after the first valid vaccination.
  • ACF reservationThe CDC sets this as the minimum lead time. The facility reviews the documents before confirming.
  • Certification and SENASA endorsementNot earlier: the form expires 30 days after signature.
  • CDC Dog Import FormOne per dog. Free. Online.
  • ArrivalDog at least 6 months old, healthy, at the airport of the reserved ACF.

Common errors

  • Assuming a foreign vaccine "counts the same". It counts — but it triggers a different regime. This is not about the quality of the vaccine; it is about traceability for the CDC.
  • Travelling with a puppy under 6 months. The minimum entry age is 6 months, with no documentary exception.
  • Submitting documents in another language without a certified translation. A translation "done by the veterinarian" does not qualify: a licensed translator is required, with stamp and licence number.
  • Confusing the regime of a US-vaccinated dog with that of a foreign-vaccinated one. They are two different files. Copying the wrong neighbour's checklist is expensive.
  • Arriving with a sick dog. An animal showing signs of a zoonotic disease may be examined and treated at the owner's expense — or denied entry.

Frequently asked questions

What exactly is a "foreign-vaccinated dog" to the CDC?
It is a dog whose rabies vaccine was given outside the United States and is recorded on a foreign certificate. If the dog has also been in a country at high risk for dog rabies during the previous 6 months — the situation of every dog leaving a high-risk country — the full documentary regime applies: titre or quarantine, ACF reservation, endorsed certification and the CDC form.
My dog was vaccinated abroad. Can I revaccinate him in the United States to simplify the paperwork?
It does not work that way. The category is set by the vaccine on record at the moment of entry, and revaccination in the United States happens after entry — precisely at the ACF, as part of the mandatory procedure. It cannot be brought forward to switch regimes.
What is the minimum age for my dog to enter the United States?
6 months of age at the moment of entry. In addition, if this is the first rabies vaccine, it must have been given at 12 weeks (84 days) of age or later and at least 28 days before entry. For dogs from high-risk countries, the CDC does not accept vaccines given before 12 weeks, even when the manufacturer authorises them.
Is a translation done by my veterinarian acceptable?
No. The CDC requires a certified translation: a signed statement on letterhead from a licensed translator, with their name, address, contact details and stamp or licence number. A courtesy translation, however good, does not meet the requirement.
What happens if my dog arrives sick?
Dogs are subject to inspection at the port of entry. If the animal shows signs of a zoonotic disease, additional examination, testing and treatment may be required at the owner's expense before admission — and in some cases entry may be denied.
