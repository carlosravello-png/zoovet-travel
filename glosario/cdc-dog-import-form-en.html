CDC Dog Import Form — the mandatory entry form

United States · Free, online, one form per dog, valid for a single arrival date

Author: Carlos Eduardo Ravello Joo Clinical review: Dra. Jessica Ysabel Camacho García, CMVP 12434 Published: 11 July 2026

Definition based on documentary practice 2013-2026 · Zoovet Travel, Trujillo, Peru

Quick definition

Free · 1 form per dog

The CDC Dog Import Form is the cheapest and most overlooked step in the whole file. It is free, it is filed online in a matter of minutes, and it generates a receipt that arrives by email. Without that receipt, the dog does not enter the United States.

Three rules catch almost everyone out: you need one form per dog — not one per family; the receipt is valid only for the arrival date you declared; and if anything changes — the flight date, the arrival airport — you must submit a brand-new form.

It is completed by the importer, owner or carrier, not by the veterinarian. It is available in English and Spanish; anyone who files it in Spanish receives the receipt in both languages, so that the officer at the port of entry can read it.

Formal technical definition

The form was created by the CDC's Final Rule of August 2024 and replaced the previous regime of paper declarations. It is a declaration made by the importer to the US government, not a veterinary certificate: nobody signs it and nobody endorses it.

What it asks for: the microchip number — transcribed exactly, digit by digit — and a recent photograph of the dog (jpg, png or gif) showing both face and body. For dogs under one year old, the photo must have been taken within the 15 days before travel: at that age the animal changes appearance fast enough that an older photo stops identifying it.

When to file it: it can be done on the day of travel itself, although the CDC recommends doing it a few days earlier. It is the only document in the file that allows that kind of margin — everything else runs on deadlines counted in months.

How it is presented: the receipt can be carried printed or on a phone screen, and is shown to the airline and to Customs and Border Protection. It is the only piece of the file that the CDC accepts in digital form.

Regulatory context in export

The form closes the documentary circuit for entry into the US, but it replaces nothing. It is an additional requirement that sits alongside the other three pillars: the Certification of Foreign Rabies Vaccination and Microchip endorsed by the official veterinary authority of the country of origin — in Peru, SENASA —, the rabies titre report from an approved laboratory, and the booking confirmation at the ACF.

There is one detail linking the form to the ACF that is worth understanding properly: the arrival airport declared on the form must be the same one where the reserved facility is located. If the flight changes and the dog lands at a different airport, the receipt is void and the booking is worthless too. One mistake, two consequences.

It applies to every dog entering the United States, whether or not it comes from a high-risk country. What changes with the origin are the other requirements, not this one.

Common errors

  • One single form for several dogs. There is no such thing as a family form: each dog needs its own. It is the most common error among families travelling with two or three animals.
  • Filing it and then changing the flight. If the date or the arrival airport changes, the receipt is no longer valid and a new form must be submitted. It is not corrected: it is redone.
  • Mistyping the microchip. A single wrong digit turns the entire file into another dog's file.
  • An old photo of a puppy. For dogs under one year old the CDC requires a photo taken within the previous 15 days.
  • Believing it replaces the veterinary certificate. It replaces nothing. It is one more layer, and the only one you complete on your own.

Frequently asked questions

How much does the CDC Dog Import Form cost?
It is free. The CDC charges nothing for this form. If someone charges you to "process" it, they are charging you to fill in an online form that the owner can complete in a few minutes.
I am travelling with two dogs. Can I put both on one form?
No. Every dog needs its own form. It is an explicit CDC rule and one of the most frequent causes of trouble at the check-in counter when a family travels with several animals.
I already filed it and my flight was changed. Is it still valid?
No. The receipt is valid only for the arrival date shown on it. If the flight date or the arrival airport changes, a new form must be submitted. The previous one cannot be edited.
Do I have to print the receipt?
It is not mandatory: the CDC accepts it being shown on a phone screen to both the airline and Customs and Border Protection. Even so, printing it costs nothing and spares you from depending on battery or signal in a foreign airport.
Does this form replace the veterinarian's certificate?
No. They are different documents and both are mandatory. The CDC Dog Import Form is completed by the importer; the Certification of Foreign Rabies Vaccination and Microchip is completed by the veterinarian and endorsed by an official government veterinarian of the country of origin — in Peru, SENASA.
