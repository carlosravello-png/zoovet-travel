CDC Import from Peru: 2024 Final Rule and DMRVV requirements

CDC Final Rule 89 FR 38450 classifies Peru as DMRVV. Since August 2024, importing dogs to the USA requires CDC registration, ISO microchip and USDA serology or ACF quarantine.

Author: Carlos Eduardo Ravello Joo Clinical review: Dra. Jessica Ysabel Camacho García, CMVP 12434 Published: 12 May 2026

Definition based on documentary practice 2013-2026 · Zoovet Travel, Trujillo, Peru

Quick definition

CDC · 89 FR 38450 · Agosto 2024 · Perú = DMRVV · ACF

The CDC regulates dog imports to the USA from a public health perspective. Final Rule 89 FR 38450 (August 1, 2024) classified Peru as a DMRVV country, requiring prior registration, ISO microchip, and approved serology or ACF quarantine.

Formal technical definition

DMRVV (Dog-Mediated Rabies Virus Variant) is the CDC classification for countries where the domestic dog-transmitted rabies virus variant circulates or circulated recently. Peru is on this list due to national epidemiological history. This does not mean every Peruvian dog has rabies, but that the US public health system applies additional controls. US-born and raised dogs returning are exempt with adequate documentation.

Regulatory context in export

The CDC works in coordination with USDA-APHIS for animal imports. The 2024 Final Rule establishes two pathways: (1) the dog has USDA-approved rabies serology performed at an accredited laboratory (result must be less than 12 months old), or (2) the dog goes to ACF quarantine in the USA. The owner must register the trip on the CDC portal before the flight and the registration document is required upon arrival.

Typical timeline from Peru

  • Start USDA serology protocolIf choosing serology path, start microchip + vaccine + wait 30 days + blood draw + result. Everything must complete before travel.
  • Register trip on CDC portalOwner registers import at cdc.gov/importation with dog data and expected arrival date.
  • Issue CSE + USDA-APHIS endorsementUSDA-accredited vet issues certificate; APHIS endorses via VEHCS.
  • CDC/CBP inspectionPresent CDC registration, APHIS certificate, serology result. If deficiencies: ACF or repatriation.

Common errors

  • Not registering the trip on the CDC portal before the flight. Mandatory since August 2024. Without registration, the dog may be detained at the border regardless of veterinary documentation.
  • Presenting RFFIT serology instead of USDA-approved serology. Verify which lab and method USDA recognizes for the serology path (may differ from FAVN required by EU).
  • Serology result more than 12 months old. The Final Rule requires a recent result. An old FAVN result valid for the EU may not be valid for the USA if more than a year old.
  • Transiting through the USA thinking requirements don't apply. If the flight has a technical stopover in the USA, verify whether import requirements are triggered in transit.

Frequently asked questions

What does DMRVV mean?
Dog-Mediated Rabies Virus Variant. It is the CDC classification for countries where the domestic dog-transmitted rabies virus variant is present or was recently. Peru is on the list due to epidemiological history, not because there is an active outbreak in Trujillo or Lima.
Can I travel to the USA with my dog if I don't have serology ready?
Yes, but the dog will go to ACF quarantine at your cost. ACF quarantine can last days or weeks until required documentation is complete. It is the most expensive and most stressful option for the animal.
Does the CDC Final Rule apply to cats?
No. Final Rule 89 FR 38450 applies specifically to dogs. Cats have separate and generally less strict requirements for US import.
My dog already has FAVN for Spain — does it work for the USA?
Depends on whether the lab that performed the FAVN is on the USDA-approved list and whether the result is less than 12 months old. A KSVDL FAVN is likely acceptable to USDA, but the specific requirements of the CDC/USDA serology pathway must be verified.
Is registration on the CDC portal free?
Yes, registration in the CDC tracking system is free for the owner. Costs are those of the veterinary process (certificates, serology, etc.) and airline fees — there is no direct CDC charge.
