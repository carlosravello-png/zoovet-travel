USDA-APHIS: everything about pet import to the USA from Peru

USDA-APHIS regulates dog entry into the USA. Since August 2024, dogs from Peru (DMRVV country) require CDC registration, ISO microchip and USDA serology or ACF quarantine.

Author: Carlos Eduardo Ravello Joo Clinical review: Dra. Jessica Ysabel Camacho García, CMVP 12434 Published: 12 May 2026

Definition based on documentary practice 2013-2026 · Zoovet Travel, Trujillo, Peru

Quick definition

USDA-APHIS · 7 CFR § 2.22 · VS Form 7001 · VEHCS

USDA-APHIS (Animal and Plant Health Inspection Service) is the USDA agency responsible for regulating animal imports. For pet export from Peru to the USA, APHIS requires endorsement of veterinary documents issued at origin. The USDA-accredited veterinarian in Peru certifies the animal's status, and APHIS endorses that certificate to make it valid at the US border.

Formal technical definition

APHIS Veterinary Services (VS) operates under the 7 CFR Part 93 framework. For dogs imported from DMRVV countries like Peru, the CDC Final Rule 89 FR 38450 (August 2024) imposed new requirements coordinated with APHIS: the dog must have an ISO microchip, the owner must register the import, and USDA serology or quarantine at an Approved Confinement Facility (ACF) may be required. The key form is VS 7001 (health certificate), manageable through the VEHCS digital system.

Regulatory context in export

Since August 2024, with the CDC Final Rule, importing dogs from Peru to the USA requires: (1) readable ISO microchip, (2) prior registration in the CDC/USDA system, (3) if the dog lacks USDA-approved serology, mandatory quarantine at an accredited ACF. The owner must plan the process months in advance and coordinate with a USDA-accredited vet in Peru for certificate endorsement.

Typical timeline from Peru

  • Verify if dog needs USDA serologyIf destination is continental USA and dog comes from Peru (DMRVV), confirm with USDA whether serology or ACF quarantine path applies.
  • Registration in CDC/USDA systemOwner must register the dog import in the CDC/USDA portal before travel.
  • USDA serology (if applicable)Blood draw + send to USDA-approved lab. Different from FAVN for EU, though may coincide.
  • VS Form 7001 + APHIS endorsementUSDA-accredited vet issues the form; APHIS endorses via VEHCS. Critical timing.
  • CBP/APHIS border inspectionCustoms and Border Protection verifies documents. If irregularity, animal may be detained or repatriated.

Common errors

  • Not registering the import in the CDC/USDA system before travel. Since August 2024 it is mandatory. Without prior registration, the dog may be detained at the border.
  • Confusing USDA serology with FAVN for EU. These are different processes even though both measure rabies antibodies. Verify which lab and which form USDA vs. EU requires.
  • Not having a USDA-accredited vet in Peru. Not every SENASA vet is simultaneously USDA-accredited. Verify the specific accreditation before starting.
  • Underestimating APHIS endorsement time. The VEHCS process can take several business days. An endorsement requested the day before travel almost always arrives too late.

Frequently asked questions

What is CDC Final Rule 89 FR 38450?
It is the CDC regulation published on August 1, 2024, that restructured dog import requirements to the USA from DMRVV countries like Peru. It introduced the prior registration requirement, mandatory ISO microchip, and the USDA serology or ACF quarantine route for dogs from high dog-mediated rabies risk countries.
How much does ACF quarantine cost if I don't have USDA serology?
Quarantine costs at an Approved Confinement Facility are borne by the owner and can be significant (several hundred to thousands of dollars depending on the facility, duration and animal size). The option of USDA serology before travel, though requiring months of planning, is usually less expensive than ACF quarantine.
Can I export my cat to the USA without serology?
Cats are not subject to the same DMRVV requirements as dogs under the CDC Final Rule. For cats, US import requirements are generally less strict: microchip recommended but not always mandatory, rabies vaccine depending on specific destination. Verify with USDA-APHIS for current status.
Are APHIS and CDC the same?
No. The CDC (Centers for Disease Control and Prevention) is under the Department of Health and regulates animal imports from the human public health perspective. USDA-APHIS is under the Department of Agriculture and regulates from the animal health perspective. To import a dog from Peru to the USA, requirements of both agencies must be met.
Where do I find the ACF quarantine list?
The updated list of Approved Confinement Facilities is on the official CDC portal: cdc.gov/importation. Costs, availability and conditions vary by facility. Advance booking is necessary as spots are limited.
