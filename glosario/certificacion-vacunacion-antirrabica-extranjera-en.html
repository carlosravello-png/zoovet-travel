Certification of Foreign Rabies Vaccination and Microchip

United States · The form the veterinarian signs and the official authority endorses · Valid 30 days, single use

Author: Carlos Eduardo Ravello Joo Clinical review: Dra. Jessica Ysabel Camacho García, CMVP 12434 Published: 11 July 2026

Definition based on documentary practice 2013-2026 · Zoovet Travel, Trujillo, Peru

Quick definition

30 days · single use

This is the document that turns a dog's clinical record into a piece of paper the United States recognizes. It is completed by the veterinarian who examines the animal and — this is the part almost nobody sees coming — it must be endorsed by an official government veterinarian of the country the dog is leaving. In Peru, that endorsement comes from SENASA.

Two deadlines govern the entire travel calendar: the form may be completed no more than 30 days before the dog flies, and it is valid for 30 days from the veterinarian's signature. It is also good for one single entry: this is not a reusable passport.

It must be printed. The original travels with the dog and is shown to the airline and to Customs and Border Protection. The CDC recommends travelling with two copies, in case the airline keeps one.

Formal technical definition

The form records the animal's identity, the microchip number, the rabies vaccination data and — where one exists — the result of the serological titre. It is the point where every date in the file converges, which is precisely why it is also where sequencing errors surface.

The microchip rule: it must be implanted before the rabies vaccination. If it was implanted afterwards, that vaccination is not valid in the CDC's eyes, and the dog must be revaccinated with all timelines restarted. This is not a recoverable technicality.

The age rule: if this is the dog's first rabies vaccination, the animal must be at least 12 weeks (84 days) old when vaccinated, and the vaccine must be given at least 28 days before entering the United States. For dogs that have been in high-risk countries, the CDC does not accept vaccines administered before 12 weeks of age even when the manufacturer licenses them for younger animals.

Who may endorse: an official government veterinarian of the exporting country. It may be the same veterinarian who examined the dog, provided that person also holds official status — this varies from country to country. Each country maintains its own list of official veterinarians cleared to endorse.

Regulatory context in export

In the export file, this form is the hinge between the clinic and the state: it is signed by the licensed veterinarian (in Peru, with CMVP registration) and endorsed by the official government veterinarian — in Peru, SENASA. Without that official endorsement the paper is useless for entry into the United States, no matter how correct its clinical content.

That endorsement is also what sets the real tempo of the trip. Because the form can only be completed within the previous 30 days and expires 30 days after signature, the paperwork cannot be "front-loaded": it has to be synchronized with the flight date. This is the document you do last, once everything else — microchip, vaccine, titre, ACF reservation — is already settled.

And there is an asymmetry worth keeping in mind: the rabies titre is valid for the life of the dog (as long as vaccination never lapses and the laboratory remains approved), but this certification expires in 30 days. A dog can hold a perfectly valid titre from three years ago and still need this form redone for every single trip.

Typical timeline

  • The veterinarian completes the formNot a day earlier: the CDC sets 30 days as the ceiling. The dog must already have its microchip, a valid vaccination and — where required — the titre.
  • Official endorsement (in Peru, SENASA)The official government veterinarian endorses the document. Without the endorsement, the form is worthless.
  • The validity clock starts30 days from the veterinarian's signature. After that, it expires.
  • Printed in duplicateThe CDC recommends two copies: the airline may keep one.
  • Check-in desk and border controlPresented on paper together with the CDC Dog Import Form receipt and the ACF confirmation.

Common errors

  • Microchip after the vaccine. The costliest error in the file. The vaccination is voided: the dog must be revaccinated and both clocks restarted — the 30 days before the blood draw and the 28 days before entry.
  • Filling in the form "in advance". It expires 30 days after signature. Doing it early does not save time: it wastes it.
  • Vaccinating a puppy before 12 weeks. Even if the manufacturer allows it, the CDC does not accept it for dogs from high-risk countries.
  • Forgetting the official endorsement. A form signed only by the clinical veterinarian, without the endorsement of an official government veterinarian, does not authorize entry.
  • Assuming it will work for the next trip. It is single use. Another trip, another form.

Frequently asked questions

Who endorses this form?
An official government veterinarian of the country the dog is leaving. In Peru, that authority is SENASA. The licensed veterinarian (in Peru, with CMVP registration) completes and signs the form; the official government veterinarian endorses it. Without that endorsement the document does not authorize entry into the United States, however impeccable its clinical content may be.
How long is the certification valid?
30 days from the veterinarian's signature, and it may only be completed within the 30 days before travel. It is also good for a single entry: it cannot be reused on a later trip.
My dog was microchipped after the rabies vaccine. Does it still count?
No. The CDC requires the microchip to be implanted before the rabies vaccination; otherwise that vaccination is considered invalid. The microchip must be implanted, the dog revaccinated, and every timeline counted again from zero — including the 30 days that must elapse before the blood draw.
Is it the same as the export health certificate?
No, even though both pass through the same authority. The export health certificate is the exit document issued by the country of origin; the Certification of Foreign Rabies Vaccination and Microchip is a United States form, on the CDC's own template, which the official authority endorses. A dog travelling to the U.S. needs both.
Can the same veterinarian sign and endorse the form?
It depends on the country. The CDC allows it if that veterinarian is also an official government veterinarian. Each country keeps its own list of official veterinarians authorized to endorse on behalf of the state, and that list must be checked — it cannot be assumed.
