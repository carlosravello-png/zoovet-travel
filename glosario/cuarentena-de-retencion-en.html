Detention quarantine — Japan

Up to 180 days · The owner pays · The animal may not leave under any circumstances

Author: Carlos Eduardo Ravello Joo Clinical review: Dr. Jessica Ysabel Camacho García, CMVP 12434 Published: 11 July 2026

Definition based on documentary practice 2013-2026 · Zoovet Travel, Trujillo, Peru

Quick definition

Up to 180 days · at the owner's expense

This is what happens when something in the file fails. It is not a fine and it is not a delay: the animal stays confined, for up to 180 days, in an AQS facility.

It applies to dogs and cats that do not meet the import requirements — and the AQS expressly includes among them deficiencies in the certificates. The animal does not have to be sick. It is enough for the paperwork to be wrong.

During detention, the animal may not leave the facility under any circumstances, except to be sent back to the country of origin. The owner may visit, but visiting hours and visitors are restricted. And the owner pays everything: care, feeding and any associated cost.

Formal technical definition

Two different causes, one consequence. Detention may follow a substantive failure —the second vaccination is missing, the titre falls short of the threshold, the microchip cannot be read— or a deficient certificate. The AQS treats both the same way.

The most frequent case, and the most avoidable: arriving with the 180-day waiting period unfinished. In that situation the animal is not sent back — it serves out the remaining days in the facility. In other words: bringing the flight forward brings nothing forward. It only changes where the dog waits, and it changes it for the worse.

Who looks after the animal. The importer is responsible for care and feeding. This may be delegated to a keeping company, at the importer's expense — but a keeping company is only stationed at Narita, Haneda and Kansai airport. At the other premises, care falls to the importer.

If symptoms appear of rabies or, in dogs, of leptospirosis, the quarantine period may be extended and additional examinations added.

The AQS further recommends that animals due to be detained receive combination vaccination and antiparasitic treatment in advance.

Regulatory context in export

Detention quarantine is the ultimate reason why every other part of the Japanese file is handled so carefully. It is not a procedure that turns out expensive: it is an animal confined for half a year, in a foreign country, in a facility it cannot leave.

And there is a logistical reality almost nobody considers when buying the ticket: if the facility is full, the AQS warns that it may be necessary to change the arrival date or even the port of entry. Detention, then, is not only the penalty for getting it wrong — it is also a limited resource that constrains those who get it right.

The way to avoid it is dull and it works: notify in time, complete the 180 days before flying, and ask the AQS to review the Form AC in advance. All three are free.

Typical timeline on arrival

  • Arrival and inspectionThe AQS reviews the file and the animal.
  • The non-compliance is detectedIt may be a health issue or simply a documentary one.
  • Transfer to the facilityThe animal will not be able to leave under any circumstances.
  • Care and feedingAt the importer's expense. Keeping company only at Narita, Haneda and Kansai.
  • End of detentionUp to 180 days. May be extended if symptoms appear.

Common errors

  • Flying before completing the 180 days. The animal finishes the wait confined, not at home. Not a single day is gained.
  • Not requesting the advance review of the certificate. It is free, the AQS offers it, and it prevents the most absurd cause of detention: a typo.
  • Assuming someone will look after the animal. Only Narita, Haneda and Kansai have a keeping company. Elsewhere, care falls to the importer.
  • Arriving with a sick animal. Symptoms of rabies or leptospirosis can extend the detention and add examinations.
  • Counting on visiting the dog whenever you like. Visits are allowed, but visiting hours and visitors are restricted.

Frequently asked questions

How long does detention quarantine in Japan last?
Up to 180 days. The exact length depends on the cause: if the animal arrived with the waiting period unfinished, it serves out the days it still owes; if there is a health non-compliance or a deficiency in the certificate, it can reach the maximum — or the animal may be sent back to the country of origin.
Who pays for the detention?
The importer, in full: care, feeding and all costs. Care may be delegated to a keeping company, also at the importer's expense, but a keeping company is only stationed at Narita, Haneda and Kansai airport.
Can I take my dog out of the facility while it is being held?
No, under no circumstances — except to send it back to the exporting country. You may visit, but visiting hours and visitors are restricted. If the animal needs treatment, a veterinarian may come to the facility.
Can an error on the certificate really send my dog into quarantine?
Yes. The AQS states it expressly: animals that do not meet the import requirements, including deficiencies in the certification, are subject to detention for the period required, up to 180 days. The animal does not have to be sick — it is enough for the paperwork to be wrong.
If I fly before completing the 180 days, is the time already waited deducted?
Yes, but there is nothing to gain: the animal serves out the remaining days in the facility. It is exactly the same wait, only confined, far from home and paid for. The AQS itself strongly recommends completing the waiting period before travelling.
