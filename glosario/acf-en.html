ACF — CDC-registered animal care facility

⚠ Regulatory alert · 11 July 2026

The CDC has removed laboratories from its approved list. A rabies serology titre is valid for life only while the laboratory that ran it remains approved by the CDC. If that approval is withdrawn, the test stops counting — even if it was correct and paid for. Read the full alert →

United States · Mandatory reservation for foreign-vaccinated dogs coming from high-risk countries

Author: Carlos Eduardo Ravello Joo Clinical review: Dr. Jessica Ysabel Camacho García, CMVP 12434 Published: 11 July 2026

Definition based on documentary practice 2013-2026 · Zoovet Travel, Trujillo, Peru

Quick definition

6 airports · 7 facilities

An ACF (CDC-registered Animal Care Facility) is an animal care facility registered by the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention. Every foreign-vaccinated dog that has been in a country at high risk for dog rabies during the 6 months before entering the United States must hold a reservation at an ACF before it flies. Peru is on that list.

At the ACF the dog receives two mandatory services: a veterinary examination and a rabies revaccination with a U.S.-licensed vaccine. If the dog arrives without a valid rabies serology titre, the reservation must also cover 28 days of quarantine. Every cost falls on the importer.

Formal technical definition

The ACF was created by the CDC's Final Rule published on 1 August 2024 (89 FR 38450), which rewrote the rules for importing dogs into the United States from top to bottom. It is not just any boarding kennel: it is a facility the CDC has inspected and registered specifically to receive dogs from high-risk countries, and it is physically tied to one particular airport.

Hence the operational consequence that wrecks the most journeys: the dog must land at the airport where it holds the reservation. Landing somewhere else and driving over does not work, and neither does taking a domestic connecting flight before going through the ACF. The airport of arrival shown on the CDC Dog Import Form has to match the one where the facility is located.

The facility itself reviews the dog's file before issuing the reservation: if the paperwork does not meet the CDC's requirements, there is no reservation — and without a reservation the airline should not board the animal.

Exception: foreign-vaccinated service dogs (as defined in 14 CFR 382.3) may arrive by sea and, in that case, do not need an ACF reservation, provided they meet every other requirement and travel with the person with a disability they assist. Emotional support animals do not fall within that definition.

Regulatory context in export

The ACF is the last link in the export file: the point where the United States physically checks what the paperwork claims. For a dog leaving Peru, the full circuit is SENASA → airline → ACF.

Who makes the reservation: the importer or the dog's owner, not the vet. You contact the facility at the arrival airport directly, with copies of the veterinary paperwork to hand. The CDC recommends getting in touch well in advance, because each facility may have additional requirements of its own.

What document it produces: a reservation confirmation. That document must travel with the dog, be shown to the airline before boarding and to Customs and Border Protection on arrival. Print it out.

What the ACF does not do: it does not issue the titre, it does not replace the health certificate and it does not waive the CDC Dog Import Form. It is an additional requirement, not an alternative one.

Typical timeline from Peru

  • The arrival airport is chosenThis is not merely a flight decision: it determines which ACF can receive the dog. From Lima, the usual connections are MIA, JFK, IAD and ATL.
  • The ACF is contacted and the reservation madeThe CDC sets this as the minimum lead time. The facility reviews the file before confirming.
  • The choice is made: with a titre or with quarantineIf the dog will not have a valid titre on arrival, the reservation must include the 28 days of quarantine.
  • Printed confirmation added to the fileIt is shown to the airline before boarding. Without it, boarding may be refused.
  • Arrival and transfer to the ACFVeterinary examination and rabies revaccination with a U.S. vaccine. No domestic flights are allowed before this step.
  • Release to the ownerWith a valid titre, the same day or the next. Without one, after 28 days of quarantine.

Common mistakes

  • Landing at an airport with no ACF. It is the costliest mistake and the most common one. A flight arriving at Newark when the reservation is at JFK voids the reservation: they are different airports.
  • Booking too late. Facilities have limited capacity and review the paperwork before confirming. Buying the ticket before you have the reservation is doing it backwards.
  • Assuming the titre replaces the ACF. It does not. A valid titre spares the dog the 28 days of quarantine, but the ACF reservation — with examination and revaccination — remains mandatory.
  • Planning a domestic connecting flight. The dog cannot fly within the United States until the ACF has completed its services.
  • Not bringing a printed confirmation. The airline may ask for it at the counter. No paper, no boarding.

Frequently asked questions

Does a rabies serology titre exempt my dog from the ACF?
No. A valid titre spares your dog the 28 days of quarantine, but not the reservation at the facility. Every foreign-vaccinated dog that has been in a high-risk country in the past 6 months must still go through an ACF for the veterinary examination and the rabies revaccination with a U.S.-licensed vaccine.
Can I land at one airport and drive the dog to the ACF?
No. The CDC requires the dog to land at the airport where the facility is located — the one holding its reservation. That same airport must appear on the CDC Dog Import Form receipt. Domestic connecting flights and transfers to other cities are not allowed before the ACF has completed its services.
How much does the ACF cost?
The CDC sets no fees: every cost falls on the importer and covers the examination, the revaccination and, where applicable, the quarantine. Prices vary by facility and by the dog's situation, so they must be confirmed directly with the chosen ACF.
Who makes the reservation, the vet or the owner?
The importer or the owner does, not the vet. You contact the facility at the arrival airport with the veterinary paperwork to hand: the ACF reviews it before confirming the reservation.
Can the 28 days of quarantine be shortened?
The CDC allows the quarantine period to be shortened if certain criteria are met, as set out in its official frequently asked questions. It is neither automatic nor something the facility decides on its own: it depends on the dog's documentary situation.
