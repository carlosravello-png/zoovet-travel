Regulation (EU) 2026/131: the new framework for bringing pets into the European Union

In force since 22 April 2026, Commission Delegated Regulation (EU) 2026/131 completes the Animal Health Law (Regulation (EU) 2016/429) and supersedes 576/2013. The substantive requirements — ISO microchip, rabies vaccine, RNATT ≥ 0.5 IU/mL and a 3-month wait — remain unchanged.

Author: Carlos Eduardo Ravello Joo Scientific review: Dr. Jessica Ysabel Camacho García, CMVP 12434 · Dr. Víctor Jesús Camacho Paz, CMVP 3103 Published: 5 June 2026

Definition based on official sources (EUR-Lex, European Commission) and documentary practice 2013-2026 · Zoovet Travel, Trujillo, Peru

Quick definition

Delegated Reg. (EU) 2026/131 · in force 22 Apr 2026 · completes 2016/429 · supersedes 576/2013

Commission Delegated Regulation (EU) 2026/131 sets the animal health requirements for the non-commercial movement of pets (dogs, cats and ferrets) into the European Union. It completes Regulation (EU) 2016/429 (Animal Health Law) and, together with Implementing Regulation (EU) 2026/705 (certificate and declaration models) and Implementing Regulation (EU) 2026/636 (country and territory lists), supersedes Regulation (EU) 576/2013 and Implementing Regulation (EU) 577/2013. The substantive thresholds do not change: ISO microchip, rabies vaccine, FAVN/RNATT ≥ 0.5 IU/mL and a 3-month (90-day) wait.

Formal technical definition

Commission Delegated Regulation (EU) 2026/131 develops Regulation (EU) 2016/429 (Animal Health Law) as regards the non-commercial movement of pets. It applies from 22 April 2026. It is complemented by two implementing regulations: (EU) 2026/705, which lays down the models of the animal health certificate (Annex III) and the owner's declaration (Annex V), and (EU) 2026/636, which sets the lists of territories and third countries exempt from the antibody titration test. The technical requirements remain in their base instruments: the RNATT under Annex XXI to Delegated Regulation (EU) 2020/692, rabies vaccination under Annex VII to Delegated Regulation (EU) 2020/688 and the microchip under Delegated Regulation (EU) 2019/2035. The health certificate is valid for 10 days from issue until the documentary and identity checks at the point of entry and, from those checks, for up to 6 months for onward movement within the EU.

The key point: the cited law and the document models changed, not the substantive requirements. Whoever complied with 576/2013 complies with 2026/131; only the legal reference and the forms are updated.

New framework timeline

  • Delegated Regulation (EU) 2026/131 becomes applicableSupersedes 576/2013 and 577/2013 for the non-commercial movement of pets.
  • End of validity for 577/2013 certificatesCertificates drawn up under Annex IV to 577/2013 are only valid if issued before this date.
  • End of the transitional passport regimePassports issued under earlier models cease to be accepted under the transitional rules.

Application for Peru and Latin America

Peru is a non-listed country under Implementing Regulation (EU) 2026/636 (classification equivalent to high rabies risk). Therefore, to bring a pet into Spain, France, Italy or any EU member state, the full protocol still applies: ISO microchip implanted before the rabies vaccine, RNATT/FAVN ≥ 0.5 IU/mL from an EU-designated laboratory (none exist in Peru — the sample is sent abroad) and a 3-month (90-day) wait from the blood draw before travel, plus the SENASA-endorsed health certificate. The same logic applies to Colombia, Ecuador, Bolivia, Brazil and most countries in the region.

Common mistakes

  • Assuming "the law changed" means "the requirements changed". The microchip, vaccine, RNATT ≥ 0.5 IU/mL and 3-month wait remain identical.
  • Using the 577/2013 certificate model after 1 October 2026. From that date only the Implementing Regulation (EU) 2026/705 model is valid.
  • Citing Regulation 576/2013 as the framework in force. Since 22 April 2026 the correct reference is 2016/429 + 2026/131.

Frequently asked questions

What is Regulation (EU) 2026/131 and when does it apply?
It is the Commission Delegated Regulation that, together with Regulation (EU) 2016/429 (Animal Health Law), governs the animal health requirements for the non-commercial movement of dogs, cats and ferrets into the European Union. It applies from 22 April 2026 and supersedes Regulation (EU) 576/2013.
Does 2026/131 change the requirements to bring my dog to Spain, France or Italy from Peru?
No. The substantive requirements remain: ISO microchip before vaccination, valid rabies vaccine, RNATT/FAVN ≥ 0.5 IU/mL from a designated laboratory and a 3-month (90-day) wait before travel. What changes are the regulations cited and the document models.
What happened to Regulation 576/2013?
It was superseded on 22 April 2026 by Regulation (EU) 2016/429 and Commission Delegated Regulation (EU) 2026/131. The certificate models of 577/2013 move to Implementing Regulation (EU) 2026/705 and the country lists to Implementing Regulation (EU) 2026/636.
Is a certificate issued under 577/2013 still valid?
Yes, under a transitional rule: health certificates drawn up under Annex IV to Implementing Regulation (EU) 577/2013 remain valid if they were issued before 1 October 2026.
Is Peru listed in Implementing Regulation (EU) 2026/636?
No. Peru is a non-listed country (high rabies risk), so the RNATT/FAVN ≥ 0.5 IU/mL and the 3-month wait are mandatory to bring a pet into the EU.
