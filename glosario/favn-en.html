FAVN — Fluorescent Antibody Virus Neutralization

Rabies serology · Antibody test for international pet export

Author: Carlos Eduardo Ravello Joo Clinical review: Dra. Jessica Ysabel Camacho García, CMVP 12434 Published: 12 May 2026

Definition based on documentary practice 2013-2026 · Zoovet Travel, Trujillo, Peru

Quick definition

≥ 0.5 IU/mL

The FAVN (Fluorescent Antibody Virus Neutralization) is the international biological assay that measures the level of rabies-neutralizing antibodies in a dog or cat's blood. The internationally accepted minimum threshold is ≥ 0.5 IU/mL, established by the OIE/WOAH. A result at or above this value qualifies the animal to travel to countries that require this test as an entry condition.

From Peru, the FAVN is processed through the Kansas State Veterinary Diagnostic Laboratory (KSVDL), the only OIE/WOAH-certified laboratory operational for Latin America. Zoovet Travel has coordinated this process continuously for 13 years from Trujillo.

Formal technical definition

The FAVN is a microplate viral neutralization assay in which the animal's serum is exposed to live rabies virus, measuring the patient's antibody capacity to neutralize viral infectivity. Results are expressed in International Units per milliliter (IU/mL) using a standard curve referenced to the WHO reference serum (NIBSC 2nd International Standard).

The ≥ 0.5 IU/mL threshold is established in the OIE/WOAH Terrestrial Manual (Chapter 1.1.8) and EU Regulation 576/2013. Results below this threshold do not authorize travel even if the animal is clinically healthy and vaccinated.

The FAVN is not equivalent to the RFFIT (Rapid Fluorescent Focus Inhibition Test). Although both are viral neutralization tests, the protocols differ and results are not interchangeable for regulatory purposes.

Blood sampling must occur at least 30 days after the last valid rabies vaccination. The ISO microchip must be in place and readable before the vaccination used for timeline calculations.

Regulatory context in export

In the pet export documentary chain, the FAVN occupies the central link: it is the technical document certifying active immunity against rabies before SENASA can issue the health certificate with international validity.

Authorities requiring it: European Union (Regulation 576/2013, Annex IV), United Kingdom (if origin is not on the "listed" countries list), Japan (Animal Quarantine Service — AQS), Hawaii/CDC (Final Rule 89 FR 38450 for high-risk countries), Australia (DAFF), New Zealand (MPI).

Who signs and certifies: The licensed veterinarian (in Peru, with CMVP registration) draws the sample, sends it to KSVDL, and attaches the result certificate to the export file. SENASA endorses the health certificate including the FAVN result reference.

Common errors

  • FAVN collected before 30 days post-vaccination. The result is technically valid for the laboratory but regulatorily invalid. The 30-day minimum is part of the protocol, not just a recommendation.
  • Microchip placed after vaccination. The European authority does not recognize that vaccination as valid. The animal must be revaccinated after microchip placement, and all timelines restart.
  • Using RFFIT result instead of FAVN. Japan, Australia, EU, and most destinations specifically require FAVN. RFFIT is not a regulatory substitute.
  • Not waiting the post-result period. A ≥ 0.5 IU/mL result does not immediately authorize travel. EU requires a 3-month wait from the result date. Japan requires 180 days.
  • Non-OIE/WOAH certified laboratory. Peru has no certified domestic FAVN laboratory. Only KSVDL and laboratories on the OIE list are recognized. Results from unlisted laboratories are not accepted.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between FAVN and RFFIT?
Both FAVN (Fluorescent Antibody Virus Neutralization) and RFFIT (Rapid Fluorescent Focus Inhibition Test) are rabies virus neutralization tests, but their technical protocols differ. FAVN is the specific standard required for international pet movement by the OIE/WOAH, the European Union (Regulation 576/2013), Japan, Australia and most rabies-free countries. RFFIT is not accepted as a regulatory substitute for FAVN in any of these destinations.
Which laboratory performs the FAVN from Peru?
From Peru and the rest of Latin America, the FAVN must be performed at the Kansas State Veterinary Diagnostic Laboratory (KSVDL) in the United States, the OIE/WOAH reference laboratory for this region. Zoovet Travel coordinates sample shipping to KSVDL with cold chain and complete result management from Trujillo, with 13 years of uninterrupted operation.
How long after vaccination can the FAVN be done?
The OIE/WOAH protocol and international regulations require that blood sampling for FAVN occur at least 30 full days after the valid rabies vaccination (the last dose before the test). This timeline is non-negotiable: a result obtained before day 30 post-vaccination is regulatorily invalid even if the numerical value is ≥ 0.5 IU/mL.
What happens if the FAVN result is below 0.5 IU/mL?
A result < 0.5 IU/mL means the animal did not reach the required immunity threshold. The standard protocol is to revaccinate and wait 30 days before repeating the test. The post-result waiting period clock (3 months for EU, 180 days for Japan) only starts running from the date a ≥ 0.5 IU/mL result is obtained.
Does the FAVN result expire?
The FAVN result itself does not "expire" as a document, but its regulatory validity is subject to conditions: the animal must maintain current rabies vaccination (booster before the previous vaccine expires). If the animal loses vaccination continuity and the rabies vaccine lapses, the previous FAVN result loses its value and the entire protocol must restart from the microchip.
