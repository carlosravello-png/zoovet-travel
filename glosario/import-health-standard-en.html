Import Health Standard (IHS) — the import health standard

⚠ Regulatory alert · 11 July 2026

Quick definition

The binding document

An Import Health Standard (IHS) is the legal document in which a country writes down, in one place, everything it requires of an animal before letting it in. It is not a guide or a leaflet: it is the rule. When an IHS and a help page contradict each other, the IHS wins.

In New Zealand the IHS for dogs and cats is called CATSDOGS.GEN and is issued by the Ministry for Primary Industries (MPI). On 12 May 2026 the MPI published a new one, which came into force on 1 July 2026 with a nine-month transition.

What it contains changes the timeline for anyone planning to travel: an official identity check that must be carried out six months before export. Anyone wanting to travel from April 2027 onwards starts counting on 1 October 2026.

Formal technical definition

The IHS is an instrument of administrative law: it is issued by the competent authority, published with a date, versioned, and its amendments take effect with explicit transition periods. It sits alongside the international veterinary certificate — the IHS says what must be met; the certificate attests that it was met.

The new New Zealand CATSDOGS.GEN introduces a mechanism that breaks the usual mental model: the pre-export identity check. Until now, verification of the animal's identity was concentrated close to the flight. The new standard requires a veterinarian approved by the government of the country of origin —for an animal leaving Peru, SENASA— to verify the animal's identity at least six months before export.

The practical effect is that the travel clock no longer starts when you buy the ticket, but half a year earlier. A requirement met late cannot be recovered: six months are six months.

Regulatory context in export

The official MPI calendar has four dates, and they are best read together:

12 May 2026 — the new Import Health Standard is published. 1 July 2026 — it comes into force, with a nine-month transition during which the 2021 and 2026 standards coexist. 1 October 2026 — anyone due to enter under the new standard from April 2027 onwards needs the identity check already done. 1 April 2027 — the 2026 standard becomes mandatory: every animal enters under it.

The date that matters is neither the first nor the last: it is 1 October 2026. It is the point from which a trip planned for 2027 becomes possible or impossible depending on what was done that day. The rest of the calendar is context.

This mechanism —standard published, transition period, check brought forward— is not exclusive to New Zealand. It is the pattern followed by islands and rabies-free countries (Australia, Japan, the United Kingdom): the more protected a territory is, the further back in time it pushes its controls.

Common errors

  • Reading the help page and not the standard. The IHS is the binding rule; the guides are summaries. When they differ, the IHS prevails.
  • Planning a 2027 trip to New Zealand without having done the identity check. This is the mistake this standard will produce on a mass scale. Six months cannot be clawed back.
  • Confusing entry into force with mandatory application. The standard has been in force since 1 July 2026, but it is only mandatory from 1 April 2027. The date that decides is the one in between: 1 October 2026.
  • Assuming any veterinarian can perform the identity check. It must be done by a veterinarian approved by the government of the country of origin.
  • Applying one country's IHS to another. Each country issues its own. New Zealand's says nothing about Australia, even though they are next door.

Frequently asked questions

What is an Import Health Standard?
It is the legal document in which a country's animal health authority sets out the entry requirements for a species coming from a given origin. It is not an information leaflet: it is the binding rule. If a help page published by the agency says one thing and the IHS says another, the IHS prevails.
What does New Zealand's new standard change?
It introduces an official pre-export identity check, which must be carried out by a government-approved veterinarian in the country of origin at least six months before the animal travels. It is a requirement that pushes the start of the whole process forward by half a year.
I want to travel to New Zealand in 2027. When do I have to start?
If you are travelling from April 2027 onwards, your countdown begins on 1 October 2026: that is the date from which the pre-export identity check must already be done. This is not a planning tip — it is the arithmetic of the six-month requirement.
Who can perform the identity check?
A veterinarian approved by the government of the country of origin. For a dog or cat leaving Peru, that means the official animal health authority —SENASA— and not any clinical veterinarian, however competent.
Do other countries have Import Health Standards?
Yes, under that name or another. Australia, Japan, the United Kingdom and rabies-free territories in general publish equivalent standards. Each one is independent: the New Zealand standard says nothing about entry into Australia, even though the two countries are three hours apart by air.
