The laboratory where the blood of dogs bound for the United States is analysed

Author: Carlos Eduardo Ravello Joo Clinical review: Dra. Jessica Ysabel Camacho García, CMVP 12434 Published: 11 July 2026

Definition based on documentary practice 2013-2026 · Zoovet Travel, Trujillo, Peru

Quick definition

CDC approved

The KSVDL is the veterinary diagnostic laboratory of Kansas State University. It is where the journey of a blood tube ends — a tube that began in a veterinary clinic thousands of kilometres away: the test that decides whether a dog enters the United States without quarantine.

It appears in Table 1 — the table of currently approved laboratories — of the official CDC list, verified on 11 July 2026. It was not affected by the purge of 12 January 2026, in which the CDC removed laboratories in Brazil, China, Japan, Russia, South Korea and Tunisia.

Alongside it, the other approved United States laboratories are Auburn University and the Veterinary Medical Diagnostic Laboratory at the University of Missouri. There is also one run by the Department of Defense, available only to United States military personnel.

Formal technical definition

The KSVDL performs the FAVN (Fluorescent Antibody Virus Neutralization), the viral neutralization assay that measures, in international units per millilitre, the amount of rabies-neutralizing antibodies present in the animal's serum. The international threshold is ≥ 0.5 IU/mL.

Why the blood travels so far. Peru has no laboratory of its own on the CDC list — and neither do most high-risk countries. The CDC rule is explicit: if there is no approved laboratory in the country of origin, the veterinarian must send the sample to the nearest approved laboratory. And the CDC does not accept results from any laboratory that is not on its list, however good it may be.

Straight from the laboratory. The result must arrive directly from the approved laboratory: the CDC does not accept a report forwarded by a third party who in turn received it from the laboratory. And it must be in English or carry a certified translation.

The timings. The CDC warns that turnaround times vary between laboratories and recommends collecting the sample 60 days or more before travel. In documented practice, international shipping plus processing usually adds up to about two and a half weeks — but that period runs inside the mandatory 28 days, it is not added on top of them.

Regulatory context in export

What makes the KSVDL a regulatory piece and not merely a technical one is a condition the CDC states in a single line and almost nobody explains: an approved titre is valid for the life of the dog — provided there are no lapses in vaccination and provided the laboratory remains approved.

In other words: the piece of paper you paid for depends on a third party — the CDC — continuing to consider valid the laboratory that issued it. In January 2026 several laboratories ceased to be approved, and with them tests that were correct, paid for and properly performed ceased to count. That is why choosing a laboratory is not a minor formality: a laboratory with a stable track record on the CDC list is safer than a recently added one, even if the price is better.

In the Peruvian circuit, the KSVDL sits between the clinic and the State: the accredited veterinarian draws the sample and ships it; the result is attached to the file and entered on the Certification of Foreign Rabies Vaccination and Microchip endorsed by SENASA.

Typical timeline from a high-risk country

  • Microchip and rabies vaccinationThe microchip always comes before the vaccine.
  • Blood sample collectionMinimum 30 days after the first valid vaccination. And at least 28 days before entry into the United States.
  • International shippingNon-infectious biological sample, with specific permits and forms. It leaves the country of origin for Kansas.
  • KSVDL resultRoughly two and a half weeks between shipping and report, according to Zoovet Travel's documentary practice.
  • The 28-day clockCounted from the sample collection, not from the result: the laboratory days run inside that period, not after it.
  • Entry into the United StatesThe original report travels with the dog, in English and issued directly by the laboratory.

Common errors

  • Adding the laboratory days to the CDC's 28. They are not added. The 28 days are counted from the blood draw; the analysis time runs inside. Confusing this makes people believe the trip is a month further away than it really is.
  • Using an unlisted laboratory. The CDC does not accept results from laboratories outside its list, without exception and however good the analysis may be.
  • Forwarding the result through a third party. The report must arrive directly from the approved laboratory.
  • Choosing a laboratory on price alone. A laboratory recently added to the list is more fragile than one with a stable track record: if the CDC removes it, the test stops counting.
  • Drawing the sample before 30 days post-vaccination. The laboratory will process it all the same, but the result will be regulatorily invalid.

Frequently asked questions

Is Kansas State still approved by the CDC after the January 2026 purge?
Yes. Verified in Table 1 of the official CDC list on 11 July 2026. The 12 January purge removed laboratories in Brazil, China, Japan, Russia, South Korea and Tunisia; none of the United States laboratories were affected.
Why does my dog's blood have to travel all the way to Kansas?
Because many high-risk countries — Peru among them — have no laboratory on the CDC approved list. The CDC rule is that, if there is no approved laboratory in the country of origin, the sample must be sent to the nearest approved one. And the CDC does not accept results from any laboratory outside that list.
Do the laboratory's turnaround days add to the 28-day waiting period?
No. This is the most common confusion. The CDC's 28 days are counted from the date the sample is drawn, not from the date the result arrives. Shipping and analysis both happen inside that window. That is why a laboratory turnaround of about two and a half weeks does not delay the trip: it fits comfortably within the 28 days.
Can I use a cheaper laboratory?
Only if it is on the CDC list. And even then it is worth thinking twice: the validity of the titre depends on the laboratory still being approved on the day of travel. A laboratory with a stable track record on the list protects the investment better than a recently added one.
Does the KSVDL result expire?
The result does not expire as a document: it is valid for the life of the dog, provided vaccination coverage is never interrupted and provided the laboratory remains approved by the CDC. If the dog loses vaccination continuity, it must be revaccinated and the titre repeated with a sample drawn at least 30 days after the booster.
