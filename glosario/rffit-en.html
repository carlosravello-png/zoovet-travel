RFFIT vs FAVN: why the wrong test invalidates pet export

RFFIT is a rabies antibody neutralization test. It technically measures the same thing as FAVN, but is NOT accepted by the EU, Japan or Australia for pet export.

Author: Carlos Eduardo Ravello Joo Clinical review: Dra. Jessica Ysabel Camacho García, CMVP 12434 Published: 12 May 2026

Definition based on documentary practice 2013-2026 · Zoovet Travel, Trujillo, Peru

Quick definition

RFFIT ≠ FAVN · No válido UE · No válido Japón · No válido Australia

RFFIT (Rapid Fluorescent Focus Inhibition Test) is a laboratory technique that measures the neutralizing capacity of rabies antibodies in animal serum. It technically measures the same parameter as FAVN (≥ 0.5 IU/mL) but uses a different methodology. The regulatory problem is that the European Union, Japan, Australia and New Zealand specifically require FAVN as the accepted method. An RFFIT result, even if positive, is not interchangeable with a FAVN result for these destinations.

Formal technical definition

Both tests (RFFIT and FAVN) are seroneutralization techniques in cell culture. RFFIT uses immunofluorescence to detect inhibited viral infection foci, while FAVN uses fluorescent antibodies to mark the live virus. Both express results in IU/mL on the same WHO reference scale. The difference is methodological and regulatory: the OIE/WOAH, the EU (Reg. 576/2013 Annex IV) and the Japanese AQS explicitly specify FAVN. RFFIT is widely used in the USA by the CDC for human assessment and some USDA-APHIS internal protocols, which creates confusion: in the USA it may be accepted in certain contexts, but for export from Peru to the EU, Japan or Australia, RFFIT is not valid.

Regulatory context in export

The error of ordering RFFIT instead of FAVN is one of the most costly in pet export from Peru. If the owner contacts a local or foreign laboratory that performs RFFIT (not FAVN), the result is not accepted in the EU or Japan, and the process must be repeated from the blood draw, implying additional months of waiting. From Peru, the internationally accepted laboratory for FAVN is KSVDL (Kansas State Veterinary Diagnostic Laboratory), which is OIE/WOAH certified. Some US laboratories offer both tests (RFFIT and FAVN); it is critical to explicitly specify FAVN in the request.

Typical timeline from Peru

  • Verify which test the destination requiresBefore any blood draw: confirm with the destination country's regulations. EU, Japan, Australia, NZ → FAVN mandatory.
  • Specify FAVN explicitlyWhen sending the sample to KSVDL or another certified lab, state "FAVN" on the request. Never assume it is automatic.
  • Check the report headerThe report must say "FAVN" or "Fluorescent Antibody Virus Neutralization". If it says "RFFIT" or "VNT", it is not valid for EU/Japan/Australia.
  • Restart blood drawIf RFFIT was obtained instead of FAVN, the blood draw must be repeated and a new result awaited. There is no way to "convert" an RFFIT into a FAVN.

Common errors

  • Assuming RFFIT and FAVN are equivalent for all countries. They technically measure the same thing, but are not regulatory interchangeable. The EU, Japan, Australia and NZ only accept FAVN.
  • Not verifying the test type on the lab report. Some labs issue reports with generic terminology. Always request that the report explicitly states "FAVN".
  • Using RFFIT because it is cheaper or faster. RFFIT may be less expensive at certain labs, but if the destination does not accept it, the saving becomes total loss of the process time and cost.
  • Trusting that the local vet knows the difference. Not all vets know the regulatory distinction between RFFIT and FAVN. It is the owner's responsibility to verify with the destination before the blood draw.

Frequently asked questions

What is the technical difference between RFFIT and FAVN?
Both are seroneutralization tests: they measure how many antibodies the animal's serum has to neutralize the rabies virus. The difference is in the detection method: FAVN uses the complete fluorescent virus marked directly, while RFFIT detects the inhibition of infection foci in cells. Both express results in IU/mL. The choice between them is, in practice, a matter of what the destination country's regulator accepts.
Why don't all countries require FAVN if RFFIT measures the same thing?
It is a historical and official validation issue. The OIE/WOAH validated FAVN as the reference method for international animal trade. Countries that adopted that standard (EU, Japan, Australia) require FAVN. The USA has its own regulatory framework for internal use where RFFIT has a tradition. When exporting from Peru to any destination following the OIE standard, FAVN is mandatory.
If I have a positive RFFIT result, can I use it for anything?
Depends on the destination. For continental USA with certain USDA programs it may be valid in some contexts. For the EU, Japan, Australia and NZ, it is not valid and the process must be repeated with FAVN. Before assuming it is useful, verify with the destination authority.
Does KSVDL perform RFFIT or FAVN?
KSVDL (Kansas State Veterinary Diagnostic Laboratory) performs FAVN, which is the OIE/WOAH certified method required by the EU, Japan and Australia. That is why KSVDL is the reference laboratory from Peru: it is OIE-certified for the correct method.
Can I ask a local laboratory in Peru to perform FAVN?
There is no OIE/WOAH-certified laboratory in Peru for FAVN. The sample must be sent abroad, with KSVDL being the reference laboratory. Any result from a non-OIE-certified laboratory, regardless of the method, is not internationally accepted.
