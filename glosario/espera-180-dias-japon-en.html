180-day waiting period — Japan

The longest period in the world · Counted from the blood sampling, not from the result

Author: Carlos Eduardo Ravello Joo Clinical review: Dra. Jessica Ysabel Camacho García, CMVP 12434 Published: 11 July 2026

Definition based on documentary practice 2013-2026 · Zoovet Travel, Trujillo, Peru

Quick definition

180 days from the blood sampling

It is six months of waiting, and it is the longest period imposed by any developed country. It is counted from the day the blood was drawn for the antibody test —that day is day 0—, not from the day the result arrives from the laboratory.

But there is a second half of the rule that almost nobody knows and that changes the planning of any family moving to Japan: the test result is valid for two years from the sampling date. And if those two years lapse, the 180-day wait does not have to be done again — it is enough to keep the vaccination current and obtain a new result.

An animal that arrives with the wait incomplete is not sent back: it is held at an AQS facility for the time remaining. But the agency itself says it plainly: it is far better to finish the wait before flying.

Formal technical definition

The date of arrival in Japan must satisfy three conditions at once, and all three are different dates:

1. That 180 days or more have passed since the sample was drawn. 2. That arrival falls within the period of efficacy of the rabies vaccine administered. 3. That it falls within the validity of the antibody test, which is two years from the sampling date.

That sandwich —not before 180 days, not after 2 years— defines an eighteen-month window in which the animal may travel. Not a day earlier, not a day later.

When the two-year validity lapses. Here is the part that saves many people half a year. If the test expires, the 180-day wait does not have to be redone, provided three conditions are met: that vaccination has been kept continuously current from the first dose until arrival in Japan; that the new sampling is at least 180 days after the first sampling; and that the new titre is ≥ 0.5 IU/ml. The AQS underlines it: it is not mandatory to repeat the test every two years.

And one condition that ruins everything: the blood must be drawn within the period of efficacy of the vaccine. A lapse breaks the chain.

Regulatory context in export

For a family relocating to Japan —or for anyone given a work transfer with six months' notice— this rule is the one that decides whether the pet travels with them or months later.

The clock starts with the blood sampling, so the first operational decision is not buying the ticket: it is drawing the blood as soon as possible. Everything else —the paperwork, the 40-day notification, the Form AC, the clinical inspection— fits comfortably inside those six months.

And it is worth saying without decoration: if the animal arrives with the wait half done, it serves the remainder confined, at the owner's expense, in an AQS facility it cannot leave under any circumstances. Bringing the flight forward brings nothing forward: it only changes the place where the dog waits.

Typical timeline from Peru

  • Second rabies vaccinationBlood may only be drawn after this dose. They can be the same day.
  • Blood samplingDay 0. From here run the 180 days and the 2 years of validity.
  • Result from the designated laboratory≥ 0.5 IU/ml. The analysis time runs inside the 180 days.
  • Advance notificationIt fits comfortably inside the wait. There is no excuse for filing it late.
  • First possible flight dayNot a day earlier. Arriving sooner means finishing the wait in detention.
  • Last possible flight dayThe validity of the test expires. After that a new one is needed — but not a new wait.

Common errors

  • Counting the 180 days from the result. They are counted from the blood sampling. The error costs weeks of unnecessary waiting.
  • Bringing the flight forward "by a few days". The animal serves the remaining days in detention, at the owner's expense. Nothing is gained.
  • Believing the test must be repeated every two years. It does not: if vaccination was never interrupted, a new test is enough — without waiting another 180 days.
  • Letting the vaccine lapse during the wait. Arrival must fall within the vaccine's period of efficacy. A lapse breaks the whole chain.
  • Drawing the blood before the second vaccination. Japan requires the sample to be taken after the second dose.

Frequently asked questions

When do the 180 days start counting?
From the date the blood sample was drawn, which counts as day 0. Not from the date of the laboratory result. The time the analysis takes runs within those 180 days; it is not added to them.
What happens if my dog arrives before completing the 180 days?
It is not sent back: it is held at an AQS facility for however long is left to complete the wait, with all care and feeding costs borne by the owner. Bringing the flight forward does not bring entry forward — it only changes the place where the animal waits, and makes it worse.
Does the antibody test expire?
Yes: it is valid for two years from the sampling date. But that does not mean the wait must be done again. If vaccination has been kept continuously current and the new sampling is at least 180 days after the first one, it is enough to obtain a new result ≥ 0.5 IU/ml. The AQS says so explicitly: it is not mandatory to repeat the test every two years.
What is the real window for flying to Japan?
Between day 180 and day 730 from the sampling date: eighteen months. Before day 180 the animal is held; after day 730 the test has expired. In addition, arrival must fall within the period of efficacy of the rabies vaccine.
Are the 180 days negotiable?
No. It is the longest period in the developed world and it admits no exceptions for urgency, for work or for payment. The only decision that shortens the real calendar is drawing the blood as soon as possible: everything else fits inside those six months.
