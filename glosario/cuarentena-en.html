Animal Quarantine — Zoosanitary Isolation in Pet Import

Biosecurity · Sanitary control mechanism in rabies-free and high-risk disease-free countries

Author: Carlos Eduardo Ravello Joo Clinical review: Dra. Jessica Ysabel Camacho García, CMVP 12434 Published: 12 May 2026

Definition based on documentary practice 2013-2026 · Zoovet Travel, Trujillo, Peru

Quick definition

10 days (AUS) · 180 days (JPN) · ACF (USA)

Animal quarantine is the period of supervised isolation to which an imported animal is subjected in approved zoosanitary facilities, before being authorized to enter the receiving country's territory. Its purpose is to verify the animal's health status and prevent the introduction of diseases — primarily rabies — into disease-free zones.

Three main regulatory models: Australia/New Zealand (mandatory quarantine regardless of documentation), USA/CDC (conditional quarantine in Animal Care Facility for animals from high-risk countries without valid FAVN) and European Union (no quarantine if the file is complete and correct).

Formal technical definition

Quarantine operates as the last filter in the zoosanitary control system: when the prior documentary file is insufficient to guarantee sanitary safety, or when the epidemiological risk of the country of origin requires it, the receiving state applies isolation in approved facilities with accredited veterinary staff.

In Australia, quarantine is 10 days at DAFF facilities in Melbourne (Mickleham), costing approximately AUD 2,000-3,500. In the USA, ACF quarantine applies for dogs from high-risk countries without valid FAVN or complete CDC documentation. In Japan, quarantine ranges from 12 hours (perfect file) to 180 days (no FAVN or incomplete documentation).

Regulatory context in export

Peru appears on the CDC high-risk country list (DMRVV) along with Bolivia, Brazil, Colombia, Ecuador, Venezuela and over 20 other countries. This means every dog entering the USA from Peru requires a valid FAVN (≥ 0.5 IU/mL) and a confirmed ACF reservation. Without both elements, the process is blocked.

The CDC Dog Import Rule came into effect on August 1, 2024 (Final Rule 89 FR 38450). ACF reservations must be made weeks in advance because capacity is limited.

Typical timeline from Peru

  • Start of protocol (long quarantine destinations)For Japan, the 180-day clock starts from FAVN ≥ 0.5 IU/mL result. For Australia, DAFF approval process takes months.
  • Quarantine facility reservation (if applicable)Australia/NZ: reserve DAFF/MPI slot 6-8 weeks in advance. USA: reserve ACF before buying the flight.
  • SENASA certificate and final documentationConfirm all documents are in order before embarkation.
  • Arrival at entry pointDocumentary and physical inspection. If file is incomplete: animal enters extended quarantine.
  • Quarantine release (Australia)After 10 days at Mickleham, animal can be collected by owner if final DAFF inspection is passed.

Common errors

  • Not making ACF reservation before buying the flight. ACF slots are limited. Without confirmed reservation, the animal cannot board at origin (airline desk verifies this).
  • Arriving without a completed CDC Dog Import Form. The form must be completed before the flight. Without it, the process is blocked at the first checkpoint.
  • Assuming a valid EU file exempts from quarantine in Australia. Australia has its own system. A file valid for Spain does not automatically mean reduced quarantine in Australia.
  • Not budgeting the real cost of quarantine. ACF quarantine (USA) can cost USD 1,400-5,000+. In Australia, AUD 2,000-3,500.
  • Traveling with a brachycephalic breed through a stopover with document control. Brachycephalic breeds face additional airline restrictions; if the stopover involves control, the risk of detention increases.

Frequently asked questions

Is Peru on the CDC high-risk country list?
Yes. Peru appears on the CDC DMRVV list (countries with endemic canine rabies) along with Bolivia, Brazil, Colombia, Ecuador, Venezuela and over 20 additional countries. This means every dog entering the USA from Peru must comply with CDC Final Rule 89 FR 38450 requirements: valid FAVN (≥ 0.5 IU/mL), ISO microchip, confirmed ACF reservation and completed CDC Dog Import Form.
How much does ACF quarantine cost in the USA?
The cost of quarantine at a CDC-authorized ACF in the USA ranges from USD 1,400 to USD 5,000 or more, depending on the facility, quarantine duration and services included. This cost is entirely borne by the owner.
Is it possible to avoid quarantine in Australia?
No. Australia applies mandatory 10-day quarantine at DAFF facilities in Mickleham (Melbourne) for all imported dogs and cats, regardless of documentary file quality. It is the strictest model in the world in this regard.
What is the difference between Australian and Japanese quarantine?
Australia applies a fixed 10-day quarantine without exceptions. Japan applies variable quarantine: if the file is perfect (pre-vaccination microchip, FAVN ≥ 0.5 IU/mL, 180-day post-FAVN wait, AQS certificate), quarantine is only 12 hours. If there is any file defect, quarantine extends until the condition is met, up to 180 days.
Does quarantine apply to cats as well?
Yes, though requirements may differ slightly. For Australia and New Zealand, cats must also undergo quarantine. For USA (CDC), the current rule (Final Rule 89 FR 38450) applies specifically to dogs; cats have different and generally less strict requirements. For Japan, cats must also comply with the AQS protocol, though timelines may vary.
