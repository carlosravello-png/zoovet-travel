Form AC — the certificate Japan requires

Filled in by the veterinarian, endorsed by the government · No pencil, no correction fluid, no crossings-out

Author: Carlos Eduardo Ravello Joo Clinical review: Dr. Jessica Ysabel Camacho García, CMVP 12434 Published: 11 July 2026

Definition based on documentary practice 2013-2026 · Zoovet Travel, Trujillo, Peru

Quick definition

One crossing-out = up to 180 days

Form AC is the certificate with which Japan verifies, on a single sheet of paper, that everything else was done properly. It is filled in by the private veterinarian and endorsed by the government veterinarian of the exporting country — in Peru, SENASA. The government veterinarian may also fill it in directly.

It must record five blocks of information: the animal's identity (with date of birth or age), the microchip number and the date it was implanted, the dates and details of the rabies vaccinations, the blood sampling date together with the titre and the designated laboratory, and the result of the clinical inspection prior to export.

And one rule that sounds minor and is not: no pencil, no erasable ink, no correction fluid, no correction tape. The AQS expressly prohibits them. Because a deficiency in the certificate —not in the animal— is enough for the dog to be held for up to 180 days.

Formal technical definition

The AQS is explicit about the consequence: "if there is any deficiency in the certificates, the dog or cat will be subject to detention quarantine for up to 180 days or will be sent back". It does not say "might". It draws no distinction between a serious error and a minor one. A badly completed sheet of paper has the same effect as a sick animal.

That is why the agency itself recommends something worth taking seriously: sending them a copy of the certificates in advance so they can review them before the trip. It is a free review that can spare an animal six months of confinement.

How it is completed. The private veterinarian fills in Form AC and then obtains the endorsement of the government veterinarian. If more pages are needed, the ATTACH form is used under the same criteria and attached to Form AC.

Electronic certificates. Since 1 December 2022, Japan has accepted electromagnetic records of health certificates, provided the government agency of the exporting country has given prior notice that it will issue them this way, that the required information appears in the record or its copy, and that it can be confirmed that the agency issued it.

Regulatory context in export

Form AC is the point where the clinic and the state meet: it is signed by the licensed veterinarian and endorsed by the government veterinary authority of the exporting country — SENASA, in the Peruvian case. Without that official endorsement the document is worthless, however clinically correct its content may be.

What sets this certificate apart from its European and US counterparts is zero tolerance for material error. The CDC form expires after 30 days; the European certificate, after 10. Form AC has no such deadline problem — it has the opposite one: it admits no corrections. If something is written wrongly, it is redone. It is not crossed out.

And there is an asymmetry worth understanding: the other Japanese requirements fail because of the calendar. This one fails because of the hand that writes.

Typical timeline before departure

  • Clinical inspectionWithin the 10 days before shipment. Free from signs of rabies and —in dogs— of leptospirosis.
  • The veterinarian fills in Form ACWith the five blocks of information. No pencil, no correction fluid.
  • Official endorsementThe government veterinarian (SENASA, in Peru) endorses it.
  • Advance review by the AQSOptional, free and strongly advisable: a copy is sent to them and they review it.
  • Import inspectionThe original is presented. If there are deficiencies: detention of up to 180 days or return.

Common errors

  • Correcting with correction fluid or correction tape. The AQS expressly prohibits it. If there is an error, the document is redone.
  • Filling it in with pencil or erasable ink. Same outcome: invalid document.
  • Omitting the date the microchip was implanted. The number alone is not enough: the AQS requires the date, because that is what proves the vaccination came afterwards.
  • Omitting the vaccine's period of efficacy. It is a mandatory field, and it is not the same as the expiry date.
  • Not requesting the advance review. The AQS offers to review a copy before the trip. It is free. Skipping it means betting six months of the animal's life on there not being a single typo.

Frequently asked questions

Who fills in Form AC and who signs it?
It is filled in by the private veterinarian and endorsed by the government veterinarian of the exporting country — in Peru, SENASA. The AQS also allows the government veterinarian to complete it directly, where the circumstances of the country justify it, provided it contains all the required information and is endorsed.
Can I fix a mistake with correction fluid?
No. The AQS expressly prohibits pencil, erasable ink, correction fluid and correction tape. If there is an error, the document is redone. It is not crossed out, not corrected, not amended.
What happens if the certificate has a deficiency?
The animal becomes subject to detention quarantine for up to 180 days, or is sent back to the country of origin. The AQS draws no distinction between serious and minor errors: a deficiency on paper has the same effect as a failure to meet a health requirement.
Can I ask for the certificate to be reviewed before travelling?
Yes, and it is the most sensible thing to do. The AQS itself recommends sending them a copy of the certificates in advance so they can review them and prevent errors. It is free and it can spare the animal six months of confinement.
Does Japan accept electronic certificates?
Yes, since 1 December 2022, in the form of electromagnetic records — but subject to conditions: the government agency of the exporting country must have given prior notice that it will issue them this way, the required information must appear in the record or its copy, and it must be possible to confirm that the agency issued it.
